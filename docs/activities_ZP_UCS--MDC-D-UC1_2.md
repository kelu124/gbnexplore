# Executive summary## UNCHAIN Project – Sustainable Urban Logistics and Low Emission Zones

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** UNCHAIN – Urban logistics and plaNning: AntiCipating urban freigHt generAtion and demand including dIgitalisation of urbaN freight
- **Alternative Names:** UNCHAIN Project
- **Location:** Madrid, Spain (living lab site); broader network includes Berlin, Florence, and others
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 40.4168° N, 3.7038° W (Madrid city center)
- **Timeline:**  
  - **Start Date:** May 2023  
  - **Key Milestones:**  
    - January 2022: Initial LEZ policy implementation in Madrid  
    - By 2025: Full city-wide LEZ coverage planned  
    - Ongoing: Micro-hub deployment, dynamic curbside management, data platform rollout  
  - **Completion/Ongoing Status:** Scheduled to conclude September 2026; currently ongoing[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:**  
  - **Area:** 604 km² (entire Madrid municipality designated as LEZ by 2025)[^3]  
  - **Population Affected:** Over 3.3 million residents, plus logistics operators and daily commuters  
  - **Investment Value:** €8.43 million total budget (EU contribution: €6.96 million)[^1][^2]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - ETRA Investigación y Desarrollo, S.A. (project coordinator)  
  - Ayuntamiento de Madrid (Madrid City Council)  
  - Empresa Municipal de Transportes de Madrid S.A.  
  - Consortium includes DHL, UPS, POLIS Network, University of Lancaster, and others[^4][^1]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Commission Horizon Europe Programme  
  - City budgets (Madrid, Berlin, Florence, etc.)  
  - Partners' own investment[^5][^1][^2]

#### **Executive Summary**
The UNCHAIN project represents a transformative step in sustainable urban logistics across major European cities, with Madrid as a flagship "living lab." Responding to the explosive growth in e-commerce deliveries and the resulting urban congestion, air pollution, and inefficiency, UNCHAIN pilots a combination of **low emission zones (LEZs)**, micro-logistics hubs, and real-time data-driven management tools. The initiative’s core objective is to optimize freight distribution by leveraging advanced digital platforms for dynamic curbside management, congestion forecasting, and safe route planning.

Madrid’s approach is integrated with its Madrid 360 Strategy and Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan (SUMP), aiming to declare the entire city a LEZ by 2025 and to deploy shared logistics micro-hubs for last-mile delivery consolidation. This fosters reduced vehicle emissions, improved air quality, and more efficient space usage. UNCHAIN’s collaborative governance model actively involves public authorities, transport operators, and technology providers to break down data silos and ensure interoperability in urban mobility data, facilitating better compliance, policy-making, and stakeholder coordination.

As a cross-city blueprint, UNCHAIN is designed to be scalable and transferable. It offers operational, environmental, and social benefits, such as cleaner air, safer streets, and cost savings for logistics operators. The initiative is an exemplar of how digitalization and public-private partnerships can drive urban sustainability, serving as a model for cities worldwide seeking to balance economic vitality with environmental stewardship[^3][^6][^5][^4][^1][^2].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Madrid is Spain’s capital and largest city, with a population exceeding 3.3 million. It is a major economic hub in Europe, with a diversified base spanning finance, commerce, culture, and logistics. Geographically, Madrid sits at the center of the Iberian Peninsula, serving as a critical node in national and international transport networks. The city faces significant challenges in managing urban growth, environmental impact, and mobility[^3][^5].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The initiative spans the entire city, but focuses on high-traffic and pollution-prone districts, including commercial centers and residential neighborhoods with high delivery density.  
  - **Historical Development Patterns:** Madrid has evolved from a compact historic core to sprawling urban and suburban districts, with increasing mixed-use developments.  
  - **Current Land Use:** Predominantly mixed residential, commercial, and institutional.  
  - **Demographic Profile:** Diverse, with income disparities between central and peripheral areas; significant immigrant and youth populations.  
  - **Infrastructure:** Extensive public transport (metro, buses), but challenged by car dependency, road congestion, and limited logistics space.  
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Regular exceedances of air quality thresholds, especially for NOx and PM10; urban heat island effects and traffic-related noise are persistent issues[^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Rapid rise in e-commerce and last-mile deliveries, straining road networks and curbside space  
  - Air pollution and traffic congestion causing health and safety risks  
  - Fragmented logistics operations and lack of real-time data for urban management  
  - Regulatory gaps in enforcing low-emission zones and sustainable delivery practices[^6][^7]
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Madrid’s policy commitment under Madrid 360 and SUMP to meet EU air quality and climate targets  
  - Introduction of new LEZ regulations in January 2022  
  - Growing public concern over pollution and urban livability[^3]
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Public sector: Madrid City Council, transport agencies  
  - Private sector: DHL, UPS, local logistics SMEs  
  - Residents, retailers, and advocacy groups with varying interests in mobility, business, and environmental health[^4]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by municipal authorities, integrated into EU urban mobility frameworks  
  - Collaboration with national government on transport and environment policies[^3][^5]
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Madrid 360 Strategy  
  - Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan (SUMP)  
  - LEZ legislation and Urban Vehicle Access Regulations (UVARs)[^3]
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - EU funding and research mandates facilitate innovation  
  - Regulatory complexity in harmonizing data sharing, LEZ enforcement, and stakeholder compliance[^5][^1]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- **Comprehensive, data-driven approach** to urban logistics management[^3][^6]
- **Strong public-private collaboration** and stakeholder engagement, enhancing innovation and buy-in[^5][^4]
- **Scalable and transferable model** with clear policy alignment and measurable impacts[^6][^4]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- **Potential uneven distribution of benefits**, with central districts favored over peripheral ones
- **Complexity of data integration** and regulatory compliance across multiple actors[^3][^5]
- **Financial sustainability** post-EU funding requires ongoing commitment

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- **Intentional focus on environmental justice** via LEZ and air quality improvement  
- Need for **continuous monitoring** to ensure vulnerable populations are not disadvantaged by delivery changes or rising costs[^6][^7]

#### **Future Outlook**
- Full city-wide LEZ coverage by 2025 as a major milestone  
- Ongoing deployment and improvement of micro-hubs and digital services  
- Potential for further policy innovation and expansion to other cities  
- Challenges remain in adapting to shifting delivery patterns, urban growth, and maintaining stakeholder engagement over time[^3][^6][^5]
