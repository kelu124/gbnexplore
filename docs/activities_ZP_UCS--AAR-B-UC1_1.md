# Executive summary## READY Project Demo Site – Aarhus

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** READY Site Aarhus  
- **Alternative Names:** READY – Resource Efficient cities implementing ADvanced smart citY solutions  
- **Location:** Aarhus, Denmark (specifically Hasle/Skejby, Trige, and Ringgården districts)  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 56.1629° N, 10.2039° E (approximate center of Aarhus)  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Start: December 2014  
  - Project End: November 2020  
  - Ongoing replication and scaling: 2020–present  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Over 400,000 m² (including Gjellerupparken transformation)  
  - Buildings: 817 apartments, 1 single-family house, 1 administrative building  
  - Population Affected: Estimated 2,000–3,000 residents  
  - Investment: Not publicly disclosed, but part of EU Horizon 2020 and municipal co-funding  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Aarhus Municipality  
  - Housing associations (e.g., Ringgården)  
  - Energy companies (e.g., E.ON)  
  - Universities (e.g., Aarhus University)  
  - Private sector partners (e.g., Racell, RAC-DK)  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union (Horizon 2020, Smart Cities and Communities call)  
  - Danish government and municipal funds  
  - Private sector investment  
  - Public-private partnerships (PPP)

#### **Executive Summary**

The READY Project Demo Site in Aarhus is a pioneering urban transformation initiative designed to accelerate the city’s transition toward carbon neutrality by 2030. Centered on the retrofitting of social housing, public buildings, and private homes, the project integrates advanced energy efficiency measures, renewable energy systems, and smart grid technologies. Key interventions include low-temperature district heating, photovoltaic-thermal (PVT) modules, waste heat recovery, and electric vehicle infrastructure. The initiative also emphasizes participatory governance, involving residents, businesses, and public agencies in co-creating solutions. By combining technological innovation with social engagement, READY has achieved significant reductions in energy consumption and CO₂ emissions, improved indoor comfort, and set a replicable model for sustainable urban regeneration in Europe. The project’s holistic approach—spanning buildings, energy, mobility, and governance—positions Aarhus as a leading example of integrated smart city development.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Aarhus is Denmark’s second-largest city, with a population of approximately 300,000. It is a major educational, cultural, and economic hub, home to Aarhus University and a vibrant student population. The city is characterized by a mix of historic architecture, modern urban development, and proximity to natural landscapes, including forests, agricultural land, and the coast. Aarhus plays a key role in the regional economy, with strengths in healthcare, education, and technology.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The READY project focuses on several districts in the northwestern part of Aarhus, including Hasle/Skejby, Trige, and Ringgården. These areas are a mix of social housing, private single-family homes, and administrative buildings. Historically, these neighborhoods were developed in the mid-20th century and have faced challenges related to aging infrastructure, energy inefficiency, and social segregation. The demographic profile is diverse, with a mix of income levels, ages, and cultural backgrounds. The area is well-connected to public transport and is undergoing significant redevelopment, including the construction of a new hospital (DNU).

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High energy consumption and CO₂ emissions from buildings  
  - Aging building stock with poor insulation and outdated heating systems  
  - Limited integration of renewable energy and smart grid technologies  
  - Social challenges, including resident engagement and equity concerns  
  - Need for sustainable mobility solutions to reduce transport emissions

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - National and municipal climate targets (carbon neutrality by 2030)  
  - EU Smart Cities and Communities call (2013)  
  - Local community mobilization and political leadership  
  - Construction of the new University Hospital (DNU), which provided an opportunity for integrated energy solutions

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Municipal government: responsible for urban planning, climate policy, and public services  
  - Housing associations: manage social housing and resident engagement  
  - Energy companies: provide district heating, electricity, and technical expertise  
  - Residents: end-users and beneficiaries of the retrofitting and energy solutions  
  - Private homeowners: owners of single-family homes  
  - Universities and research institutions: provide technical and social science expertise

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  The project is led by Aarhus Municipality, with coordination among multiple departments (urban planning, energy, environment, social services). The municipality works closely with housing associations, energy companies, and private partners.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Aarhus Climate Action Plan (aiming for carbon neutrality by 2030)  
  - Danish Building Regulations (energy efficiency standards)  
  - EU Urban Agenda and Smart Cities and Communities call  
  - National and municipal renewable energy targets

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Enablers: Strong municipal leadership, supportive national policies, EU funding, and a tradition of public-private collaboration  
  - Barriers: Bureaucratic complexity, coordination challenges among multiple stakeholders, and the need for regulatory flexibility to accommodate innovative technologies

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  The project is guided by a “whole city design approach,” integrating energy efficiency, renewable energy, and smart grid technologies across buildings, energy systems, and mobility.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Retrofitting of social housing, private homes, and administrative buildings  
  - Installation of low-temperature district heating, PVT modules, and waste heat recovery systems  
  - Development of EV charging infrastructure and car sharing platforms

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Retrofitting of social housing and administrative buildings (2014–2017)  
  - Phase 2: Integration of renewable energy systems and smart grid technologies (2017–2020)  
  - Phase 3: Scaling and replication in other districts (2020–present)

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Low-temperature district heating  
  - District cooling with heat recovery  
  - Photovoltaic-thermal (PVT) modules  
  - Smart grid ICT systems  
  - Second-life batteries  
  - EV charging infrastructure  
  - Car sharing platforms

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: PVT modules, smart grid systems, waste heat recovery  
  - Social: Participatory governance, co-creation with residents  
  - Governance: Integrated planning and stakeholder engagement

- **Integration Approach:**  
  Technologies are integrated with existing urban systems through coordinated planning, stakeholder engagement, and technical expertise.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Implementers were selected through competitive tendering, with a focus on technical expertise and social engagement.

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  Residents, businesses, and public agencies were involved in co-creating solutions through participatory planning processes.

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Community workshops and solution rooms  
  - Resident feedback and input on design and implementation  
  - Cross-departmental collaboration within the municipality

- **Project Management:**  
  The project was managed by a dedicated team within Aarhus Municipality, with coordination among multiple stakeholders and regular monitoring and evaluation.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  Total investment not publicly disclosed, but includes EU funding, municipal co-funding, and private sector investment.

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020: 50%  
  - Municipal and national funds: 30%  
  - Private sector investment: 20%

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - User fees for energy services  
  - Tax increments and land value capture  
  - Public-private partnerships

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  The project is designed for long-term financial viability through energy savings, user fees, and public-private partnerships.
