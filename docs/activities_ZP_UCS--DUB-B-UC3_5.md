# Executive summary

## RESPONSE Dijon – Positive Energy Block

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** RESPONSE Dijon – Positive Energy Block  
- **Alternative Names:** Dijon PEB (Positive Energy Block), RESPONSE Fontaine d’Ouche  
- **Location:** Fontaine d’Ouche district, Dijon, Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, France  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** ~47.3250° N, 5.0060° E  
- **Timeline:**  
    - Project inception: 2020  
    - Renovation and implementation: 2022–2023  
    - Monitoring & operation: from late 2023 onward  
    - Completion of building renovations: 2025 (full district scale)  
- **Project Scale:**  
    - Area: ~30,000–34,239 m² of buildings impacted  
    - Population affected: ~1,200 residents  
    - Investment value: Multi-million euro (Horizon 2020, EU, local and private contributions)  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
    - Dijon Métropole (local authority)  
    - Grand Dijon Habitat (social landlord)  
    - Eiffage Construction (main contractor)  
    - EDF, Kemiwatt, SIREA, ELITHIS, ART&FACT, GODART+ROUSSEL, ICAR, GEOCONCEPT (consortium partners)  
- **Funding Sources:**  
    - European Union Horizon 2020 Programme  
    - Local government (Dijon Métropole)  
    - Private sector (construction, engineering)  
    - PPP mechanisms  

#### **Executive Summary**

The RESPONSE Dijon Positive Energy Block project is a flagship demonstration of deep urban decarbonization and social inclusion in the Fontaine d’Ouche district. Funded under the Horizon 2020 programme, it transforms a dense, socially diverse neighborhood—composed mainly of 1960s-era social housing—into a resilient, energy-positive urban block. The project integrates smart energy management platforms, solar photovoltaic arrays, advanced sensors, and Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) electric vehicle infrastructure to balance energy demand and supply. Large-scale building renovations, including insulation upgrades and window replacements, dramatically improve energy efficiency, with the goal of reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 75% and lowering consumption below 60 kWh/m²/year. The initiative also prioritizes social equity and community engagement, providing improved comfort, reduced energy vulnerability, and opportunities for participatory governance.

Beyond technical innovation, RESPONSE Dijon serves as a European-scale laboratory for carbon-neutral neighborhoods, showcasing replicable business models and operational dashboards for facility managers. The project’s adaptive approach addresses interoperability challenges, fosters behavior change, and ensures broad stakeholder involvement. The resulting model offers substantial economic savings, environmental benefits, and a blueprint for other cities seeking to achieve Positive Energy Districts (PEDs) through integrated, citizen-centered strategies[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
    Dijon, the capital of Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, is a mid-sized French city known for its cultural heritage and wine industry. As of 2022, it has a population of around 160,000 within a metropolitan area of ~250,000. Dijon is a regional hub for administration, commerce, and education, with a proactive stance on climate and energy transition (committed to 40% GHG reduction by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2050)[^3].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
    Fontaine d’Ouche is a low-income, high-density residential district developed in the late 1960s, dominated by social housing blocks.  
    - Land use: Primarily residential, interspersed with public spaces and limited commercial activity.  
    - Demographics: Diverse, with significant immigrant and aging populations; average incomes below citywide median.  
    - Infrastructure: Aging buildings with high energy needs; limited energy efficiency prior to intervention.  
    - Environmental: Urban heat island effects, limited permeable surfaces, and biodiversity constraints.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
    - High energy consumption and poor building envelopes in social housing  
    - Energy poverty and resident vulnerability to price shocks  
    - Urban heat stress and low environmental resilience  
    - Lack of renewable energy integration and insufficient smart infrastructure  
    - Social fragmentation and limited civic participation

- **Triggering Events:**  
    - EU climate policy and Horizon 2020 call for PEDs  
    - Dijon’s selection as Lighthouse City and pilot for NetZeroCities  
    - Local commitment to energy transition and equitable urban renewal

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
    - Residents (tenants, vulnerable groups)  
    - Grand Dijon Habitat (social landlord, project client)  
    - Dijon Métropole (municipal authority)  
    - Eiffage Construction and consortium partners (implementation)  
    - NGOs (e.g., Ligue de Protection des Oiseaux for biodiversity)  
    - Local businesses, artists, and integration organizations

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
    - Dijon Métropole as project leader; Grand Dijon Habitat as district landlord  
    - EU reporting and compliance requirements (Horizon 2020)

- **Relevant Policies:**  
    - Territorial Climate-Air-Energy Plan (SECAP equivalent)  
    - Energy renovation targets (citywide by 2030)  
    - Zoning and planning rules for urban rehabilitation

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
    - EU funding and regulatory support for PEDs  
    - French building codes and energy performance standards  
    - Barriers: Coordination complexity, procurement rules, data privacy, and interoperability requirements[^1][^3][^4].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
    - Achieve positive annual energy balance at block scale  
    - Reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 75% in the district  
    - Enhance energy equity and reduce vulnerability for residents  
    - Demonstrate replicable PED models and business cases

- **Vision Statement:**  
    “Transform Fontaine d’Ouche into a resilient, inclusive, and energy-positive neighborhood, serving as a model for Europe’s carbon-neutral cities.”

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
    - Fits into Dijon’s climate neutrality roadmap and NetZeroCities pilot  
    - Supports EU and national strategies for sustainable cities and social inclusion

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
    Energy-positive blocks, enabled by smart management and deep renovation, reduce energy poverty, emissions, and grid stress, while improving comfort and urban resilience.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
    - Primary: Social housing residents (~1,200 people)  
    - Secondary: City, regional stakeholders, replication partners

- **Innovation Elements:**  
    - V2G EV integration as grid flexibility tool  
    - Real-time energy data platforms  
    - Collective self-consumption models  
    - Advanced storage (redox flow batteries) and predictive demand management[^1][^4][^5].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
    - City leadership prioritizing climate and equity  
    - EU funding and international recognition

- **Economic Drivers:**  
    - Long-term savings for residents and municipality  
    - Local business and job creation

- **Social Drivers:**  
    - Inclusion of vulnerable groups  
    - Participatory governance and civic arts

- **Environmental Drivers:**  
    - Decarbonization and climate adaptation  
    - Preservation of urban biodiversity

- **Technological Drivers:**  
    - Availability of smart city platforms  
    - Maturity of PV, battery, and EV technologies

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
    - Positive Energy Block (PEB) as urban module for PED transition  
    - Five Transformation Axes and ten Integrated Solutions (IS) for holistic impact[^1]  
    - Collective self-consumption and participatory urban renewal

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
    - Deep renovation of 399 housing units (insulation, window replacement, facade redesign)  
    - Installation of ~43,000 PV panels (citywide solar farm)[^3]  
    - V2G EV charging stations and battery storage  
    - Biodiversity enhancements (nesting boxes, permeable surfaces)

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
    - Initial diagnostics and planning: 2020–2021  
    - Major renovation and technology deployment: 2022–2023  
    - Full district upgrade and monitoring: 2023–2025

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
    - District energy management platform (Super EMS)  
    - Building Energy Management Systems (BEMS)  
    - Solar photovoltaic arrays  
    - Redox flow batteries and Li-ion storage  
    - V2G electric vehicle infrastructure  
    - Advanced sensors and facility manager dashboards  
    - Demand-response control systems[^1][^4]

- **Innovation Type:**  
    - Technological (energy systems, EVs, sensors)  
    - Social (community engagement, participatory art)  
    - Financial (collective self-consumption, new business models)  
    - Governance (multi-stakeholder management)

- **Integration Approach:**  
    - Real-time and historic tracking of building/EV energy flows  
    - Predictive modeling for demand/supply balancing  
    - Integration with district heating network and waste management

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
    - Competitive tendering via Grand Dijon Habitat  
    - Consortium selection (Eiffage, ELITHIS, ART&FACT, etc.)

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
    - Neighborhood workshops and participatory bodies  
    - Continuous resident training and involvement  
    - Collaboration with NGOs and local artists

- **Participatory Elements:**  
    - Citizen workshops, artistic projects within the block  
    - Feedback channels for operational dashboards

- **Project Management:**  
    - Work package structure (WP6, TA#1–3)  
    - Cross-sectoral coordination led by Dijon Métropole

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
    - Not publicly disclosed in full; estimated tens of millions of euros[^1][^2]

- **Funding Mix:**  
    - EU Horizon 2020 grant (primary)  
    - Local government, private sector, PPP contributions

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
    - Savings from energy self-production  
    - Reduced peak tariffs  
    - Integration with district energy billing

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
    - Emphasis on long-term reduction of energy costs for residents  
    - Replicable business models for PEDs in other cities

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
    - Interoperability of varied energy systems and data platforms  
    - Supplier changes and delays in battery/V2G deployment  
    - Adapting legacy buildings to advanced retrofit standards[^1]

- **Financial Challenges:**  
    - Budget management and cost overruns  
    - Ensuring affordability for residents

- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
    - Engaging diverse, sometimes vulnerable populations  
    - Behavior change and adoption of new technologies

- **Administrative Challenges:**  
    - Complex coordination among multiple stakeholders  
    - Regulatory compliance and reporting

- **Environmental Challenges:**  
    - Minimizing construction impacts  
    - Preserving urban biodiversity during renovation

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
    - Flexibility in supplier and technology selection  
    - Continuous project monitoring and adjustment  
    - Enhanced communication via civic workshops

- **Design Modifications:**  
    - Shift of battery supplier and adjustment of V2G location  
    - Additional biodiversity measures (nesting boxes, permeable surfaces)[^2]

- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
    - Importance of stakeholder buy-in and ongoing training  
    - Need for robust interoperability standards  
    - Value of participatory governance for trust-building

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
    - Renovation of 399 homes (insulation, windows, facades)[^2]  
    - Installation of advanced energy systems (PV, batteries, V2G)

- **Service Delivery:**  
    - Improved energy comfort and indoor climate  
    - Real-time energy monitoring and management platforms

- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
    - ~75% reduction in GHG emissions in district[^3][^5]  
    - Consumption below 60 kWh/m²/year  
    - 30,000–34,239 m² of buildings now energy-positive[^5]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
    - Reduced energy bills and vulnerability  
    - Increased comfort and health  
    - Civic engagement through participatory art and workshops

- **Equity Outcomes:**  
    - Targeting low-income and vulnerable populations  
    - Job creation for local residents (13,000 integration hours)[^2]

- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
    - No major displacement reported; project focused on preserving resident mix

- **Social Capital:**  
    - Strengthened networks and participatory governance

- **Cultural Impacts:**  
    - Artistic projects embedded in daily life

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
    - Construction, technical, and integration roles created locally[^2]

- **Economic Activity:**  
    - Boost to local suppliers and service providers

- **Property Values:**  
    - Likely increase in social housing asset value (energy label D to A)

- **Cost Savings:**  
    - Lower energy costs for residents  
    - Reduced municipal energy expenditures

- **Economic Inclusion:**  
    - Focused on supporting social housing and vulnerable groups

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**

- **Environmental Quality:**

- **Policy Influence:**  
    - Model for future French/EU PEDs

- **Transparency and Accountability:**

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
