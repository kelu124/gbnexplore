# Executive summary

## mySMARTLife – Smart City Lighthouse Project

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** mySMARTLife – Smart City Lighthouse Project
- **Alternative Names:** My Smart Life; H2020 MySMARTLife
- **Location:** Nantes, France (Île de Nantes focus, metropolitan scope); also Hamburg (Germany) and Helsinki (Finland)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Nantes – approximately 47.2184° N, 1.5536° W; Île de Nantes spans 4.9 km by 1 km within the city center[^1][^2].
- **Timeline:** December 2016 – November 2021 (core project duration)[^3][^4]; ongoing impact and follow-up actions.
- **Project Scale:** 
  - **Area:** Île de Nantes (demo district), metropolitan Nantes (600,000 inhabitants, 24 municipalities)[^1][^2].
  - **Population Affected:** Directly, residents of Île de Nantes; indirectly, wider metropolitan population.
  - **Investment Value:** €21.5 million total, including €18.6 million from EU Horizon 2020[^5].
- **Leading Organization(s):** Nantes Métropole, Cerema, ENGIE, Nantes Métropole Habitat, Semitan, Armines, Atlanpole, Télécom Bretagne, Ecole des Mines de Nantes, Enedis, Nobatek[^5][^3].
- **Funding Sources:** EU Horizon 2020, municipal funds, private sector partners, research grants[^5].

#### **Executive Summary**
The mySMARTLife project, launched under the EU Horizon 2020 program, is a flagship smart city initiative aimed at transforming urban neighborhoods through integrated energy management, digital innovation, and citizen engagement. In Nantes, the project focused on the Île de Nantes district, implementing a suite of forty-seven coordinated actions across new construction, retrofitting, mobility, and data infrastructure. Key interventions included the creation of a digital twin for urban energy flows, deployment of IoT sensors and smart meters, optimization of district heating networks, large-scale renewable energy installations, and the rollout of electric mobility options such as e-buses and EV charging stations. An urban data platform was developed to centralize real-time information and enable both public administration and citizen access to energy and mobility data.

The project’s strategic objectives were to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, boost the share of renewables, empower citizens through transparent data and co-creation opportunities, and build a scalable toolkit for urban sustainability. mySMARTLife’s approach addressed legacy infrastructure challenges, interoperability issues, and the complexities of stakeholder engagement in a rapidly growing urban context. The initiative’s outcomes included measurable increases in renewable energy use, improved district operation efficiency, cost savings, and progress toward climate neutrality targets. As a model for replication, mySMARTLife offers actionable lessons in digital urban transformation, participatory planning, and multi-level governance, and has been recognized for its contribution to sustainable city management, including a Eurocities Award in 2023[^6].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Nantes, the sixth largest city in France, is a dynamic urban center with a metropolitan population of about 600,000. The city has experienced rapid growth (22% increase over 25 years), with 100,000 new residents expected by 2030. Nantes is recognized for its high quality of life, economic vitality, and leadership in creative urban planning and sustainable development[^1][^2].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Île de Nantes** is a centrally located island district (4.9 km x 1 km), bounded by the Loire River, historically industrial with ongoing transformation into a mixed-use urban hub. The area features new residential and commercial developments, retrofitted multi-owner buildings, and innovative public spaces.
  - **Land Use:** Mixed-use, with new construction, retrofitting of existing residential blocks, public infrastructure, and commercial spaces[^1][^3].
  - **Demographics:** Diverse, including social housing, young professionals, families, and a growing elderly population.
  - **Infrastructure:** High-performance district heating, electric mobility options, robust digital networks, and pilot renewable energy installations.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban heat island effect, air quality challenges, potential flooding risk due to river proximity.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Rising energy consumption and GHG emissions.
  - Aging building stock with poor energy performance.
  - Need for scalable renewable energy integration.
  - Mobility congestion and fossil fuel dependence.
  - Data silos and lack of real-time urban monitoring.
- **Triggering Events:** Commitment to EU climate targets, selection for H2020 funding, local demand for sustainable development, and recognition of demographic pressures[^5][^2].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Municipal government, housing agencies, urban planners, technology partners, citizen groups, and service operators.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Led by Nantes Métropole, coordinated with local housing, transport, and energy authorities; supported by EU frameworks and cross-city partnerships[^5][^3].
- **Relevant Policies:** Local Climate Plan, SCoT (territorial coherence), PDU (urban mobility), PLUm (urban planning), PLH (housing)[^5][^3].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** Support from EU directives, local procurement rules promoting sustainability, citizen engagement mandates; challenges included data privacy regulations and integration with legacy systems.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:** 
  - Decarbonize urban energy and mobility systems.
  - Increase renewable energy share and energy efficiency.
  - Empower citizens through data transparency and participation.
  - Develop scalable, replicable models for other cities.
- **Vision Statement:** Transform Nantes into a climate-neutral, digitally enabled, and socially inclusive urban environment through innovation and participatory governance[^2][^6].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Integrated with Nantes’ citywide sustainability strategy, EU Smart City Lighthouse objectives, and regional development plans[^5][^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** Upgrading energy infrastructure and digital systems, combined with citizen engagement, leads to reduced emissions, improved efficiency, and enhanced quality of life.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents of Île de Nantes and metropolitan Nantes, local businesses, public agencies, and marginalized populations (e.g., social housing)[^1][^2].
- **Innovation Elements:** Urban digital twin, open data platforms, hybrid solar-thermal systems, electric mobility rollout, participatory retrofitting schemes[^1][^7].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:** EU climate commitments, local leadership on sustainability, public demand for transparency.
- **Economic:** Attract investment, reduce operational costs, stimulate green jobs.
- **Social:** Address energy poverty, foster inclusion, improve health and safety.
- **Environmental:** Meet climate neutrality targets, enhance resilience, manage resources sustainably.
- **Technological:** Leverage IoT, big data, and digital platforms for smarter urban management[^4].

---

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Guided by sustainable urbanism, climate adaptation, and digital transformation principles.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Zone 1: New builds, high-performance district heating, smart public lighting.
  - Zone 2: Retrofitting multi-owner buildings and houses (insulation, solar, smart thermostats).
  - Zone 3: Citywide mobility and infrastructure (e-buses, charging stations, urban data platform)[^1][^2].
- **Scope and Phasing:** Sequenced rollout starting with baseline diagnosis, followed by pilot interventions and broader deployment over five years[^3].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Urban digital twin and data analytics platform.
  - IoT sensors, smart meters, building management systems.
  - Hybrid solar thermal/PV, battery storage, district heating optimization.
  - Digital boilers in social housing and public facilities.
  - Smart charging infrastructure, e-buses, e-bikes, EV stations.
  - Urban data platform and citizen engagement web tools ("monprojetrenov", solar cadaster)[^1][^2][^7].
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digital systems, renewables), social (citizen co-creation), financial (innovative retrofitting finance), governance (participatory planning)[^6].
- **Integration Approach:** Retrofit legacy systems, ensure interoperability, use open specifications, engage multi-sector partners[^1][^7].

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Environmental clauses in tenders, selection of qualified firms for retrofitting and tech deployment[^5].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Multi-phase citizen dialogue, participatory workshops, feedback loops for co-creation[^3].
- **Participatory Elements:** Residents audit homes, join collective PV projects, contribute to scenario analyses and urban planning.
- **Project Management:** Consortium oversight, municipal coordination, regular reporting to EU bodies, adaptive management structures[^5][^3].

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** €21.5 million total; €4 million for Nantes actions[^5].
- **Funding Mix:** 86% EU H2020 grant, remainder from city and partners.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Energy savings, service fees, potential land value increases.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Long-term costs offset by operational savings, scalable models for replication, ongoing maintenance built into city budgets[^5][^2].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:** Integrating legacy infrastructure, ensuring interoperability, managing complex data flows, grid impacts from e-mobility[^1][^7].
- **Financial:** High upfront costs, balancing grants and local contributions, risk of budget overruns.
- **Social and Political:** Achieving broad citizen engagement, managing resistance to change, ensuring equitable access.
- **Administrative:** Navigating EU reporting, coordinating across multiple agencies and partners.
- **Environmental:** Addressing site-specific constraints, mitigating unintended impacts from new infrastructure.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Iterative pilot testing, flexible design modifications, responsive data-driven management.
- **Design Modifications:** Adjusted retrofitting schemes, added new citizen engagement tools, refined district heating operations.
- **Lessons Learned:** Importance of interoperability, value of transparent communication, need for ongoing stakeholder involvement[^3][^6].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:** 
  - Retrofit of multi-owner buildings and houses.
  - Installation of digital boilers, solar PV, battery storage.
  - New e-bus fleet and charging infrastructure.
  - Deployment of urban data platform and public Wi-Fi[^1][^2][^3].
- **Service Delivery:** Enhanced district heating, smart energy management, new mobility options, energy audit and retrofitting support for residents.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 
  - >40 actions in Nantes[^5].
  - 22 e-buses, 90 EV charging stations, 1,500 e-bikes, 200 bike charging plugs.
  - Solar PV installations (30,000 m² roof area, 5MWp capacity)[^1][^2].

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:** Improved comfort and health, reduced energy bills, increased urban safety, stronger civic engagement.
- **Equity Outcomes:** Targeted retrofitting and renewable energy projects in social housing, inclusive engagement strategies.
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No reported large-scale displacement; efforts to ensure benefits for vulnerable populations.
- **Social Capital:** Expanded citizen networks, participatory platforms, collective self-consumption projects.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Preservation of district identity, innovation in public space use.

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:** Construction, retrofitting, tech deployment, maintenance.
- **Economic Activity:** Attracted investment, supported local green businesses, increased property values.
- **Cost Savings:** Reduced energy bills for residents, lower operational costs for city services.
- **Economic Inclusion:** Programs for social housing, accessible energy retrofitting, citizen solar projects.

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate and Emissions:** Significant GHG reduction through energy optimization, renewables, and clean mobility[^1][^2].
- **Resource Management:** Improved energy efficiency, water and material savings in retrofitting.
- **Environmental Quality:** Enhanced air quality, quieter streets with e-mobility, increased green infrastructure.
- **Waste and Pollution:** Efficient waste management, lower emissions from heating and transport.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Progress toward EU climate neutrality targets, EcoDistrict certification, strong performance on renewable energy share (e.g., 54% RES thermal production estimate)[^3].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Institutional Capacity:** Strengthened data management, improved cross-sector collaboration.
- **Policy Influence:** Informed new procurement standards, urban energy and mobility policies.
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Open data platforms, citizen audit tools, participatory planning.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:** Increased renewables, reduced emissions, expanded citizen engagement, scalable toolkit for replication.
- **Unintended Consequences:** Some delays from technical integration; learning curve for digital platforms.
- **Long-term Viability:** Built-in mechanisms for maintenance, ongoing data collection, potential for regional scaling.

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**
- **Contextual Dependencies:** Success supported by strong local governance, EU funding, and existing digital infrastructure.
- **Critical Success Factors:** Integrated planning, robust stakeholder engagement, adaptive management, interoperable tech systems.
- **Scalability:** Model tested in Hamburg and Helsinki, designed for European urban contexts; modular actions (e.g., PV cadaster, digital retrofitting desk) transferable[^5][^6].

#### **Comparative Analysis**
- **Similar Initiatives:** Shares features with other EU Lighthouse projects (e.g., SmartEnCity, REMOURBAN).
- **Best Practices:** Data-driven decision-making, open specification frameworks, citizen co-creation, hybrid financing models.
- **Cautionary Lessons:** Need for upfront planning on legacy integration, ongoing engagement to avoid digital exclusion.

#### **Parallels to [Your Specific Use Case]**
- **Contextual Similarities:** Urban energy transition in a fast-growing city, balancing retrofitting and new builds, need for integrated data platforms.
- **Methodological Parallels:** Centralized monitoring, scenario analysis, customizable stakeholder reports.
- **Technological Overlaps:** IoT sensors, digital twins, solar cadaster, smart metering.
- **Stakeholder Dynamics:** Multi-actor governance, participatory retrofitting, public-private partnerships.
- **Transferable Strategies:** Use of web-based platforms for citizen engagement, modular energy retrofitting schemes.
- **Adaptation Considerations:** Tailor citizen engagement to local context, assess digital infrastructure readiness, ensure inclusion of vulnerable groups.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- Comprehensive integration of digital, energy, and mobility systems.
- High level of citizen engagement and co-creation.
- Strong performance against climate and sustainability targets.
- Recognition by European awards and policy circles[^6].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- Complexity of retrofitting legacy infrastructure.
- Challenges in achieving full interoperability.
- Some actions required lengthy adaptation and iterative redesign.
- Digital exclusion risk for less tech-savvy populations.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Proactive inclusion of social housing and marginalized groups.
- Commitment to transparency and participatory planning.
- Ongoing need to monitor equity outcomes and avoid indirect gentrification.

#### **Future Outlook**
- Continued scaling across metropolitan Nantes and potentially wider region.
- Ongoing data-driven management and refinement.
- Next phases may include deeper integration of AI, expansion of citizen-led energy projects, and broader regional replication.
- Challenges remain in long-term financial sustainability, digital literacy, and adapting to evolving urban conditions[^1][^5][^3][^6].
