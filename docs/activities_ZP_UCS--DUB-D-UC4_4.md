# Executive summary## mySMARTLife Urban Digital Platform

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** mySMARTLife Urban Digital Platform
- **Alternative Names:** mySMARTLife Nantes, H2020 mySMARTLife project
- **Location:** Nantes, Île de Nantes (demonstration area), Nantes Metropolitan Area, France
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 47.2173° N, 1.5536° W (City center)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Launch: December 2016  
  - Key Milestones:  
    - 2016–2018: Context analysis, planning, and initial demonstration setup  
    - 2018–2020: Major technology deployment (digital platform, sensors, e-mobility, retrofit actions)  
    - 2020–2021: Monitoring, impact assessment, replication planning  
  - Status: Completed main demonstration phase by 2021, ongoing replication and scaling activities
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Île de Nantes (4.9 km x 1 km), plus citywide digital platform coverage  
  - Population affected: Up to 600,000 (metropolitan area); direct demonstration impacts in several thousand dwellings and public buildings  
  - Investment value: €21.5 million total (with €18.6 million European Commission H2020 grant); €4M+ in Nantes-specific actions[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Nantes Métropole (city authority); consortium of 30 European partners including Cerema, ENGIE, Enedis, Nantes Métropole Habitat, Semitan, Nobatek, Atlanpole, Télécom Bretagne, Ecole des Mines de Nantes[^1]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union H2020 program  
  - Public funds (Nantes Métropole)  
  - Private sector (ENGIE, Enedis, contractors)  
  - PPP elements for certain subprojects

#### **Executive Summary**

mySMARTLife Nantes was conceived as a flagship *smart city* demonstration under the EU Horizon 2020 program, targeting deep decarbonization, digital transformation, and citizen-centric urban innovation. The initiative centered on the Île de Nantes district as a living laboratory, deploying an urban digital platform that integrated *digital twins, IoT sensors, cloud-based analytics,* and automated demand response across public buildings and neighborhoods[^2][^3]. These technologies enabled real-time monitoring and control of energy use, supported data-driven city planning, and facilitated automated sustainability actions—such as grid-responsive demand management and optimization of district heating. The project complemented digital innovation with ambitious physical retrofits (building insulation, renewable energy systems), large-scale e-mobility rollouts (electric buses and charging infrastructure), and citizen engagement platforms for energy retrofitting and solar adoption.

A total of 47 actions in Nantes addressed new construction, retrofitting, mobility, and open data, with the digital platform serving as the backbone for coordination, monitoring, and impact assessment. The project achieved notable outcomes, including a **30% reduction in municipal building energy use**, enhanced public service resilience, and substantial reductions in citywide CO₂ emissions. Implementation required overcoming challenges in data harmonization, cross-departmental integration, and ongoing citizen engagement. The initiative demonstrated the feasibility and benefits of *data-driven urban energy management* and informed scalable models for replication across Europe[^1][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Nantes is France’s sixth largest city, with a metropolitan population of over 600,000. It is recognized for advanced urban planning, cultural vibrancy, and sustainable development leadership. The city has experienced a 22% population increase over 25 years, with growth expected to continue, challenging its infrastructure and environmental standards[^2].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Île de Nantes**: Historic island at the city center, bounded by two Loire River branches.  
  - **Development patterns**: Former industrial district, now a focus of urban renewal and innovation.  
  - **Land use**: Mixed-use (residential, commercial, public spaces, mobility infrastructure).  
  - **Demographics**: Diverse, with a mix of social housing, young professionals, and public service users.  
  - **Infrastructure**: Upgraded district heating, public transport, and digital networks.  
  - **Environmental constraints:** Flood risk, legacy pollution, need for climate adaptation[^2].
- **Pre-Initiative Conditions:**  
  - **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
    - Rising energy consumption and CO₂ emissions  
    - Inefficient public building stock  
    - Need for integrated mobility and energy systems  
    - Disconnected data silos across city services  
    - Social equity concerns and public engagement gaps  
  - **Triggering Events:**  
    - EU H2020 funding opportunity (2016)  
    - Local climate action commitments  
    - Anticipation of population growth and strain on resources  
  - **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
    - City agencies, building owners, utility providers, technology firms, housing associations, local citizens[^1]
- **Policy and Regulatory Environment:**  
  - **Governance Framework:** Nantes Métropole as lead agency, collaborating with national and EU entities  
  - **Relevant Policies:**  
    - SCoT (urban development), PDU (mobility), PLUm (land use), PLH (housing), local Climate Plan[^1]  
  - **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
    - EU data privacy and interoperability standards  
    - Local procurement rules (green clauses in tenders)  
    - Multi-owner building governance complexities

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Transform Nantes into a model smart city with integrated energy, mobility, and digital platforms  
  - Drastically reduce municipal energy use and citywide CO₂ emissions  
  - Enhance citizen quality of life and urban resilience[^2][^1]
- **Vision Statement:**  
  Build a digitally enabled, sustainable, and inclusive urban environment, pioneering data-driven energy management for European cities.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  The initiative aligns with Nantes’ Climate Plan, the EU’s Smart Cities Marketplace, and France’s national energy transition goals[^2][^1].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By deploying digital platforms and IoT for real-time energy control, combined with physical retrofits and mobility upgrades, the city can optimize resource use, reduce emissions, and improve public service delivery.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Municipal building users  
  - Residents of retrofitted homes and social housing  
  - Public transport users  
  - City planners and policymakers
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - City-scale digital twin and open data urban platform  
  - Automated demand response systems  
  - Citizen-facing retrofit and solar adoption platforms  
  - Integration of energy, mobility, and ICT systems[^2][^3]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** Local government commitment to sustainability; leveraging EU leadership status.
- **Economic Drivers:** Public cost savings; investment in green industries; attracting tech innovation.
- **Social Drivers:** Equity in energy access; improved public engagement; better urban services.
- **Environmental Drivers:** Climate neutrality targets; adaptation to population growth and environmental risks.
- **Technological Drivers:** Emergence of IoT, big data, cloud analytics, and digital twin solutions; smart mobility trends[^2][^1][^3].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Integrated urban regeneration, climate adaptation, and digital transformation—guided by smart city principles and participatory planning.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - **Zone 1:** New construction with high-performance district heating, smart energy, and mobility systems  
  - **Zone 2:** Retrofitting multi-owner buildings and individual houses (insulation, smart thermostats, solar hybrid systems)  
  - **Zone 3:** Citywide actions (e-mobility, urban data platform, public lighting upgrades)[^2][^3]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  Staged deployment: initial context analysis, demonstration pilots, scaling to metropolitan level, impact assessment[^1].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Urban digital twin and cloud analytics  
  - IoT sensors for real-time energy and environment monitoring  
  - Automated demand response and smart metering  
  - Digital boilers and hybrid solar systems  
  - Open data APIs and citizen retrofit platforms (“monprojetrenov”)[^2][^3]
- **Innovation Type:** Primarily technological with governance and social innovation (citizen engagement platforms, new financing models).
- **Integration Approach:**  
  Extension of existing city information systems; interoperability across building, transport, and utility networks[^2].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Competitive tenders with environmental clauses; partnerships with tech firms (ENGIE, Enedis).
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Public consultations for project planning  
  - Citizen platforms for retrofit and solar adoption  
  - Demonstration pilots in social housing and schools[^2][^1]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - “monprojetrenov” platform for citizen-led retrofitting  
  - Citizen solar projects and collective self-consumption pilots[^2]
- **Project Management:**  
  Consortium coordination (30 partners), local steering committees, periodic EU reporting[^1].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total: €21.5M  
  - Nantes-specific: €4M+  
  - Cost breakdown: digital platform, retrofit actions, mobility infrastructure, citizen engagement[^1]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - 86% EU subsidy (H2020); remainder from local, regional, and private sources[^1]
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Not primarily revenue-generating, but cost savings for public services and energy bills.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Ongoing value through energy savings, reduced operating costs, and scalable business models for retrofit and solar diffusion[^1].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Data harmonization across legacy systems and departments  
  - Integration of IoT devices and interoperability issues  
  - Scaling from pilot to citywide deployment[^2][^1]
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Complexity of multi-source funding and grant management  
  - Long-term sustainability of digital services post-grant
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Ensuring meaningful citizen engagement and co-creation  
  - Overcoming resistance in multi-owner buildings[^1]
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Coordination among 30 consortium partners  
  - Bureaucratic delays and compliance with EU reporting
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Legacy pollution in Île de Nantes  
  - Flood risks and climate adaptation requirements

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Modular digital platform architecture for easier integration  
  - Dedicated “single desk” for energy retrofitting (monprojetrenov)[^2]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Iterative prototyping and phased rollouts  
  - Adjustments to retrofit plans based on building owner buy-in
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of open data standards and cross-sectoral collaboration  
  - Need for ongoing citizen engagement beyond initial pilots[^1]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Retrofitted social housing and individual homes (insulation, smart thermostats, solar systems)  
  - New municipal buildings with digital boilers  
  - E-mobility infrastructure (22 electric buses, 90+ charging stations)  
  - Urban digital platform and open data APIs[^2][^1][^3]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Improved energy management in public buildings  
  - Citizen-facing information and audit services for retrofitting
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 30% reduction in municipal building energy use  
  - 5 MWp solar PV capacity deployed in demonstration areas  
  - 47 actions completed in Nantes; 150+ across all lighthouse cities[^1][^4]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Public cost savings  
  - Enhanced resilience and service continuity  
  - Citizen empowerment via digital engagement platforms[^2][^1]
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Social housing prioritized for retrofit pilots  
  - Platforms accessible to all residents
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No significant displacement reported; focus on inclusion
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Increased civic engagement through participatory platforms
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Reinforcement of Nantes’ identity as a leader in sustainable urban innovation

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Up to several hundred jobs in retrofitting, technology deployment, and public transport upgrades
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Attracted investment in green technologies  
  - Stimulated local construction and energy services sectors
- **Property Values:**  
  - Likely uplift in retrofitted and smart-enabled districts
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Lower energy bills for municipal buildings and retrofitted homes
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Subsidies and support for low-income households in retrofit programs

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Significant reduction in city CO₂ emissions (quantified for municipal buildings; broader city impact ongoing)  
  - Contribution to climate neutrality goals[^2][^1][^4]
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Smarter energy use; renewable energy integration; district heating optimization
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality through e-bus deployment  
  - Noise reduction in public transport corridors
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Digital boilers repurpose waste heat from data centers[^2]
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Performance assessed against local and EU standards; ongoing monitoring via digital platform[^1]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened data-driven planning and cross-departmental collaboration  
  - New models for citizen engagement and participatory governance
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Informed new sustainable procurement and planning policies
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Open data platform improved public access to information

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Objectives met for municipal energy reduction, digital integration, and citizen engagement  
  - Some challenges in scaling retrofit adoption and sustaining engagement beyond pilots
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Revealed complexities in data integration and multi-owner building governance
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Digital platform and retrofit desk remain active; scaling and replication ongoing[^1]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Integrated approach** to energy, mobility, and digital transformation
- **Quantifiable impacts** (energy reduction, emissions cuts, public cost savings)
- **Robust citizen engagement** via digital platforms and participatory pilots
- **Scalable technology** and governance models validated in real-world context[^2][^1][^4]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity of data harmonization** across legacy systems
- **Challenges in sustaining citizen engagement** beyond pilot phase
- **Financial sustainability** for ongoing digital services post-grant not fully proven
- **Scaling retrofits** in multi-owner buildings remains difficult

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Proactive inclusion of social housing and low-income households in retrofit programs
- Open access to digital platforms and information
- No major negative equity impacts reported; focus on inclusive benefits

#### **Future Outlook**

- Ongoing scaling and replication in Nantes and other European cities
- Continued evolution of digital platforms with AI, predictive analytics
- Persistent challenges in engagement and integration as cities grow and legacy infrastructure ages
- Next phases may focus on deeper citizen co-creation, broader private sector engagement, and advanced climate resilience strategies[^1][^4]
