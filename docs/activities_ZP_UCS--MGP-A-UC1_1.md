# Executive summary## Triple – Eco-Carbon Neutral Co-Working and Event Space

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Triple – Eco-Carbon Neutral Co-Working and Event Space
- **Alternative Names:** Triple Ferraz; Triple Balance Space
- **Location:** Calle Ferraz 56, Argüelles, Central Madrid, Spain
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 40.4275° N, 3.7166° W
- **Timeline:** Renovation initiated 2019; Completed and launched 2020; Ongoing operation
- **Project Scale:** 600 m² industrial building; ~55 daily users; Multi-floor workspace and event venue
- **Leading Organization(s):** Triple S.L. (private operator); sAtt Arquitectura (ecological architects)
- **Funding Sources:** Private investment; Bank loans; No public or international aid reported[^1][^2]

#### **Executive Summary**
Triple represents Madrid’s first eco-carbon neutral co-working and event space, pioneering a holistic approach to sustainability, health, and community in the heart of the city. Renovated in 2020 from a repurposed industrial building, Triple applies Passive House principles, life cycle analysis, and meticulous material selection to minimize environmental impact while maximizing occupant well-being. It achieves Nearly Zero Energy Building (NZEB) status, with 90% energy savings and full carbon offsetting of all renovation emissions through reforestation.

All materials—insulation, furnishings, finishes—are biocompatible, locally sourced, and certified ecological, reflecting a rigorous approach to human and environmental health. The design incorporates advanced mechanical ventilation, circadian lighting, recycled acoustic panels, and abundant vegetation, creating an environment that supports physical, emotional, and psychosocial health. The space is powered by 100% renewable electricity and maintains zero operational CO₂ emissions, endorsed by external certification[^1][^2][^3].

Triple is more than a building: it is a vibrant community of entrepreneurs, creatives, and organizations committed to triple bottom line values—people, planet, profit. The initiative demonstrates that green renovation, though incurring ~30% higher upfront costs, yields lower running expenses and superior workplace outcomes, serving as a replicable model for future urban workspaces. Its significance lies in setting a new benchmark for healthy, low-impact urban development, integrating ecological principles, participatory culture, and long-term sustainability into Madrid’s dense city fabric[^1][^2][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Madrid is Spain’s capital, with a population of ~3.3 million. It is a leading European business hub, known for its vibrant cultural scene, diverse economy (finance, tech, creative industries), and a dense, historic urban core with mixed-use districts.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** Argüelles is central, well-connected, and characterized by a mix of residential, commercial, and institutional uses. The area is known for historic architecture, proximity to parks (Parque del Oeste), and a lively, diverse population ranging from students and professionals to artists and entrepreneurs.
  - **Historical Patterns:** The district’s industrial buildings have increasingly been repurposed for modern uses, reflecting broader urban trends in adaptive reuse.
  - **Land Use:** Dense urban block with retail, housing, offices, and public amenities.
  - **Demographics:** Middle to upper-middle income, culturally diverse, relatively young and professional.
  - **Infrastructure Quality:** High accessibility, transit connections, but older building stock often energy inefficient.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban heat island effects, air pollution challenges, limited green space[^1][^2].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Poor indoor air quality and thermal comfort in conventional office buildings.
  - High energy consumption and carbon footprint.
  - Lack of healthy, community-oriented workspaces.
  - Underutilized industrial building stock.
- **Triggering Events:**
  - Growing demand for healthy and sustainable work environments.
  - Emergence of ecological architecture expertise in Madrid.
  - Rising regulatory pressure for eco-renovation and energy efficiency[^1][^2].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Initiators included ecological architects, entrepreneurs, and sustainability advocates. Prospective users sought healthy, inspiring, and planet-friendly workspaces.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Municipal authority over building permits, zoning; compliance with Spanish and EU energy efficiency and sustainability standards.
- **Relevant Policies:** Passive House/Passivhaus adoption, NZEB targets (EU directives), local green building incentives.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** No major reported barriers; local authorities supportive of adaptive reuse and green building but limited direct funding.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:**
  - Create Madrid’s first eco-carbon neutral co-working and event space.
  - Demonstrate triple bottom line (social, environmental, economic) impact in workplace design.
  - Foster a healthy, inclusive, and collaborative community.
- **Vision Statement:** To set a new standard for urban workspaces where sustainability, health, and human connection are at the core of every decision.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Supports EU and citywide goals for energy efficiency, carbon reduction, and urban regeneration; aligns with the SDGs and local “Triple Balance” movement[^2].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** By integrating advanced ecological design, responsible material sourcing, and community-building into urban workspace renovation, the initiative enables improved health, lower emissions, and replicable sustainable business practices.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Entrepreneurs, freelancers, small organizations, event hosts, and the broader ecological business ecosystem.
- **Innovation Elements:** First 360° ecological co-working space in Madrid; rigorous application of life cycle analysis and carbon offsetting; participatory, community-centric operational model[^2][^1].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:** Rising public and regulatory focus on sustainability; anticipated future requirements for eco-renovation.
- **Economic:** Growing market for green workspaces; long-term cost savings on operations.
- **Social:** Demand for healthy, collaborative, and meaningful work environments; desire for community among values-aligned professionals.
- **Environmental:** Climate action, resource efficiency, urban biodiversity.
- **Technological:** Availability of Passive House standards, AI-driven comfort management, and energy monitoring systems[^1][^2].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Triple Balance—integrating people, planet, and profit; passive design for energy and comfort; biophilic and biocompatible materials; participatory community creation.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Retrofitting a 600 m² industrial building into flexible, open-plan workspaces and event zones across three floors; intensive indoor greening; zoning for varied activities.
- **Scope and Phasing:** Full renovation completed in 2020; continual operational improvements and community programming[^1][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Passive House design principles—thermal insulation, airtightness, heat recovery ventilation.
  - Mechanical ventilation with air purification and heat recovery (double-flow system).
  - Circadian lighting systems (AI-driven).
  - FSC-certified wood, recycled insulation (wood fiber, cotton), ecological paints.
  - Acoustic insulation with recycled panels.
  - Energy monitoring and real-time environmental quality control.
  - 100% renewable electricity; zero operational CO₂ emissions.
  - Life cycle analysis tools and external carbon offsetting via reforestation[^1][^2][^3].
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (Passive House, AI comfort management), Social (community model), Financial (carbon offset), Governance (triple balance integration).
- **Integration Approach:** All systems integrated into building’s operational management, prioritizing user experience and minimal environmental impact.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Led by sAtt Arquitectura (experienced green architects); local material suppliers prioritized over global distributors; rigorous ecological criteria.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** User and community input during design and operation; events and community-building activities foster ongoing participation.
- **Participatory Elements:** Regular meetings, feedback sessions, co-created programming, social events, and wellness activities[^1].
- **Project Management:** Core team coordinated renovation and launch; ongoing management by Triple S.L. and community facilitators.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Total cost estimated at ~30% higher than conventional renovation for ecological materials and systems[^1].
- **Funding Mix:** 100% private investment and bank loans; no public or donor funding.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Membership fees for workspace use; event space rental; virtual office services[^3].
- **Financial Sustainability:** Lower running costs due to energy savings; high occupancy rates; strong community retention.

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical Challenges:** Sourcing suitable ecological materials locally; ensuring compatibility of all systems; maintaining Passive House standards in a dense urban retrofit.
- **Financial Challenges:** Higher upfront costs (~30% more than conventional); balancing investment with long-term savings[^1].
- **Social and Political Challenges:** Educating users about intangible health benefits; shifting market norms toward green renovation.
- **Administrative Challenges:** Managing complex procurement and supplier relationships; coordinating among diverse project stakeholders.
- **Environmental Challenges:** Rigorously offsetting embodied emissions; adapting to changing regulatory standards.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Rejected non-local/global suppliers; iteratively modified material choices to meet ecological criteria; prioritized functionality and health in every decision[^1][^2].
- **Design Modifications:** Substituted materials when ecological certification unavailable; adjusted spatial zoning to maximize comfort and flexibility.
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:** Importance of local supply chains; community engagement improves satisfaction and retention; ecological priorities require persistent attention to detail[^1].

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:** 600 m² of renovated workspace and event venue; high-performance building envelope; healthy indoor environment.
- **Service Delivery:** Open-plan workspaces, meeting rooms, event hosting, wellness amenities; virtual office services.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** Accommodates ~55 users daily; achieves 90% energy savings; certified zero CO₂ emissions[^1][^3][^2].

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:** Improved physical and psychosocial well-being; low fatigue, high satisfaction; strong collaborative culture; regular wellness and social events[^1].
- **Equity Outcomes:** Inclusive space supports diverse professionals (architects, consultants, journalists, ecologists, etc.); supports ecological, LGBTQ+, and social impact groups.
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No reported displacement; adaptive reuse of existing building.
- **Social Capital:** High social trust, active network of values-aligned organizations; increased civic engagement.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Promotes ecological and regenerative culture; hosts events advancing SDGs and creative innovation[^2].

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:** Direct employment for management, maintenance, community facilitation; indirect benefits for local suppliers and artisans.
- **Economic Activity:** Attracts green businesses and startups; strengthens local sustainable business ecosystem.
- **Property Values:** Likely positive impact due to improved building quality and reputation.
- **Cost Savings:** ~90% reduction in energy costs; lower operational expenses over building lifecycle[^1].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Accessible pricing structure; support for transition to triple impact business models.

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate and Emissions:** Achieved carbon neutrality; 90% operational energy savings; offset 196 tonnes of construction-phase CO₂ via reforestation[^2].
- **Resource Management:** Efficient water, energy, and material use; minimal waste.
- **Environmental Quality:** High indoor air and acoustic quality; abundant vegetation and natural light.
- **Waste and Pollution:** Use of recycled materials; non-toxic finishes and paints; avoidance of harmful chemicals.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** NZEB certification; CO₂ zero endorsement; compliance with Passive House standards[^1][^2][^3].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Institutional Capacity:** Demonstrates capacity for complex, values-driven renovation.
- **Policy Influence:** Sets precedent for future eco-renovations in Madrid; supports adoption of triple balance and SDG-aligned practices.
- **Transparency and Accountability:** High transparency in sourcing and operational decisions; user feedback integral to management.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:** Met goals of ecological renovation, carbon neutrality, community creation, and operational sustainability.
- **Unintended Consequences:** Higher initial cost, but not seen as a barrier; stronger than anticipated community cohesion.
- **Long-term Viability:** Model is replicable; operational sustainability proven; regulatory trends favor continued relevance[^1][^2].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- **Holistic Sustainability:** Integrates ecological, health, and social dimensions in every decision.
- **Operational Performance:** Achieves NZEB status and full carbon neutrality; delivers low running costs.
- **Community Creation:** Fosters strong, values-driven networks and collaborative culture.
- **Replicability:** Serves as a model for future urban renovations, influencing policy and market norms[^1][^2].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- **Upfront Cost:** Initial investment ~30% higher than conventional renovation; may deter some developers.
- **Supply Chain Constraints:** Reliance on local suppliers can limit material options and increase complexity.
- **Market Education:** Users may need education to fully appreciate intangible benefits of healthy buildings.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- **Advances Inclusion:** Supports diverse professionals and social impact organizations; prioritizes health and well-being for all users.
- **Accessibility:** Pricing and community ethos support economic and social inclusion; however, location in central Madrid may limit access for some.

#### **Future Outlook**
- **Long-term Trajectory:** As regulations increasingly require ecological renovation, Triple’s model will become more mainstream.
- **Ongoing Challenges:** Maintaining ecological standards as space or community grows; adapting to evolving user needs and regulatory changes.
- **Evolution/Next Phases:** Potential for expansion, deeper integration of smart technology, and wider dissemination of the triple balance model across Madrid and beyond[^1][^2][^3].
