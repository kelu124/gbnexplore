# Executive summary## Green Corridor Project

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Green Corridor Project
- **Alternative Names:** Promenade Verte (Green Walk), Healthy Corridor Urban Plan, Blue-Green Corridor
- **Location:** Brussels, Belgium; key urban zones including Anderlecht-Drogenbos and Neder-Over-Heembeek
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.8503° N, 4.3517° E (Brussels center; specific corridor coordinates vary)
- **Timeline:** Initiated in the early 2000s, major upgrades and integration in policy since 2016; ongoing with milestones in 2018 (regional ecological network), and 2023 (Healthy Corridor deliverable)
- **Project Scale:** 60 km of interconnected pathways; affects approximately 2,779 ha of green public spaces, serving the whole Brussels Capital Region (population ~1.2 million)[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )
- **Leading Organization(s):** Brussels Capital Region, Bruxelles Environnement, CES (Healthy Corridor partner), local housing agencies, multiple municipal departments[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )
- **Funding Sources:** Regional government, EU Horizon 2020 (URBiNAT grant agreement No 776783), municipal budgets, potential PPPs[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )

#### **Executive Summary**
The Green Corridor Project is a transformative urban development initiative in Brussels, Belgium, focused on redeveloping and enhancing an active transport pathway that links major urban zones through a network of green infrastructure. The project aims to provide safe, attractive, and efficient routes for pedestrians and cyclists, while also restoring ecological connectivity and fostering urban biodiversity. Rooted in the “Promenade Verte” concept—a 60 km rehabilitated former railway—the corridor connects parks, nature reserves, and woodlands, forming a continuous green belt around the city and linking inner city green spaces. 

Driven by a regional vision to improve public access to green spaces and strengthen ecological networks, the project combines physical infrastructure upgrades, integrated landscaping, and traffic-calming measures. It addresses critical challenges such as urban fragmentation, car dependency, and climate adaptation, while promoting modal shifts to walking and cycling. The initiative is characterized by strong participatory planning, involving residents, businesses, and stakeholders through living labs, workshops, and local councils. Outcomes include improved connectivity and safety for non-motorized transport, increased footfall supporting local commerce, and measurable environmental benefits like reduced urban heat and enhanced biodiversity. The Green Corridor Project serves as a prominent example of leveraging nature-based solutions to achieve sustainable urban mobility, social inclusion, and resilience in a European capital[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Brussels is the capital of Belgium and the de facto capital of the European Union. It has a population of approximately 1.2 million, a diverse economic base (services, government, international organizations), and is characterized by dispersed urbanization, significant green heritage, and a mix of historic and modern development[^1][^4].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** Key implementation areas include Anderlecht-Drogenbos (Southern Senne Valley) and Neder-Over-Heembeek, neighborhoods with fragmented urban landscapes, social housing districts, and a mix of industrial and residential land use. The corridor traverses zones with varying income levels, strong multicultural composition, and pockets of underutilized or degraded infrastructure[^5][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Existing Infrastructure Quality:** Prior to the initiative, green space accessibility was uneven, public realm quality was mixed, and active transport infrastructure was discontinuous—especially in social housing neighborhoods and areas bisected by rail, canal, and industrial facilities[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Environmental Conditions:** The region faces urban heat island effects, flooding risk (Senne River corridor), air and noise pollution, and habitat fragmentation impeding biodiversity[^5][^6].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** 
  - Disconnected green spaces and lack of safe pedestrian/cycle routes
  - High car dependency and traffic congestion
  - Social isolation and lack of public realm quality in social housing districts
  - Urban flooding and environmental degradation (Senne Valley)[^5][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^6]
- **Triggering Events:** Inclusion in the Regional Nature Plan (2016-2020), EU policy alignment, local advocacy, and the co-diagnostic phase of URBiNAT revealed urgent needs for connectivity and environmental health[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Regional and municipal governments, housing associations, environmental NGOs, resident groups, and EU partners all held stakes, with overlapping interests in mobility, biodiversity, and social inclusion[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Brussels Capital Region government, local municipalities, and consortia like URBiNAT provided multi-level governance; statutory frameworks included sustainable development plans and zoning regulations (PRAS)[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Relevant Policies:** Regional Nature Plan, PRAS (Plan Régional d’Affectation du Sol), EU Biodiversity Strategy, and Horizon 2020 co-creation mandates[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** Nature protection laws, land-use policies, and participatory mechanisms enabled action; fragmented land ownership and rigid administrative processes sometimes posed barriers[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:** 
  - Enhance continuity and legibility of green pathways for active mobility
  - Restore ecological connectivity across urban zones
  - Improve safety and accessibility for pedestrians and cyclists
- **Vision Statement:** To create a resilient, healthy city where green infrastructure and active transport are central to urban life, supporting social cohesion, economic vitality, and biodiversity[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Integrated with regional ecological network strategies, citywide sustainable mobility plans, and EU-level nature-based solution policies[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** By redesigning urban corridors to prioritize green infrastructure and non-motorized transport, the project reduces car dependency, improves public health, enhances ecosystem services, and supports socially inclusive urban transformation[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents (especially in vulnerable neighborhoods), commuters, local businesses, and wildlife species[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Innovation Elements:** Combination of landscape design, traffic-calming, participatory co-creation, and integration of biodiversity objectives in mobility corridors[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:** Regional commitments to sustainability, EU project funding, local leadership priorities[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Economic:** Desire to stimulate local commerce, attract investment, and reduce infrastructure costs (e.g. flood prevention)[^5][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Social:** Community advocacy for better public spaces, equity concerns in social housing districts[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Environmental:** Climate adaptation, biodiversity, flood resilience, urban cooling[^1][^5][^6].
- **Technological:** Use of urban design software, GIS-based planning, innovative construction and landscape management practices[^6][^7].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Grounded in principles of green infrastructure, nature-based solutions, and “garden city” ideals; prioritizes connectivity, multifunctionality, and co-creation[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^4].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Rehabilitation of former railway lines into green pedestrian/cycle paths (“Promenade Verte”), creation of new green public spaces, integrated landscaping, and traffic-calming features (e.g. speed reduction, safe crossings)[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Scope and Phasing:** Initial focus on headline corridors (outer ring and inner city axes), followed by incremental extensions and infill improvements; iterative co-design and validation cycles with local stakeholders[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - Pathway redesign and resurfacing
  - Native landscaping and habitat restoration
  - Smart lighting and wayfinding
  - Stormwater management and floodplain design (Senna Valley)[^5][^6][^3]
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (mobility infrastructure, landscape restoration), social (participatory planning), governance (living labs, stakeholder councils)[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^7].
- **Integration Approach:** New infrastructure is woven into existing urban systems, leveraging vacant/underutilized land, and aligning with public transport and social housing upgrades[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Mix of public procurement for infrastructure, partnership agreements for co-creation activities, and direct municipal delivery for landscaping and maintenance[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Living labs, workshops, neighborhood councils (e.g. NOH-M), and continuous feedback via iterative participatory design[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Participatory Elements:** Residents, local associations, and “unusual suspects” (e.g. passers-by) involved in co-diagnostics, co-design, and pilot projects[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Project Management:** Embedded project coordinators, cross-departmental task forces, and regular monitoring/reporting cycles[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Not fully disclosed; significant investments via regional government, EU Horizon 2020 funding, and municipal budgets[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Funding Mix:** Predominantly public sector, with EU research/innovation support and potential for PPPs or private sponsorship for specific sites[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** No user fees; indirect economic benefits via increased footfall, local business activity, and land value uplift[^3].
- **Financial Sustainability:** Long-term maintenance plans through municipal budgets and continued community stewardship[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:** Coordinating upgrades across fragmented infrastructure, managing complex floodplain and biodiversity requirements, construction impacts on mobility[^5][^6][^3].
- **Financial:** Budget constraints, need for sustained investment across multiple phases[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Social and Political:** Balancing interests of diverse stakeholders, overcoming resistance to land use changes, ensuring equitable benefits (esp. in social housing zones)[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Administrative:** Bureaucratic silos between agencies, slow permitting, need for multi-level governance integration[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Environmental:** Unforeseen impacts on wildlife, challenges with soil permeability and heat island mitigation[^5][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving:** Iterative participatory design, flexible phasing, synergy with existing projects, ongoing stakeholder engagement[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Design Modifications:** Responsive adjustments to landscaping, path alignment, and amenities based on user feedback and ecological monitoring[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Lessons Learned:** Need for continuous co-creation, importance of local stewardship, value of integrating biodiversity and mobility goals[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^1].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:** 60 km of green pathways, numerous new and restored green public spaces, upgraded crossings and amenities[^1][^3].
- **Service Delivery:** Safe, attractive routes for walking and cycling; improved access to nature and recreation; enhanced public realm quality[^3].
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 2,779 ha of public green space; significant increases in non-motorized transport use; improved scores on urban biodiversity indices[^1][^3].

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:** Safer, more convivial public spaces; improved health and well-being; stronger social cohesion in target neighborhoods[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Equity Outcomes:** Focus on social housing districts; inclusive engagement processes; efforts to prevent displacement and gentrification[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Displacement/Gentrification:** No major reported negative impacts; vigilance required in ongoing monitoring[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Social Capital:** Enhanced networks among residents, local associations, and municipal actors; increased civic engagement[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Cultural Impacts:** Reinforcement of green heritage; preservation of cultural landscapes (e.g. former railway lines); celebration of local identity[^1][^4].

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:** Direct employment via construction, landscaping, and maintenance; indirect stimulus for local businesses[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Economic Activity:** Increased footfall supports commerce; potential for investment attraction and productivity gains[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Property Values:** Uplift in adjacent real estate; potential risk of gentrification mitigated by social housing protections[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Cost Savings:** Flood prevention and climate adaptation reduce public expenditure; active mobility supports lower health costs[^5][^6].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Targeted benefits for low-income and marginalized groups; improved access to jobs and services[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate/Emissions:** Modal shift reduces GHG emissions; restored floodplain and green space improve climate resilience[^1][^5][^6].
- **Resource Management:** Improved stormwater handling, increased soil permeability, energy-efficient lighting[^6][^7].
- **Environmental Quality:** Enhanced air and noise quality, reduced urban heat island, increased biodiversity[^1][^5][^3].
- **Waste/Pollution:** Improved waste management and pollution control in corridor zones[^5].
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Project aligns with EU and regional standards for nature-based solutions and urban sustainability[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Capacity:** Strengthened municipal project management and stakeholder engagement structures[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Policy Influence:** Model for future green infrastructure projects; reference point for Brussels and other European cities[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Transparency/Accountability:** Open participatory processes, regular public reporting, and feedback channels[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:** Significant progress on stated goals; increased active mobility, improved ecological connectivity, enhanced public space[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Unintended Consequences:** Minor construction disruptions; vigilance against uneven benefit distribution or gentrification[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Long-term Viability:** Ongoing maintenance and stewardship frameworks; embedded in regional and municipal policy[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- **Integrated Approach:** Combines mobility, biodiversity, and social inclusion in a coherent strategy[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^3].
- **Participation:** Deep stakeholder and resident engagement ensures relevance and ownership[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Environmental Gains:** Significant improvements in urban cooling, flood resilience, and habitat connectivity[^1][^5].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- **Complexity:** Requires sustained multi-agency coordination and long-term investment[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Equity Risks:** Potential for uneven benefit distribution; gentrification must be monitored[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Data Gaps:** Some outcomes (e.g. economic impacts, long-term biodiversity trends) still require robust monitoring and evaluation[^6].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- **Advances:** Meaningful engagement with social housing communities, deliberate focus on inclusive design, and avoidance of displacement[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Risks:** Vigilance needed to ensure benefits reach marginalized populations and that public spaces remain accessible to all[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).

#### **Future Outlook**
- **Trajectory:** Project is embedded in regional policy, with plans for ongoing corridor extensions, upgrades, and participatory stewardship[^1][^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Challenges/Opportunities:** Continual adaptation to climate, demographic, and mobility trends; potential for stronger digital integration and expanded social programming[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf ).
- **Next Phases:** Further network densification, inclusion of smart city technologies, and deeper evaluation of social and ecological impacts[^2]/040_Livrable%20_plan%20urbain%20local_%20(D2.7)%20version%20EN.pdf )[^6].
