# Executive summary

## EDGE Olympic – Smart Climate Control and Air Quality Monitoring

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** EDGE Olympic – Smart Climate Control and Air Quality Monitoring
- **Alternative Names:** EDGE Olympic Redevelopment, EDGE Technologies Amsterdam
- **Location:** Amsterdam, Zuidas Business District, Netherlands
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 52.3407° N, 4.8672° E (corner of Amstelveenseweg and Fred. Roeskestraat)[^1]
- **Timeline:** Original building constructed in 1990; major redevelopment and smart retrofit completed in 2018; ongoing operation[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:** Office building with extension; two additional floors added (Cradle2Cradle certified); approx. 16,000 m² gross floor area; hundreds of building users daily[^1][^2]
- **Leading Organization(s):** EDGE Technologies (formerly OVG Real Estate), de Architekten Cie., Fokkema & Partners Architecten, PLP Architecture[^1][^2][^3]
- **Funding Sources:** Private real estate investment (EDGE Technologies/OVG Real Estate); tenant contributions; possible green financing incentives through BREEAM and WELL certifications

#### **Executive Summary**

EDGE Olympic in Amsterdam is a paradigm of sustainable urban densification and smart building integration. The project transformed a 1990s office structure into a digitally enabled, energy-efficient, and healthy workspace, aligning with the city’s broader environmental ambitions. Leveraging a digital twin platform that connects thousands of IoT sensors, EDGE Olympic continuously monitors and optimizes indoor climate, including temperature, humidity, CO₂, and air flow. These technologies allow for demand-driven, remote-controlled HVAC and ventilation, maximizing occupant comfort while minimizing energy use.

The building’s design incorporates sustainable materials (notably a timber Cradle2Cradle extension), extensive natural light, solar energy generation, and advanced water management. EDGE Olympic is distinguished by its BREEAM Excellent and WELL Platinum certifications, demonstrating best-in-class performance in energy consumption, air quality, and user well-being. Implementation challenges, such as integrating legacy systems and ensuring cybersecurity, were overcome through modular digital infrastructure and stakeholder collaboration.

EDGE Olympic’s outcomes include substantial reductions in energy costs and carbon footprint, improved air quality and comfort, and enhanced user satisfaction. As a multi-tenant facility, it serves as both a flagship for innovative real estate and a replicable model for smart, green building neighborhoods, contributing to Amsterdam’s sustainable urban future[^3][^4][^1][^2].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Amsterdam is the capital of the Netherlands, with a population of ~900,000 in the city proper, renowned for its knowledge economy, progressive environmental policies, and high quality of life. The Zuidas district is a key financial and business center, hosting multinational corporations and major institutions[^4][^5].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - **Historical Development:** Zuidas has evolved from peripheral land to a high-density business hub over the past three decades.
  - **Current Land Use:** Mixed-use, dominated by office towers, some residential developments, retail, and public spaces.
  - **Demographics:** Professional workforce, high education and income levels, international diversity.
  - **Infrastructure Quality:** Excellent public transport, cycling facilities, modern utilities, but facing challenges of urban densification and energy transition.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban heat island effects, air quality concerns due to density and traffic; city-wide push for low-carbon infrastructure.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Aging 1990s office stock lacking in energy efficiency, comfort, and digital capability[^1][^2].
  - Need for sustainable densification without expanding the city footprint[^3].
  - Growing demand for healthier workplaces and real-time environmental monitoring.
- **Triggering Events:** EDGE Technologies’ ambition to pioneer smart, healthy buildings; regulatory incentives for green building; market demand from major tenants (e.g., Deloitte).
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Real estate developers, architects, tenants, city planners, technology providers (IoT, cloud), certification bodies (BREEAM, WELL).

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** Local building authorities (Gemeente Amsterdam), Dutch national building codes, EU directives on energy and sustainability.
- **Relevant Policies:** Amsterdam’s Smart City strategy; national targets for energy-neutral buildings; zoning plans allowing vertical extensions[^3][^1].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** Support from green building certification schemes; some complexity in integrating new technologies with legacy systems.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:** Transform an outdated office into a cutting-edge, energy-efficient, healthy, and flexible workspace; demonstrate scalable smart building solutions[^3][^4][^1].
- **Vision Statement:** To create a building that showcases the future of sustainable urban development—where technology and design converge for human well-being and environmental stewardship.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Supports Amsterdam’s climate neutrality and smart city targets; aligns with EU directives for energy efficiency and healthy buildings.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** By integrating real-time climate monitoring and automated control (IoT + digital twin), EDGE Olympic reduces energy waste and improves indoor air quality, directly benefiting occupants and the urban environment.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Building users (employees, tenants), facility managers, city authorities, broader community (air quality, urban resilience).
- **Innovation Elements:** First in the Netherlands to achieve WELL Platinum for a retrofitted office; modular digital infrastructure; timber Cradle2Cradle extension; deep integration of real-time data analytics[^1][^6].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** Amsterdam’s leadership in sustainable urbanism; city branding as a smart city.
- **Economic Drivers:** Rising demand for high-quality office space; higher rental yields for green buildings; reduced operational costs.
- **Social Drivers:** Focus on occupant health, productivity, and new work cultures (flexible, comfortable environments).
- **Environmental Drivers:** Urgent need for climate adaptation, decarbonization, urban air quality improvement.
- **Technological Drivers:** Advances in IoT, cloud analytics, digital twin platforms; global momentum for smart buildings.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Circular economy principles (reuse, Cradle2Cradle); biophilic and human-centric design; “No Office” concept—spaces that feel like home[^7][^1].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Two-storey timber glass extension; atrium as central social hub; open-plan flexible workspaces; maximized daylight; natural ventilation.
- **Scope and Phasing:** Retrofit of existing building; addition of new floors; technology integration; phased handover to tenants.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - **Digital Twin Platform:** Cloud-based, real-time monitoring and control of climate parameters[^6][^2].
  - **IoT Sensors:** Thousands of sensors tracking temperature, humidity, CO₂, occupancy, light, noise[^4][^5][^6].
  - **Automated HVAC/Ventilation:** Demand-driven, remotely adjustable via app; climate ceilings with bGrid sensors[^1].
  - **Solar Energy:** 6,000 m² of solar panels; net-zero energy achievement[^5][^8].
  - **Water Management:** Aquifer thermal energy storage; rainwater harvesting for toilets/gardens[^5][^8].
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digital twin, sensors), architectural (Cradle2Cradle timber), social (flexible workspaces), process (integrated project delivery).
- **Integration Approach:** Modular cloud infrastructure compatible with legacy systems; user-centered control interfaces; adaptive management for multi-tenant use[^6].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** EDGE Technologies led development; design by de Architekten Cie. and Fokkema & Partners; technology partners for IoT and cloud platforms[^1][^2].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Tenants involved in design and choice of workspace configurations; iterative feedback on comfort and usability[^7].
- **Participatory Elements:** App-based control for individual users; flexible adaptation to user preferences.
- **Project Management:** Digital platform for coordination; phased construction to minimize disruption.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** Not publicly disclosed; estimated at tens of millions of euros for retrofit and extension.
- **Funding Mix:** Private capital (EDGE Technologies, investors), tenant contributions, possible green incentives.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Premium rents for sustainable certification, cost savings shared with tenants.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Lower operational costs via energy savings; enhanced asset value and marketability.

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:** Integrating legacy building systems with new IoT platforms; calibrating thousands of sensors; ensuring robust cybersecurity[^6][^2].
- **Financial Challenges:** Upfront investment for smart technologies; justifying costs to stakeholders.
- **Social and Political Challenges:** Ensuring user adoption of digital controls; balancing privacy with data-driven management.
- **Administrative Challenges:** Coordinating multi-tenant needs and flexible layouts; navigating zoning for vertical expansion.
- **Environmental Challenges:** Managing embodied carbon in retrofit; optimizing solar and geothermal systems for local climate.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Modular sensor and cloud platforms for flexible integration; phased technology deployment.
- **Design Modifications:** Use of timber and prefabricated modules to reduce retrofit disruption; continuous feedback loops for system optimization[^1].
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:** Importance of intuitive interfaces for user adoption; value of real-time data in maintenance and cleaning logistics (e.g., skipping unused rooms)[^4][^1].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** Two-storey timber-glass extension; modernized interiors; climate ceilings; expanded bicycle and EV parking[^1][^8].
- **Service Delivery:** Real-time, user-controlled indoor climate; improved cleaning and maintenance efficiency; flexible workspace allocation[^4][^5].
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 70% reduction in electricity consumption compared to typical offices; highest WELL Platinum score in the Netherlands[^1][^4].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Enhanced occupant well-being, productivity, and satisfaction; flexible, inclusive workspaces; gender-neutral facilities[^7][^1].
- **Equity Outcomes:** Access to healthy, comfortable work environments for all tenants; universal design features.
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No residential displacement; positive impact on surrounding business community.
- **Social Capital:** Fostered collaboration through open atrium, community hubs, and app-based interaction.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Innovative work culture; recognition in architecture and sustainability circles.

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Construction and retrofit jobs; ongoing facility management and tech maintenance positions.
- **Economic Activity:** Premium office rental; increased attractiveness for high-value tenants; local business stimulation.
- **Property Values:** Enhanced asset value due to green certifications and technology.
- **Cost Savings:** Substantial reductions in energy bills; efficiencies in cleaning and maintenance.
- **Economic Inclusion:** Improved affordability for tenants through lower operational costs.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** Net-zero energy performance; estimated 42 million kg CO₂ saved over a decade[^8].
- **Resource Management:** Efficient water and energy use; rainwater harvesting and aquifer storage[^5][^8].
- **Environmental Quality:** Improved indoor air quality; reduced urban heat island effects.
- **Waste and Pollution:** Circular design with reused materials (e.g., stone flooring); waste minimization.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** BREEAM Excellent, WELL Platinum certifications; industry benchmark for green buildings[^1][^4][^6].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** EDGE Olympic sets precedent for data-driven building management; strengthens partnerships in real estate innovation.
- **Policy Influence:** Inspiration for city and EU policies on smart buildings; demonstration of regulatory flexibility for vertical extension.
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Real-time dashboards improve governance; user access to data enhances accountability[^4].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Exceeded targets for energy consumption, air quality, and user comfort; recognized with multiple awards[^5].
- **Unintended Consequences:** Potential privacy concerns with data collection; need for ongoing cybersecurity vigilance.
- **Long-term Viability:** Building continues to operate at high performance; model for future retrofits in Amsterdam and beyond.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Integration of Smart Technologies:** Real-time data analytics optimize energy and comfort; sets industry benchmark[^4][^1][^6].
- **Sustainability Credentials:** Both BREEAM and WELL certifications showcase environmental and health leadership[^1][^6].
- **User-Centric Design:** Flexible workspaces, app-based controls, inclusive facilities enhance satisfaction[^7].
- **Circular and Modular Retrofit:** Timber extension, reused materials, and prefabrication minimize impact and disruption[^1][^2].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Data Privacy and Cybersecurity:** Large-scale sensor networks require robust data protection and transparency with users[^6].
- **Legacy System Integration:** Retrofitting older infrastructure is complex and may limit full technology potential[^2].
- **Cost Barriers:** High upfront investment may deter replication in less well-capitalized markets.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Advances Equity:** Universal design (e.g., gender-neutral toilets, flexible spaces) and health focus benefit all users[^7][^1].
- **Potential Gaps:** High-end office setting may not address broader urban equity challenges (e.g., affordable housing).

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Trajectory:** Continued high-performance operation; serves as a pilot for wider deployment of smart building technologies.
- **Ongoing Challenges:** Maintaining cybersecurity, updating systems, scaling to neighborhood or city level.
- **Next Phases:** Integration with district energy and air quality networks; adaptation of model to other building types (residential, mixed-use); deeper engagement with urban resilience strategies.
