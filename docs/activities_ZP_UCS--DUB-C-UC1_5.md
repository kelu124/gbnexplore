# Executive summary## Circular Canteen Management – Amsterdam, Netherlands (Horizon Europe Project: CityLoops)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Circular Canteen Management (part of CityLoops)
- **Alternative Names:** CityLoops Circular Canteen Pilot, Amsterdam Circular Canteen Project
- **Location:** Amsterdam, Netherlands (municipal canteens citywide)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 52.3676° N, 4.9041° E (Amsterdam city center)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Launch: October 2019 (start of CityLoops)[^1]  
  - Key milestones: Circular dashboard deployment (2021), interim progress evaluation (2022)
  - Completion/Ongoing Status: Project concluded September 2023, with ongoing replication and scale-up[^1]
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Several municipal facilities throughout Amsterdam
  - Population Affected: Hundreds of municipal employees; indirect reach via procurement and supply chain
  - Investment Value: Part of CityLoops’ Horizon 2020 budget (CityLoops total: ~€10 million across seven cities)[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Municipality of Amsterdam
  - ICLEI – Local Governments for Sustainability (CityLoops coordinator)[^1]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme (grant agreement No. 821033)[^1]
  - In-kind and operational support from the Municipality of Amsterdam

#### **Executive Summary**
Circular Canteen Management in Amsterdam, a demonstration under the Horizon 2020 CityLoops project, piloted a holistic circular economy approach in the city’s municipal canteens between 2019 and 2023. The initiative sought to minimize food waste, optimize resource use, and foster local supply chains through digital tracking, innovative procurement, and multi-stakeholder engagement. At its core was a technology-driven dashboard providing real-time analytics on food inputs, consumption, and waste, enabling ongoing performance monitoring and transparency. By engaging suppliers and staff, the project shifted procurement toward local, seasonal products and established feedback mechanisms to continually refine operations. The pilot achieved a 30% reduction in food waste and increased local sourcing rates, delivering replicable guidelines for circular canteen operations. Environmentally, the project reduced carbon intensity and resource consumption; economically, it cut costs and supported local producers. Socially, it enhanced awareness and contributed to citywide sustainability culture. The initiative’s comprehensive design, stakeholder collaboration, and robust data-driven monitoring have made it a model for circular food service management, supporting Amsterdam’s broader ambition to become a fully circular city by 2050[^2][^3][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Amsterdam, population ~870,000, is the Netherlands’ capital and a leading European innovation hub. Renowned for its dense urban fabric, waterways, and progressive environmental agenda, the city’s economy is driven by services, technology, tourism, and creative industries. It ranks as a top-tier node in both the national and regional urban hierarchy[^2][^3].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The circular canteen initiative encompassed several municipal buildings and canteens, typically located in mixed-use, centrally accessible areas. These venues reflect Amsterdam’s broader urban patterns: a blend of historic and modern land uses, diverse demographic groups, and high public transport accessibility.
  - Historical development: Transformation from industrial/port city to a post-industrial, service-oriented metropolis.
  - Land use: Predominantly administrative, educational, and civic purposes for participating canteens.
  - Demographics: Municipal staff mirror Amsterdam’s diversity, with a mix of ages and backgrounds.
  - Infrastructure: High-quality facilities, modernized kitchens, and robust waste collection systems.
  - Environmental constraints: Amsterdam faces challenges from high population density, limited landfill space, and climate adaptation needs[^2].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High levels of food waste and inefficient resource use in municipal catering.
  - Fragmented procurement practices, often favoring non-local and non-seasonal suppliers.
  - Limited real-time data on food flows, resulting in missed opportunities for optimization.
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Adoption of the Amsterdam Circular Strategy 2020–2025, emphasizing food and organic waste as priority sectors[^2][^4].
  - EU policy momentum around circularity and sustainable urban food systems.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Key actors: Municipality departments (procurement, sustainability, facility management), local food producers, catering service providers, municipal employees.
  - Interests: Cost reduction, operational efficiency, sustainability leadership, and support for the local economy.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  Municipal government oversight, with strategic alignment to citywide and EU circular economy objectives[^4].
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Amsterdam Circular Strategy 2020–2025: aims to halve new raw material use by 2030, achieve 100% circular procurement by 2030, and reach full circularity by 2050[^2][^4].
  - EU Circular Economy Action Plan, supporting circular procurement and sustainable food systems.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Enablers: Strong political commitment, established sustainability mandates, and access to EU funding.
  - Barriers: Rigid procurement frameworks, supplier contracts not tailored for circularity, and administrative inertia.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Reduce food waste in municipal canteens by at least 30%.
  - Increase the share of locally and seasonally sourced food.
  - Demonstrate and scale circular canteen management practices.
- **Vision Statement:**  
  To create a zero-waste, resource-efficient food service model that supports local communities and sets a replicable standard for sustainable urban catering.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  Integral to Amsterdam’s ambition to become Europe’s first fully circular city by 2050, and directly supporting city-level and EU circularity targets[^2][^3][^4].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By introducing digital tracking and analytics, engaging suppliers, and adapting procurement, the project aimed to reveal and address inefficiencies, enabling substantial waste and cost reductions while promoting sustainability culture.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Direct: Municipal employees (improved catering services, awareness)
  - Indirect: Local food producers, city residents (via knowledge transfer)
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Open-access circularity dashboard for real-time monitoring.
  - Integration of procurement and waste data for process optimization.
  - Stakeholder engagement and feedback loops.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Strong city leadership on sustainability, response to public demand for climate action[^2].
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Cost containment and efficiency in public food services.
  - Support for regional economic development through local sourcing.
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Growing civic engagement and awareness on sustainability.
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Urgency of climate adaptation, waste reduction, and resource efficiency.
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Advances in supply chain analytics, digital inventory, and monitoring tools.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Circular economy principles: closing material loops, minimizing waste, and prioritizing local/regional resource flows[^2][^4].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  Retrofitting of canteen kitchens for better waste separation, installation of tracking tech, and creation of feedback points.
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial assessment and baseline data collection (2019–2020)
  - Technology deployment and stakeholder onboarding (2020–2021)
  - Full operationalization, monitoring, and adaptive management (2021–2023)

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - **Circularity Dashboard:** Real-time digital platform for tracking food inputs, consumption, and waste.
  - **Supply Chain Analytics:** Monitoring supplier deliveries, inventory, and local sourcing rates.
  - **Open Data Sharing:** Dashboard accessible to internal and external stakeholders for transparency.
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digital monitoring), governance (procurement reform), and process (new workflows).
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Technologies embedded within existing municipal IT and procurement systems, ensuring interoperability.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Revised procurement criteria to prioritize local, seasonal, and circular suppliers.
  - Competitive tendering and supplier engagement workshops.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Cross-departmental steering group, supplier forums, employee feedback sessions.
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Staff training, suggestion boxes, and open data access for feedback.
- **Project Management:**  
  - Led by the city’s sustainability office, with regular coordination meetings and progress reviews.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Proportional allocation from CityLoops’ total budget; exact breakdown for Amsterdam not public.
  - Primary expenditures: technology deployment, training, monitoring, and evaluation.
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 grant (majority), city operational budget (staff time, facilities).
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Cost savings from waste reduction and local procurement reinvested into program.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term savings expected from reduced food purchases and waste disposal fees.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Integrated, data-driven approach** that links procurement, operations, and waste management.
- **Clear, measurable outcomes** (notably food waste reduction and increased local sourcing).
- **Strong alignment with citywide strategy** and policy momentum.
- **Open, replicable guidelines** and transparent performance monitoring.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Reliance on digital infrastructure** may challenge cities with less capacity.
- **Procurement adaptation** required significant administrative effort and cultural change.
- **Scale limitations**: Initial focus on municipal canteens limits immediate mass impact.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Positive impacts**: Support for local producers, opportunities for small suppliers, and potential for expansion to more inclusive institutions (e.g., schools, hospitals).
- **Potential gaps**: Need ongoing vigilance to ensure small suppliers are not excluded by new requirements or data/reporting burdens.

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory:** The initiative is expected to expand across other city departments and inspire similar efforts in other European cities[^5][^1].
- **Ongoing challenges:** Ensuring continual improvement, supplier adaptation, and integration with broader food system reforms.
- **Next phases:** Further technology enhancements, wider replication, and incorporation of circularity into all municipal procurement[^2][^3][^5][^1][^4].
