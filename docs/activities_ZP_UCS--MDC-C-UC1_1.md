# Executive summary

## Cleanwatts Living Lab – AI-Powered Renewable Energy Community

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Cleanwatts Living Lab – AI-Powered Renewable Energy Community
- **Alternative Names:** CER Cleanwatts Living Lab; Cleanwatts Living Lab Coimbra
- **Location:** Coimbra and surrounding municipalities (Condeixa-a-Nova, Anadia), Centro region, Portugal[^1][^2]
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 40.2056° N, 8.4196° W (central Coimbra)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Initiation: 2021  
  - ERSE pilot approval: July 2023[^1]  
  - Ongoing implementation as of 2025
- **Project Scale:**  
  - 40 residential units (apartments and single-family homes)  
  - ~6,000 m² built area[^2]  
  - Population affected: ~100+ direct residents, broader community engagement
- **Leading Organization(s):** Cleanwatts (private energy tech firm), local municipalities, ERSE (Energy Services Regulatory Authority)[^1][^2]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Horizon 2020 (EU research funding)[^2]  
  - Private investment (Cleanwatts, Verdane)[^2]  
  - Local stakeholders  
  - PPP elements (demonstration/pilot status)

#### **Executive Summary**

The **Cleanwatts Living Lab** in Coimbra, Portugal, is a pioneering open innovation ecosystem designed to test and demonstrate advanced energy management strategies for urban renewable energy communities. Integrating **solar PV, energy storage, electric vehicles, and smart appliances** across 40 residential buildings, the Living Lab leverages **IoT sensors, AI-driven digital twins, and a proprietary operating system (Cleanwatts™ OS)** to enable real-time monitoring, control, and optimization of energy flows. It operates under both current legislation and a “regulatory sandbox,” allowing Cleanwatts to pilot disruptive models for collective self-consumption, dynamic energy sharing, and local energy markets[^1][^2].  
The initiative seeks to maximize **energy efficiency**, **sustainability**, and **user engagement**, achieving substantial reductions in energy costs and carbon emissions (up to 40% cost savings, 35% higher self-consumption, 40% CO₂ reduction). By empowering residents to become active participants in their local energy market, the Living Lab supports the transition to decentralized, low-carbon urban energy systems, while serving as a scalable template for similar neighborhoods across Europe. Its collaborative approach, robust stakeholder engagement, and commitment to sharing findings position it as a leader in urban decarbonization and community-driven innovation[^1][^2].

  - The Living Lab spans residential zones in Coimbra, Condeixa-a-Nova, and Anadia[^1].

- **Relevant Policies:**

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Advanced technology integration** (AI, IoT, digital twins) creates real-time optimization and empowers residents as active energy market participants[^2].
- **Clear, measurable sustainability impacts**: significant reductions in energy costs and carbon emissions.
- **Strong participatory model**: +50% engagement, with transparent feedback and co-creation.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Dependence on regulatory flexibility**: success hinges on pilot status and willingness of authorities to accommodate new models[^1].
- **Potential scalability barriers**: upfront costs and technical complexity may limit replication in less-resourced contexts.
- **Reliance on continuous user engagement**: risk of participation fatigue or uneven benefits.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Pro-equity orientation**, with efforts to include broad segments of the community and potential to address energy poverty.
- No evidence of displacement or gentrification effects; focus on inclusive benefit-sharing.

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Trajectory:** Ongoing expansion, increased integration of advanced technologies, and broader replication planned.
- **Challenges:** Ensuring long-term engagement, adapting to evolving regulatory and market conditions.
- **Next phases:** Scaling to more neighborhoods, refining business models, and contributing to national/international policy development.
