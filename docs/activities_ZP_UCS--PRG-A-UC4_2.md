# Executive summary## Smíchov City – Comprehensive Urban Brownfield Redevelopment

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Smíchov City – Comprehensive Urban Brownfield Redevelopment
- **Alternative Names:** Smíchov City, Smíchovské Město
- **Location:** Smíchov, Prague 5, Prague, Czech Republic
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.0660°N, 14.4085°E (Smíchov railway station area)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Planning and concept development: 2005–2020  
  - Groundbreaking: 2020  
  - Phase 1 completion: Expected by 2022  
  - Full completion: Targeting 2030–2035[^1][^2][^3]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Area: Nearly 20 hectares  
  - Built area: ~400,000 m²  
  - Population: Up to 12,000 residents and workers  
  - Investment value: CZK 20 billion (approx. €800 million)[^2][^3]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Sekyra Group (private developer), in partnership with the City of Prague, Prague 5 municipal district, and multiple architectural studios[^2][^3]
- **Funding Sources:** Primarily private investment (Sekyra Group); additional public sector support for infrastructure and public amenities; potential for public-private partnerships[^2]

#### **Executive Summary**

Smíchov City represents one of the most ambitious and comprehensive urban regeneration initiatives in Central Europe, transforming a derelict brownfield adjacent to Prague’s main Smíchov railway station into a vibrant, sustainable urban district. The project prioritizes the creation of a mixed-use neighborhood, blending residential, commercial, and civic functions with a kilometer-long pedestrian boulevard, extensive green spaces, and state-of-the-art energy-efficient buildings. The redevelopment is guided by participatory planning, with a particular focus on public space quality, social inclusion, and climate adaptation. The design process involved extensive stakeholder engagement, including public consultations and international architectural competitions, resulting in a consensus-driven approach that avoided legal appeals and set new standards for urban development in Prague. Upon completion, Smíchov City will offer over 3,300 new housing units, 9,000 jobs, and more than two hectares of new parks and green corridors. The project’s innovative integration of smart technologies, green infrastructure, and community governance positions it as a model for resilient, people-centered urbanism in the region[^1][^2][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Prague is the Czech Republic’s capital and largest city, with a population of ~1.3 million. It is a major economic, political, and cultural hub in Central Europe, renowned for its historic architecture and robust service-based economy. The city enjoys a high quality of life and plays a leading role in national and regional urban policy[^2][^3].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical development:** Smíchov is a historic industrial district west of the city center, shaped by heavy industry, transport infrastructure, and working-class housing. The late 20th century saw decline and underuse of large sites, particularly around the railway station[^5][^1].
  - **Current land use:** Before the initiative, the area was dominated by underutilized rail yards, warehouses, and vacant lots, with fragmented public space and limited residential amenities[^2][^3].
  - **Demographic profile:** Prague 5 is diverse, with a mix of aging populations, young professionals, and families attracted by new developments. Income levels and social backgrounds are mixed.  
  - **Infrastructure quality:** While well-connected by road, metro, and tram, the site suffered from poor-quality public space, environmental degradation, and barriers to pedestrian movement[^1][^2].
  - **Environmental conditions:** The area lacked greenery, was exposed to noise and air pollution from transport infrastructure, and had limited climate adaptation measures in place[^6][^7].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Severely underutilized land and urban decay in a strategic location  
  - Deficient public realm and green infrastructure  
  - Barriers to connectivity and walkability  
  - Environmental pollution and urban heat island effects  
  - Social fragmentation and lack of community spaces[^1][^2][^6][^7]

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Post-socialist economic restructuring left large brownfields  
  - Policy and market shifts favoring urban revitalization  
  - Community and political support for transforming neglected sites[^5][^2]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City of Prague and Prague 5 authorities (planning, infrastructure, policy)  
  - Sekyra Group (site owner and lead developer)  
  - Local residents, civic associations, heritage stakeholders  
  - International design experts and architects[^2][^3]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Municipal authority led zoning, permitting, and oversight  
  - Integrated with city and district planning agencies[^2]
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - City of Prague Master Plan and brownfield redevelopment strategies  
  - Emphasis on participatory planning, sustainable mobility, and green infrastructure[^2]
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Need for changes to city zoning plan  
  - Heritage and environmental review (including UNESCO oversight)  
  - Strong legal framework for stakeholder engagement[^2]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Transform a neglected brownfield into a modern, sustainable, and inclusive urban neighborhood  
  - Deliver high-quality public space and green infrastructure  
  - Promote mixed-use development and reduce reliance on car transport  
  - Set new standards for participatory and resilient urbanism in Prague[^2][^3]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  Create a new, people-centered city district integrating living, working, recreation, and community life, while enhancing environmental and social sustainability[^2][^3].

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  The project aligns with Prague’s citywide strategy for brownfield regeneration, compact development, and climate adaptation, as well as national objectives for sustainable urban growth[^2][^4].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By redeveloping derelict land into a mixed-use, green, and walkable district, the initiative aims to address housing shortages, improve environmental conditions, foster social cohesion, and stimulate economic growth[^2][^3][^4].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents (existing and new)  
  - Businesses and employees  
  - The wider city population, benefiting from new public spaces and services[^2][^3]
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Participatory planning and consensus building  
  - Integration of advanced urban greening and smart digital infrastructure  
  - Flexible, context-sensitive urban design[^2][^3][^4]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - City leadership prioritizing brownfield regeneration and sustainable urbanism  
  - Desire to boost Prague’s international image and livability[^2]
- **Economic:**  
  - High demand for housing, offices, and retail in a prime location  
  - Long-term return on investment for developer and city[^2][^3]
- **Social:**  
  - Community advocacy for better public space and amenities  
  - Need for social inclusion and participatory governance[^2][^3]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Climate resilience, air quality improvement, and green space expansion[^6][^4][^7]
- **Technological:**  
  - Adoption of energy-efficient building standards and digital platforms for engagement and operations[^2][^4]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Guided by principles of sustainable urbanism, walkability, mixed-use density, and public space activation. Emphasizes the "city of short distances" (everything needed within walking reach)[^2][^4].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Kilometer-long central boulevard as the project’s spine  
  - Block structure reflecting historic Smíchov patterns  
  - Varied architecture by multiple studios for organic diversity  
  - Integration of housing, offices, retail, parks, school, and civic center[^1][^2][^3]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Four main phases over ~12–15 years  
  - Phase 1: 195 housing units, initial office and retail, major public spaces  
  - Later phases: additional housing, major office campus, public amenities, parks[^2][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Energy-efficient building envelopes and systems  
  - Smart infrastructure for environmental monitoring and resource management  
  - Digital platforms for resident feedback and maintenance optimization  
  - Advanced urban greening and stormwater management (e.g., rain gardens, green roofs)[^2][^4][^7]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  Technological (energy, digital), Social (participatory planning), Governance (multi-stakeholder process)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  New systems integrated with existing transport, utilities, and city data platforms; emphasis on interoperability and adaptability[^2][^4]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Multiple architectural competitions judged by city, developer, and experts  
  - Construction led by consortia of prominent contractors (e.g., Strabag)[^2]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Extensive public hearings, workshops, and consultations  
  - Inclusion of civic associations and cultural stakeholders (e.g., preservation of Radlická Kulturní Sportovna)[^2][^3]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Residents and local groups shaped design of public spaces and amenities  
  - Digital and physical forums for feedback and co-creation[^2]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Dedicated coordination team within Sekyra Group  
  - Close collaboration with city planning and regulatory authorities[^2]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total: CZK 20 billion (~€800 million); Phase 1: CZK 3.5 billion[^2][^3]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Primarily private capital (Sekyra Group), with public sector support for infrastructure
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Sales and leasing of residential, commercial, and retail units  
  - Value capture from land appreciation and enhanced amenities[^2]
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Phased sales and leasing; revenue from long-term property management  
  - Public amenities supported through tax revenues and developer contributions

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Complexity of integrating new infrastructure with existing rail, metro, and utilities[^1][^2]
- **Financial:**  
  - Large upfront investment; managing risk over a long timeline  
  - Navigating market fluctuations and sales pace[^2]
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Achieving alignment among diverse stakeholders  
  - Ensuring inclusion and preventing gentrification/displacement[^2][^3]
- **Administrative:**  
  - Navigating complex permitting and regulatory processes  
  - Coordinating multiple design and construction teams[^2]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Remediation of contaminated land  
  - Mitigating noise, air pollution, and urban heat during and after construction[^6][^7]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Phased implementation to manage risks and adapt to feedback  
  - Early and ongoing stakeholder engagement to build consensus[^2]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments based on public input (e.g., more green space, preservation of cultural venues)[^2][^3]
- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of transparent communication and participatory planning  
  - Flexibility in design and phasing to accommodate evolving needs[^2]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - ~400,000 m² of new built area  
  - >3,300 housing units, 9,000 jobs, new school, parks, pedestrian boulevard[^2][^3]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Enhanced public transport accessibility  
  - New educational, medical, and recreational facilities[^2][^3]
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 2+ hectares of new green space  
  - 12,000 people living and/or working in the district upon completion[^2][^3]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved quality of life, increased safety, and social cohesion  
  - Participatory governance and civic engagement[^2][^3]
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Commitment to mixed-income housing and inclusive public amenities  
  - Monitoring of potential gentrification risks[^2][^3]
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No significant displacement reported; ongoing attention required  
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened community networks through shared public spaces  
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Preservation and revitalization of local cultural venues (Radlická Kulturní Sportovna)[^2][^3]

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Thousands of construction and permanent jobs  
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - New business opportunities in retail, services, office sectors  
- **Property Values:**  
  - Anticipated appreciation in local real estate  
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Efficient infrastructure and energy use reduce long-term costs  
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Attention to accessible jobs and services for a broad population[^2][^3]

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Improved urban microclimate, reduced emissions via energy-efficient buildings and sustainable mobility[^2][^4][^7]
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Water recycling, rainwater harvesting, energy efficiency, and smart resource allocation[^4][^7]
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Enhanced air quality and biodiversity through green corridors and parks  
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Remediation of contaminated brownfield; improved waste management[^7]
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Designed to meet high environmental standards; ongoing performance monitoring via digital tools[^2][^4]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened collaboration between city, district, and private sector  
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Set new standards for participatory planning and brownfield redevelopment in Prague[^2][^3]
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Consensus-driven process; no legal appeals during zoning and permitting[^2]

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - High levels of stakeholder satisfaction; rapid sales in first phase  
  - Recognition by city and international observers as a model project[^2][^3]
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Ongoing risk of gentrification; continuous monitoring required  
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Strong prospects for lasting positive impact due to robust design and governance[^2][^3]

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**

- **Contextual Dependencies:**  
  - Success depended on Prague’s strong planning institutions, market demand, and civic engagement traditions  
  - Some elements (e.g., scale, heritage context) may be unique to Prague, but core strategies are broadly transferable[^2][^4]
- **Critical Success Factors:**  
  - Early and sustained stakeholder engagement  
  - Integrated, flexible master planning  
  - Balanced public and private investment
- **Scalability:**  
  - Approach can be adapted to other large urban brownfields, with appropriate localization[^2][^4]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Holistic integration of land use, mobility, and green space**
- **Commitment to participatory governance and consensus-building**
- **Setting new urban development standards for Prague and the Czech Republic**
- **Resilience-oriented design and long-term adaptability**

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **High cost and long timeframe require sustained commitment**
- **Potential for rising property values and affordability pressures**
- **Challenges in fully integrating new district with surrounding neighborhoods**

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Proactive inclusion of community voices and civic associations**
- **Efforts to mix housing types and preserve cultural venues**
- **Risks of gentrification and displacement remain and must be managed proactively**

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term evolution monitored via digital platforms and ongoing stakeholder engagement**
- **Further phases will test capacity to maintain affordability and inclusiveness**
- **Success likely to inspire similar large-scale regeneration efforts in Prague and beyond**
