# Executive summary

## Porto Sustainable Neighbourhood Living Lab (PROBONO Horizon Project)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Porto Sustainable Neighbourhood Living Lab (PROBONO Horizon Project)
- **Alternative Names:** PROBONO Living Lab Porto, PROBONO Porto, PROBONO Green Building Neighbourhood (GBN) Living Lab
- **Location:** Porto, Portugal (specific district: details not publicly disclosed, but part of the city’s broader urban regeneration and climate neutrality strategy)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Not publicly available; situated within the Porto metropolitan area
- **Timeline:** 2021–2026 (project duration); Living Lab activities ongoing since 2022
- **Project Scale:** Pilot-scale urban district transformation, targeting multiple residential and mixed-use buildings; population affected: several thousand residents; investment: part of a €25.25M Horizon 2020 project
- **Leading Organization(s):** Municipality of Porto (public authority), in partnership with local businesses, building owners, and the PROBONO consortium (including the Technical University of Crete, technology providers, and research institutions)
- **Funding Sources:** European Union (Horizon 2020), national and local government, private sector partners

#### **Executive Summary**
The Porto Sustainable Neighbourhood Living Lab is a flagship pilot within the EU-funded PROBONO project, aiming to transform selected urban districts into Green Building Neighbourhoods (GBNs) with positive energy balance and zero carbon emissions. The initiative leverages smart technologies, community engagement, and innovative urban planning to pilot solutions for energy efficiency, sustainability, and social well-being. Key interventions include the deployment of IoT-enabled smart elevator management systems, real-time analytics for building usage, and targeted behaviour change campaigns to promote energy conservation and healthier lifestyles. The project is designed to be scalable, replicable, and aligned with Porto’s broader climate neutrality and urban regeneration goals. By integrating digital innovation with participatory urban governance, the Porto Living Lab serves as a model for sustainable, people-focused urban development in Europe.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Porto is Portugal’s second-largest city, with a population of approximately 230,000 (metropolitan area: 1.3 million). It is a historic port city with a strong cultural identity, a diversified economy (services, tourism, industry), and a Mediterranean climate. Porto is a key node in the Iberian Peninsula’s urban network and has been recognized for its urban innovation and sustainability efforts.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The Living Lab is situated in a mixed-use urban district undergoing regeneration. The area features a blend of historic and modern buildings, with a mix of residential, commercial, and public spaces. The district has faced challenges related to aging infrastructure, energy inefficiency, and social inequality. Demographically, it is diverse, with a mix of income levels, age groups, and cultural backgrounds. Existing infrastructure includes public transport, utilities, and green spaces, but there are gaps in accessibility and sustainability.
- **Environmental Conditions:** The district is subject to urban heat island effects, air pollution, and climate change impacts (e.g., increased temperatures, extreme weather events). There is a need for improved energy efficiency, green infrastructure, and climate resilience.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** The district faced high energy consumption, inefficient building systems, limited public engagement in sustainability, and a lack of integrated urban planning. There was also a need to address social and health inequalities, particularly in relation to mobility and building access.
- **Triggering Events:** Porto’s commitment to climate neutrality (target: carbon neutrality by 2030), EU urban regeneration funding, and the PROBONO project’s call for innovative GBN pilots.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Key actors included the Municipality of Porto, local residents, building owners, businesses, technology providers, and research institutions. Interests ranged from urban regeneration and climate action to economic development and social inclusion.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** The project is governed by a multi-stakeholder consortium, with the Municipality of Porto as the lead public authority. The PROBONO consortium provides technical and research support.
- **Relevant Policies:** Porto’s Climate Neutrality Plan, Urban Regeneration Strategy, and EU urban sustainability directives. The project aligns with the EU Green Deal and Horizon 2020 priorities.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** Enablers include EU funding, supportive local policies, and strong stakeholder networks. Barriers include regulatory complexity, public participation challenges, and the need for capacity building.

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:** To create a sustainable, energy-positive, and socially inclusive urban district through the integration of smart technologies, community engagement, and innovative urban planning.
- **Vision Statement:** “A people-focused, climate-neutral urban district where technology and community work together to enhance sustainability, health, and quality of life.”
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** The initiative is aligned with Porto’s Climate Neutrality Plan, the EU Green Deal, and the PROBONO project’s vision for scalable, sustainable GBNs.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** By deploying smart technologies (e.g., IoT sensors, digital platforms) and engaging the community in co-design and co-delivery, the project aims to reduce energy consumption, improve building efficiency, and promote healthier lifestyles. The causal pathway is: technology deployment → real-time data and analytics → targeted interventions → behaviour change → improved sustainability and health outcomes.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents, building owners, businesses, and the broader community. The project aims to benefit all stakeholders through improved energy efficiency, reduced costs, and enhanced quality of life.
- **Innovation Elements:** The integration of smart elevator management, real-time analytics, and community engagement platforms is distinctive. The project also pilots new business models and governance approaches for sustainable urban development.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political Drivers:** Porto’s climate neutrality ambitions, EU urban innovation priorities, and local political leadership.
- **Economic Drivers:** Investment in urban regeneration, economic development, and job creation.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The project is guided by the principles of smart cities, sustainable urban development, and participatory governance. The design emphasizes integration, scalability, and replicability.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** The project involves the retrofitting of existing buildings with smart technologies, the creation of green spaces, and the improvement of public infrastructure. The spatial design focuses on connectivity, accessibility, and sustainability.
- **Scope and Phasing:** The project is structured in phases: baseline assessment, technology deployment, community engagement, monitoring and evaluation, and replication. Each phase is designed to build on the previous one, with continuous feedback and adaptation.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** IoT sensors, cloud-based incident reporting platforms, digital engagement tools, smart elevator management systems, real-time analytics platforms.
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (smart sensors, digital platforms), social (community engagement, behaviour change campaigns), and governance (multi-stakeholder consortium, participatory planning).
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies are integrated with existing urban systems through partnerships with local authorities, businesses, and residents. The project uses a cloud-based decision support-planning tool to optimize design and operations.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Implementers were selected through competitive tendering and partnership agreements. The Municipality of Porto leads procurement, with technical support from the PROBONO consortium.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** The project uses participatory methods, including workshops, co-design sessions, and digital platforms, to engage residents, businesses, and other stakeholders.
- **Participatory Elements:** Community input is sought at all stages, from design to implementation and evaluation. Residents are involved in co-creating solutions and providing feedback.
- **Project Management:** The project is managed by a dedicated team within the Municipality of Porto, with support from the PROBONO consortium. Regular monitoring and evaluation ensure continuous improvement.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Total investment: part of a €25.25M Horizon 2020 project; Porto’s share: not publicly disclosed.
- **Funding Mix:** EU funding (Horizon 2020), national and local government, private sector partners.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Not applicable (publicly funded project).
- **Financial Sustainability:** The project is designed to be scalable and replicable, with potential for future funding and investment.
