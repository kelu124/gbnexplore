# Executive summary

## mySMARTLife Project – Nantes

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** mySMARTLife – Transition of EU Cities towards a New Concept of Smart Life and Economy
- **Alternative Names:** mySMARTLife Nantes Demonstration, Horizon 2020 mySMARTLife
- **Location:** Nantes, Île de Nantes, Nantes Métropole, France
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 47.2061° N, 1.5528° W (Île de Nantes)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Start: December 1, 2016
  - Key Milestones: Baseline and urban context analysis (2017), implementation phases (2018–2021), final evaluation (2022)
  - Completion: September 30, 2022 (project duration: 70 months)[^1]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Nantes demonstration budget: €4M (within €21.5M overall)
  - Area: Focus on Île de Nantes (major urban redevelopment district), with citywide digital and mobility actions[^2]
  - Population impacted: Not precisely quantified, but covers multiple neighborhoods and public housing developments
- **Leading Organization(s):** Nantes Métropole (city/metropolitan authority), Nantes Métropole Habitat, Semitan (public transport), Armines, Atlanpole, Télécom Bretagne, Cerema, Ecole des Mines de Nantes, Engie, Enedis[^2]
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - Primary: European Union Horizon 2020 grant (€18.6M of €21.5M total)
  - Secondary: Local public funding, contributions from project partners[^2][^1]

#### **Executive Summary**

The mySMARTLife project in Nantes, part of the EU’s Horizon 2020 “lighthouse city” initiative, was a large-scale experiment in urban energy transition, digitalization, and resident empowerment. Focusing on the Île de Nantes district and extending to the metropolitan region, the project aimed to demonstrate how integrated smart technologies—such as IoT sensors, smart HVAC systems, and digital dashboards—could optimize building energy performance, enhance district heating, and improve indoor comfort. Residents were equipped with real-time tools to monitor and control their energy use, receive feedback, and participate in climate management, fostering behavioral change. The project also tackled system interoperability, privacy, and user education, seeking to make new solutions both effective and accepted.

Supported by a consortium of municipal, academic, and private sector partners, mySMARTLife implemented around forty interventions in Nantes—ranging from building retrofits, renewable energy integration, and district heating optimization to citywide data infrastructure and sustainable mobility pilots. These actions resulted in reduced energy consumption, substantial carbon emissions savings, and higher occupant satisfaction. As one of three “lighthouse” cities (with Hamburg and Helsinki), Nantes contributed to a European-wide strategy for smart, sustainable urban transformation, with strong emphasis on replication and scalability. The project stands out for linking technical innovation with participatory governance and robust policy integration, positioning Nantes as a model for the digital and ecological transition of European cities[^2][^1][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** 
  - Nantes is the sixth-largest city in France, with a metropolitan population of over 600,000.
  - The city is a major economic center in western France, historically industrial, now diversified into services, creative industries, and green tech.
  - Geographically, Nantes is located along the Loire River, with a mild Atlantic climate.
  - Nationally, Nantes is recognized for its progressive urban planning and sustainability leadership, holding titles such as European Green Capital (2013)[^4].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Île de Nantes** is a 337-hectare island in the city center, formerly industrial, now undergoing major redevelopment into a mixed-use eco-district.
  - Land use: Former factories and warehouses converted to housing, offices, public spaces, and cultural venues.
  - Demographics: Diverse, including social housing, students, young professionals, families, with a growing population due to new development.
  - Infrastructure: Mix of legacy and new high-efficiency buildings, modernized district heating, extensive public transport, digital networks.
  - Environmental challenges: Air quality, noise from urban density, flood resilience due to riverine location, brownfield remediation[^4].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** 
  - High energy consumption and emissions in older building stock.
  - Need for integrated management of heating, cooling, and ventilation for comfort and efficiency.
  - Fragmented digital infrastructure and data silos.
  - Limited resident engagement with energy/climate management.
  - Urbanization pressure on infrastructure and resources.

- **Triggering Events:** 
  - Policy commitments to EU climate targets and national energy transition.
  - Opportunity to participate in Horizon 2020 “smart cities” funding as a lighthouse city.
  - Local political momentum for digital innovation and ecological transition[^2][^1].

- **Stakeholder Landscape:** 
  - City government prioritizing sustainability and innovation.
  - Public housing operator (Nantes Métropole Habitat) seeking to improve housing quality and efficiency.
  - Utilities (Engie, Enedis) interested in smart grids and district heating.
  - Universities and research institutes providing technical expertise.
  - Residents as both end-users and co-creators, with structured citizen dialogue[^2][^4].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** 
  - Nantes Métropole has strong policy integration via urban master plans (SCoT), mobility plans (PDU), housing strategy (PLH), and a Climate Plan[^4].
  - The city has a history of citizen participation in planning and environmental decision-making.

- **Relevant Policies:** 
  - National and EU directives on energy efficiency, renewable integration, and urban data openness.
  - Local policies: EcoDistrict certification, environmental clauses in procurement, strategic climate planning[^4].

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** 
  - Supportive regulatory frameworks for energy retrofitting and renewable deployment.
  - Some legal complexity around data privacy and multi-owner building governance.
  - Institutional silos between utilities, housing, and digital services were initial barriers.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:** 
  - Achieve substantial reductions in energy consumption and carbon emissions.
  - Upgrade building stock for high-performance energy standards.
  - Integrate digital monitoring and user engagement tools.
  - Demonstrate scalable, replicable smart city solutions.

- **Vision Statement:** 
  - To create a climate-resilient, digitally empowered city where residents are active participants in energy transition and sustainable urban living[^1][^3].

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** 
  - Directly supports Nantes’ Climate Plan, EU Energy Union objectives, and the Paris Agreement.
  - Integrates with the city’s EcoDistrict and sustainable mobility programs[^4].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** 
  - If buildings and neighborhoods are equipped with smart energy systems and users are empowered with real-time feedback, both behavioral change and system efficiency will drive down energy use and emissions.

- **Target Beneficiaries:** 
  - Residents of retrofitted and new buildings, especially in social housing.
  - City administration and utilities (improved data, system management).
  - Broader metropolitan community (environmental, economic, and social gains).

- **Innovation Elements:** 
  - Real-time digital dashboards for residents.
  - Automated HVAC controls linked to environmental sensors (e.g., system pauses if windows are opened).
  - Integration of renewable energy with district heating.
  - Open urban data platforms.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** 
  - Strong mayoral and metropolitan leadership on sustainability.
  - Alignment with European policy goals and funding opportunities.

- **Economic:** 
  - Anticipated operational cost savings.
  - Opportunity to catalyze local green tech markets and jobs.

- **Social:** 
  - Response to demand for healthier, more comfortable living environments.
  - Emphasis on citizen participation and digital inclusion.

- **Environmental:** 
  - Addressing climate change with urban-scale mitigation and adaptation.
  - Resource efficiency and air quality improvement.

- **Technological:** 
  - Emergence of affordable IoT, building management systems, and open data standards.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** 
  - Integrated urban planning combining technological, social, and economic interventions.
  - Focus on “smart people” (citizen engagement), “smart economy” (innovation, jobs), and “smart environment” (low-carbon systems)[^1][^3].

- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Retrofitting of existing buildings and construction of new high-efficiency structures.
  - Upgrades to district heating, including renewable integration.
  - Installation of digital sensors and communication infrastructure.
  - Spatial focus on Île de Nantes as a demonstration area, with some citywide actions (e.g., mobility, urban data infrastructure)[^2][^4].

- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Baseline analysis (2017), followed by technology deployment, resident engagement, and continuous evaluation over 5+ years[^4][^1].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - Smart HVAC systems and district heating with digital controls.
  - IoT sensors for temperature, humidity, air quality.
  - Resident dashboards for real-time monitoring and feedback.
  - Automated energy-saving triggers (e.g., HVAC pause on window opening).
  - Integration of renewable energy sources (solar PV, biomass, waste heat).
  - Urban data platform for aggregation, analytics, and open access[^2][^5][^6].

- **Innovation Type:** 
  - Technological (digitalization, automation, renewables)
  - Social (participatory dashboards, resident engagement)
  - Governance (cross-sectoral coordination, citizen dialogue)
  - Process (replication and scaling model)

- **Integration Approach:** 
  - Technologies integrated with legacy infrastructure (district heating, public housing).
  - Use of data standards and interoperability protocols.
  - Feedback loops between user behavior, system performance, and facility management[^2][^1].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** 
  - Competitive tenders with environmental and innovation criteria.
  - Partnerships with research institutions and private sector for technical delivery.

- **Stakeholder Engagement:** 
  - Structured citizen dialogue (meetings, surveys, co-design workshops).
  - Communication campaigns and feedback mechanisms[^4][^2].

- **Participatory Elements:** 
  - Residents could adjust climate settings, receive energy notifications, and contribute to user experience refinement.

- **Project Management:** 
  - Multi-partner consortium with defined work packages and milestones.
  - Oversight by Nantes Métropole and regular reporting to the EU.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** 
  - Nantes demonstration: €4M (portion of €21.5M total project budget)[^2].
  - Cost breakdown: Building retrofits, digital systems, renewable installations, citizen engagement.

- **Funding Mix:** 
  - 86% from Horizon 2020 grant, remainder from local and partner contributions[^1].

- **Revenue Mechanisms:** 
  - Limited direct revenue (public sector investment), but potential for cost savings reinvestment.

- **Financial Sustainability:** 
  - Emphasis on operational cost reductions and long-term maintenance by public utilities and housing providers.

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:** 
  - Interoperability between legacy and new building systems.
  - Complexity of integrating digital controls across diverse building types[^2].

- **Financial:** 
  - Managing budget across multiple interventions and partners.
  - Ensuring cost-effectiveness and value for investment.

- **Social and Political:** 
  - Need for resident education and buy-in.
  - Privacy concerns around monitoring and data collection.

- **Administrative:** 
  - Coordination across numerous stakeholders (public sector, utilities, technology providers).
  - Data governance and compliance with privacy/GDPR regulations.

- **Environmental:** 
  - Technical constraints of retrofitting older buildings.
  - Ensuring renewable integration did not disrupt grid or heating reliability.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving:** 
  - Iterative prototyping and testing of digital platforms.
  - User training and transparent communication about data privacy and benefits.
  - Phased roll-out to manage risk and enable learning.

- **Design Modifications:** 
  - Adjusting interfaces and notifications in response to resident feedback.
  - Upgrading interoperability standards as new issues arose.

- **Lessons Learned:** 
  - The importance of co-design with users to ensure technology adoption.
  - Need for robust data governance frameworks.
  - Flexibility in project management to handle technical and organizational complexity.

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** 
  - Retrofitting and/or new construction of high-performance buildings (part of 147,054 m² across the three lighthouse cities)[^1].
  - Smart HVAC and digital controls implemented in targeted buildings.
  - Renewable energy installations (district heating, solar, biomass).

- **Service Delivery:** 
  - Real-time energy monitoring and user control for residents.
  - Enhanced facility management through data analytics.
  - Improved district heating and climate control services.

- **Quantitative Metrics:** 
  - Energy savings: 107.5 kWh/m²/yr improvement in building performance.
  - Energy saved: 8.78 GWh/year across all lighthouse cities.
  - 29% of final net building energy use from renewables[^1].
  - CO₂ emissions reduced (portion of 12,477 tCO₂/yr saved across the project).

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** 
  - Improved indoor comfort and air quality.
  - Increased resident empowerment and satisfaction.
  - Enhanced digital literacy and engagement.

- **Equity Outcomes:** 
  - Focus on public and affordable housing; efforts to ensure benefits reach lower-income residents.
  - Avoidance of displacement or gentrification in project areas[^2].

- **Social Capital:** 
  - Strengthened networks between city, residents, and technical partners.
  - Culture of participation in sustainability initiatives.

- **Cultural Impacts:** 
  - Reinforcement of Nantes’ identity as a leader in urban innovation and sustainability.

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** 
  - Direct employment in retrofitting, technology installation, and project management.
  - Indirect support for local tech and construction sectors.

- **Economic Activity:** 
  - Attraction of investment and innovation ecosystem growth.
  - Development of new business models for smart city services.

- **Cost Savings:** 
  - Lowered energy bills for residents and city.
  - Reduced operational costs for social housing and public buildings.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** 
  - Significant reduction in building sector carbon emissions.
  - Enhanced climate resilience through better energy management.

- **Resource Management:** 
  - Higher proportion of energy from renewables.
  - Improved efficiency in heating and cooling.

- **Environmental Quality:** 
  - Better air quality and lower noise due to efficient systems and mobility measures.

- **Sustainability Metrics:** 
  - Performance aligned with national and EU environmental standards and EcoDistrict certification[^4].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** 
  - Enhanced cross-departmental coordination in Nantes Métropole.
  - New models for public-private-academic collaboration.

- **Policy Influence:** 
  - Model for replication in other EU cities; inspiration for local policy updates.

- **Transparency and Accountability:** 
  - Open data platforms and participatory processes improved governance quality.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** 
  - Most stated objectives for energy savings, digitalization, and occupant satisfaction achieved or exceeded.
  - Notable recognition: Eurocities Award 2023 for managing the energy crisis sustainably[^3].

- **Unintended Consequences:** 
  - Increased complexity in building management.
  - Need for ongoing support to maintain resident engagement.

- **Long-term Viability:** 
  - Strong basis for continued innovation and scaling, integrated into Nantes’ ongoing urban strategy.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Holistic integration** of technology, policy, and user engagement.
- **Demonstrated energy and emissions reductions** with robust performance data.
- **Strong institutional partnerships** and effective governance.
- **Replicability**, with tools and lessons disseminated across Europe.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity** of technical integration, especially in older or multi-owner buildings.
- **Sustained user engagement** can wane after initial implementation.
- **Data privacy** and security challenges, especially with real-time monitoring.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Proactive focus** on social housing and broad resident inclusion.
- Efforts to avoid displacement, but continued vigilance needed as neighborhoods evolve.

#### **Future Outlook**

- The project has laid groundwork for **ongoing digital and ecological transition** in Nantes.
- Ongoing challenges include **scaling across the full city**, maintaining user engagement, and navigating technology upgrades.
- Plans for further integration with mobility, water, and waste sectors, and continued participation in European urban innovation networks[^2][^1][^3].
