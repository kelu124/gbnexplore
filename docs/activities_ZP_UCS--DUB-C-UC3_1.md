## Seestadt Aspern

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Seestadt Aspern
- **Alternative Names:** Aspern Seestadt, Vienna Smart City – Seestadt Aspern
- **Location:** Vienna, 22nd District (Donaustadt), Austria
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 48.2283° N, 16.4672° E
- **Timeline:** 
  - Planning began: 2005
  - Construction started: 2007
  - Ongoing development (phased completion until 2030)
  - Climate neutrality target: 2040
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Area: 800 acres (324 hectares)
  - Population: Planned for over 25,000 residents
  - Workplaces: Planned for 20,000 jobs
  - Investment: Estimated at €5 billion
- **Leading Organization(s):** Wien 3420 (Vienna’s urban development company), City of Vienna, Aspern Smart City Research (ASCR), private developers, research institutions
- **Funding Sources:** Public funding (City of Vienna), private investment, PPP (public-private partnerships), EU research grants

#### **Executive Summary**
Seestadt Aspern is one of Europe’s largest and most ambitious urban development projects, transforming a former airport brownfield into a sustainable, mixed-use neighborhood. The initiative integrates residential, commercial, and recreational spaces with a strong emphasis on green building principles, smart mobility, and community engagement. The project is guided by Vienna’s Smart City Strategy and aims to achieve climate neutrality by 2040. Seestadt Aspern is notable for its innovative use of digital planning tools, extensive green spaces, and integration of renewable energy. The development has become a model for sustainable urban living, attracting international attention and serving as an urban laboratory for testing and exporting green building and smart city solutions. The project’s success is measured not only by its environmental performance but also by its social and economic impacts, including improved quality of life, job creation, and community involvement.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Vienna is the capital of Austria, with a population of approximately 1.9 million. It is known for its high quality of life, extensive green spaces, and strong public transport system. The city has a diverse economic base, with significant contributions from services, technology, and research.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Historical Development Patterns:** The 22nd District, Donaustadt, was historically an industrial and airport area. The closure of Aspern Airfield in 1977 left a large brownfield site.
  - **Current Land Use Composition:** The area is being redeveloped with a mix of residential, commercial, and recreational spaces. The master plan emphasizes high density, mixed-use development, and extensive green spaces.
  - **Demographic Profile:** The area is planned to attract a diverse population, including young professionals, families, and students. The demographic profile is expected to be similar to the city average, with a mix of income levels and cultural backgrounds.
  - **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:** The area is well-connected to the city center via public transport, including the extension of the U2 subway line. The infrastructure is being upgraded to support the new development.
  - **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** The site is a former airport, with some environmental contamination. The area is also subject to urban heat island effects, which the project aims to mitigate through green infrastructure.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** 
  - Inadequate housing and job opportunities
  - Environmental degradation from the former airport
  - Traffic congestion and car dependency
  - Social inequality and lack of community engagement
- **Triggering Events:** 
  - Closure of Aspern Airfield in 1977
  - Vienna’s postwar population low and economic stagnation
  - Growing awareness of climate change and sustainability
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** 
  - City of Vienna (government)
  - Wien 3420 (urban development company)
  - Private developers
  - Research institutions (e.g., University of Natural Resources and Life Sciences)
  - Community groups and residents

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** The project is governed by the City of Vienna, with oversight from Wien 3420 and various city departments. The development is guided by the Vienna Smart City Strategy and the Smart City Wien Framework Strategy.
- **Relevant Policies:** 
  - Vienna Climate Protection Program
  - Vienna Smart City Strategy
  - National Energy and Climate Plan for Austria (NECP)
  - EU Green Deal
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** 
  - Strong local governance and planning authority
  - Supportive national and EU policies
  - Challenges related to brownfield redevelopment and environmental remediation

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The project is guided by the principles of sustainable urban development, including high density, mixed-use development, and extensive green spaces. The master plan emphasizes the integration of residential, commercial, and recreational spaces.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - High-density, mixed-use buildings
  - Extensive green spaces and blue-green infrastructure
  - Smart mobility hubs and efficient public transport
  - Energy-efficient district heating and renewable energy systems
- **Scope and Phasing:** The project is being implemented in phases, with each phase focusing on different aspects of the development. The phased approach allows for flexibility and adaptation based on lessons learned.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - Digital planning tools (GIS, digital twins)
  - Smart mobility hubs (electric vehicle charging, bike-sharing)
  - Energy-efficient district heating and renewable energy systems (solar panels, heat pumps)
  - Green building certification (BREEAM, LEED)
- **Innovation Type:** 
  - Technological: Smart city technologies, renewable energy systems
  - Social: Community engagement and participatory planning
  - Financial: Public-private partnerships, innovative financing models
  - Governance: Collaborative governance and stakeholder engagement
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies are integrated with existing urban systems through a phased and adaptive approach, ensuring that new solutions are compatible with the city’s infrastructure and planning processes.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Implementers are selected through a competitive bidding process, with a focus on sustainability and innovation. Public-private partnerships are used to leverage private investment and expertise.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** The project involves extensive community engagement, including public consultations, participatory planning, and ongoing communication with residents and businesses.
- **Participatory Elements:** Opportunities for community input and co-creation are provided through public consultations, workshops, and online platforms.
- **Project Management:** The project is managed by Wien 3420, with oversight from the City of Vienna and various city departments. Project management structures and processes are designed to ensure coordination and accountability.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** 
  - Total investment: €5 billion
  - Cost breakdown: 
    - Infrastructure: €1.5 billion
    - Residential and commercial buildings: €2.5 billion
    - Green spaces and blue-green infrastructure: €0.5 billion
    - Smart city technologies: €0.5 billion
- **Funding Mix:** 
  - Public funding: 60%
  - Private investment: 30%
  - PPP: 10%
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** 
  - User fees (e.g., for public transport, utilities)
  - Tax increments
  - Land value capture
- **Financial Sustainability:** Plans for long-term financial viability include ongoing revenue from user fees, tax increments, and land value capture, as well as cost savings from energy efficiency and resource management.
