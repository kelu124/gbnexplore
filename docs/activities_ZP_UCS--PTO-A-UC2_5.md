# Executive summary## La Pinada Eco-Neighbourhood (MAKING-CITY)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** La Pinada Eco-Neighbourhood (Barrio La Pinada), part of the Horizon 2020 MAKING-CITY Project  
- **Alternative Names:** Barrio La Pinada, La Pinada District  
- **Location:** Paterna, Valencia Metropolitan Area, Spain (frequently referenced as near Madrid in EU project materials, but administratively and geographically in Valencia)[^1][^2][^3]  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 39.5155° N, 0.4451° W  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Conception: 2017  
  - MAKING-CITY Project Launch: December 2018  
  - Current Status: Design phase, with planned construction and demonstration activities ongoing through the 2020s[^4][^2][^5][^6]  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: 250,000 m² (25 hectares, ~61 acres)  
  - Intended Population: ~1,000 families (~3,000–4,000 residents)[^2][^3][^7]  
  - Investment: Not publicly disclosed; likely multi-million EUR, with significant EU funding support  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Private: ZubiLabs (project developer), Sustainable Towns, Imagine Montessori School  
  - Public: Supported by the MAKING-CITY consortium, CARTIF Technology Centre (project coordination)[^4][^2][^6][^8]  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Horizon 2020 (EU research and innovation program)  
  - Private investment (impact funds, Zubi Group)  
  - Co-financing by project partners[^4][^5][^8]  

#### **Executive Summary**

La Pinada Eco-Neighbourhood, a flagship demonstration within the Horizon 2020 MAKING-CITY project, represents Spain’s first large-scale, co-created eco-district. Located at the edge of Valencia in Paterna, it is conceived not only as a residential development, but as a prototype for **positive energy districts**—urban areas that generate more renewable energy than they consume annually. The initiative is grounded in deep **citizen co-design**, with over 400 future residents and multi-disciplinary experts shaping its vision, spatial layout, and service offerings. The project integrates a suite of advanced technologies, including **district energy management platforms**, real-time demand response, and renewable energy generation and storage systems. Social and behavioral innovations—such as user-driven energy platforms, digital interfaces for mobility and energy scheduling, and ongoing sustainability education—aim to foster a culture of participation and environmental stewardship.

La Pinada’s master plan prioritizes **car-free design**, sustainable mobility, circular resource flows, and close integration with nature, all anchored by the Imagine Montessori School as a community hub. The project addresses pressing urban challenges: climate resilience, resource efficiency, and social inclusion, while aiming to deliver measurable reductions in carbon emissions, utility costs, and environmental impacts. As a demonstration site in the MAKING-CITY project, La Pinada will provide lessons for the upscaling of positive energy districts throughout Europe, advancing policy, business models, and citizen engagement strategies for the climate-neutral cities agenda[^4][^2][^3][^6][^8].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Valencia is Spain’s third largest city (pop. ~800,000; metro area ~1.6 million), with a dynamic economy based on services, tourism, industry, and innovation. The city is a Mediterranean port with a mild climate, rapid urban growth, and a strong tradition of civic engagement and design excellence.  
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  La Pinada is sited in Paterna, a fast-growing suburb northwest of Valencia, adjacent to green spaces and well-connected to urban infrastructure. The area was previously undeveloped peri-urban land, with a transition from agricultural and forested uses to new residential and educational facilities.  
  - Land use: Planned as a mixed-use, medium-density neighborhood  
  - Demographics: Targeting a diverse, multi-generational, and socially inclusive population, with an emphasis on families and young professionals[^2][^3][^7]  
  - Infrastructure: Proximity to transit, road networks, and the city’s innovation corridor; baseline infrastructure required significant upgrading for energy, water, and mobility integration.  
  - Environmental conditions: Mediterranean climate, moderate water stress, high solar gain, and need for local climate adaptation (heat mitigation, urban biodiversity)[^2][^9][^3]

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Urban sprawl, car-dependency, and lack of sustainable housing models in Spanish suburban development  
  - Rising energy costs and emissions, low adoption of renewables at neighborhood scale  
  - Fragmented communities and weak social capital in new developments  
  - Inadequate integration of nature and public space in conventional urbanization  
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Growing policy focus on climate-neutral cities (EU and national level)  
  - Private sector leadership (ZubiLabs) seeking to create a demonstration for regenerative urbanism and impact investment[^8]  
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Future residents (co-creators), project developers, local government, technology partners, educational institutions (Imagine Montessori School), EU research networks[^2][^9][^8]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Project developed under local municipal planning authority, but also aligned with regional and national strategies for sustainable urbanization and climate action[^10][^3]  
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 (MAKING-CITY) PED framework  
  - Spanish national and Valencia regional sustainable development policies  
  - Local zoning adapted to integrate mixed uses, car-free zones, and advanced energy systems  
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive: EU funding, innovation-friendly planning codes  
  - Barriers: Complexity of integrating new technologies, scaling co-design, and aligning multi-level governance[^4][^9][^6]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Develop a **net-positive energy neighborhood** that generates more renewable energy than it consumes  
  - Foster **social cohesion** and active citizenship through co-design and participatory management  
  - Demonstrate scalable solutions for **climate-neutral urbanization**, resource efficiency, and digital innovation  
- **Vision Statement:**  
  “A vibrant, healthy, and inclusive community, co-created by residents, where energy, mobility, and nature are integrated to regenerate people and planet.”[^2][^3][^9][^8]  
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - MAKING-CITY’s Positive Energy District (PED) roadmap for EU cities  
  - Valencia’s and Spain’s climate neutrality and smart city goals  
  - UN Sustainable Development Goals (especially SDGs 7, 11, 13)[^4][^5][^6]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By integrating advanced energy technologies, participatory governance, and sustainable design at district scale, La Pinada will deliver measurable social, economic, and environmental benefits, serving as a replicable model for European urban transformation[^4][^5][^6].  
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  Future residents (including families, elderly, and socially diverse groups), local government, educational community, and broader urban innovation networks[^2][^3][^8].  
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Co-creation methodology (resident-led design)  
  - PED technical systems (renewables, storage, smart grids)  
  - Digital platforms for energy and mobility management  
  - Integration of circular economy, nature-based solutions, and social innovation[^4][^2][^9][^6]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** EU and local government climate commitments, post-carbon city vision  
- **Economic:** Impact investment, business model innovation for sustainable real estate  
- **Social:** Resident demand for healthy, inclusive, family-friendly neighborhoods  
- **Environmental:** Urgent need for decarbonization, resource efficiency, and urban adaptation  
- **Technological:** Advances in energy management, IoT, digital co-creation tools, and sustainable construction[^4][^2][^9][^6][^8]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Positive Energy District (PED) model: annual net-zero or positive energy and carbon balance  
  - Co-creation and participatory urbanism: residents as active designers and ongoing stewards  
  - Integration of built/natural environment, mobility, and digital infrastructure[^4][^2][^9][^6]
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Car-free streets and shared spaces  
  - Mixed-use buildings with high performance envelopes  
  - Central public plaza, community gardens, green corridors  
  - Imagine Montessori School as anchor institution[^2][^3][^9][^8]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Design phase (2017–2020): extensive co-design workshops, master planning  
  - Implementation phase (2021–onwards): infrastructure, building construction, energy systems roll-out  
  - Ongoing: monitoring, community development, technology demonstration[^2][^9][^8]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - District energy management platforms  
  - Renewable energy systems (solar PV, potentially geothermal/biomass)  
  - Real-time demand response and energy storage  
  - Smart mobility: tram, bike lanes, shared electric vehicles  
  - Water reuse and circular resource management  
  - Digital interfaces for resident engagement and energy scheduling[^4][^2][^3][^9][^6]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: PEDs, smart metering, energy platforms  
  - Social: co-creation, participatory governance  
  - Process: integrated planning and design, impact investment models  
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Technologies are designed from the outset to be interoperable and user-friendly  
  - Digital platforms connect energy, mobility, and environmental monitoring  
  - Close partnership with public agencies and research actors for regulatory and technical alignment[^4][^2][^9][^6]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - ZubiLabs as lead developer, selected international design and engineering teams[^9]  
  - Technology partners via MAKING-CITY consortium  
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Over 400 future residents involved in co-design workshops  
  - Partnerships with local schools, businesses, and public agencies[^2][^9][^11][^8]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Regular workshops, surveys, and digital feedback channels  
  - Residents involved in decisions on energy, mobility, landscape, and governance[^9][^11]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Cross-sectoral project team, supported by MAKING-CITY coordination mechanisms  
  - Iterative, adaptive management with continuous learning loops[^4][^2][^9][^6]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not fully disclosed; substantial EU Horizon 2020 support, private investment, and in-kind contributions  
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Horizon 2020 MAKING-CITY (EU)  
  - Private capital (Zubi Group, impact funds)  
  - Co-financing by project partners[^4][^5][^6][^8]
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Sale/rental of residential units  
  - Service fees for energy and mobility solutions  
  - Value capture from enhanced land and property values  
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Business models tested for PEDs, including local energy markets  
  - Focus on cost savings for residents, reinvestment in community services[^5][^6][^8]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integrating diverse renewable energy sources and ensuring grid stability  
  - Deploying real-time demand response at neighborhood scale  
  - Achieving interoperability between digital platforms and legacy infrastructure[^4][^9][^6]
- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront costs for advanced infrastructure  
  - Ensuring affordability for all resident segments  
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Aligning diverse resident preferences in co-design  
  - Navigating regulatory ambiguities for new business models  
- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordinating among multiple public and private actors  
  - Managing a complex, phased development process  
- **Environmental:**  
  - Ensuring biodiversity and ecosystem integration  
  - Managing construction impacts and climate resilience[^4][^9][^6]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Iterative design and prototyping, learning from demonstration phases  
  - Close dialogue with regulators and city authorities  
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusting energy system sizing based on technical and user feedback  
  - Revising public space design to enhance accessibility and biodiversity  
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Co-creation requires significant time and facilitation resources, but increases buy-in  
  - Technology adoption is accelerated by user-friendly digital interfaces and ongoing education[^4][^2][^9][^6]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Master-planned eco-district, central school and community facilities  
  - Energy, water, and mobility infrastructure (in progress)[^2][^3][^9]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Smart energy and mobility services, sustainability education, public spaces  
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Target: net-positive energy balance (PED) for ~1,000 families  
  - 70% car-free households, 600 hectares of accessible green spaces (projected for the district and surroundings)[^7]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved well-being, comfort, and health  
  - High resident engagement and sense of ownership  
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Intentional social inclusion in resident recruitment and service design  
  - Efforts to ensure affordability and diversity of residents  
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No prior residential population on site; risk managed by inclusive policies  
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened community networks through co-creation and shared facilities  
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - School as a hub for sustainability culture and civic engagement[^2][^3][^9][^11]

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Direct construction, technology, and service jobs  
  - Projected 4,358 new jobs across MAKING-CITY PED roll-out (all cities)[^5]
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - New business models for energy, mobility, and circular economy services  
- **Property Values:**  
  - Likely increase due to green premium and quality of life enhancements  
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Utility cost reductions from energy efficiency and renewables  
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Targeted support for local businesses and social enterprises[^4][^2][^3][^5]

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Substantial GHG emission reductions via renewable energy and efficiency  
  - Support for Madrid and Valencia’s climate neutrality targets[^4][^5][^6]
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Water reuse, circular waste systems, sustainable materials  
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Enhanced air quality, noise reduction, expanded green space  
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Improved waste management, minimized construction impacts  
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Designed to meet or exceed EU PED and eco-district standards[^4][^2][^3][^6]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened planning and project management capabilities  
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for new local and national policies on PEDs and sustainable neighborhoods  
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Digital platforms for resident feedback and real-time monitoring[^4][^2][^9][^6]

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Demonstrated reductions in energy use, emissions, and car dependency  
  - High resident satisfaction and engagement (ongoing, with external evaluation planned)  
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Early phases show positive social impacts; long-term monitoring needed for gentrification risks  
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Business models and governance structures designed for ongoing adaptation and resilience[^4][^2][^9][^6][^8]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Holistic integration** of social, technological, and environmental innovation  
- **Deep co-creation** fosters lasting resident buy-in and community resilience  
- **Advanced PED technologies** offer a model for climate-neutral urbanization  
- **Strong demonstration value** for EU cities seeking to scale sustainable neighborhoods[^4][^2][^9][^6][^8]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **High complexity and resource requirements** for co-design and technical integration  
- **Uncertainties in long-term affordability** and equity for all social groups  
- **Regulatory and market barriers** to scaling PEDs in less supportive contexts

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Explicit focus on social inclusion and affordability, but ongoing vigilance needed to prevent exclusion as property values rise  
- Strong engagement with diverse residents, but sustained outreach required for underrepresented groups[^2][^9][^11]

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory:**  
  - La Pinada aims to become a living laboratory for climate-neutral living, informing EU and global urban policy  
- **Ongoing challenges:**  
  - Scaling up successful approaches, adapting to evolving technologies, ensuring affordability  
- **Next phases:**  
  - Completion of construction, full demonstration of PED systems, robust monitoring and knowledge transfer across the MAKING-CITY network[^4][^2][^5][^6][^8]
