# Executive summary## Waterschei Positive Energy Neighbourhood (oPEN Lab)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Waterschei Positive Energy Neighbourhood (PEN)
- **Alternative Names:** oPEN Lab Genk; oPEN Living Lab Genk; Positive Energy Neighbourhood Genk
- **Location:** Waterschei district, Genk, Limburg, Belgium
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 51.0033° N, 5.5147° E (Waterschei, Genk)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project inception: 2021  
  - Key milestone: Demo house opening (October 2025)[^1]  
  - Ongoing: Multi-year rollout through at least 2026
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Suburban residential neighbourhood (Garden City Waterschei and adjacent social housing)  
  - Population: Directly impacts several hundred residents (initial phase: ~30+ family homes)[^2][^3]  
  - Investment: EU Horizon 2020 funding (Grant agreement ID: 101037080)[^4]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - VITO (Flemish Institute for Technological Research)  
  - EnergyVille  
  - City of Genk  
  - Thor Park NV  
  - STEBO (social housing facilitator)  
  - Consortium: 32 partners from 7 countries[^4]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme[^5][^4]  
  - Regional/local government support  
  - Private sector (industrial partners, e.g., SOLTECH)[^2]

#### **Executive Summary**

The Waterschei Positive Energy Neighbourhood (PEN) is a pioneering urban regeneration initiative under the oPEN Lab umbrella, located in Genk, Belgium. It seeks to transform the historic Waterschei district—once a coal mining hub—into a model of climate-neutral living by integrating advanced energy systems, collective renewable generation, and participatory renovation processes. The project aims to demonstrate that net positive energy performance is achievable at the neighbourhood scale, moving beyond single-building interventions to coordinated district-level solutions.

Central to the initiative is the deployment of smart grids, district heating, collective solar PV arrays, neighbourhood-scale energy storage, and real-time digital energy management. These technologies are complemented by a participatory approach, engaging residents, industry, and government in co-creating solutions tailored to local needs. The project leverages economies of scale and aggregated renovation strategies to reduce costs, accelerate upgrades, and maximize renewable self-consumption.

By fostering energy independence, reducing operational costs, and lowering carbon emissions, the Waterschei PEN sets out to address pressing urban challenges—aging infrastructure, economic decline, and climate vulnerability—while supporting social inclusion and economic renewal. Its outcomes serve as a blueprint for replicable, scalable, and sustainable urban transformation across Europe, with lessons in technical integration, governance, and community empowerment that inform broader positive energy neighbourhood policy and practice[^5][^2][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - Genk: Population ~66,000[^6], industrial legacy (notably coal mining and heavy industry), currently transitioning toward a knowledge-driven, sustainable economy.  
  - Economic base: Mix of manufacturing, logistics, research, and emerging clean tech sectors[^6].  
  - Geographically well-connected, part of the Limburg province, with strategic regional significance.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - Waterschei: Former mining district, now a suburban residential area known for its garden city layout and social housing estates[^7][^8].  
  - Historical patterns: Dense worker housing, post-mining economic decline, underutilized land, and aging building stock.  
  - Land use: Predominantly residential (low-rise homes, apartments), with proximity to Thor Park (science and business park).  
  - Demographics: Diverse, mix of long-term residents and newer populations; moderate-to-low income levels, with pockets of youth and elderly[^7].  
  - Infrastructure: Aging energy systems, limited previous renovation, variable accessibility.  
  - Environmental conditions: Legacy contamination from mining, moderate green space, air quality issues, and need for improved resilience.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Poor energy performance of housing stock  
  - High carbon emissions, fossil fuel dependency  
  - Economic stagnation and social exclusion in post-industrial neighbourhoods  
  - Suboptimal infrastructure for heating, electricity, and mobility  
  - Fragmented stakeholder landscape, low resident engagement

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU climate neutrality targets (2050) and Renovation Wave strategy[^5]  
  - Horizon 2020 funding opportunity for demonstration projects  
  - Local government prioritization of sustainable urban transition[^4]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (policy and planning)  
  - Social housing authorities (STEBO)  
  - Residents (beneficiaries and co-creators)  
  - Research and technology institutes (VITO, EnergyVille)  
  - Industrial partners (e.g., SOLTECH for PV integration)[^2]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Multi-level: EU (Horizon 2020), federal/regional (Flanders), city of Genk, local neighbourhood committees[^4]
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - EU Renovation Wave, EPBD recast, REPowerEU (solar rooftop provisions)[^5]
  - Local urban planning and sustainability strategies
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive for innovation and demonstration  
  - Gaps: No binding EU/national targets for positive energy neighbourhoods; regulatory adaptation needed for integrated local energy systems[^5]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Achieve net positive energy performance at the neighbourhood scale  
  - Maximize renewable energy self-consumption  
  - Empower residents through participatory renovation and innovation  
  - Foster economic, environmental, and social regeneration[^5][^4]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  - "Redesign and retrofit existing buildings and neighbourhoods to deliver climate-neutral, resilient, and socially inclusive urban spaces"[^4]

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Supports EU climate neutrality 2050  
  - Advances local sustainable development and energy transition strategies  
  - Contributes to the European Recovery Plan and regional innovation agendas[^5][^4]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Aggregated neighbourhood approaches enable economies of scale, easier technical integration, and greater community buy-in, leading to deeper decarbonisation, lower costs, and enhanced quality of life[^5].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Residents (improved living conditions, reduced energy bills)  
  - Local economy (job creation, market development)  
  - Grid operators (energy flexibility, balancing)[^5][^4]
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - First demonstration of integrated district-level positive energy systems in a post-industrial setting  
  - Digital twin-style monitoring and automated system optimization[^5]  
  - Real-time anomaly detection, EV charging, cross-building energy sharing[^9]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - Support for EU decarbonisation and local development priorities[^5]
- **Economic:**  
  - Lower renovation costs, new market opportunities, investment attraction[^5]
- **Social:**  
  - Health, comfort, equity, and social inclusion[^5][^4]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Climate adaptation, resource efficiency, emissions reduction[^5]
- **Technological:**  
  - Availability of smart grids, advanced PV, digital control, and storage[^9][^2]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Integrated, participatory, neighbourhood-based approach  
  - Focus on industrialized renovation, circularity, and co-creation[^4][^5]
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Collective renovation of ~30+ homes in Waterschei and 'Nieuw Texas' social housing estate[^2]  
  - Installation of district heating, neighbourhood batteries, collective PV arrays, EV charging stations, and smart metering[^9][^7]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial demonstration (2021–2025): demo houses, resident engagement, technology rollout  
  - Subsequent scaling: extension to additional buildings, integration with Thor Park, and broader community facilities[^2][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Smart home energy management systems  
  - Collective solar PV (including building-integrated photovoltaics)[^2][^8]  
  - Neighbourhood-scale battery storage  
  - Smart metering, digital twins for energy flows[^9]  
  - District heating (low-temperature CollecThor network)[^2]  
  - Real-time data visualization and automated anomaly detection[^5][^9]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: advanced energy systems, digital twins  
  - Social: participatory co-design, community engagement  
  - Governance: multi-stakeholder coordination  
  - Financial: pooling of investments, new business models[^5][^4]
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Seamless connection between buildings, district infrastructure, and urban mobility (EV charging)[^5][^9]  
  - Embedded within existing community fabric, leveraging Thor Park's research and business ecosystem[^2][^6]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Consortium-based selection (VITO, EnergyVille, local partners)[^4]  
  - Industrial partners supply tech (e.g., SOLTECH for PV panels)[^2]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Resident guidance (STEBO), co-creation workshops, feedback loops[^7][^3]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Residents involved from design to implementation; tailored solutions for local needs; demo house as educational tool[^1]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by VITO/EnergyVille; structured through EU Horizon 2020 project governance[^4]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Major funding via EU Horizon 2020; cost savings through aggregation and industrialized renovation[^5][^4]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EU grants, regional/local government, private sector co-investment[^4][^2]
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Not detailed; likely includes energy savings, possible service fees[^5]
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Focus on long-term viability through reduced operational costs, increased energy autonomy, and scalable business models[^5][^4]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Synchronizing diverse technologies (heating, PV, storage, mobility)[^5]  
  - Ensuring interoperability, reliability, and real-time optimization[^5][^9]
- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront investment needs, aligning funding streams[^5]
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Resident participation, overcoming scepticism, ensuring equity[^5][^7]
- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordinating multi-actor consortium, navigating regulatory requirements[^4]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Legacy contamination and site constraints from mining era[^8]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Early and ongoing resident engagement (STEBO as facilitator)[^7]  
  - Phased technology rollout, demonstration projects (e.g., demo house)[^1]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Customization to local housing types, incremental integration of new tech[^2]
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of neighbourhood-level co-design  
  - Need for regulatory adaptation to allow innovative energy sharing models[^5][^4]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Holistic, integrated approach** to energy, social, and economic challenges[^5]
- **Participatory co-design** with residents and stakeholders[^7][^3]
- **Demonstrated technical and financial feasibility** of neighbourhood-scale positive energy systems[^5][^4]
- **Clear social and environmental benefits**, including equity focus and emissions reduction[^5]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Regulatory barriers** to full energy sharing and integration[^5]
- **Challenges in synchronizing diverse technologies** and systems[^5][^9]
- **Potential financial constraints** for scaling up beyond pilot phase[^5][^4]

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Explicit attention to **social inclusion and housing equity**, targeting historically excluded populations[^2]
- **No major displacement or exclusion** reported, but ongoing vigilance required[^5]

#### **Future Outlook**

- Continued scaling and integration with broader city and regional initiatives[^4]
- Ongoing learning around regulatory adaptation, technology upgrades, and financial models[^5]
- Potential for replication in other European cities and districts facing similar challenges[^5][^4]
