# Executive summary## Sharing Cities – Smart Building Neighborhood Monitoring (Milan, Italy)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Sharing Cities – Smart Building Neighborhood Monitoring  
- **Alternative Names:** Sharing Cities Milan Demonstration Site, Smart District Porta Romana/Chiaravalle  
- **Location:** Porta Romana and Chiaravalle Districts, Milan, Lombardy, Italy  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.4481° N, 9.2097° E (Porta Romana)  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Start: January 2016  
  - Key Milestones:  
    - 2016: Project launch  
    - 2017-2018: District retrofits and mobility deployments  
    - 2019-2020: Full smart platform operations, citizen engagement scaling  
    - December 2020: Official completion, with post-project monitoring  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Over 216,000 m² (Porta Romana brownfield redevelopment area)  
  - Direct population affected: ~3,000–5,000 residents (estimate for pilot area)  
  - Investment Value: €25 million total project funding, €8.6 million specifically for Milan  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Comune di Milano (Municipality of Milan)  
  - Fondazione Politecnico di Milano  
  - Politecnico di Milano (DASTU, DENG, Poliedra)  
  - Siemens, NHP S.r.l., AMAT, ATM, A2A, Kiunsys  
  - European partners: Greater London Authority, Lisboa e Nova, Eurocities, others  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Horizon 2020 EU grant (Smart Cities and Communities, SCC1)  
  - Local public and private co-financing  

#### **Executive Summary**

Sharing Cities is a major European Union Horizon 2020 “lighthouse” project designed to pilot and demonstrate integrated smart city solutions across key urban challenges—energy use, mobility, and environmental quality—in three leading cities: Milan, London, and Lisbon. Milan’s demonstration site centers on the Porta Romana and Chiaravalle districts, areas historically marked by industrial decline and physical separation from the city’s core. The initiative’s core innovation is the deployment of an integrated network of IoT sensors and digital platforms that enable real-time monitoring and control of building energy systems, street lighting, mobility infrastructure, and environmental quality. Facility managers, residents, and city authorities are empowered to optimize energy usage, enhance comfort, and proactively manage the urban environment. The project also includes public engagement mechanisms—such as participatory co-design, public dashboards, and a digital social market app—that foster citizen involvement and behavior change. Over its five-year implementation, Sharing Cities in Milan achieved deep energy retrofits in both public and private buildings, installed smart street lighting and e-mobility infrastructure, and built a robust “urban sharing platform” for data integration and service management. Early results demonstrated substantial energy savings (up to 70% in retrofitted buildings), improved air quality, and strengthened community trust in sustainability efforts. The project’s design, multi-sectoral partnerships, and replicability focus have established a scalable model for smart, climate-resilient neighborhoods in Europe and beyond[^1][^2][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - Milan is Italy’s second-largest city (population ~1.4 million, metro area ~3.2 million), the country’s financial and industrial hub, and a leader in design, fashion, and innovation.  
  - The city is relatively flat, with a dense urban core surrounded by post-war districts and significant brownfield sites, especially near former railway yards.  
  - Milan has been a national leader in sustainable urban policy, with advanced plans for mobility, green space, and the sharing economy[^2].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Porta Romana/Vettabbia:**  
    - Former railway brownfield (~216,000 m²), physically and socio-economically separated from both the historic center and adjacent agricultural belt[^2].  
    - Undergoing major redevelopment to include mixed private/social housing, a new multimodal transport hub, and a large park (~187,000 m²).  
    - Population: Diverse, with a mix of lower-income residents and new arrivals; significant social housing stock.  
    - Existing infrastructure was outdated, with energy-inefficient buildings and limited green mobility options[^2][^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High energy consumption and carbon emissions in buildings.  
  - Poor air quality and environmental monitoring.  
  - Mobility challenges: congestion, limited e-mobility infrastructure.  
  - Social and economic fragmentation between neighborhoods[^1][^2][^3].
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU’s Horizon 2020 call for Smart City “lighthouse” projects.  
  - Milan’s strategic push for climate action and digital innovation[^1].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Municipality seeking urban renewal and sustainability.  
  - Local residents, including vulnerable populations in social housing.  
  - Private sector partners (technology, energy, mobility firms).  
  - Academic and research institutions (Politecnico di Milano, others)[^1].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Milan operates under a directly elected municipal government with delegated authority for energy, transport, and urban planning.  
  - Sharing Cities coordinated through public-private-academic consortia, with strong city leadership[^1][^2].
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Milan’s Smart City Plan (since 2012), Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan, and city-level climate/energy strategies[^2].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - EU-level support for collaborative innovation projects.  
  - Challenge of cross-sector data sharing and differing technical standards[^2][^3].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Reduce energy consumption and emissions in urban districts.  
  - Demonstrate replicable, citizen-centered smart city solutions.  
  - Foster social inclusion and economic vitality via digital innovation[^3][^4].
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - To create a “near-zero energy” smart district that is livable, resilient, and inclusive through integrated technology and citizen engagement[^1][^2].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Embedded in Milan’s citywide smart and sustainable development strategies.  
  - Contributes to EU climate and innovation goals[^1][^2].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Deploying IoT-enabled monitoring, digital integration, and citizen participation will drive measurable reductions in energy use, emissions, and resource consumption, while improving urban quality of life and trust in public action[^1][^3].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents, especially in social housing.  
  - City authorities (improved management).  
  - Broader Milanese population (environmental and economic gains)[^1].
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Digital twin and cloud analytics for real-time building and neighborhood optimization.  
  - Citizen-facing dashboards and gamified engagement apps.  
  - Use of open standards for interoperability and replicability[^2][^3].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - Mayoral commitment to sustainability and digital leadership[^1].
- **Economic:**  
  - Attract investment, stimulate green jobs, improve property values[^3][^5].
- **Social:**  
  - Reduce inequalities, empower residents, improve quality of life.
- **Environmental:**  
  - Address climate change, reduce pollution, enhance resilience[^1][^2].
- **Technological:**  
  - Leverage advances in IoT, cloud computing, smart mobility[^2].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Principles: Citizen-centered smart city, cross-sector integration, open innovation.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Deep retrofit of 20+ buildings (insulation, HVAC, digital controls)[^1][^2].  
  - Smart street lighting, environmental sensors, and e-mobility hubs.  
  - Redevelopment of brownfield into mixed-use, green, and mobility-rich district[^2][^3].
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Sequential: Planning and engagement → infrastructure upgrades → digital systems rollout → monitoring and engagement scaling (2016-2020).

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - IoT sensors (temperature, humidity, air quality, occupancy)[^2].  
  - Digital twin integration and cloud-based analytics.  
  - Urban Sharing Platform (USP) for data aggregation and service management.  
  - Smart lamp posts (LoRaWAN network, environmental and traffic sensors)[^2].  
  - E-mobility infrastructure (charging stations, e-bikes, car-sharing)[^1][^3].
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (IoT, digital twin, cloud analytics).  
  - Social (participatory co-design, gamification).  
  - Governance (cross-sectoral data sharing, public-private partnerships)[^2][^5].
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - All systems connected via the USP, supporting both technical and business interoperability and enabling “plug-and-play” for new services[^2].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Consortium model with roles for city, academia, and private sector.  
  - EU-compliant public procurement; some competitive tenders for tech supply.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Extensive participatory co-design workshops with residents.  
  - Digital Social Market (SharingMi app) for ongoing engagement[^1][^2].
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Citizen voting/input on retrofit priorities and service design.  
  - Incentive program for sustainable behaviors[^2].
- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by the Municipality of Milan, with dedicated project office and regular consortium meetings[^1][^2].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total: €25 million (project-wide); €8.6 million for Milan.  
  - Major costs: building retrofits, digital infrastructure, mobility systems[^1][^4].
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Horizon 2020 grant (majority), local public and private contributions[^1][^5].
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Not-for-profit pilot; potential for future service fees (e.g., e-mobility, data services).
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Focus on demonstrating savings (energy, O&M) to justify long-term reinvestment and scalability[^1][^2].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integrating diverse data streams and legacy systems[^2].  
  - Ensuring interoperability across vendors and platforms.  
  - Retrofitting old buildings without major disruption[^1][^3].
- **Financial:**  
  - Managing upfront costs and aligning funding timelines.
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Building trust with residents wary of surveillance and disruption.  
  - Ensuring equitable access and benefit[^2].
- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination across multiple agencies and consortia partners.  
  - Navigating EU project reporting and compliance[^1].
- **Environmental:**  
  - Limited site-specific environmental risks, but need to manage material waste from retrofits.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Use of open standards and modular systems to address technical integration.  
  - Phased engagement and transparent communications for resident buy-in[^2].
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusting retrofit packages based on building/user feedback.  
  - Expanding the range of mobility options based on user demand.
- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Early and ongoing engagement is crucial.  
  - Interoperability and data governance must be addressed from the start[^2][^3].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Retrofit of 20+ buildings (21,000 m² private, 4,000 m² public)[^3].  
  - Installation of 300 smart lamp posts, 60 EV charging points (20 rapid), 14 e-bike stations[^3].
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Smart energy management for buildings.  
  - Real-time environmental monitoring and reporting.  
  - E-mobility and car/bike sharing services.
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Up to 70% reduction in building energy needs after retrofit[^1][^2].  
  - >1,100 users on SharingMi platform in first 3 months[^2].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved indoor comfort and air quality.  
  - Enhanced neighborhood safety (smart lighting, reduced traffic)[^1][^2].
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Social housing prioritized for retrofits.  
  - Digital inclusion through citizen-facing tools[^2].
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No significant displacement reported; monitoring ongoing.
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Increased civic engagement through co-design and digital participation[^2].
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Preservation of community identity through participatory redevelopment.

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Green jobs in retrofit and technology sectors.
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Increased business for local suppliers and service providers.
- **Property Values:**  
  - Anticipated uplift in redeveloped areas.
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Substantial energy savings for residents and city[^1][^2].
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Social housing upgrades improve living conditions and affordability.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Significant reduction in GHG emissions via energy retrofits and e-mobility[^1][^2].
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Improved energy and water efficiency.
- **Environmental Quality:**

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**

- **Policy Influence:**

  - Public dashboards and open data tools improve trust and oversight[^2].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Demonstrated deep energy savings and improved comfort in a challenging urban context[^1][^2].
- Robust integration of data and digital tools, with open standards supporting future adaptability.
- High degree of citizen engagement, both in planning and ongoing operations.
- Strong replicability focus, with lessons shared across Europe[^4].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Complexity of multi-partner governance slowed decision-making.
- Upfront costs and financing model may be harder to replicate without EU-scale funding.
- Some challenges maintaining engagement and platform use post-pilot.
- Integration with legacy systems posed technical hurdles[^2][^3].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Notable focus on social housing and vulnerable populations in retrofits.
- Efforts to ensure digital inclusion and equitable access to new services.
- Ongoing need to monitor for unintended gentrification or displacement as district improves.

#### **Future Outlook**

- The digital and physical infrastructure is intended to serve as a foundation for Milan’s ongoing smart city transformation.
- Further scale-up across Milan and adaptation in other EU cities are underway.
- Continued challenges will include ensuring financial sustainability, updating technologies, and maintaining high levels of citizen participation as the project transitions beyond its initial EU funding envelope[^2][^4].
