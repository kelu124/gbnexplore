# Executive summary## Cleanwatts Living Lab: AI-Powered Renewable Energy Communities

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Cleanwatts Living Lab: AI-Powered Renewable Energy Communities
- **Alternative Names:** CER Cleanwatts Living Lab, Cleanwatts Energy Community Pilot Project
- **Location:** Coimbra, Portugal (with dynamic energy sharing also in Condeixa-a-Nova and Anadia)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approximate center: 40.2056° N, 8.4108° W (Coimbra)
- **Timeline:** Launched as a pilot project in 2023; ongoing status[^1][^2][^3]
- **Project Scale:** 40 residential homes (apartments and single-family), 6,000 m²; part of five areas for dynamic energy sharing[^1][^2]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Cleanwatts (private sector lead); in partnership with Galp and E-REDES (grid operator); regulatory oversight by ERSE (Portugal’s Energy Services Regulatory Authority)[^1][^2]
- **Funding Sources:** Private investment (Cleanwatts, Galp), EU research funds (Horizon 2020 grants), regulatory pilot support[^2][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**
The Cleanwatts Living Lab in Coimbra, Portugal, exemplifies a pioneering approach to advancing renewable energy communities in urban environments. This initiative integrates 40 residential units with solar photovoltaic (PV) generation, batteries, electric vehicles, and smart appliances, all orchestrated by a robust digital platform utilizing AI and digital twin technologies[^2][^3]. The Living Lab’s core objective is to optimize energy consumption, storage, and sharing across participants, thereby reducing energy costs (targeted at 40%), increasing local self-consumption (by 35%), and cutting CO₂ emissions (by 40%)[^2].

Functioning both as a demonstration site and a “regulatory sandbox,” the project tests disruptive business models and technical solutions in real time, enabling participants to become proactive agents in local energy markets. Cleanwatts OS, the proprietary management system, ensures seamless integration of distributed energy resources and real-time interaction with the grid. The Living Lab emphasizes user engagement, energy literacy, and social innovation, delivering comfort improvements and substantial economic and environmental benefits[^3].

By sharing implementation results and engaging with regulatory frameworks, the Cleanwatts Living Lab is not only accelerating the decarbonization of urban energy systems but also informing broader policy and market transformation across Portugal and Europe[^1][^2][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Coimbra is a historic university city in central Portugal, with a population of approximately 140,000. It serves as a regional hub for education, healthcare, and services, and is characterized by a mix of urban and suburban development. The city faces challenges typical of mid-sized European cities: aging infrastructure, demographic shifts, and the need for sustainable economic growth[^2].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The Living Lab is situated in residential zones comprising both apartments and single-family homes, spanning 6,000 m². These areas are marked by diverse housing types, moderate population density, and a demographic mix of families, working professionals, and elderly residents. Local land use is primarily residential with some mixed-use elements[^2][^3].
  - Historical development: Transition from traditional housing to modern, energy-efficient dwellings.
  - Current infrastructure: Adequate but with potential for upgrades, especially in energy systems and digital connectivity.
  - Environmental conditions: Favorable solar irradiation, moderate climate; constraints include legacy grid infrastructure and energy poverty among segments of the population[^4].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - High energy costs and dependence on centralized fossil-fuel electricity.
  - Limited local production and self-consumption of renewable energy.
  - Low energy literacy and engagement among residents.
  - Environmental concerns: urban carbon footprint, air quality, and energy waste[^2][^3][^4].
- **Triggering Events:** EU directives promoting energy communities; amendments to Portugal’s Self-Consumption Regulation; Cleanwatts’ strategic drive to test innovative models under “regulatory sandbox” conditions[^1][^2][^3].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Residents seeking cost savings and sustainability; Cleanwatts as technology provider; Galp as energy partner; ERSE and E-REDES as regulatory and grid authorities[^1][^2].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Oversight by ERSE under Portugal’s Self-Consumption Regulation; pilot status allows for regulatory flexibility and dynamic energy sharing[^1].
- **Relevant Policies:** EU Renewable Energy Directive; Portuguese regulations on energy communities and collective self-consumption; local climate action plans[^1][^2].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** Recent regulatory changes enabling energy sharing and community models; ongoing requirement for licensing and compliance with grid codes[^1][^2].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:** To demonstrate and validate the technical, economic, and social viability of AI-powered renewable energy communities; maximize local clean energy production and consumption; empower residents[^2][^3].
- **Vision Statement:** Building a cleaner, fairer, and more sustainable future through community-driven energy innovation[^2].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Supports Portugal’s national climate targets, EU decarbonization strategies, and local sustainability agendas; aligns with Horizon 2020 research objectives[^2][^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** By integrating distributed renewable energy, smart technologies, and user engagement, the Living Lab reduces energy costs, emissions, and energy poverty, while improving comfort and grid stability[^2][^3].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residential users within the community, including vulnerable groups impacted by energy poverty; broader urban population through policy and market transformation[^2][^4].
- **Innovation Elements:** AI-based digital twins for energy optimization; real-time dashboards; regulatory sandbox for testing new business models; peer-to-peer energy sharing[^2][^3].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:** Alignment with EU Green Deal and national climate action plans.
- **Economic:** Need for energy cost reduction and local value creation; mitigation of energy poverty[^2][^4].
- **Social:** Empowerment and engagement of residents; community resilience[^2][^3].
- **Environmental:** Climate adaptation; CO₂ mitigation; reduction of energy waste[^2][^3].
- **Technological:** Advances in AI, IoT, and digital platforms; interoperability of smart devices[^2][^3].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Based on principles of community energy, participatory innovation, and urban sustainability; draws from smart city and Living Lab models[^2][^3].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Integration of solar PV arrays, home batteries, EV chargers, and smart appliances across 40 homes; digital backbone via Cleanwatts OS[^2][^3].
- **Scope and Phasing:** Initial pilot covering five areas; iterative expansion based on pilot results and regulatory feedback[^1][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Solar PV systems for local generation.
  - Home batteries for storage.
  - IoT sensors and smart meters for data collection.
  - Electric vehicle (EV) chargers.
  - AI-based digital twin for optimization.
  - Cloud-based management platform (Cleanwatts OS)[^2][^3].
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (AI, IoT, digital twins); social (community engagement, energy literacy); regulatory (sandbox, dynamic sharing); process (new business models)[^3].
- **Integration Approach:** Seamless interoperability of devices using Cleanwatts OS; real-time energy management at unit, building, and community levels; interaction with grid via E-REDES[^2][^3].

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Led by Cleanwatts with technology partners; supported by EU Horizon 2020 funding[^2][^3].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Residents involved in planning, operation, and feedback; webinars and dashboards for transparency and education[^3].
- **Participatory Elements:** Co-creation of energy sharing rules; user-facing dashboards and control panels; ongoing community feedback loops[^2][^3].
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by Cleanwatts; regulatory supervision by ERSE; periodic reporting and public dissemination[^1][^2][^3].

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Not publicly disclosed for this specific pilot; similar Cleanwatts projects range from €100,000–€500,000 for comparable scales[^5][^6].
- **Funding Mix:** Private investment (Cleanwatts, Galp), EU Horizon 2020 research grants; potential for community contributions[^2][^3].
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Local energy tariffs, savings from self-consumption, flexibility services to grid, possible carbon credit sales[^2][^3].
- **Financial Sustainability:** Focus on maximizing community savings and market competitiveness; pilot status allows for testing new revenue streams[^2][^3].

- **Social and Political:** Engaging residents in new energy practices; overcoming skepticism and ensuring equitable participation[^2][^4].
- **Administrative:** Navigating regulatory approvals; coordinating multiple partners and pilot areas[^1].

#### **Environmental Impacts**

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- **High integration of advanced technologies** (AI, digital twins, IoT) for real-time optimization.
- **Strong user engagement** and energy literacy initiatives.
- **Effective regulatory collaboration**, enabling testing of new models.
- **Robust environmental and economic impacts**, including substantial cost and emissions reductions[^2][^3].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- **Technical complexity** may pose barriers for less tech-savvy users.
- **Financial sustainability** dependent on scaling and ongoing regulatory support.
- **Limited scale** (40 homes) restricts immediate broader impact; expansion needed for systemic change[^2][^3].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- **Deliberate focus on energy poverty** and inclusion of vulnerable groups.
- **Transparent, participatory processes** promote equity and community empowerment.
- **No evidence of displacement or gentrification**, though long-term vigilance is warranted[^2][^4].

#### **Future Outlook**
- Planned expansion to more neighborhoods and regions.
- Ongoing evaluation to refine business models and technology integration.
- Potential for influencing national policy and scaling throughout Europe.
- Continued challenges: maintaining user engagement, adapting to evolving regulatory frameworks, ensuring long-term financial viability and technical robustness[^1][^2][^3].
