# Executive summary## Lisbjerg Sustainable Neighborhood

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Lisbjerg Sustainable Neighborhood
- **Alternative Names:** Lisbjerg Bakke; Circle House Lisbjerg (specific pilot project within the neighborhood)
- **Location:** Lisbjerg, Aarhus, Central Denmark Region, Denmark
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 56.215° N, 10.184° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - Planning initiated: ca. 2015  
  - Construction: 2017 – 2020 (Circle House demonstrator); major phases ongoing  
  - Circle House pilot completed: 2020  
  - Full build-out: Phased, ongoing, projected through late 2020s
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Several hectares on the northern edge of Aarhus  
  - Residential units: 260 homes (main Lisbjerg development)[^1], plus 60 units (Circle House pilot)[^2]  
  - Population affected: Several hundred new residents, with planned expansion
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Aarhus Municipality (planning/regulation)  
  - Lendager Architects (master planning/design)[^1]  
  - Lejerbo Housing Association (social housing)[^2]  
  - Multiple Danish architectural firms (e.g., Vandkunsten, 3XN, Lendager Group)[^2]  
  - GXN Innovation (circularity coordination)[^3]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Danish Environmental Protection Agency (public grant)[^3][^2]  
  - Realdania Philanthropic Association (private grant)[^2]  
  - Municipal investment (infrastructure, support)  
  - Lejerbo (public/social housing funds)

#### **Executive Summary**
Lisbjerg Sustainable Neighborhood is a pioneering urban development project on the outskirts of Aarhus, Denmark, designed to showcase scalable solutions for circular economy-based construction, sustainable urban living, and inclusive community design. The initiative encompasses over 260 residential dwellings and significant green infrastructure, integrating modular and hybrid timber construction, recycled building materials, and district heating powered by renewable sources. The neighborhood prioritizes active transport—public transit, cycling, and walking—while minimizing car dependence, and features extensive parks, bioswales, and ecological corridors to foster biodiversity.

A key innovation is the Circle House pilot, a 60-unit demonstration project built to ensure 90% reusability of construction materials, employing design-for-disassembly principles and digital material passports for traceability. The broader Lisbjerg development builds on these principles, embedding real-time feedback mechanisms for design and sustainability monitoring, and emphasizing accessibility for diverse user groups, including elderly and disabled residents. Implementation required complex cross-sector collaboration and addressed significant challenges in affordability, circular supply chain scaling, and long-term community engagement.

Outcomes include substantial reductions in construction waste and carbon emissions, improved air quality, and a vibrant, inclusive urban environment. The project serves as a lighthouse for sustainable suburban expansion, informing policy and practice both locally and internationally. Its adaptability, innovative procurement, and participatory design processes mark a new paradigm in European green neighborhood development[^4][^1][^5][^6].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Aarhus, Denmark’s second-largest city, has a population exceeding 350,000. It is a major regional hub for education, culture, and innovation, with a strong economy based on services, knowledge industries, and green technology[^4]. Aarhus is known for ambitious sustainability targets, including aims for carbon neutrality and advanced urban mobility.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  Lisbjerg is located north of the city center on previously agricultural land. Historically, it was a rural village, now designated for substantial urban expansion.  
  - Land use: Transitioning from fields to mixed-use urban, with a focus on medium-density housing, green corridors, and community facilities[^7].  
  - Demographics: Intended for a diverse mix—families, singles, elderly, and disabled residents, with both market-rate and social housing components[^1].  
  - Infrastructure: New roads, district heating, public transport links, and cycling networks; high accessibility standards.  
  - Environmental conditions: Hilly terrain with rainwater management challenges; proximity to natural habitats[^7].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Need for sustainable suburban growth to accommodate Aarhus’s expanding population  
  - Environmental concerns: carbon emissions, construction waste, loss of biodiversity  
  - Social: Ensuring affordability, accessibility, and community cohesion  
  - Infrastructure gaps: Upgrading transport, utilities, and public spaces

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - National and municipal sustainability policy shifts  
  - Increased demand for housing, especially inclusive and affordable options  
  - Advocacy for circular economy principles in Denmark’s construction sector

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Aarhus Municipality: Urban planning authority, infrastructure provision  
  - Housing non-profits (Lejerbo): Social housing delivery  
  - Architects, engineers, material suppliers: Design and execution  
  - Community groups: Input on accessibility, inclusivity

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Aarhus Municipality leads planning under Danish urban development laws  
  - National policies supporting circular economy and climate adaptation

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Citywide densification strategies; sustainable building codes  
  - Circular construction guidelines; subsidies for innovative housing

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive regulatory context for pilot projects  
  - Complexity in scaling circular supply chains and developing standards for material passports[^3]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Circular economy principles (reuse, design-for-disassembly)[^3][^2]  
  - Landscape urbanism: integrating green infrastructure and water management[^7]  
  - Universal accessibility and user experience

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Medium-density housing (terraced houses, tower blocks)[^2]  
  - Green corridors, parks, bioswales for stormwater management  
  - District heating infrastructure

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Demonstrator phase (Circle House, 60 units)[^3][^2]  
  - Main development (260 homes, multi-phase rollout)[^1]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Modular timber and hybrid construction[^5][^6]  
  - Recycled materials (tiles, wood, acoustic panels)[^3]  
  - Digital material passports for traceability[^3]  
  - District heating and smart mobility integration

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (construction, mobility, digital systems)  
  - Social (participatory design, inclusivity)  
  - Governance (multi-party collaboration)

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Collaboration among over 30 industry partners[^3][^2]  
  - Embedded monitoring and feedback systems

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive selection of designers, contractors  
  - Collaboration agreements for circular supply chains

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Community workshops, participatory planning[^7]  
  - Input from accessibility advocates

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Real-time design feedback  
  - Transparent decision-making (material selection, spatial layout)

- **Project Management:**  
  - Cross-sector steering committees  
  - Phased project delivery

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Circle House pilot: DKK 9.9 million (approx. €1.3 million), with DKK 6.9 million public grant[^3]  
  - Full neighborhood: larger scale, mix of public, private, and philanthropic funds

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Public grants (environmental agency)  
  - Philanthropic support (Realdania)[^2]  
  - Municipal investment

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Social housing rents  
  - Potential land value capture

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Lower life-cycle costs via circular construction and district heating[^1]
