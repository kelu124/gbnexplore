# Executive summary

## Sharing Cities: Smart Parking and e-Mobility Pilot (Horizon 2020 Project)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Sharing Cities: Smart Parking and e-Mobility Pilot
- **Alternative Names:** Sharing Cities, SharingMI, Smart District Milan, Sharing Cities Milan Demonstrator
- **Location:** Porta Romana and Vettabbia districts, Milan, Lombardy, Italy
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.4533° N, 9.2014° E (Porta Romana)
- **Timeline:**
  - **Start Date:** January 2016
  - **Key Milestones:**
    - Initial infrastructure deployment: 2016–2018
    - Launch of SharingMI app: 2018
    - Building retrofits and mobility pilots: 2018–2019
    - Monitoring and evaluation: 2019–2020
  - **Completion/Ongoing Status:** Project funding and formal activity concluded December 2020; ongoing local innovation and scaling
- **Project Scale:**
  - **Area:** Demonstration site covers approx. 216,614 m² (Porta Romana brownfield redevelopment) plus surrounding urban fabric[^1]
  - **Population Affected:** Directly impacts thousands of residents and workers in the district; broader benefits to Milan’s citywide population[^2]
  - **Investment Value:** €25 million EU funding for Sharing Cities consortium, €8.6 million for Milan’s local partnership[^2][^3]
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - City of Milan (Comune di Milano)
  - Fondazione Politecnico di Milano
  - Multiple partners: Politecnico di Milano (DASTU, DENG), Siemens, ATM, Legambiente, RSE SPA, A2A Smart City, NHP S.r.l., Kiunsys, and others[^2][^4][^5]
- **Funding Sources:** Primarily Horizon 2020 (EU grant), with additional support from local government, private partners, and PPP models[^2][^4][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**
Sharing Cities Milan is a flagship European smart city demonstration under the Horizon 2020 programme, designed to accelerate the transition to sustainable, digitally enabled urban environments. Focused on the Porta Romana and Vettabbia districts, the initiative integrates smart parking, dynamic electric vehicle (EV) charging, shared mobility, and digital citizen engagement with advanced energy retrofits and environmental monitoring. Milan’s approach emphasizes not only technological innovation but also citizen co-design, social inclusion, and interoperability between systems.

The project addresses pressing urban challenges—traffic congestion, air pollution, and fragmented mobility—by deploying 75 smart parking sensors, 60 EV charging stations (including 20 rapid chargers), shared e-cars and e-bikes, and a city mobility app. The SharingMI platform incentivizes sustainable behaviour, allowing users to earn rewards for environmentally conscious choices.

Key outcomes include a 15% reduction in time spent searching for parking, increased adoption of EVs, and real-time data integration for city planning. The initiative has improved mobility, reduced emissions, and fostered more equitable access to sustainable transport. Its collaborative governance and open urban platform model have made Milan a European reference point for replicable, citizen-centred smart city solutions[^2][^1][^6][^4][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**
- **Population:** Milan is Italy’s second largest city, with a metropolitan population exceeding 3 million.
- **Economic Base:** Major European financial, commercial, and cultural hub; strong manufacturing, design, and service sectors.
- **Geographic Characteristics:** Located in the Lombardy Region, Milan is a dense, urbanized area surrounded by agricultural land.
- **Urban Hierarchy:** Milan is a leading city nationally and regionally, driving innovation and economic development[^1][^6].

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
- **Historical Development:** Porta Romana is historically industrial, featuring former railway yards and brownfield sites. Vettabbia connects to the city’s agricultural belt[^1].
- **Land Use:** Ongoing transformation from industrial/brownfield to mixed-use (residential, commercial, parkland, mobility hub)[^1].
- **Demographics:** Mixture of long-term residents, social housing tenants, new arrivals; diverse age and income profiles, growing cultural diversity.
- **Infrastructure Quality:** Previously fragmented; post-initiative features upgraded energy systems, mobility infrastructure, and public spaces.
- **Environmental Conditions:** Prior to intervention, faced issues of air pollution, limited green space, and traffic congestion.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Chronic traffic congestion and inefficient parking
  - High emissions and poor air quality
  - Limited adoption of EVs and shared mobility
  - Energy-inefficient buildings and infrastructure
  - Social fragmentation between central and peripheral areas[^2][^1][^6]

- **Triggering Events:**
  - EU’s Horizon 2020 call for Smart Cities Lighthouse projects
  - Local policy drive for sustainability and innovation
  - Redevelopment of Porta Romana brownfield area[^1][^6]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - City government, local residents, business owners, technology providers, NGOs, academic partners, and EU agencies[^2][^4]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** Municipality of Milan led implementation, supported by regional and EU-level structures; collaborative public-private partnership model[^2][^3].
- **Relevant Policies:** Milan’s smart city plan, sustainable mobility strategies, climate action frameworks, and national energy efficiency regulations[^1][^6].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** EU procurement rules, interoperability standards, data privacy laws, and local zoning regulations facilitated and occasionally constrained innovation[^1][^6][^3].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Reduce carbon emissions and improve air quality
  - Promote sustainable, integrated urban mobility
  - Enhance energy efficiency in buildings
  - Foster citizen engagement and behaviour change[^2][^1][^6]

- **Vision Statement:** Transform Milan into a near-zero-emission smart district, where technology and citizen participation combine to create sustainable, inclusive urban living[^2][^1].

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Directly supports Milan’s climate action, mobility, and urban redevelopment strategies; aligns with EU smart city and sustainability directives[^2][^1][^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** By integrating smart mobility, energy retrofits, and digital participation, the initiative aims to reduce emissions, improve social equity, and boost urban resilience. Technology deployment is paired with behavioural incentives and co-design to maximize impact[^2][^1].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents, commuters, businesses, city managers, and the wider community benefit through improved mobility, air quality, and urban services[^2][^1][^6].
- **Innovation Elements:** First large-scale integration of smart parking sensors, dynamic EV charging, interoperable data platforms, and citizen reward systems in Milan[^2][^1][^6][^4].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** Local election commitment to sustainability; international reputation; EU policy alignment[^2][^1].
- **Economic Drivers:** Attracting investment, supporting new business models, boosting competitiveness[^6][^4].
- **Social Drivers:** Addressing equity and accessibility, fostering inclusion and participation[^1].
- **Environmental Drivers:** Urgency of climate adaptation and pollution mitigation[^2][^1].
- **Technological Drivers:** Advances in IoT, data analytics, and digital platforms; EU smart city agenda[^1][^6][^4].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Citizen-centred smart city development, integrating mobility, energy, and ICT with participatory co-design[^1][^6].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Retrofit of 20 buildings for energy efficiency, installation of 75 smart parking sensors, 60 EV charging stations, creation of mobility hubs, upgrade of public lighting and environmental monitoring infrastructure[^2][^6].
- **Scope and Phasing:** Initial infrastructure and technology deployment (2016–2018), followed by digital platform launch, citizen engagement, and monitoring (2018–2020)[^2][^1].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Smart parking sensors with real-time data and enforcement capabilities
  - Dynamic EV charging stations (including rapid chargers)
  - E-car and e-bike sharing systems
  - Urban Sharing Platform for data integration (USP)
  - LoraWAN-enabled smart lamp posts with environmental monitoring
  - SharingMI app for citizen engagement and rewards[^2][^1][^6]

- **Innovation Type:** Technological (IoT, EV infrastructure), social (citizen co-design, behavioural incentives), governance (platform interoperability, PPP), process-oriented (integrated planning and engagement)[^1][^6][^4].

- **Integration Approach:** Interoperable digital platform links parking, mobility, and energy data; technologies are integrated with existing urban systems and public transport networks[^1].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** Competitive EU procurement, selection of technology partners, and collaboration with local innovators[^2][^1][^6].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Extensive citizen consultation, participatory workshops, ongoing feedback via SharingMI app[^1].
- **Participatory Elements:** Co-design of building retrofits, mobility solutions, and digital services; reward systems for sustainable behaviour[^1].
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by Municipality of Milan, with regular multi-stakeholder meetings and EU reporting[^2][^1][^6].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** €25 million for consortium, €8.6 million for Milan; allocated across mobility, energy, and engagement components[^2][^3].
- **Funding Mix:** EU grant (Horizon 2020), municipal co-investment, private sector contributions, PPP arrangements[^2][^4].
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** User fees for shared mobility; potential for data-driven services; indirect savings via efficiency gains[^2][^4].
- **Financial Sustainability:** Designed for long-term cost savings and scalability; models tested for replication in other cities[^4][^3].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:** Harmonizing diverse technology providers; integrating new systems with legacy infrastructure; ensuring data interoperability[^2].
- **Financial Challenges:** Managing budget constraints; aligning multi-source funding streams[^2][^4].
- **Social and Political Challenges:** Engaging residents in co-design; overcoming scepticism towards new mobility models; ensuring inclusive access[^1].
- **Administrative Challenges:** Coordinating large consortium; navigating EU reporting requirements; managing cross-sector partnerships[^2][^3].
- **Environmental Challenges:** Initial site contamination; balancing brownfield redevelopment with green space creation[^1][^6].

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Iterative technology pilots; flexible design modifications based on user feedback; continuous stakeholder engagement[^1][^6].
- **Design Modifications:** Adjustments to sensor placement and app functionality based on usage data; expansion of e-bike offerings for families[^1].
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:** Importance of interoperability, iterative user engagement, and visible quick wins to build trust and adoption[^2][^1].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** Retrofit of 20 buildings; installation of 75 smart parking sensors, 60 EV charging stations, 14 e-bike stations; smart lamp posts; mobility hubs[^2][^6].
- **Service Delivery:** Enhanced mobility options (car/bike sharing, dynamic parking), improved energy services, real-time city data for managers and users[^2][^1].
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 15% reduction in parking search time; 70% reduction in energy needs for retrofitted buildings; 1,100+ app users in first three months; over 3,600 sustainable behaviour stories shared[^1][^6].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Improved mobility for residents and workers; increased accessibility; better public spaces; participatory governance[^1][^6].
- **Equity Outcomes:** Focus on social housing retrofits; inclusive mobility solutions; engagement of diverse user groups[^1].
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No major displacement reported; renewal intended to bridge social divides[^1].
- **Social Capital:** Strengthened civic engagement via SharingMI; new networks and collaborations[^1][^6].
- **Cultural Impacts:** Reinforcement of community identity through shared urban challenges and solutions[^1].

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Direct employment in construction, technology, and management; support for local startups and service providers[^6][^4].
- **Economic Activity:** Attraction of investment; boost in local businesses via increased accessibility[^4].
- **Property Values:** Likely uplift in retrofitted and connected areas, though detailed data not public.
- **Cost Savings:** Reduced energy bills; lower mobility costs for users; efficiency gains for city administration[^2][^4].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Improved access to affordable mobility and energy for lower-income residents[^1].

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** Reduced greenhouse gas emissions from mobility and buildings; improved air quality[^2][^1].
- **Resource Management:** Increased energy and water efficiency; enhanced monitoring of usage[^1].
- **Environmental Quality:** Installation of green space, reduction in noise and air pollution, improved public lighting[^1][^6].
- **Waste and Pollution:** Enhanced waste management logistics via electric vehicles; reduced pollution from traffic[^2][^1].
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Performance measured against EU standards; targeting near-zero energy district[^2][^1][^6].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** Strengthened municipal and partner capabilities for smart city management; new collaborative mechanisms[^2][^1].
- **Policy Influence:** Inspiration for citywide and regional policies in mobility, energy, and citizen engagement[^1][^3].
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Real-time dashboards, open data platforms, and participatory reporting[^2][^1].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Most objectives met or exceeded—emissions reductions, mobility improvements, citizen engagement, technology integration[^2][^1][^6].
- **Unintended Consequences:** Some challenges with technology harmonization; need for ongoing support for digital inclusion.
- **Long-term Viability:** Demonstrated pathways for sustainability; ongoing integration into Milan’s smart city strategy[^4][^3].

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**

- **Contextual Dependencies:** Success reliant on strong governance, EU funding, technical expertise, and local engagement. Some tech solutions (e.g., interoperability platform) highly transferable; social engagement model requires adaptation[^1][^3].
- **Critical Success Factors:** Cross-sector partnerships, citizen co-design, robust digital infrastructure, clear policy alignment[^2][^1].
- **Scalability:** Model replicated in Bordeaux, Burgas, Warsaw; scalable to other European and global cities with adjustments for local conditions[^4][^3].

- **Cautionary Lessons:** Need for clear interoperability standards, early user engagement, and flexible funding structures[^2][^1].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Integrated, citizen-centred approach** combining technology, social innovation, and governance[^2][^1].
- **Substantial environmental and mobility impacts**, including emissions reduction and improved accessibility[^2][^1][^6].
- **Replicable models** for other cities, supported by robust data and open standards[^4][^3].
- **Strong stakeholder engagement** and participatory co-design[^1].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity of interoperability** among diverse technology providers[^2].
- **Ongoing need for digital inclusion** and support for less tech-savvy residents[^1].
- **Financial sustainability** beyond EU grant phase requires continued innovation[^4].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Promotes social equity** via retrofits in social housing and inclusive mobility solutions[^1].
- **Risk of unequal access** if digital divide not addressed; strong efforts made but ongoing vigilance needed[^1].

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory:** Milan’s smart district innovations are being scaled citywide; ongoing integration with new mobility and energy projects[^1][^4].
- **Ongoing challenges:** Maintaining citizen engagement, updating technology, ensuring financial and digital inclusion.
- **Next phases:** Expansion of digital platforms, further integration with regional transport and energy strategies, continued monitoring and co-design[^1][^4].
