# Executive summary## Transforming Collectivity – Community Housing for Socially Empowered Living

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Transforming Collectivity – Community Housing for Socially Empowered Living
- **Alternative Names:** Transforming Collectivity in Belgium, Transforming Collectivity (Holcim Awards Next Generation Prize 2020)
- **Location:** Brussels, Belgium (specifically, social housing blocks in the Brussels-Capital Region)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 50.8503° N, 4.3517° E (approximate center of Brussels)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Master’s thesis project: 2018
  - Holcim Awards Next Generation Prize: June 2020
  - Ongoing conceptual and pilot implementation (as of 2025)
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Focus on adaptive reuse of existing social housing blocks (typically 5–10 buildings per pilot cluster)
  - Target population: 100–200 residents per cluster
  - Investment: Primarily conceptual and pilot-scale; no large-scale public funding yet, but recognized as a model for future public and private investment
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - Annik Keoseyan (architect and urban designer, lead designer)
  - Holcim Foundation for Sustainable Construction (awarding and promoting body)
  - Brussels-Capital Region (policy and regulatory context)
  - Local housing associations and cooperatives (potential future partners)
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - Academic research (thesis funding)
  - Holcim Foundation prize (USD 15,000, ex aequo)
  - Potential future public and private investment for pilot implementation

#### **Executive Summary**
Transforming Collectivity is a pioneering social housing concept developed by Annik Keoseyan as part of her master’s thesis at the Brussels-Capital Region. The project reimagines outdated social housing by minimizing private space and maximizing shared communal areas—such as kitchens, living rooms, and utility spaces—thereby optimizing both spatial and energy efficiency. The approach is rooted in adaptive reuse, participatory design, and the promotion of shared living as a means to foster social sustainability, urban densification, and decarbonization. Recognized by the Holcim Foundation with a Next Generation Prize in 2020, the initiative offers a pragmatic, scalable model for upgrading existing housing stock in dense urban environments. Its core innovation lies in transforming social housing from a static, individualized model to a dynamic, collective one, where residents actively participate in the management and evolution of their living environment. The project has significant implications for urban policy, housing affordability, and climate resilience, serving as a blueprint for future urban regeneration in Europe and beyond.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Brussels is the capital of Belgium and the de facto capital of the European Union. With a population of approximately 1.2 million, it is a highly urbanized, multicultural city with a strong service economy, significant public sector employment, and a legacy of post-war social housing. The city faces challenges related to urban sprawl, aging infrastructure, and a growing demand for affordable housing.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The project is situated in the Brussels-Capital Region, which includes a mix of historic neighborhoods, post-war social housing estates, and areas of urban renewal. Many social housing blocks date from the 1960s–1980s and suffer from poor energy performance, social isolation, and underutilized communal spaces. The demographic profile is diverse, with a mix of low- and middle-income residents, immigrants, and young professionals.
- **Historical Development Patterns:** Brussels experienced rapid urbanization in the post-war period, leading to the construction of large-scale social housing estates. These estates were often designed with minimal communal facilities and a focus on individual units, resulting in social fragmentation and inefficiencies in space and energy use.
- **Current Land Use Composition:** The area is characterized by a mix of residential, commercial, and public uses, with a high density of social housing blocks. Many buildings are underutilized or vacant, particularly in the wake of changing demographics and economic shifts.
- **Demographic Profile:** The target neighborhoods have a diverse population, including low-income families, elderly residents, and young professionals. Income levels are generally moderate to low, with a significant proportion of residents relying on social housing.
- **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:** Infrastructure is generally adequate but aging, with challenges related to energy efficiency, accessibility, and maintenance. Public transport is well-developed, but many social housing estates are isolated from key amenities.
- **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** Brussels faces urban heat island effects, air pollution, and a need for climate adaptation. The city has set ambitious targets for carbon neutrality and circular economy, making energy-efficient housing upgrades a priority.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The project is guided by principles of adaptive reuse, participatory design, and shared living. The design philosophy emphasizes flexibility, community, and sustainability.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** The project involves the retrofitting of existing social housing blocks to minimize private space and maximize shared areas, such as kitchens, living rooms, and utility spaces. The design also includes smart energy monitoring systems and modular retrofit components.
- **Scope and Phasing:** The project is structured as a pilot, with initial implementation in a cluster of social housing blocks. The approach is scalable and can be replicated in other neighborhoods and cities.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** Smart energy monitoring, modular retrofit components, digital platforms for community engagement.
- **Innovation Type:** The project combines technological, social, and governance innovations.
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies are integrated with existing urban systems through adaptive reuse and participatory design.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** The project is led by the architect and urban designer, with potential future involvement of housing associations and local government.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** The project emphasizes participatory design, with opportunities for community input and co-creation.
- **Participatory Elements:** Residents are involved in the design and management of shared spaces, fostering a sense of ownership and community.
- **Project Management:** The project is coordinated by the lead designer, with support from the Holcim Foundation and local partners.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** The project is primarily conceptual and pilot-scale, with no large-scale public funding yet. The Holcim Foundation prize provides seed funding for pilot implementation.
- **Funding Mix:** Academic research, prize funding, potential future public and private investment.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Not applicable at this stage.
- **Financial Sustainability:** The project is designed to be financially sustainable through energy savings, reduced maintenance costs, and potential future public and private investment.
