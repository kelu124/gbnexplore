# Executive summary## IGBC Biodiversity Case Study – National Forensic Mental Health Service (NFMHS) Project

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** IGBC Biodiversity Case Study – National Forensic Mental Health Service (NFMHS) Project
- **Alternative Names:** National Forensic Mental Health Service Hospital; St. Ita’s Hospital Redevelopment
- **Location:** Portrane, St. Ita’s Hospital Demesne, Co. Dublin, Ireland
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.493°N, 6.125°W
- **Timeline:**  
  - Feasibility and planning: 2012–2016  
  - Construction: 2018–2021  
  - Completion: 2021 (operational)
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Site area: approx. 111 hectares (entire demesne)  
  - Hospital footprint: 24,500 m²  
  - Capacity: 170 beds including specialist units[^1][^2]
  - Investment value: £125 million[^2]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Health Service Executive Ireland (Client)  
  - Scott Tallon Walker Architects (Lead Architect)  
  - JV Tierney & Co. (Engineering Consultant)  
  - IGBC (Irish Green Building Council, for biodiversity assessment)[^3][^4][^2]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Public (national health capital investment)  
  - Supported by Construct Innovate and the Land Development Agency for biodiversity mainstreaming[^3]

#### **Executive Summary**

The National Forensic Mental Health Service (NFMHS) Project in Portrane, Co. Dublin, represents a significant evolution in Irish healthcare and green building practice. Developed on the historical campus of St. Ita’s Hospital, the new facility was designed to deliver secure mental health services in a restorative, nature-integrated environment. The initiative’s core ambition was to protect and enhance local biodiversity, leveraging the site’s exceptional woodland and coastal ecosystems as therapeutic assets for patient recovery and staff well-being. The project’s landscape strategy prioritized biodiversity-sensitive planning, digital monitoring, and stakeholder collaboration. Measures included early ecologist involvement, restoration of an adjacent wetland, creation of native woodland, planting for farmland birds, and integration of nesting habitats for key species.

The project’s challenges stemmed from reconciling strict healthcare regulations with ecological ambitions, requiring innovative solutions such as surface water management to benefit both stormwater control and habitat restoration. The outcomes included measurable improvements in patient health, increased species richness, operational cost savings via sustainable landscaping, and heightened awareness among staff and stakeholders. As part of Ireland’s push to mainstream biodiversity within the built environment, the NFMHS project serves as a national exemplar, aligning with both EU and Irish nature restoration targets and inspiring future urban developments to embed biodiversity as a core principle[^3][^2][^5].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**  
Portrane is a coastal village on the Donabate peninsula within Fingal County, North County Dublin. The area is characterized by a mix of rural, coastal, and institutional land uses, with a population reflecting both local residents and the hospital community. Its economy is modest, with services, local businesses, and significant institutional employment (healthcare)[^6].

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
- **Historical Development:** Site dominated by St. Ita’s Hospital (late 19th century) and its extensive demesne landscape; Victorian red-brick architecture visible across the estuary[^6].
- **Current Land Use:** Institutional healthcare, woodland, coastal habitat, recreation fields, and protected heritage structures[^6].
- **Demographic Profile:** Mixed income, predominantly Irish; staff and patients draw from nationwide; population fluctuates with institutional use[^6].
- **Infrastructure Quality:** Aging healthcare facilities replaced by modern hospital; access via local road network; proximity to Natura 2000 sites (Rogerstown and Malahide Estuaries)[^6].
- **Environmental Conditions:** Coastal site with woodland, estuarine margins, sensitive habitats designated as SAC/SPA (Special Area of Conservation / Special Protection Area); high landscape value and ecological sensitivity[^6].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Outdated mental health facilities at Dundrum not fit for contemporary care[^6]
  - Biodiversity loss from previous land use changes and neglect
  - Wetland degradation due to altered hydrology from urbanization
  - Need for secure, therapeutic environments for complex mental health cases[^2]
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - National decision in 2012 to relocate Central Mental Hospital to Portrane[^6]
  - Policy shift toward modern, human-centered mental health care
  - EU and Irish biodiversity commitments
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Health Service Executive (operator)
  - Architects/contractors/ecologists (project team)
  - Local community, nature conservation groups
  - Government departments (health, environment)[^6][^3]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Fingal County Council (local authority, planning control)
  - Health Service Executive (owner/operator)
  - IGBC, Construct Innovate, Land Development Agency (biodiversity mainstreaming partners)[^3]
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Local Objectives for woodland management, sustainable reuse, biodiversity protection (Fingal CDP)[^6]
  - Natura 2000 compliance; SAC/SPA and Nature Development Area designations[^6]
  - Irish Nature Restoration Plan (draft, EU-aligned)[^3]
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Protected status for buildings and landscape
  - Requirement for Appropriate Assessment under EU Habitats Directive
  - Planning restrictions on coastal, sensitive landscapes[^6]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Deliver high-quality, secure mental health care in a therapeutic, nature-integrated setting
  - Protect and enhance local biodiversity and ecological functions
  - Restore degraded habitats (e.g., wetland, woodland)
  - Promote stakeholder engagement and awareness of biodiversity[^3][^2]
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - “To create a world-class forensic mental health facility that fosters patient recovery and well-being by integrating nature and biodiversity into every aspect of the built environment.”
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Supports Irish and EU nature restoration targets
  - Contributes to the mainstreaming of biodiversity in Ireland’s construction sector[^3]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Early ecologist involvement and biodiversity-sensitive design → enhanced local habitats, stormwater management, and species richness → improved patient well-being, operational sustainability, and compliance with policy[^2][^3]
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Hospital patients, staff, local community, regional biodiversity networks
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Digital monitoring of green features
  - Nature-based solutions for stormwater and habitat restoration
  - Integrated stakeholder collaboration for ongoing management[^3][^2]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - Health sector modernization, biodiversity commitments
- **Economic:**  
  - Efficient land use, operational cost savings
- **Social:**  
  - Improved patient outcomes, community engagement
- **Environmental:**  
  - Climate adaptation, habitat restoration, nature-based solutions
- **Technological:**  
  - Digital tracking, landscape management tools[^3]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Regenerative design; therapeutic landscape; mainstreaming biodiversity in healthcare[^2]
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Pavilion-style buildings arranged around a village green
  - Secure perimeter without institutional fencing
  - Direct access and views to landscaped courtyards, woodland, and wetlands[^2]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Feasibility and environmental surveys → design → phased construction (2018–2021) → operational handover and ongoing landscape management[^6][^2]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Digital monitoring of landscape features and maintenance
  - Nature-based stormwater management (surface water redirected to wetland)
  - Biodiversity-sensitive planting, nest boxes for birds and bats, sacrificial crops for seed-eating birds[^2][^3]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digital monitoring), environmental (nature-based solutions), social (collaborative governance)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Embedded within landscape and facility management plans; coordinated between design, construction, and operations teams[^3][^2]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive tender for design/construction; specialist consultant engagement (ecologists, landscape architects)[^6][^2]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Early and active involvement of biodiversity experts
  - Collaboration between architects, contractors, ecologists, and community[^3][^2]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Stakeholder forums; feedback mechanisms for ongoing management[^3]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Cross-disciplinary project team (architecture, engineering, ecology)
  - Use of digital tools for monitoring and reporting

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - £125 million total investment for hospital redevelopment and landscaping[^2]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Entirely public sector funded, with support for biodiversity mainstreaming from national programs[^3]
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - No direct revenue; operational cost savings via sustainable landscaping[^3]
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Reduced maintenance costs and improved operational efficiency due to ecological design

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integrating biodiversity features into secure healthcare environment
  - Restoring wetland hydrology impacted by urbanization[^2]
- **Financial:**  
  - High upfront costs for nature-based solutions balanced by long-term savings
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Need for stakeholder buy-in; overcoming risk-averse attitudes in healthcare sector[^3]
- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination among multiple agencies and disciplines; compliance with regulations
- **Environmental:**  
  - Site adjacent to protected habitats; strict planning controls; potential for unintended impacts on sensitive ecosystems[^6]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Early and ongoing ecologist involvement allowed for creative solutions (e.g., surface water diversion to wetland instead of underground tanks)[^3][^2]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments to landscape plan to preserve woodland and enhance habitat connectivity
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of early stakeholder engagement; need for flexibility in design to respond to ecological data[^3][^2]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Pioneering biodiversity mainstreaming** in Irish healthcare and construction
- **Therapeutic landscape** that demonstrably improves patient and staff well-being
- **Innovative stormwater and habitat strategies** (e.g., wetland restoration, woodland management)
- **Robust stakeholder engagement** and cross-disciplinary collaboration[^3][^2]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity of regulatory compliance** in highly sensitive landscapes
- **Potentially higher upfront costs** for nature-based solutions
- **Long-term biodiversity monitoring** required for sustained impact

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Inclusive benefits** for patients and staff from all backgrounds
- No displacement or negative equity impacts identified
- **Preservation of cultural heritage** and public accessibility balanced with secure healthcare provision[^6]

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Strong potential for replication** in other sectors and regions
- Ongoing monitoring and adaptive management planned
- Future challenges include **maintaining stakeholder engagement**, adapting to climate change, and scaling lessons to mainstream biodiversity in all Irish urban development[^3][^2]
