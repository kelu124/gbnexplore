# Executive summary## LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN: Falcão Primary School Green Roof Demonstration

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN – Falcão Primary School Green Roof Demonstration
- **Alternative Names:** Escola do Falcão Green Roof Project; LIFE-mBiG Porto Pilot
- **Location:** Falcão Primary School, Campanhã, Porto, Portugal
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 41.1539° N, 8.6038° W
- **Timeline:**
  - Project Start: 2018 (LIFE17 CCA/ES/000088)
  - NBS Implementation at Falcão: 2021
  - Initial Monitoring: 2021–2022
  - After-LIFE Monitoring and Educational Programming: Ongoing (2024–2025)[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:**
  - Green area at Falcão: 700 m²[^3]
  - Total NBS intervention across three pilot schools: 3,911 m²[^4]
  - Direct beneficiaries: School students and staff (~hundreds); indirect: local community, municipal staff
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - Câmara Municipal do Porto (Porto City Council)
  - CARTIF Technology Centre (Spain, project coordination)
  - Local and EU partners
- **Funding Sources:**
  - European Union’s LIFE Programme (Climate Change Adaptation)
  - Co-funding from project partners and Porto Municipality[^1][^4]

#### **Executive Summary**

The Falcão Primary School Green Roof Demonstration, a flagship component of the LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN initiative, exemplifies cutting-edge application of **nature-based solutions (NBS)** for climate adaptation in urban educational environments. Implemented in Porto, Portugal, this project transformed the school’s flat roof into a multifunctional green space, integrated with photovoltaic panels, vegetated façades, and real-time digital monitoring. The primary objectives were to reduce heat stress, improve indoor thermal comfort, enhance biodiversity, and lower building operational costs—all while serving as a living laboratory for environmental education and community outreach.

As part of a broader EU-funded effort, the Falcão pilot demonstrates the technical and social feasibility of integrating NBS in public buildings, particularly in southern European climates increasingly vulnerable to extreme heat. Early results show measurable reductions in indoor and roof surface temperatures, increased urban biodiversity, and improved water management. The initiative also pioneered participatory educational programs, involving students directly in monitoring and communicating environmental performance.

By combining technological innovation, robust stakeholder engagement, and a strong emphasis on transferability, the Falcão School project has become a replicable model for green retrofitting of schools, inspiring further adoption of NBS across urban public infrastructure in Portugal and beyond[^4][^1][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**
Porto, Portugal’s second-largest city, is a key economic and cultural hub in northern Portugal. With a metropolitan population over 1.7 million, Porto is characterized by a dense urban fabric, a mix of historic and contemporary neighborhoods, and a maritime-influenced climate (Csb, Köppen classification: warm, temperate, with wet winters and dry, mild summers). The city faces pronounced urban heat stress due to climate change, especially in densely built areas[^4][^5].

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
The Falcão Primary School is located in Campanhã, an eastern district of Porto with a legacy of industrial, residential, and transport uses. Historically a working-class area, Campanhã has seen recent redevelopment efforts aimed at improving infrastructure, public spaces, and social services. The area’s demographic profile skews toward lower-middle income, with a mix of children, elderly, and diverse ethnic backgrounds. Infrastructure quality varies, with some older public buildings requiring modernization for energy, comfort, and accessibility. Environmental challenges include limited green space and vulnerability to heat waves[^5][^1].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

**Problems/Challenges Identified:**
- **Thermal discomfort and heat stress:** School buildings experienced excessive indoor temperatures during spring and summer, adversely affecting learning environments and health.
- **High energy costs:** Reliance on air conditioning during heat waves led to increased operational expenses.
- **Biodiversity loss:** Limited green infrastructure and sealed surfaces reduced local ecological value.
- **Low resilience to climate change:** Existing buildings were not designed for contemporary climate extremes[^4][^5][^6].

**Triggering Events:**
- Recognition of climate change impacts on vulnerable populations (children) and educational outcomes.
- EU policy emphasis on climate adaptation and NBS.
- Porto’s municipal sustainability strategy prioritizing green infrastructure in public buildings[^4][^2].

**Stakeholder Landscape:**
Key stakeholders included Porto City Council (building owner/operator), teachers, students, parents, municipal environmental and education departments, local NGOs, and EU project partners[^1][^4].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

**Governance Framework:**
- Led by Porto City Council, in collaboration with EU LIFE Programme governance and technical partners.
- Coordination with national and local education and environment authorities[^4][^1].

**Relevant Policies:**
- EU Climate Adaptation Strategy
- National and municipal sustainability action plans
- Building codes and urban greening regulations

**Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
- Enabling: EU LIFE Programme support, local political commitment, growing regulatory focus on NBS.
- Barriers: Integration of NBS with existing school infrastructure, ensuring compliance with safety and building codes[^4][^5].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

**Primary Goals:**
- **Increase climate resilience** of educational buildings through NBS.
- **Improve indoor environmental quality** and comfort for students and staff.
- **Reduce energy consumption and operational costs.**
- **Enhance urban biodiversity** and ecosystem services.
- **Serve as a demonstration and educational tool** for NBS replication[^4][^1].

**Vision Statement:**
“To transform public educational buildings into climate-resilient, energy-efficient, and nature-integrated community assets, fostering wellbeing, sustainability, and environmental awareness.”[^4]

**Alignment with Broader Plans:**
The initiative supports citywide and EU strategies for climate adaptation, sustainable urban development, and the mainstreaming of NBS in public infrastructure[^4][^7].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

**Theory of Change:**
- **Inputs:** Install green roofs, vegetated façades, photovoltaic panels; deploy digital sensors; engage educational community.
- **Activities:** Retrofit building, monitor environmental parameters, deliver educational programs.
- **Outputs:** Improved thermal comfort, reduced energy use, increased biodiversity, enhanced awareness.
- **Outcomes:** Healthier, lower-carbon, more resilient school; model for replication[^4][^5][^8].

**Target Beneficiaries:**
- Primary: Students, staff, and families of Falcão School.
- Secondary: Wider municipal community, education sector, policymakers[^1][^4].

**Innovation Elements:**
- Data-driven environmental performance monitoring.
- Integration of NBS and renewable energy.
- Use of the school as a living laboratory for sustainability education[^1][^4][^8].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** Commitment by Porto’s municipal leadership to climate action and educational modernization.
- **Economic:** Desire to reduce public sector energy costs; access to EU funding.
- **Social:** Advocacy for child and community health; educational enrichment.
- **Environmental:** Need to address urban heat, biodiversity loss, and water management.
- **Technological:** Availability of advanced green roofing, sensor technologies, and digital platforms[^4][^1][^8].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

**Conceptual Framework:**
- Urban ecology and climate adaptation principles.
- NBS as multifunctional infrastructure: cooling, biodiversity, stormwater management, educational value[^4][^5].

**Physical/Spatial Design:**
- 700 m² extensive green roof system (sedum and local species).
- Vegetated/green façades for shading.
- Photovoltaic solar panels for renewable energy.
- Digital sensors for real-time climate and environmental monitoring.
- Demonstration areas for educational activities[^3][^9][^8].

**Scope and Phasing:**
- Initial design and planning (2019–2020).
- Construction/installation (2021).
- Monitoring and educational integration (2021–2025 and continuing)[^1][^4][^8].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Extensive green roof system (lightweight, drought-tolerant vegetation)
  - Green façades (vertical gardens on select walls)
  - Photovoltaic panels (rooftop solar generation)
  - Digital environmental sensors (temperature, humidity, soil moisture, biodiversity tracking)
  - Automated reporting platforms[^8][^1][^9]
- **Innovation Type:** Technological, educational, process-oriented.
- **Integration Approach:** Retrofit integrated with existing building structure and curriculum; data from sensors used in student learning and facility management[^1][^4][^8].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** Public procurement by Porto City Council with technical input from project partners.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Participatory design with school community, municipal staff, and technical experts.
- **Participatory Elements:** Students involved in monitoring, research projects, and communication of results; interpretive visits and outreach to other schools[^1][^10][^8].
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by Porto’s municipal departments and CARTIF, with regular partner meetings and EU reporting[^4][^2].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN total budget ~€2.7 million (for three pilot sites); Falcão share not separately specified[^11].
- **Funding Mix:** Majority from EU LIFE Programme; co-funding from Porto Municipality and project partners[^4][^11].
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Indirect savings via reduced energy costs.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Reduced operational costs and integration into municipal sustainability budgets[^4].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:** Integrating green roofs and facades with existing school building; ensuring effective waterproofing and load-bearing capacity.
- **Financial:** Upfront costs for NBS retrofitting; ongoing maintenance funding.
- **Social and Political:** Aligning project with school schedules; ensuring buy-in from all stakeholders.
- **Administrative:** Coordination across municipal departments and with external partners.
- **Environmental:** Establishing vegetation in the context of climate variability; managing water use[^4][^5][^8].

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Technical studies and modeling (e.g., EnergyPlus simulations) to optimize NBS selection and placement; phased implementation to minimize disruption[^5].
- **Design Modifications:** Adjustments to green roof substrate and plant mix based on local conditions; adaptive irrigation strategies.
- **Lessons Learned:** Importance of stakeholder participation, need for robust maintenance plans, value of real-time monitoring for adaptive management[^4][^5][^6].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** 700 m² green roof and vegetated façade installed; photovoltaic panels operational; digital sensor network deployed[^3][^1][^8].
- **Service Delivery:** Enhanced learning and recreational environment; improved thermal comfort; educational programming on climate and sustainability[^1][^10].
- **Quantitative Metrics:**
  - Surface temperature reduction: >5°C on green roof vs. conventional roof[^4]
  - 25+ new plant and animal species recorded onsite post-intervention[^4]
  - Rainwater run-off loss reduced by 31%[^4]
  - Indoor temperature reductions of ~1°C on average, with hourly peaks up to 3°C[^5]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Improved wellbeing and comfort for students and staff; increased awareness of climate adaptation[^1][^10].
- **Equity Outcomes:** Targeted at a public school in a socioeconomically mixed district; benefits accessible to all students.
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No evidence of negative impacts.
- **Social Capital:** Strengthened community engagement and environmental stewardship.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Fostered a culture of sustainability and innovation in the school community[^1][^10][^8].

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Temporary employment for construction and maintenance; educational programming supports local green economy.
- **Economic Activity:** Stimulus for local green infrastructure sector; demonstration effect for other public buildings.
- **Property Values:** Potential improvement in perceived value of school and neighborhood amenities.
- **Cost Savings:** Documented reduction in energy costs for cooling and heating (11.2% electricity, up to 30% diesel for heating in comparable pilot sites)[^4].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Public sector focus ensures broad access to benefits.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** Reduced energy consumption lowers greenhouse gas emissions.
- **Resource Management:** Increased rainwater retention and reduced runoff; improved microclimate.
- **Environmental Quality:** Enhanced biodiversity (flora and fauna); improved air quality; noise reduction.
- **Waste and Pollution:** Potentially reduced water pollution from stormwater runoff.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Project contributes to EU climate adaptation, biodiversity, and sustainability targets[^4][^5].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** Strengthened municipal experience with NBS and performance monitoring.
- **Policy Influence:** Informs municipal and national guidelines for school retrofitting; supports EU mainstreaming of NBS.
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Automated monitoring and reporting shared with stakeholders[^4][^1][^2].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Achieved significant reductions in heat stress and energy use; improved biodiversity and educational outcomes; strong interest from other schools in replication.
- **Unintended Consequences:** No major negatives reported; ongoing monitoring to detect long-term issues.
- **Long-term Viability:** Maintenance and monitoring plans in place; integrated into municipal sustainability programs[^1][^2].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Integrated NBS with real-time digital monitoring and educational programming.
- Demonstrated measurable environmental, economic, and social benefits.
- Effective stakeholder engagement and model for replication in public buildings[^4][^1][^10].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Upfront capital costs and ongoing maintenance requirements may challenge replication in resource-constrained settings.
- Some technical uncertainties remain regarding long-term vegetation performance and maintenance in changing climate conditions.
- Monitoring period at Falcão is still relatively short; full impacts will emerge over time[^4][^1][^5].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Project targeted a public school in a diverse and mixed-income area.
- No evidence of exclusion or negative social impacts; project improved environmental justice by providing benefits to a vulnerable population (children).
- Participatory approaches enhanced inclusion and local ownership[^4][^1].

#### **Future Outlook**

- Ongoing monitoring and educational programming through the After-LIFE phase (2024–2025 and beyond).
- Potential for citywide and national scaling as part of broader climate adaptation and school modernization programs.
- Continuous knowledge sharing and best practices dissemination to support further replication and innovation[^2][^1].
