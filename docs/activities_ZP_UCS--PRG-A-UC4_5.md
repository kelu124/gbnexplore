# Executive summary## SmartEnCity – Lighthouse District Smart Renovation (Vitoria-Gasteiz, Spain)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** SmartEnCity – Lighthouse District Smart Renovation
- **Alternative Names:** Vitoria-Gasteiz Lighthouse District; SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz Demonstrator
- **Location:** Coronación District, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Basque Country, Spain
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 42.8460° N, 2.6716° W (city center)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project start: 2016  
  - Retrofitting works: 2017–2021  
  - Project monitoring and evaluation: 2021–2023  
  - Ongoing operation and assessment
- **Project Scale:**  
  - District area: ~60,000 m²  
  - 655–750 dwellings retrofitted (corresponding to approx. 1,300 residents)  
  - Total investment (all SmartEnCity LH cities): €94.5 million (public/private)[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Ayuntamiento de Vitoria-Gasteiz (City Council)  
  - Basque Government (Department of Energy)  
  - Veolia (private energy operator)  
  - VISESA (public housing and urban renewal agency)[^2]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020 (SmartEnCity)  
  - Basque Government  
  - City of Vitoria-Gasteiz  
  - Private investment (Veolia, building owners)[^1][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**

The SmartEnCity Lighthouse District project in Vitoria-Gasteiz is a flagship urban decarbonization initiative under the European Union’s Horizon 2020 SmartEnCity program. Centered on the Coronación neighborhood—a densely populated, aging district facing significant energy inefficiency—the project aimed to demonstrate a holistic approach to sustainable urban renewal. Its core interventions included deep energy retrofitting of over 650 dwellings, the establishment of an innovative biomass-fueled district heating network, and the deployment of advanced digital and ICT tools for real-time energy monitoring, predictive maintenance, and user engagement.

A unique public-private partnership model was adopted, combining municipal leadership, regional government involvement, and private sector operation (Veolia). The project’s ambitions extended beyond technical upgrades: it sought to catalyze behavioral change, foster resident participation, and build institutional capacity for long-term climate neutrality. With documented reductions of up to 45% in energy demand and major cuts in CO₂ emissions, the initiative has delivered healthier, more affordable housing and improved quality of life for its residents. The project is recognized for its replicability, leveraging digital twin and smart monitoring technologies to enable proactive energy management and scalable learning.

SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz positions itself as a model for other European cities, aligning with broader decarbonization strategies and contributing to the EU’s mission of creating 100 climate-neutral cities by 2030[^1][^4]. Its experience highlights both the opportunities and complexities of large-scale, citizen-centered urban energy transformation.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Vitoria-Gasteiz is the administrative capital of the Basque Country with a population of approximately 240,000. The city is renowned for its environmental leadership, having received accolades such as European Green Capital (2012). Its economic base includes administration, services, industry, and logistics. The city features a compact urban form, extensive green spaces, and a strong commitment to sustainable urban planning[^3].
  
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical development:** Coronación is a traditional working-class district developed in the mid-20th century with multi-family residential blocks.
  - **Land use:** Primarily residential, with supporting commercial and community services.
  - **Demographics:** Lower-to-middle income households, diverse age groups, and some vulnerable populations.
  - **Infrastructure quality:** Buildings were energy-inefficient, with outdated heating (mostly individual gas boilers or electric heaters), limited insulation, and poor accessibility for the elderly.
  - **Environmental conditions:** The area faced urban heat loss, high energy bills, and moderate air quality concerns due to fossil fuel use[^5][^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High district energy consumption and CO₂ emissions.
  - Poor thermal comfort and high utility bills for residents.
  - Aging building stock with insufficient insulation and inefficient heating.
  - Social vulnerability and risk of fuel poverty.
  - Lack of collective energy infrastructure and digital monitoring.
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - City’s commitment to the Covenant of Mayors and local climate action plans.
  - EU funding opportunity via Horizon 2020 SmartEnCity call.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City council (leadership and coordination).
  - Basque Government (policy and financial support).
  - Veolia (private operator for district heating).
  - Residents and neighborhood associations (target beneficiaries and co-creators).
  - EU (strategic and financial framework)[^3][^1].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Multi-level governance involving municipal, regional, and EU actors.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - City’s Climate Change Prevention Strategy (2006), Plan against Climate Change (2010), Covenant of Mayors.
  - National and regional incentives for renewable energy and building retrofitting.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Innovative public-private ownership and operation model for district heating (shared 50-50 between Basque Energy Agency and Veolia).
  - National and EU regulations on energy efficiency and renewable deployment.
  - Some regulatory unfamiliarity with district heating as a “natural monopoly” and need for new skills among planners[^3].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Drastically reduce energy demand and CO₂ emissions in the Coronación district.
  - Improve resident comfort and affordability through deep retrofitting and renewable-based heating.
  - Demonstrate scalable, replicable solutions for smart, zero-carbon urban neighborhoods[^1][^5].
- **Vision Statement:**  
  Toward a “Smart Zero Carbon City” where carbon footprint is minimized, energy is clean and efficiently managed, and citizens actively participate in the energy transition[^1].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Supports Vitoria-Gasteiz’s long-term goal of carbon neutrality by 2050.
  - Fits EU’s climate-neutral cities mission and Basque Country energy transition strategies.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  Deep retrofitting and renewable energy infrastructure, coupled with digital monitoring and citizen engagement, will reduce energy demand, lower emissions, and improve quality of life. These interventions will serve as a model for other cities.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  Residents of the Coronación district, particularly vulnerable groups at risk of energy poverty.
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Digital twin and smart metering for real-time performance management.
  - Proactive, predictive maintenance using analytics.
  - Integrated public-private governance for district heating—a first in Spain[^3][^1].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Alignment with EU and city climate targets; leadership in urban sustainability.
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Reducing energy costs for residents; stimulating investment in local construction and energy sectors.
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Improving housing quality, health, and comfort; addressing energy poverty.
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Reducing fossil fuel dependence and emissions; promoting renewable energy.
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Leveraging digitalization, IoT, and data analytics for smarter energy management[^1][^5].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  A holistic, systemic approach combining physical retrofitting, renewable energy, digitalization, and citizen participation.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Deep retrofitting of building envelopes (insulation, façade/roof upgrades).
  - Replacement of individual boilers with connections to a new biomass-fueled district heating network.
  - Installation of smart meters and wireless sensor networks.
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Baseline assessment and planning.
  - Phase 2: Building retrofitting and district heating construction.
  - Phase 3: ICT deployment, resident engagement, and monitoring.
  - Phase 4: Evaluation and replication planning[^5][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - **Building and district digital twin** for simulation and predictive maintenance.
  - **Smart meters** and **wireless sensor networks** for real-time monitoring.
  - **Biomass-based district heating** (wood chips).
  - **Integrated energy management systems** at dwelling, building, and district level.
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digitalization, renewables), social (user engagement), governance (public-private partnership).
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Seamless integration of ICTs with physical infrastructure.
  - Digital tools for both technical optimization and resident feedback[^1][^5].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive tendering process; Veolia consortium selected as private operator.
  - 25-year service contract with shared ownership between public and private sectors[^3].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Extensive resident consultation and education.
  - Joint decision-making on district heating design and operation.
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Co-creation workshops.
  - Feedback loops via digital platforms.
- **Project Management:**  
  - Multi-actor steering committee (city, region, private operator, EU representatives).
  - Dedicated project management office[^1].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Project-specific investment: significant portion of €94.5 million SmartEnCity LH allocation.
  - Breakdown: building retrofitting, district heating infrastructure, ICT and monitoring systems[^1].
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 (grant).
  - Regional and municipal co-financing.
  - Private sector (Veolia and co-investment by building owners).
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - User fees for district heating.
  - Potential energy savings reinvested for maintenance and upgrades.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Public-private partnership ensures risk-sharing and long-term viability[^3].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Integration of digital monitoring systems with legacy building stock.
  - Ensuring reliability of biomass district heating and digital twins[^5].
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Upfront investment needs; securing sufficient connections to ensure profitability.
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Resident skepticism toward district heating (loss of individual control, pricing concerns).
  - Need for extensive engagement to build trust and understanding.
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Navigating new forms of public-private governance and legal frameworks.
  - Coordination among multiple agencies and stakeholders[^3].
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Sourcing sustainable biomass; managing potential local air quality impacts.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Iterative design: technical solutions adapted to building typologies.
  - Continuous resident dialogue and demonstration of comfort/cost benefits.
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments in heating network phasing based on user uptake.
  - Enhanced digital interfaces for clearer resident feedback.
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of transparent communication and education in shifting user habits and expectations.
  - Need for flexibility in governance structures to accommodate unanticipated issues[^3][^1].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Over 650 dwellings retrofitted with high-efficiency envelopes[^5].
  - New biomass-fueled district heating network constructed.
  - Smart metering and sensor systems installed in all participating buildings.
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Reliable, centralized renewable heating.
  - Real-time performance monitoring and automated maintenance alerts.
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 45% reduction in district energy demand.
  - ~1,300 residents served.
  - CO₂ emissions savings: documented at over 1,500 tCO₂eq/year (project-wide)[^1].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved indoor comfort and health.
  - Lower utility costs; reduced risk of fuel poverty.
  - Enhanced sense of community ownership and engagement.
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Focus on lower-income and vulnerable groups.
  - Mitigation of displacement/gentrification risks through public oversight.
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened networks among residents and between public/private actors.
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Preserved neighborhood identity while upgrading infrastructure.

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Construction and retrofitting jobs; ongoing maintenance and monitoring roles.
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Stimulated local supply chains for construction, digital tech, and biomass fuel.
- **Property Values:**  
  - Likely increases due to improved building standards (data pending).
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Lower energy bills for residents; reduced municipal energy expenditure.
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Targeted support for at-risk households to ensure participation[^1][^2].

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Significant reduction in fossil fuel use; major CO₂ emissions drops.
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Efficient use of biomass; optimization of heat distribution and consumption.
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality (biomass vs. gas/electric) and noise reduction from upgraded infrastructure.
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Reduced energy waste; improved waste management from building upgrades.
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Alignment with low-energy building standards and EU climate goals[^1][^5].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Enhanced skills in digital energy management and participatory planning.
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for national/regional policy on district heating and retrofitting.
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Public-private partnership increases oversight; digital systems improve data transparency.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Achieved targeted energy and emissions reductions; upscaling potential validated.
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Some initial resident resistance; ongoing need for engagement.
- **Long-term Viability:**

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Demonstrated major energy and emissions reductions.
- Pioneered integrated digital and physical retrofitting at district scale.
- Advanced equity by targeting socially vulnerable populations.
- Established a replicable blueprint for smart, climate-neutral urban transformation[^1][^4].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Complexity of district heating governance and operation required significant adaptation.
- Some resident skepticism and difficulty in shifting away from individual control.
- High upfront investment and dependence on public/EU funding for initial viability.
- Delays in implementation due to technical and social learning curves[^3][^5].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Focused on affordable, healthier housing for lower-income groups.
- Actively engaged residents, mitigating risks of exclusion and gentrification.
- Public oversight in partnership model helped ensure accountability.

#### **Future Outlook**

- Long-term monitoring and optimization continue, with potential for further digital integration (AI, advanced analytics).
- Ongoing challenge: ensuring biomass supply remains sustainable and costs stable.
- Future phases may include expansion to other districts and further integration with urban mobility and renewable energy systems.
- Continued dissemination of lessons learned through the SmartEnCity Network and EU “100 Climate-Neutral Cities” mission[^1][^6].
