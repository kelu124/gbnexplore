# Executive summary## Urban Sense: Monitoring Greenhouse Gas Emissions in Real Time Across Dublin

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Urban Sense: Monitoring Greenhouse Gas Emissions in Real Time Across Dublin
- **Alternative Names:** Urban Sense Pilot, Dublin Urban GHG Sensor Network
- **Location:** Dublin, Ireland (citywide; sensors deployed in both residential and commercial areas)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approximate city centroid: 53.3498° N, 6.2603° W
- **Timeline:** Official launch – 26 January 2024; initial pilot duration – one year (2024–2025)[^1][^2][^3]
- **Project Scale:** 20 sensors across the city; covers diverse land uses; city population ~1.4 million; total area 117 km²[^3][^4]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Terrain-AI (Maynooth University-led consortium), Edgeliot (DCU spinout), University College Dublin, Dublin City Council, Cellnex, Delmec
- **Funding Sources:** Microsoft Ireland, Science Foundation Ireland, Enterprise Ireland (Commercialisation Fund), in-kind city infrastructure support[^1][^2][^4]

#### **Executive Summary**
Urban Sense is a pioneering, year-long pilot program initiated in 2024 to provide **real-time measurement and visualization of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions** across Dublin. The project leverages a network of 20 low-cost, edge-computing sensors mounted on mobile phone mast infrastructure to capture concentrations of **CO₂, methane, and key air quality parameters**. This system delivers a dynamic, high-resolution "visual pulse" of the city's atmospheric conditions, enabling the identification of emissions hotspots, tracking of temporal changes due to weather or human activity, and empirical assessment of policy interventions[^1][^2][^3][^5][^4]. 

The initiative supports Dublin's commitment under the **EU Cities Mission** to become climate neutral by 2030 and aligns directly with Ireland's Climate Action Plan to halve GHG emissions by 2030 and reach net zero by 2050. By providing actionable, granular data to policymakers, urban planners, and the public, Urban Sense facilitates evidence-based climate action, improved air quality, and greater public engagement in sustainability efforts. The project is led by a partnership of leading Irish universities, technology start-ups, and city agencies, with funding from Microsoft and national research bodies. Urban Sense is intended as a scalable, replicable model for other European cities seeking to implement targeted climate mitigation and adaptation measures[^1][^2][^3][^5].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**
- **Dublin** is Ireland's capital and largest city, with a population of 1.4 million in the greater urban area.
- It is the national economic, political, and cultural center, with a diverse economy encompassing technology, finance, tourism, and services[^1][^3][^5].
- Geographically located on Ireland’s east coast, Dublin is compact, with a mix of historic core, suburban districts, and expanding commercial zones.

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
- Sensors are distributed across a range of land uses, including dense city center, residential neighborhoods, and commercial/industrial areas[^1][^2][^3].
- Dublin’s development pattern features a historic city core surrounded by 19th- and 20th-century suburbs.
- The city is ethnically diverse and houses a range of income groups.
- Infrastructure is generally modern in the center, but some outer areas face congestion, air quality, and accessibility challenges.
- Environmental constraints include air pollution from transport, energy use in older buildings, and vulnerability to sea-level rise and extreme weather[^5].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

**Problems/Challenges Identified:**
- Lack of **granular, real-time data** on GHG emissions and air quality at the urban scale[^5][^4].
- Difficulty in **measuring the effectiveness of climate policies** due to the absence of dense monitoring infrastructure.
- Rising concern over **urban air pollution**, traffic congestion, and their impacts on health and livability.
- Need for **public engagement** and transparency in climate action.

**Triggering Events:**
- Ireland’s declaration of a biodiversity and climate emergency in 2019[^1].
- Dublin’s selection for the **EU Cities Mission** (climate neutrality by 2030)[^2][^3][^5].
- Publication of the National Biodiversity Action Plan 2023–2030[^1].

**Stakeholder Landscape:**
- **Local government (Dublin City Council):** Key for implementation and policy response.
- **Academic partners:** Provided technical expertise and innovation.
- **Private sector (Edgeliot, Cellnex, Delmec, Microsoft):** Developed technology, provided infrastructure, and funding.
- **General public:** End-users of cleaner air and transparency.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

**Governance Framework:**
- Multi-level: Dublin City Council leads locally; national policy frameworks apply; EU Cities Mission provides strategic direction[^2][^3].
- Coordination across city agencies, academic consortia, and private partners.

**Relevant Policies:**
- Ireland’s **Climate Action Plan 2021** (GHG reductions by 51% of 2018 levels by 2030, net zero by 2050)[^2][^5].
- EU Cities Mission for 100 climate-neutral cities by 2030.
- National Biodiversity Action Plan 2023–2030.

**Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
- Supportive policy frameworks for emissions reduction and smart city innovation.
- Collaboration agreements between academic, public, and private entities facilitated deployment[^2][^3][^4].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

**Primary Goals:**
- Provide **real-time, spatially resolved GHG and air quality data** to inform and evaluate urban climate policy[^2][^3][^5].
- Identify **emissions hotspots** and temporal patterns to enable targeted interventions.
- Demonstrate the effectiveness of **data-driven climate action** at the city scale.

**Vision Statement:**
- To establish a **scientific infrastructure** enabling Dublin to become a climate neutral, healthy, and resilient city through actionable, evidence-based policy.

**Alignment with Broader Plans:**
- Integral to Dublin’s **EU Cities Mission** roadmap.
- Supports national and EU climate and biodiversity targets[^2][^5].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

**Theory of Change:**
- Deploy low-cost sensors → Generate real-time, high-resolution data → Visualize and analyze emissions patterns → Inform and test policy interventions → **Reduce emissions, improve air quality, enhance public engagement**[^2][^3][^5].

**Target Beneficiaries:**
- **Policymakers:** Evidence for effective climate action.
- **Urban planners:** Insights for sustainable design and planning.
- **Public:** Cleaner air, transparency, and opportunities for engagement.
- **Academic community:** Rich data for research and innovation.

**Innovation Elements:**
- Use of **low-cost, edge-computing sensors** for widespread deployment.
- Integration with city telecom infrastructure (mobile phone masts)[^1][^2][^4].
- Real-time data visualization for broad accessibility.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** Ireland’s climate emergency declaration, EU Cities Mission participation[^1][^2].
- **Economic:** Potential for green tech innovation, efficient resource allocation.
- **Social:** Public demand for cleaner air, transparency, and participation in climate action.
- **Environmental:** Urgent need to reduce GHG emissions, improve air quality, and meet legal targets.
- **Technological:** Advances in low-cost environmental sensors, IoT networks, and cloud computing[^2][^4].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

**Conceptual Framework:**
- Systems-based, integrating **urban atmospheric science, smart city technologies, and participatory governance**[^2][^5].

**Physical/Spatial Design:**
- 20 sensors (“lunch box” sized) mounted on existing **mobile phone masts** in diverse locations (residential, commercial, traffic corridors)[^1][^2][^3][^4].
- Minimal disruption to infrastructure; leverages existing telecom power and connectivity.

**Scope and Phasing:**
- One-year pilot (2024–2025) as proof-of-concept; scope for expansion depending on results[^1][^2][^4].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

**Technologies Deployed:**
- **Low-cost climate sensors** (measure CO₂, methane, PM, NOx, SO₂, weather variables)[^2][^4].
- **Edge computing** for local, accurate real-time measurements.
- **LoRa wireless network** for data transmission.
- **Azure cloud backend** for data storage, processing, and visualization[^2][^4].

**Innovation Type:**
- Primarily **technological** (sensor, IoT, cloud); also **governance-related** (multi-actor partnership) and **process-oriented** (data-driven policy).

**Integration Approach:**
- Sensors integrated into existing **city telecom infrastructure**.
- Data feeds into Terrain-AI models for emissions analysis and visualization[^2][^3][^5].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

**Procurement and Contracting:**
- Sensors developed by Edgeliot (DCU spinout); deployment in partnership with Cellnex and Delmec.
- Academic and city partners collaborated under formal agreements[^2][^4].

**Stakeholder Engagement:**
- Led by Dublin City Council and universities, with regular updates to policymakers.
- Public engagement through open data visualizations and media coverage.

**Participatory Elements:**
- Emphasis on **public visibility** of emissions data; potential for community feedback in future phases.

**Project Management:**
- Coordinated by Terrain-AI (Maynooth University), with project management shared among partners.

#### **Financing Model**

**Budget and Costs:**
- Low-cost sensors (<€1,000 each) make city-scale deployment feasible; compare to traditional flux towers (~€250,000)[^4].

**Funding Mix:**
- **Microsoft Ireland**: principal funder.
- **Science Foundation Ireland**, **Enterprise Ireland**: research and commercialization support[^1][^2][^4].
- In-kind contributions from Cellnex (infrastructure), Delmec (deployment), and the city.

**Revenue Mechanisms:**
- No direct revenue; public good model.

**Financial Sustainability:**
- Pilot intended to establish cost-effectiveness and catalyze broader, potentially self-sustaining rollout[^4].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:** Sensor accuracy and calibration, ensuring reliable data across diverse microclimates[^1][^2][^4].
- **Financial:** Limited initial scale due to budget; need to demonstrate value for future investment.
- **Social and Political:** Ensuring data is trusted and used effectively by policymakers; engaging the public meaningfully.
- **Administrative:** Coordinating multiple institutions and infrastructure partners.
- **Environmental:** Ensuring that sensors are protected from weather and vandalism.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Iterative calibration and cross-validation with established monitoring stations[^4].
- **Design Modifications:** Pilot phase allows for relocation of sensors and technology adjustments as needed.
- **Lessons Learned:** Early feedback underscored the importance of robust, low-maintenance hardware and strong data management protocols[^2][^4].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Low-cost, scalable technology** enables citywide emissions monitoring that was previously cost-prohibitive[^4].
- **Real-time, public data** empowers both policymakers and citizens.
- **Multi-sector partnership** leverages diverse expertise and resources[^1][^2].
- **Direct alignment with ambitious climate goals** positions Dublin as a European leader in urban climate innovation.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Pilot scale:** 20 sensors may not capture all microclimates or sources; expansion needed for comprehensive coverage.
- **Sustainability:** Ongoing funding and institutional commitment are required for long-term impact.
- **Sensor calibration and data quality:** Ensuring consistent, reliable measurements remains a technical challenge.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Positive:** Open data supports equitable policy; potential to identify and address environmental inequalities.
- **Risks:** If not expanded, some communities could remain under-monitored; equitable sensor distribution is essential.

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Expansion potential:** Success could justify citywide or national rollout.
- **Integration:** Opportunities to link with other smart city and climate data platforms.
- **Ongoing challenges:** Securing long-term funding, refining technology, deepening public engagement.
- **Next steps:** Evaluate pilot results, refine deployment, seek additional partners and funding for scaling[^1][^2][^4].
