# Executive summary## Lyon Confluence Smart Mobility and Energy District

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Lyon Confluence Smart Mobility and Energy District
- **Alternative Names:** Lyon Confluence Green Urban Mobility Neighbourhood; Lyon Smart Community; Lyon-Confluence Sustainable District; ASCEND Demo Site Lyon-Confluence
- **Location:** Lyon, Confluence District, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.7436° N, 4.8197° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Initiation: 2000  
  - Major Mobility and Energy Demonstrations: 2011–2016 (Smart Community Demonstration), 2019–2024 (ASCEND, Smarter Together, ongoing)  
  - Completion/Ongoing Status: District transformation ongoing, with key phases completed and further work planned through 2030[^1][^2][^3][^4].
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: 150 hectares (entire Confluence project); Smart Mobility & Energy pilot: 33 ha demo area[^1][^2]
  - Population affected: Over 12,000 residents, 15,000 employees, millions of annual visitors[^5]
  - Investment Value: Not fully specified, but includes significant public and private investments, with co-funding from EU programs (Smarter Together, ASCEND, etc.)
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - SPL Lyon Confluence (public urban development company created by Lyon Métropole)
  - Lyon Métropole (Greater Lyon)
  - Multiple private partners (Bouygues Immobilier, Toshiba, Panasonic, Kengo Kuma & Associates, others)
  - EU project consortia (ASCEND, Smarter Together)[^2][^3]
- **Funding Sources:** Mixed model:  
  - Public (Lyon Métropole, national, EU funding)
  - Private (developers, technology providers)
  - Public-private partnerships
  - International aid (notably Japanese NEDO for Smart Community Demonstration)[^2][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**

The Lyon Confluence Smart Mobility and Energy District is a pioneering urban regeneration and smart city initiative in southern Lyon, France, transforming a former industrial wasteland into a vibrant, sustainable neighbourhood. Encompassing 150 hectares, the project is a leading European demonstration site for integrating green mobility, renewable energy, digital platforms, and participatory governance[^1][^2][^3][^4]. Core to its strategy is the deployment of a centralized mobility and energy management platform that coordinates car sharing, electric vehicle (EV) charging, and demand-responsive transport, woven into a broader vision of green urban living. 

The initiative leverages advanced technologies, including IoT sensors, digital twins, open-data APIs, and smart grids, to reduce private car use, maximize the utility of shared EV fleets, and optimize local energy supply and consumption. By 2028, new and retrofitted high-performance buildings, greened public spaces, and smart energy systems will anchor the district, supporting ambitious goals to double the size of central Lyon without increasing greenhouse gas emissions[^1][^2][^6]. Notable outcomes include a 40% modal shift to shared or electric mobility, optimized infrastructure investments, and measurable reductions in emissions and energy use[^3]. The governance model—anchored in a public-private partnership and iterative stakeholder engagement—has enabled the district to serve as a living lab for sustainable urbanism, with lessons relevant for cities worldwide.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - Lyon is France’s third-largest city, a major economic and cultural hub, and a key node within the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region.
  - Population: ~500,000 in the city proper; over 2 million in the metropolitan area.
  - Economic base: Diversified—industry, banking, biotech, digital, and creative sectors.
  - Geography: Located at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers, with the Confluence district at the southern tip of Lyon’s Presqu’île[^2][^6].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - Historical Development: Confluence was historically an industrial and logistical area with extensive rail yards, warehouses, and brownfield sites.
  - Land Use Composition: The regeneration project is mixed-use, combining residential (1,347 new dwellings, 55% social housing), office, retail, schools, and green/public spaces[^1][^2].
  - Demographics: Diverse, with an emphasis on social mix and affordable housing. Residents include a mix of ages, incomes, and backgrounds, partly due to explicit social housing targets[^1].
  - Infrastructure Quality: Previously degraded, now undergoing major upgrades in energy, mobility, and public realm.
  - Environmental Conditions: Site faced significant contamination, impermeable soils, and exposure to flooding; now subject to major remediation, greening, and climate adaptation efforts[^1][^2][^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Severely underutilized, polluted industrial land.
  - Traffic congestion, high car dependency, and inadequate sustainable transport.
  - Poor energy efficiency, limited renewable energy, and high emissions.
  - Fragmented, outdated infrastructure; social and economic isolation[^2][^3].
- **Triggering Events:**
  - Strategic decision in 2000 to regenerate the district as part of Lyon’s broader urban renewal.
  - Policy shifts emphasizing climate action and sustainable development (local, national, EU level).
  - Opportunity for EU and international demonstration funding (notably Japanese collaboration)[^2][^3].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Public: Lyon Métropole, City of Lyon, regional and national agencies.
  - Private: Developers, technology firms, mobility providers.
  - Civil Society: Residents, environmental groups, business associations.
  - International: EU project partners, Japanese NEDO, research institutes[^2][^3].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - SPL Lyon Confluence—a semi-public development company with public shareholders—manages planning, land control, construction, and consultation[^2].
  - Oversight by Lyon Métropole and integration into metropolitan and national strategies.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - French urban sustainability and eco-district policies (including eco-district label).
  - EU frameworks for smart cities, energy transition, and sustainable mobility (Horizon 2020, ASCEND, Smarter Together)[^1][^2][^4].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Public land control and strong cross-sector partnerships enabled rapid progress.
  - Complexities included aligning diverse regulatory requirements for energy, mobility, and data management across jurisdictions.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Transform a former industrial area into a model sustainable district.
  - Integrate green mobility, renewable energy, and smart technologies.
  - Double Lyon’s city centre without increasing GHG emissions[^2][^3].
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - To create "the city of tomorrow," demonstrating how digital, environmental, and social innovation can be integrated to support inclusive, low-carbon urban living[^5][^2][^6].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Fits within Lyon Métropole’s climate, air, and energy plans, the French national energy transition, and the EU’s smart city and sustainability strategies[^7][^4].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - By combining digital management, energy innovation, and mobility solutions, the initiative aims to reduce emissions, improve quality of life, and create a template for future urban regeneration[^2][^3].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents, workers, businesses, and visitors—especially those reliant on affordable housing and sustainable mobility[^1][^2].
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Digital twin and real-time dashboards for urban management.
  - Community-level energy management and prosumer models.
  - Integrated car sharing, EV infrastructure, and demand-responsive transport[^1][^3].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Strong mayoral and metropolitan leadership, with sustainability as a core agenda.
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Revitalization of derelict land, investment attraction, and support for the green economy[^2].
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Promotion of social mix, equity, and participatory urban governance[^5].
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Response to climate change, air quality, and resource depletion[^2][^7].
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Leverage of IoT, big data, digital twins, and renewable technologies[^1][^6][^3].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Guided by eco-district principles, circular economy, and smart city paradigms.
  - Emphasis on mixed-use, low-carbon, resilient urban form[^5][^2][^8].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Major new and retrofitted buildings (offices, housing, public facilities).
  - Greening and pedestrianization of public spaces, integration of blue-green infrastructure.
  - Deployment of smart energy and mobility infrastructure[^1][^2].
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phased over decades (2000–2030), with pilot/demonstration phases for smart mobility and energy (2011–2016, 2016–2024)[^1][^3][^4].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Centralized digital platform for real-time mobility and energy management.
  - IoT sensors, open-data APIs, district digital twin.
  - Smart charging points, vehicle-to-building (V2B) integration.
  - Energy-efficient building systems (no active heating/cooling, BEMS).
  - Photovoltaics, district heating from biomass, CHP (combined heat and power).
  - Community Management System for energy data.
  - Resident interfaces for energy and mobility services[^1][^6][^3].
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digital platforms, smart grids).
  - Social (co-design, prosumer energy communities).
  - Institutional (public-private partnership, living lab model)[^1][^6][^3].
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Layered integration: mobility, energy, and data services interconnected via digital infrastructure and coordinated governance[^1][^6][^3].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - SPL Lyon Confluence led competitive selection of developers and technology providers[^2].
  - EU-funded demonstration projects involved international consortia.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Regular public consultations, co-design workshops, living labs.
  - Inclusion of residents, local businesses, and civic organizations[^1][^2].
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Citizen and stakeholder co-design of public spaces, energy communities, and service interfaces[^1].
- **Project Management:**  
  - SPL Lyon Confluence as lead coordinator, supported by thematic task groups, digital dashboards, and cross-sector working groups[^2][^3].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not fully itemized; major investments in infrastructure, digital systems, and public spaces, with significant EU project budgets (Smarter Together, ASCEND).
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Local public investment, land sales, French and international grants, private capital.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - User fees for mobility and energy services, value capture from land development.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Ongoing through mixed-use revenues, cost savings from efficiency, and reinvestment from public-private partnerships[^2].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Coordination between diverse mobility providers.
  - Integration of new digital platforms with legacy energy/mobility infrastructure.
  - Ensuring interoperability of IoT and data systems[^3].
  - Technical hurdles in building PEBs (Positive Energy Buildings) and custom components (e.g., transparent PV facades)[^9][^3].
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Managing budget overruns and aligning multi-source funding.
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Achieving buy-in from residents wary of new technologies.
  - Ensuring equitable access to benefits, preventing gentrification.
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Navigating complex regulatory and procurement environments.
  - Coordination among numerous project partners and stakeholder groups[^2][^3].
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Remediation of polluted land (brownfields).
  - Managing flood risk, soil permeability, and climate adaptation[^1][^2].

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Piloting new solutions in demonstration blocks before scaling district-wide.
  - Iterative project management, with feedback loops from users and partners.
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusting infrastructure plans based on real-world usage data.
  - Adding or relocating EV charging stations per actual demand patterns[^3].
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of flexibility and modularity in digital and physical infrastructure.
  - Value of sustained stakeholder involvement and transparent communication[^1][^3].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - 1 million m² of new construction/renovation, including 1,347 new dwellings (55% social housing)[^1][^2].
  - 500,000 m² of high-performance buildings, 70,000 m² of energy-renovated buildings.
  - Over 2 MW of installed PV capacity; district heating network expansion[^2][^6].
  - Deployment of digital management platforms, smart mobility hubs, and EV charging points[^1][^3].
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Real-time dashboard interfaces for residents and staff.
  - Car sharing, EV fleet management, demand-responsive transport[^3].
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 40% of local trips shifted to shared or electric mobility.
  - Public transport now represents 22% of journeys in the area (2020)[^7].
  - Significant reductions in energy use (buildings consume 50–60% less than conventional)[^6].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved mobility access, cleaner air, quieter streets.
  - Enhanced quality of life, social mix, and public space vitality[^5][^1][^6].
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - High proportion of social housing, efforts to ensure benefits reach all groups[^1].
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - Measures in place to promote social mix, though long-term pressures remain a concern.
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Civic engagement fostered through co-design and living lab activities.
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Preservation of heritage, activation of creative and cultural programs in public spaces.

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Direct employment in construction, technology, and operations.
  - Indirect jobs through business attraction and urban regeneration[^2].
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Revitalization of a formerly declining area; increased commercial and retail activity.
- **Property Values:**  
  - Rising values and demand, reflecting improved environment and amenities.
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Energy savings for residents and businesses; efficiencies in urban management.
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Efforts to ensure affordable housing and local hiring.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - No net increase in GHG emissions despite doubling city centre size.
  - Measurable reductions in local air pollution and transport emissions[^2][^7].
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Efficient water, energy, and material use; circular economy pilots[^5][^1].
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Expanded green space, biodiversity, and urban cooling via tree planting[^1][^7].
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Remediation of brownfield sites; improved waste management systems.
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Eco-district label; performance tracked against national/EU indicators[^7].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened local government capabilities in smart city management.
  - New collaborative mechanisms across public, private, and civic sectors[^2].
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for French and European eco-districts and smart city initiatives.
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Real-time data and dashboards foster accountability and resident trust[^1][^3].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - High achievement on modal shift, energy savings, and emissions reduction.
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Some challenges with technology adoption and long-term affordability.
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Strong prospects for sustainability, with ongoing adaptation and scaling[^1][^2].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Comprehensive integration** of mobility, energy, and digital systems.
- **Strong governance** via public-private partnership, enabling flexibility and innovation.
- **Demonstrated results** in modal shift, emissions reduction, and quality of life improvements.
- **Living lab approach** fosters ongoing experimentation and iterative improvement[^5][^1][^2].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity and cost**: High technical and administrative complexity, with significant upfront investment needs.
- **Social equity risks**: Ongoing need to manage affordability and prevent displacement.
- **Technology adoption**: Some resistance or slow uptake among certain resident groups[^3].
- **Long-term sustainability**: Potential challenges in ensuring ongoing funding and adaptability as technologies and needs evolve.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Social mix and affordability** explicitly addressed via social housing quotas and participatory governance.
- **Efforts to mitigate displacement** and ensure broad access to benefits.
- **Ongoing monitoring** needed to address emerging equity gaps as district matures.

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory**: Continued evolution as part of Lyon’s smart city and climate action strategy.
- **Ongoing challenges**: Sustaining inclusive growth, integrating new technologies, managing urban change.
- **Next phases**: Expansion of digital twin applications, further greening and pedestrianization, and scaling of mobility/energy innovations to adjacent districts and the metropolitan region[^1][^2][^3].
