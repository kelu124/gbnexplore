# Executive summary## Praça de Lisboa Olive Grove Green Roof & Porto Fifth Façade Project

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Praça de Lisboa Olive Grove Green Roof & Porto Fifth Façade Project (PQAP)
- **Alternative Names:** Jardim das Oliveiras, Praça de Lisboa Revitalization, Fifth Façade Project (PQAP)
- **Location:** Praça de Lisboa, Baixa do Porto, Porto, Portugal
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 41.1438° N, 8.6139° W
- **Timeline:** 
  - Praça de Lisboa Revitalization: 2007 (competition launch), 2011–2013 (construction), 2013 (public opening)
  - Porto Fifth Façade Project (PQAP): August 2016 (launch), ongoing
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Praça de Lisboa: 4,500 m² green roof, 50 olive trees, underground car park, retail/commercial spaces
  - PQAP: Citywide strategy, covering multiple districts and pilot projects across Porto
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - Praça de Lisboa: Balonas & Menano (architects), Neoturf (green roof consulting), Capital Urbano (developer), BragaParques (contractor), City of Porto (landlord)
  - PQAP: Portuguese National Association for Green Roofs (ANCV), Porto City Council, universities, research institutions, European Federation of Green Roofs and Walls Associations, World Green Infrastructure Network
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - Praça de Lisboa: Private investment (Capital Urbano), municipal support (City of Porto)
  - PQAP: Municipal funding, research grants, international partnerships, private sector collaboration

#### **Executive Summary**
The Praça de Lisboa Olive Grove Green Roof and the Porto Fifth Façade Project (PQAP) represent a pioneering urban regeneration effort in Porto, Portugal. Praça de Lisboa, once a neglected and unsafe square, was transformed into a vibrant public space featuring a 4,500 m² green roof with 50 olive trees, local shops, cafes, and an underground car park. The project revitalized the historic city center, improved public amenities, and enhanced ecological resilience. The Porto Fifth Façade Project, launched in 2016, is a citywide strategy to mainstream green roofs in urban planning, aiming to integrate nature-based solutions into the city's environmental and urban strategy. The initiative has been recognized for its innovative approach, winning international awards and serving as a model for sustainable urban development in historic contexts. The project demonstrates the potential of green infrastructure to address urban challenges, foster community engagement, and promote climate resilience.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Porto is the second-largest city in Portugal, with a population of approximately 230,000 (2021). It is a major economic and cultural hub in the north of the country, known for its historic architecture, port wine, and vibrant urban life. The city is situated on the Douro River, with a mix of historic and modern neighborhoods.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Baixa do Porto:** The historic city center, characterized by narrow streets, historic buildings, and a dense urban fabric. The area has a rich cultural heritage, with landmarks such as the Clérigos Tower and Livraria Lello. The neighborhood is a mix of residential, commercial, and tourist uses, with a diverse demographic profile.
  - **Praça de Lisboa:** Located in the heart of Baixa do Porto, the square was historically a market and a transition space for the city. The area has faced challenges related to urban decay, overcrowding, and lack of green space.
- **Demographic Profile:** The neighborhood is home to a mix of residents, including long-time locals, students, and tourists. The area has a relatively high income level, but also faces issues of social inequality and gentrification.
- **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:** The area has good public transportation, but the historic urban fabric presents challenges for accessibility and modern infrastructure.
- **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** The city faces issues related to urban heat island effects, air pollution, and stormwater management. The historic context also presents constraints for large-scale infrastructure projects.
