# Executive summary## Ella House High-Efficiency HVAC Retrofit

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Ella House High-Efficiency HVAC Retrofit
- **Alternative Names:** Ella House Mechanical Refit; Ella House Office Energy Upgrade
- **Location:** Dublin, Merrion Square, Ireland
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.3391° N, 6.2518° W (Merrion Square)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project commencement: Early 2022  
  - Key milestones: Mechanical plant strip-out, phased installation, tenant fit-out synchronisation  
  - Completion: Late 2022 (ongoing minor works possible)
- **Project Scale:**  
  - 46,000 sq ft office venue  
  - Facilities: 55 WCs, 8 showers, multiple flexible office units, dedicated IT/comms rooms  
  - Population affected: Hundreds of daily office users, multiple tenants
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Platmech Ltd. (mechanical engineering and installation)  
  - Sonbrook Property (developer/owner)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Private funding (property owner/developer)  
  - Tenant contributions (fit-out costs)  
  - Possible green financing (sustainability upgrades; not explicitly documented)

#### **Executive Summary**
Ella House, a landmark flexible office building in Dublin’s Merrion Square, underwent a transformative mechanical retrofit aimed at drastically improving energy efficiency, indoor comfort, and sustainability performance. The project involved the removal of outdated HVAC and water systems and the installation of high-efficiency heat pumps, advanced heat-recovery ventilation (HRV), and pressure-balanced water networks. Special consideration was given to the needs of IT rooms and end-of-journey facilities for tenants, with dedicated air conditioning and upgraded water storage.

The phased approach ensured minimal disruption to ongoing tenancy and new fit-outs, with the installation schedule tailored to tenant move-in milestones. The retrofit’s sustainability strategy focused on real-time HVAC optimization, enhanced occupant control, and data-driven energy management. By integrating HRV units and high-performance heat pumps, the building significantly reduced its operational energy demand and improved indoor environmental quality.

Outcomes included lowered utility costs, improved workplace health and productivity, and a future-proofed building infrastructure capable of adapting to evolving sustainability standards. Ella House now serves as a model for green office refurbishment in historic urban contexts, balancing modern efficiency with tenant comfort and operational flexibility[^1][^2].
