# Executive summary## Casa Sophia: Automating Spain’s Most Eco-Friendly Home

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Casa Sophia: Automating Spain’s Most Eco-Friendly Home
- **Alternative Names:** Casa Sophia, La Vivienda Más Sostenible de España
- **Location:** Marbella, Province of Málaga, Andalusia, Spain
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 36.5101° N, 4.8824° W (Marbella city center; actual site coordinates may vary)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Conception and planning began: 2021
  - Construction period: 2022–2023
  - Commissioning/Completion: Early 2024 (positive energy status achieved February 2022)[^1][^2][^3]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Single-family detached home
  - Site includes main residence, landscaped gardens, vertical and green roofs, irrigation zones
  - Investment value not disclosed; indicative of high-end green home market in Spain
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - Developer and builder: 100x100BioPasiva
  - Automation system: Loxone
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - Private investment by homeowner(s)
  - No direct public or grant funding reported

#### **Executive Summary**
Casa Sophia represents a cutting-edge model for sustainable residential development in Europe, designed and built to be Spain’s most eco-friendly home. Located in Marbella, Casa Sophia integrates biopassive construction principles—combining bio-construction methodologies with the rigorous energy efficiency standards of Passivhaus Premium certification. The home leverages a Loxone automation system for holistic building management, covering energy production (14 kW solar PV), storage (virtual battery), smart consumption (lighting, HVAC, EV charging, irrigation), and health-oriented features such as advanced air quality monitoring.

With continuous real-time monitoring, the system dynamically balances energy flows, supports predictive and efficient water use, manages indoor climate, and maintains security. Casa Sophia achieved “positive home” status, generating more energy annually than it consumes, and received the top-level GREEN GBCe Certificate (5 leaves, 92% score) from the Green Building Council España[^1][^2]. The project demonstrates how digitalization, automation, and bioclimatic design can maximize occupant well-being, minimize environmental impact, and serve as a replicable model for sustainable urban living in Mediterranean climates.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - Casa Sophia is situated in a suburban or peri-urban area of Marbella, typified by low-density residential development.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** 
  - Bioclimatic architecture, Passivhaus Premium, bio-construction (natural, non-toxic, and recycled materials), digitalization of building operations.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Single-family detached home with optimized orientation, super-insulated envelope, high-performance glazing, green/vertical roofs, integrated gardens.
  - Multi-zoned irrigation and water recycling systems.
- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Design and geobiological site study
  - Material selection and passive envelope construction
  - Installation of PV, automation, and smart controls
  - Commissioning, monitoring, and certification[^1][^2]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - **Loxone Miniserver**: central brain for automation
  - Sensors: humidity, presence, air quality (MICA by InBiot), weather station
  - Smart meters, 14 kW solar PV array, virtual battery storage
  - Automated blinds and shading, LED lighting (24V, low EMF)
  - Smart irrigation, bio-depurator for greywater/rainwater reuse, electric vehicle charging management[^1][^2]
- **Innovation Type:** 
  - Technological (automation, digitalization), environmental (bio-construction, resource cycling), process (holistic integration)
- **Integration Approach:** 
  - All subsystems (energy, water, HVAC, lighting, security) unified via Loxone system with user interface for visualization and control.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** 
  - Specialist builder (100x100BioPasiva) with in-house expertise in bio-construction and Passivhaus.
  - Loxone-certified integrators for automation system[^1].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** 
  - Project tailored to homeowner(s) as primary client.
  - No reported participatory process with wider community, typical for single-family home.
- **Participatory Elements:** 
  - Residents involved in customization and data use; ongoing visualization of performance via app.
- **Project Management:** 
  - Close collaboration between builder, automation provider, and client, with iterative commissioning.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** 
  - Not publicly disclosed; reflects premium segment of green building market in Spain.
- **Funding Mix:** 
  - 100% private investment.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** 
  - Cost savings from energy and water efficiency; reduced insurance costs[^1]; potential premium on property value.
- **Financial Sustainability:** 
  - Ongoing utility savings, minimized maintenance costs, and reduced insurance contribute to lifecycle cost-effectiveness.

  - High upfront investment typical for demonstration projects at the technological frontier.
- **Social and Political Challenges:** 
  - No significant community or political opposition reported.
- **Administrative Challenges:** 
  - Navigating certification processes (Passivhaus, GREEN GBCe), which require extensive documentation and performance validation.
- **Environmental Challenges:** 
  - Ensuring water resilience in drought-prone region; sourcing sustainable materials.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** 
  - Close collaboration between builder, automation integrator, and homeowner; iterative testing and commissioning.
  - Use of predictive sensors and automation to maximize efficiency and user simplicity.
- **Design Modifications:** 
  - Integration of two HVAC systems (ventilation-based and underfloor heating/cooling) for optimal comfort and energy performance[^1].
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:** 
  - Importance of holistic system design and real-time data for achieving both performance and user satisfaction.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- Achieved the highest level of green building certification in Spain (GREEN GBCe, Passivhaus Premium)[^1][^2].
- Net positive energy performance—model for climate mitigation.
- Holistic integration of health, comfort, and sustainability.
- Real-time data and user-friendly visualization support informed occupant engagement.
- Demonstrates feasibility and ambition for green building in Mediterranean markets.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- High upfront costs restrict immediate accessibility for average households.
- Complexity may require ongoing technical support and limits replicability without skilled integrators.
- Single-family format does not address broader urban housing needs.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Advances environmental justice through non-toxic, healthy environments, but equity limited by cost and exclusivity.
- No evidence of negative displacement; however, the model is currently accessible mostly to affluent groups.

#### **Future Outlook**
- Casa Sophia is likely to serve as a demonstration and learning platform for the Spanish and European green building sector.
- Ongoing performance monitoring will provide valuable data for future refinement.
- Future phases could include adaptation for multifamily, affordable, or urban infill projects, with a focus on cost reduction and simplified user interfaces.
- Anticipated evolution toward integration with broader neighborhood or district-scale smart grids and resilience strategies.
