# Executive summary

## Sharing Cities: Interoperable Digital Twins for Smart Energy Neighbourhoods

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Sharing Cities: Interoperable Digital Twins for Smart Energy Neighbourhoods
- **Alternative Names:** Sharing Cities, Horizon 2020 Smart Cities Lighthouse Project
- **Location:** Milan, Italy (Porta Romana/Vettabbia District); pilots in London (UK), Lisbon (Portugal), with replication in Bordeaux (France), Burgas (Bulgaria), Warsaw (Poland)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Milan (45.4642° N, 9.19° E)
- **Timeline:** January 2016 – December 2021 (main implementation: 2016–2020; monitoring: 2021)
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Milan demonstration: ~216,614 m² brownfield redevelopment, 187,226 m² park  
  - Overall: >100 municipalities engaged across Europe  
  - Investment: €24–25 million EU funding; €8.6 million for Milan; €265 million additional investment leveraged
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Milan: Comune di Milano, Politecnico di Milano (DASTU, DENG), Fondazione Politecnico di Milano, Poliedra, Siemens, ATM, A2A Smart City, and others  
  - European: Greater London Authority, Municipality of Lisbon, EuroCities, Imperial College London, and more[^1][^2]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020 (Smart Cities and Communities SCC1)  
  - City government  
  - Private sector partners  
  - International consortia[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**
The Sharing Cities initiative, funded under Horizon 2020, represents one of the most ambitious pan-European efforts to deploy smart city solutions at district scale, with Milan as a key Lighthouse City. The project pioneered the use of interoperable digital twins—virtual models integrating IoT sensor data, energy systems, mobility networks, and public space information—across neighbourhoods, enabling city managers and communities to monitor, optimize, and co-create sustainable urban environments. In Milan, the focus was on transforming the Porta Romana/Vettabbia district from a disconnected brownfield site into a model for smart, low-carbon urban living. Integration of building retrofits, shared e-mobility, smart lighting, and an urban sharing platform allowed for real-time data-driven management and citizen engagement. Over six years, Sharing Cities demonstrated tangible reductions in energy consumption (up to 70% in retrofitted buildings), improved mobility (with expanded e-bike and EV sharing networks), and engaged thousands of residents in digital social markets incentivizing sustainable behavior. The approach—centered on interoperability, open data, and collaborative governance—has proven replicable, with techniques and technologies transferred to follower cities and over 100 municipalities. The project’s impact extends to policy innovation, market transformation (over €265 million in additional investment), and new models for citizen empowerment, positioning Milan and its partners at the forefront of urban climate action and smart city development in Europe[^1][^4][^2][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Milan, Italy’s second largest city, is a major economic hub with a metropolitan population of over 3 million. It is renowned for finance, industry, fashion, and research, and sits at the heart of Lombardy, one of Europe's wealthiest regions. Milan faces typical metropolitan challenges: air pollution, traffic congestion, and growing energy demand, but has positioned itself as a leader in sustainability and innovation[^4].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Porta Romana/Vettabbia:** Historically a railway yard and agricultural belt, the area was a brownfield site disconnected from Milan’s urban core.  
  - **Land Use:** Planned mix of private/social housing, multimodal mobility hub, and large urban park.  
  - **Demographics:** The district includes diverse populations—middle/lower-income, aging population, some migrant communities.  
  - **Infrastructure:** Prior to intervention, the area suffered from poor energy performance, limited transport connectivity, and underutilized public space.  
  - **Environmental Constraints:** High carbon emissions from buildings and transport, legacy soil contamination, limited green space[^4][^2].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Inefficient building stock, high energy use and emissions  
  - Traffic congestion, limited sustainable transport options  
  - Poor air quality  
  - Social fragmentation due to physical and economic separation  
  - Lack of integrated data for urban management

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU climate and energy directives  
  - Milan’s adoption of new city plans for mobility and sustainability (since 2012)  
  - Availability of Horizon 2020 funding  
  - Local pressure for brownfield redevelopment[^4][^2]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government, local energy and mobility providers, research institutions, private tech companies, community organizations, residents.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by Milan’s municipality with strong interdepartmental collaboration  
  - Supported by EU regulatory frameworks for energy efficiency, mobility, and smart city data

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Milan’s urban development, sustainable mobility, and energy efficiency plans  
  - European Smart Cities and Communities (SCC1) strategy

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - EU procurement rules required open, competitive processes  
  - Data privacy regulations (GDPR) shaped digital platform design  
  - Existing legacy systems posed integration challenges[^1][^4]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Ecosystem-based urban design (integrating energy, mobility, public space)  
  - “Citizen-centered” smart city philosophy  
  - Collaborative governance, open innovation[^4][^2]

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Retrofit of 20 buildings (deep energy renovation, insulation, digital controls)  
  - 60 electric car charging stations, 14 e-bike sharing points  
  - Smart lamp posts with LoRaWAN network and environmental sensors  
  - Redevelopment of brownfield into mixed-use housing and urban park[^1][^4]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Planning and stakeholder engagement  
  - Phase 2: Implementation (construction, technology deployment)  
  - Phase 3: Monitoring and scaling (replication in follower cities)[^1][^2]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Urban digital twin platform (real-time integrated data)  
  - IoT sensors for energy, mobility, and environmental monitoring  
  - Open data APIs and interoperability standards  
  - Smart street lighting and connected infrastructure  
  - Digital social market (SharingMi app)

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digital twins, IoT, data platforms)  
  - Social (participatory co-design, citizen engagement)  
  - Governance (collaborative networks, open standards)  
  - Financial (new business models for mobility and energy services)

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Urban Sharing Platform (USP) connecting multiple systems and stakeholders  
  - Incremental enhancement of legacy systems for interoperability  
  - Co-design with residents for building retrofits and service development[^4][^2]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive EU procurement process  
  - Partnerships with research institutions and private companies

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Citizen co-design workshops  
  - Digital social market platform for ongoing engagement  
  - Inclusion of local NGOs and business associations

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Residents involved in building retrofit specification  
  - Crowdsourcing of mobility and environmental solutions

- **Project Management:**  
  - Central coordination by Milan municipality and Fondazione Politecnico  
  - Consortium-based governance across European partners[^1][^4][^2]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - €24–25 million total EU funding  
  - €8.6 million for Milan demonstration  
  - Additional €265 million external investment

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Predominantly public (EU, local government) with private sector participation

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Mobility services (car-sharing, bike-sharing) user fees  
  - Potential land value capture from redevelopment

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Focus on replicable business models for ongoing service provision  
  - Market transformation to enable future investments[^2][^3]

  - New mobility and energy services

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Innovative integration** of digital twins, IoT, and open data for real-time urban management
- **Strong citizen engagement** and participatory co-design

- Limited evidence of displacement or negative equity impacts
