# Executive summary

## LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN: Falcão Primary School Green Roof

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN: Application of Nature-Based Solutions for Local Adaptation of Educational and Social Buildings to Climate Change (Falcão Primary School Green Roof)
- **Alternative Names:** Falcão School Green Roof Demonstration, LIFE-mBiG Porto Pilot
- **Location:** Falcão Primary School (Escola Básica do Falcão), Campanhã district, Porto, Portugal
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 41.1523° N, 8.5830° W
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Start: 2018  
  - NBS Installation at Falcão: 2021  
  - Initial Monitoring: 2022  
  - Ongoing “After-LIFE” monitoring and educational programming: 2023–2025[^1][^2][^3][^4]
- **Project Scale:**  
  - School green area: ~700 m² of green roofs/facades[^5][^6]  
  - Total NBS surface area (three schools): ~13,642 m²[^1]  
  - Population affected: ~200 students/staff (Falcão), plus broader educational/municipal community
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Project Coordinator: Spanish National Research Council (CSIC)  
  - Key Partners: Porto City Council (Câmara Municipal do Porto), CARTIF Foundation, CIMAC, DIPBA, Royal Botanical Garden of Madrid, among others[^1][^7][^8]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union LIFE Programme (LIFE17 CCA/ES/000088)  
  - Co-funding from project partners and local authorities[^1][^7]

#### **Executive Summary**
LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN is a pioneering European initiative focused on climate adaptation in educational and social service buildings using nature-based solutions (NBS). At Falcão Primary School in Porto, the project implemented integrated **green roofs, green facades, and solar photovoltaic panels** to demonstrate how school environments can be transformed to enhance **thermal comfort, energy efficiency, and biodiversity**. As a pilot for the region, this site offers a living laboratory for students, staff, policymakers, and researchers to observe and evaluate the effectiveness of NBS at the building scale.

The initiative was driven by the urgent need to **adapt urban infrastructure—especially schools serving vulnerable children—to the escalating impacts of climate change**, including extreme heat and changing precipitation patterns. Falcão School’s transformation involved retrofitting existing infrastructure, overcoming technical and administrative challenges, and embedding environmental education into the school curriculum. The project achieved measurable reductions in rooftop surface temperatures (over 5°C cooler than conventional roofs), improved indoor comfort, reduced rainwater runoff by 31%, and significant increases in local biodiversity, with more than 25 new plant and animal species observed post-implementation[^1][^9][^10][^6].

Beyond physical impacts, the initiative fostered **community engagement and environmental awareness**, with educational programs involving students in hands-on biodiversity monitoring and sustainability research. The project’s lessons, methods, and results are being disseminated locally and across Europe to encourage replication and inform policy, positioning Falcão Primary School as a model for climate-resilient, sustainable urban education environments[^1][^4][^11][^10].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

##### **City Profile**
Porto is Portugal’s second-largest city, with a population of ~230,000 (metro area ~1.7 million), and serves as a major regional economic, educational, and cultural hub. It is characterized by a temperate Atlantic climate with high humidity, moderate rainfall, and increasing risks from urban heat and climate variability. Porto is a leader in urban sustainability, with ambitious targets for green space expansion, biodiversity, and climate adaptation[^12].

##### **Neighborhood/District Characteristics**
Falcão Primary School is located in Campanhã, an eastern district of Porto with a mix of older residential, industrial, and emerging regeneration areas. Historically, this zone has faced challenges such as aging infrastructure, lower income levels, and limited access to green spaces, making it a priority for urban renewal and social investment. The school's catchment area includes a diverse student body, with many families from lower socioeconomic backgrounds.

Existing infrastructure was typical of mid-20th-century public schools: flat concrete roofs, limited thermal insulation, minimal landscaping, and high vulnerability to extreme weather. Prior to the initiative, outdoor play areas and indoor environments suffered from overheating in summer and poor comfort in winter[^13][^1].

##### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Excessive indoor temperatures during heatwaves, leading to discomfort and reduced learning performance  
  - High energy costs for heating/cooling  
  - Lack of natural green space and shade  
  - Low biodiversity and limited student exposure to nature  
  - Stormwater runoff and urban flooding risk
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Recognition of increased climate risks to vulnerable populations (children)  
  - EU policy priorities on climate adaptation and nature-based solutions  
  - Opportunity for LIFE Programme funding
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - School authorities and staff (concerned with comfort, cost, and learning)  
  - Municipal environmental and education departments  
  - Students and local families  
  - Researchers and NBS advocates  
  - EU LIFE Programme as funder and knowledge broker

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Project led by Porto City Council’s Environment and Education departments, in partnership with international research institutions and NGOs[^1][^7][^8]
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Porto Municipal Master Plan (aims to double public green space, promote NBS)[^12]  
  - EU Climate Adaptation Strategy  
  - National and local regulations for school safety, public procurement, and construction
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive city policy environment for green infrastructure  
  - Technical standards for retrofit interventions on public buildings  
  - Some bureaucratic complexity in cross-sectoral, multi-stakeholder projects

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Increase resilience of educational/social buildings to climate change  
  - Improve thermal comfort and indoor environmental quality  
  - Demonstrate and assess the effectiveness of NBS in real-world settings  
  - Promote biodiversity and reduce energy use[^1][^3][^14]
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - “To make educational buildings living examples of climate adaptation and sustainability, improving wellbeing, learning, and resilience for current and future generations.”
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Supports Porto’s urban greening and climate adaptation strategies  
  - Contributes to EU goals on sustainable, inclusive, and resilient cities

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:**  
  - By retrofitting school buildings with green roofs/facades and solar PV, the project aimed to passively reduce heat gain, increase insulation, manage rainwater, and create microhabitats. These interventions would lower energy demand, improve comfort, and boost biodiversity, while also providing educational value.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Primary: Students and staff at Falcão School  
  - Secondary: Local community, municipal authorities, policy community, wider network of schools
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Combining NBS and renewable energy as integrated retrofit  
  - Monitoring and research on performance metrics  
  - Embedding NBS into school curriculum

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Municipal commitment to climate adaptation, EU sustainability frameworks
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Desire to reduce operational costs and future-proof infrastructure
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Improving health, comfort, and educational outcomes for children
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Urgency of climate adaptation, biodiversity conservation
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Advances in green roofing technology, solar PV integration, urban ecological monitoring

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Based on nature-based solutions (NBS) principles: leveraging vegetation and ecosystem processes to deliver climate adaptation, environmental, and social benefits[^1][^7]
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Retrofitting flat concrete school roof with extensive green roof system (~700 m²)[^5][^6]  
  - Green facades installed on suitable building elevations  
  - Solar photovoltaic panels for renewable energy  
  - Associated landscaping, rainwater management, and biodiversity features[^9][^10]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Pilot phase: site selection, baseline assessment  
  - Design/engineering: adapting NBS to existing structure  
  - Construction/installation: 2021  
  - Initial monitoring: 2022  
  - Ongoing After-LIFE monitoring and education: 2023–2025

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Extensive green roof system (multi-layer substrate, drainage, drought-tolerant native plants)  
  - Green facade trellis with climbing plants  
  - Solar PV panels integrated with green roof  
  - Bio-indicator-based biodiversity monitoring (students and researchers)[^1][^15][^10][^6]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (NBS, PV, monitoring), social (education, engagement), governance (cross-sectoral partnership)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Retrofit coordinated to minimize disruption, ensure compatibility with existing roof/façade  
  - Systems designed for low maintenance and long-term durability

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Public tender for construction/installation  
  - Engagement of local contractors and NBS suppliers
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Workshops with school staff, parents, and students  
  - Coordination between municipal departments, research partners, and EU project office[^1][^4][^10]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Student involvement in monitoring, education programs, and school-wide awareness campaigns
- **Project Management:**  
  - Managed by Porto City Council’s project team, with oversight from EU LIFE and coordination with research partners

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN project budget: ~€2.5 million (across three countries/pilot sites)[^1][^7]
  - Falcão School share: Not separately reported, but proportionate to scope
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - ~60% EU LIFE grant, ~40% local/partner co-funding
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - No direct revenue; operational savings from reduced energy use
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Low-maintenance design, municipal support for ongoing care, educational programming integrated into school budget

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Integrating green roof with existing structure (load, waterproofing)  
  - Ensuring plant survival in urban conditions  
  - Maintenance of vegetation and monitoring systems[^1][^10][^6]
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Upfront capital investment required; securing co-funding
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Ensuring buy-in from school community and parents  
  - Aligning project with school calendar to minimize disruption
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Navigating public procurement and multi-level project governance
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Selecting plant species resilient to local climate extremes and pollution

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Structural assessment and engineering for retrofit compatibility  
  - Use of drought-tolerant, native plant species  
  - Regular maintenance protocols and training for school staff
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments to roof/facade systems based on site-specific conditions
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Early engagement with school stakeholders critical  
  - Importance of robust monitoring to demonstrate value and address skepticism

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - 700 m² green roof and facades installed  
  - Solar PV system operational[^5][^6]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Improved thermal comfort in classrooms  
  - Enhanced outdoor learning and play environments
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Rooftop surface temperatures over 5°C cooler than conventional roofs[^1][^9]  
  - 31% reduction in rainwater runoff[^1]  
  - More than 25 new plant/animal species observed post-intervention[^1][^16][^10]  
  - Reduction in school energy consumption (preliminary: up to 11% for cooling in pilot schools)[^1]

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved comfort, health, and learning environment  
  - Hands-on environmental education for students  
  - Higher environmental awareness among families and local community[^4][^15][^11]
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Direct benefits to a school serving a lower-income area  
  - No displacement; inclusive access to improved green space
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - None reported
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened ties between school, families, and municipal authorities
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Enhanced identity of school as a “green” model for the city

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:**  
  - Local contractors and NBS suppliers engaged  
  - Indirect support for green economy and educational programming
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Modest but positive, through procurement and ongoing maintenance
- **Property Values:**  
  - No specific data, but urban greening often has positive effects locally
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Reduced energy costs for heating/cooling (preliminary results: 11–30% reduction in cooling/heating energy demand in pilot schools)[^1]
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Project targeted at a public school in a lower-income neighborhood

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Lowered energy use, indirect reduction in carbon emissions  
  - Increased local climate resilience
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Better rainwater retention, reduced runoff  
  - Use of renewable energy (solar PV)[^1][^10]
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality, local microclimate, and increased biodiversity  
  - More than 25 new species observed at the school site[^1][^16]
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Green roofs help filter air/water; no negative impacts reported
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Project monitored against EU sustainability standards (LIFE programme)

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened municipal experience with NBS in public buildings  
  - Model for future projects in Porto and beyond
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Informing new city policies on green roofs, school retrofits, and climate adaptation[^12]
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - EU project structures required open reporting, community engagement, and dissemination

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Most stated objectives achieved: improved comfort, reduced energy use, increased biodiversity, enhanced education[^1][^4][^16]
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - None negative reported; positive educational and community engagement effects exceeded expectations
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Ongoing municipal and community support, integrated educational programming

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- Demonstrated effective, measurable benefits in thermal comfort, biodiversity, and energy efficiency
- Successfully integrated NBS with educational and community engagement
- Model for policy, replication, and further research in climate adaptation

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- Upfront investment and complexity of retrofitting existing buildings
- Some technical challenges in long-term maintenance and monitoring
- Full environmental and economic impacts will only be clear with continued “After-LIFE” monitoring[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Enhanced equity by targeting a school in a less-advantaged neighborhood
- No evidence of negative impacts on vulnerable groups; project promoted inclusivity and participation

#### **Future Outlook**
- Continued monitoring, educational programming, and dissemination planned through at least 2025
- Potential for scaling to other schools and municipal buildings in Porto and beyond
- Opportunities for integrating digital monitoring, behavioral nudges, and broader community engagement in next phases[^4][^3][^16]
