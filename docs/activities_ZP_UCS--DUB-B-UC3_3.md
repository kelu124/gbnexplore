# Executive summary

## EnergyLab Nordhavn (Nordhavn Smart Energy Living Lab)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** EnergyLab Nordhavn  
- **Alternative Names:** Nordhavn Smart Energy Living Lab  
- **Location:** Nordhavn District, Copenhagen, Denmark  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 55.7086° N, 12.6168° E  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project inception: April 2015  
  - Main demonstration period: 2015–2019  
  - Ongoing as a local network organization (EnergyLab Nordhavn brand continues)  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Nordhavn district redevelopment: 400 hectares  
  - Target: 40,000 new inhabitants and 40,000 jobs by 2050[^1][^2]  
  - EnergyLab: Demonstration sites in multiple buildings, schools, apartments  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Technical University of Denmark (DTU)  
  - City of Copenhagen  
  - By & Havn (City & Harbor)  
  - HOFOR (Greater Copenhagen Utility)  
  - Industry partners: Danfoss, ABB, Radius, COWI, Nerve Smart Systems, METRO THERM, Glen Dimplex, PowerLabDK  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Danish Energy Technology Development and Demonstration Program (EUDP)  
  - Public-private partnership (PPP) model  
  - Industry contributions[^3][^4]

#### **Executive Summary**

EnergyLab Nordhavn is a large-scale urban living lab situated in Copenhagen’s Nordhavn district, designed to pioneer the integration of smart energy solutions at the city district scale. As part of one of Europe’s largest urban redevelopment projects, the initiative tests and demonstrates advanced energy systems that combine electricity, heating, energy-efficient buildings, and electric transport in a flexible, optimized, and largely renewable energy framework. The project leverages digital twins, smart meters, vehicle-to-grid (V2G) charging, and cloud-based analytics to track, manage, and optimize energy flows across multiple infrastructures and user groups.

By bringing together academia, local government, utilities, and industry, EnergyLab Nordhavn advances both technical innovation and stakeholder engagement. The living lab approach enables real-time experimentation in actual buildings and neighborhoods, producing valuable data and insights for adaptive energy management, grid balancing, peak shaving, and carbon reduction. Key outcomes include reduced peak loads, extended asset life, improved transparency for stakeholders, and significant progress towards Copenhagen’s aim of becoming the world’s first CO2-neutral capital by 2025.

The initiative’s focus on data harmonization, regulatory adaptation, and user engagement has yielded lessons for both technical implementation and policy reform. EnergyLab Nordhavn stands out as a model for integrated urban energy systems, offering replicable strategies for cities seeking to maximize sustainability, resilience, and livability in their redevelopment efforts[^1][^4][^5][^6].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - Copenhagen: Capital of Denmark, population ~640,000 (city), 1.3 million (metro)  
  - Economic base: Services, shipping, technology, green industries  
  - Geography: Coastal, with historic harbor areas  
  - Urban hierarchy: Principal city of Denmark, key Nordic hub[^2]

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - Nordhavn: Former industrial port, now transforming into a dense, mixed-use urban district  
  - Historical patterns: Industrial docks and warehouses, now being redeveloped for residential and commercial use  
  - Land use: 165,000 m² residential, 140,000 m² commercial, surrounded by water basins and canals[^2]  
  - Demographics: First residents moved in 2014; diverse, with a mix of ages and backgrounds expected  
  - Infrastructure: Modern, with new transport links, extensive district heating, high quality utilities  
  - Environmental conditions: Coastal zone, exposed to wind and sea; significant emphasis on climate resilience[^5][^2]

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Need for sustainable urban growth and redevelopment of brownfield industrial areas  
  - Challenge of integrating high shares of renewables while maintaining grid stability  
  - Peak load management in heating and electricity  
  - Stakeholder engagement and behavior change for energy flexibility[^4][^5]

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Copenhagen’s 2025 climate plan (CO2 neutrality)  
  - Nordhavn’s redevelopment masterplan  
  - Advancements in smart energy technologies

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City planners seeking innovative, green solutions  
  - Utilities needing to adapt infrastructure  
  - Residents and businesses requiring reliable, affordable energy  
  - Researchers and industry partners driving technology demonstration[^4]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - City of Copenhagen at the lead, supported by national energy agencies  
  - Public-private partnership structure  
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Copenhagen Climate Plan 2025  
  - National energy and urban development policies  
  - DGNB sustainability certification standards[^1][^5]
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Danish legislation on energy taxation and grid access  
  - Regulatory hurdles in data sharing, smart metering, and flexible tariffs[^4]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Develop and demonstrate cost-effective, integrated energy systems using renewables  
  - Enable energy flexibility and peak shaving across electricity, heating, and transport  
  - Support grid balancing and maximize use of distributed resources[^4]
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - To create a model for future urban districts that are resilient, sustainable, and powered by intelligent energy systems[^1][^4]
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Directly supports Copenhagen’s goal of CO2 neutrality by 2025  
  - Contributes to national and EU green building, energy, and climate agendas[^1][^5]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Deploying smart energy technologies and integrated control systems will enable flexible, efficient, and carbon-neutral operation of urban energy networks, improving sustainability and reducing costs[^4]
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Residents, businesses, utilities, municipal government, and the wider Copenhagen population
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Digital twins of energy systems  
  - Real-time data collection and analytics  
  - V2G charging and dynamic grid interaction  
  - Home automation and smart metering[^4]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Strong municipal leadership and climate commitments  
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Cost savings, infrastructure efficiency, and job creation  
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Community empowerment, data transparency, livability improvements  
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Climate adaptation, emission reduction, resource optimization  
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Maturation of smart grids, IoT, and digital platforms[^4][^5][^6]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Triple helix model: partnership among academia, industry, and government  
  - Living lab approach: real-world testing in active neighborhoods[^4]
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Demonstration sites in apartments, schools, commercial buildings  
  - Integration of district heating, solar PV, batteries, EV charging, and heat pumps[^4]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial demonstration (2015–2019), ongoing expansion to more buildings and systems

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Digital twin modeling  
  - Smart meters and sensors  
  - V2G EV charging infrastructure  
  - Cloud-based analytics and data warehouse (EnergyDataDK)  
  - Ultra-low temperature district heating substations  
  - Advanced radiator thermostats  
  - Ammonia-based heat pumps  
  - Grid-connected batteries for frequency regulation and peak shaving[^4][^1][^3]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological, social (user engagement), process-oriented (data harmonization), and governance-related (PPP model)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Direct connection to existing power and district heating grids  
  - Data integration across utilities and building management systems

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive selection among industry partners  
  - Contracting for technology supply and installation[^4]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Residents involved in data collection, feedback, and system testing  
  - Utility companies as both operators and innovators
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Residents permitted remote control of heating for demonstration purposes  
  - Feedback loop for comfort and usability[^4]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by DTU and City of Copenhagen  
  - Regular reporting, cross-partner governance structure

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total budget not publicly specified, but includes substantial public and private investment[^3]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EUDP public grant, private sector, PPP contributions
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Cost savings in district heating  
  - Potential future service models (energy flexibility, data services)
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Focus on long-term cost reduction, grid efficiency, and future business models[^4][^1]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Deep integration of multiple energy systems and carriers  
- Real-world testing in active urban environments  
- High level of data transparency and stakeholder engagement  
- Contribution to citywide and national climate and sustainability goals

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Complexity of harmonizing data from diverse sources and vendors  
- Regulatory barriers to flexible energy markets and data sharing  
- High upfront costs and need for long-term investment

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Initiative advances urban equity by providing affordable, reliable energy to all residents  
- Inclusive development process, but continued attention needed for vulnerable populations in future expansion

#### **Future Outlook**

- Nordhavn district will continue to evolve, expanding demonstration sites and integrating additional data and technologies  
- Ongoing challenge: scaling flexible energy solutions citywide and nationally  
- Next phases: Refinement of business models for energy flexibility, continued policy advocacy for regulatory reform, and expansion as a model for other cities seeking sustainable, resilient urban transformation[^4][^1][^5][^7]
