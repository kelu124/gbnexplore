# Executive summary## eHUBS – Smart Shared Mobility Hubs

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** eHUBS – Smart Shared Mobility Hubs  
- **Alternative Names:** eHUBS (Electric Hubs), Interreg eHUBS Project  
- **Location:** Rotterdam, Netherlands (with additional hubs in other North-West European cities)  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Multiple locations across Rotterdam (e.g., central districts, residential neighborhoods)  
- **Timeline:** 2018–2022 (project duration), with ongoing operation and expansion in some locations  
- **Project Scale:** Multiple shared mobility hubs established in Rotterdam, each serving a neighborhood or district; estimated coverage of several thousand residents and users  
- **Leading Organization(s):** City of Rotterdam (municipal government), local mobility operators, research institutions (e.g., TU Delft), and private sector partners  
- **Funding Sources:** Interreg North-West Europe (EU funding), City of Rotterdam, private sector contributions, and research grants  

#### **Executive Summary**
The eHUBS project is a pioneering urban mobility initiative that established smart shared mobility hubs in Rotterdam, integrating electric vehicle (EV) charging, real-time energy monitoring, and user engagement platforms. The project aimed to reduce urban transportation emissions, promote shared mobility, and enhance energy efficiency by creating accessible, technology-enabled hubs for electric cars, bikes, and scooters. Each hub features monitored EV charging stations, energy flow management systems, and digital dashboards that provide real-time feedback to operators and users. The initiative was designed to address the challenges of urban congestion, air pollution, and inefficient energy use, while fostering community engagement and knowledge-sharing. By promoting shared use of electric vehicles and optimizing energy consumption, eHUBS has contributed to Rotterdam’s broader climate resilience and sustainability goals. The project’s success has inspired similar initiatives in other European cities and demonstrated the potential of smart, community-focused mobility solutions in urban environments.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

- **City Profile:** Rotterdam is the second-largest city in the Netherlands, with a population of approximately 650,000. It is a major port city and a hub for innovation, sustainability, and urban development. The city faces significant challenges related to climate change, including sea-level rise, urban heat, and air pollution. Rotterdam has a strong focus on climate adaptation, green infrastructure, and sustainable mobility, positioning itself as a leader in urban resilience.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The eHUBS hubs are located in diverse neighborhoods, ranging from central urban districts to residential areas. These areas typically have mixed land use, with a combination of housing, commercial spaces, and public amenities. The neighborhoods are characterized by varying levels of income, age distribution, and cultural diversity, reflecting Rotterdam’s broader demographic profile. Existing infrastructure includes public transportation networks, cycling paths, and a growing number of EV charging points.
- **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** Rotterdam is vulnerable to climate change impacts, particularly flooding and heat stress. The city has implemented a range of green and blue infrastructure projects to enhance resilience, including green roofs, water squares, and tidal parks. Air quality and noise pollution are ongoing concerns, especially in densely populated areas.

The city faced challenges related to the adoption of electric vehicles, including limited charging infrastructure, high costs, and lack of public awareness. There was also a need to promote shared mobility to reduce per-capita vehicle ownership and improve urban livability.
- **Triggering Events:** The project was prompted by Rotterdam’s commitment to climate resilience and sustainable urban development, as well as the availability of EU funding through the Interreg North-West Europe program. The city’s broader climate adaptation and mobility strategies provided a supportive policy environment for the initiative.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Key actors included the City of Rotterdam, local mobility operators, research institutions, private sector partners, and community organizations. The city government played a central role in coordinating the project, while mobility operators and private partners provided technical expertise and resources. Community organizations were involved in promoting public engagement and awareness.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** The project was governed by a partnership between the City of Rotterdam, Interreg North-West Europe, and local stakeholders. The city’s municipal government provided oversight and coordination, while Interreg facilitated cross-border collaboration and knowledge-sharing.
