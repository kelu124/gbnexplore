# Executive summary## Tivoli GreenCity Sustainable Neighbourhood

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Tivoli GreenCity Sustainable Neighbourhood  
- **Alternative Names:** Tivoli GreenCity, Tivoli Ecodistrict, Tivoli GreenCity Brussels  
- **Location:** Laeken, Brussels-Capital Region, Belgium  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 50.8997° N, 4.3403° E  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Conceptualization: Early 2010s  
  - Master Plan Approval: 2011  
  - Phase 1 Inauguration: 2016  
  - Phase 2 Inauguration: 2019  
  - Ongoing: Continuous monitoring, adaptive management, and further development  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: 49,958 m² (approx. 5 hectares)  
  - Housing Units: 397 (including 271 subsidised and 126 social housing units)  
  - Population: Estimated 800–1,000 residents  
  - Investment: €100–120 million (including Greenbizz incubator)  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Citydev.brussels (public urban development agency)  
  - MSA Architecture & Urbanism (master planner)  
  - Community Land Trust Brussels (CLTB)  
  - Greenbizz (eco-incubator operator)  
  - Cohousing Tivoli (resident-led cohousing group)  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Public funding (Brussels-Capital Region, Citydev.brussels)  
  - Private investment (cohousing, commercial tenants)  
  - EU and national grants for sustainability and innovation  
  - PPP (public-private partnerships for infrastructure and services)

#### **Executive Summary**
Tivoli GreenCity is a pioneering sustainable urban development project in Brussels, Belgium, designed as a “laboratory for innovation” to test and demonstrate integrated approaches to ecological, social, and economic sustainability. Located on a former industrial brownfield site in Laeken, the neighborhood was developed in two phases (2016 and 2019) and now houses over 397 units, including social, subsidised, and cohousing options. The project features passive buildings, rainwater harvesting, rooftop gardens, communal greenhouses, and a circular economy incubator (Greenbizz). It has received international recognition, including the BREEAM Communities Outstanding certification—the highest score worldwide for sustainable neighborhoods.

A comprehensive impact study, conducted by Sciences Po Urban School in 2023–2024, evaluated the project’s performance in terms of energy efficiency, social cohesion, and resident engagement. The study revealed that while the buildings met technical and environmental targets, challenges remain in the consistent use of shared spaces and long-term resident participation. The project’s legacy includes the development of an open-data evaluation matrix with 71 sustainability indicators, designed to inform and improve future urban projects. Tivoli GreenCity exemplifies a holistic approach to sustainable urban regeneration, combining innovative design, participatory governance, and adaptive management to create a resilient, inclusive, and environmentally responsible neighborhood.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:**  
  Brussels is the capital of Belgium and the de facto capital of the European Union. With a population of approximately 1.2 million in the metropolitan area, it is a multicultural, multilingual city with a strong service economy, significant public sector employment, and a mix of historic and modern architecture. The city faces challenges related to urban sprawl, aging infrastructure, social inequality, and environmental degradation, particularly in its northern districts.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical Development Patterns:** Laeken, in the northwest of Brussels, was historically a working-class district with a strong industrial presence, particularly along the canal. The area has undergone significant transformation in recent decades, with the decline of traditional industries and the redevelopment of brownfield sites.
  - **Current Land Use Composition:** The Tivoli site was a former industrial brownfield, now redeveloped into a mixed-use neighborhood with residential, commercial, and community functions. The area is characterized by a mix of old and new buildings, with a focus on green infrastructure and sustainable design.
  - **Demographic Profile:** The neighborhood is socially mixed, with a significant proportion of low- and middle-income residents, as well as a growing number of young professionals and families. The area is ethnically diverse, reflecting the broader demographic trends in Brussels.
  - **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:** The site is well-connected to public transport (trams, buses, and proximity to the canal), but some infrastructure remains outdated. The redevelopment has improved accessibility and amenities, but challenges persist in terms of social integration and community cohesion.
  - **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** The area is subject to urban heat island effects, air pollution, and limited green space. The redevelopment has prioritized biodiversity, green roofs, and sustainable water management to address these issues.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Urban decay and environmental degradation of former industrial sites  
  - Social segregation and lack of affordable housing  
  - Limited green space and biodiversity  
  - High energy consumption and carbon emissions  
  - Weak community engagement and social cohesion

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - The Brussels-Capital Region’s strategic plan for urban regeneration and sustainable development  
  - The closure of industrial sites and the availability of brownfield land  
  - Growing public and political pressure for more sustainable and inclusive urban development

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - **Public Sector:** Citydev.brussels, Brussels-Capital Region, local municipalities  
  - **Private Sector:** Developers, architects, construction companies  
  - **Civil Society:** Community groups, environmental NGOs, resident associations  
  - **Academia:** Universities and research institutions (e.g., Sciences Po Urban School)

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Citydev.brussels, as the public urban development agency, led the project with support from the Brussels-Capital Region and local authorities. The project was subject to regional planning regulations and sustainability standards.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Brussels-Capital Region’s Sustainable Development Plan  
  - Urban Renewal and Brownfield Redevelopment Policies  
  - Social Housing and Affordable Housing Policies  
  - Environmental and Climate Action Plans

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - **Enablers:** Strong public sector commitment to sustainability, availability of brownfield sites, supportive regulatory framework for mixed-use development, and access to EU and national funding.
  - **Barriers:** Bureaucratic delays, complex land ownership and zoning issues, and the need for ongoing stakeholder coordination.

  - To provide affordable and accessible housing for a diverse population  
  - To promote social cohesion and community engagement  
  - To support the circular economy and local entrepreneurship

- **Vision Statement:**  
  “To create a harmonious connection between the existing dense residential area of Laeken and the industrial zone of the Port of Brussels, through the reclassification of an urban derelict site into a vibrant, sustainable, and socially cohesive neighborhood.”
