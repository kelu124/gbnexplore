## Urban GreenUP – Nature-Based Solutions for Urban Resilience

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Urban GreenUP – Nature-Based Solutions for Urban Resilience
- **Alternative Names:** Urban GreenUP Project, URBAN GreenUP, Re-naturing Urban Plan with NbS
- **Location:** Valladolid, Spain (specifically in the city center and along key corridors such as Plaza España, Calle Santa María, and the Zorrilla Stadium area)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 41.6528° N, 4.7282° W
- **Timeline:** 
  - Project Duration: June 2017 – May 2022 (60 months)
  - Key Milestones: 
    - 2017: Project launch and diagnosis phase
    - 2018–2020: Implementation of green corridors, green roofs, living walls, and sustainable drainage systems
    - 2021–2022: Monitoring, evaluation, and dissemination of results
  - Status: Completed, with ongoing maintenance and replication in other areas
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Area: Multiple demonstration sites across Valladolid, covering several hectares
  - Population Affected: Approximately 300,000 residents
  - Investment Value: Funded by the EU Horizon 2020 program (total project budget: €16.5 million, with Valladolid’s share estimated at €2–3 million)
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - City of Valladolid (municipal government)
  - CARTIF Technological Center (technical partner)
  - SingularGreen (design and implementation partner)
  - European Commission (funding and coordination)
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - EU Horizon 2020 (public funding)
  - Municipal budget (public funding)
  - Private sector contributions (e.g., El Corte Inglés for green façade)
  - PPP (public-private partnerships for specific interventions)

#### **Executive Summary**
Urban GreenUP is a flagship European initiative that implemented large-scale nature-based solutions (NBS) in Valladolid, Spain, to enhance urban resilience, improve environmental quality, and promote sustainable development. The project focused on re-naturing the city through the creation of green corridors, vertical gardens, green roofs, and sustainable urban drainage systems (SUDs), all supported by an integrated digital monitoring platform. Key objectives included mitigating the urban heat island effect, improving air quality, reducing flood risk, and increasing biodiversity. The initiative was driven by a collaborative approach involving the city government, research institutions, private companies, and citizens. Urban GreenUP not only transformed Valladolid’s urban landscape but also served as a model for other cities seeking to address climate change and urban environmental challenges through innovative, data-driven NBS. The project’s success has influenced EU policy and inspired further investment in urban greening initiatives across Europe.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Valladolid is a city of approximately 300,000 inhabitants, located in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula. It is the seat of the Cortes and the Junta of the autonomous community of Castilla y León. The city has a continental-cold Mediterranean climate and is situated at the confluence of the rivers Pisuerga and Esgueva. Valladolid’s economy is diversified, with a strong presence in services, industry, and agriculture. The city is a regional hub for education, culture, and innovation.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Historical Development Patterns:** Valladolid has experienced significant urban expansion and densification, leading to increased distances, car dependency, and environmental degradation.
  - **Current Land Use Composition:** Mixed-use urban fabric with residential, commercial, and industrial zones. The city center is characterized by dense building stock and limited green space connectivity.
  - **Demographic Profile:** The population is relatively balanced in terms of age distribution, with a mix of middle-income and lower-income households. The city has a diverse cultural composition, with a growing immigrant population.
  - **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:** The city has a well-developed public transport network, but pedestrian and cycling infrastructure is limited. Green spaces are fragmented and poorly connected.
  - **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** Valladolid faces challenges such as poor air quality, urban heat island effect, noise pollution, and flood risk due to the river Esgueva. The city also experiences long periods of drought and increased water scarcity.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** 
  - Lack of connectivity between green areas
  - Urban heat island effect
  - Poor air quality
  - Noise pollution
  - Flood risk from the river Esgueva
  - High consumption of soil, energy, and water
  - Increased distances and car dependency
- **Triggering Events:** 
  - Growing awareness of climate change impacts
  - Public pressure for improved environmental quality
  - Policy changes at the EU and national levels (e.g., European Green Deal, Covenant of Mayors for Climate and Energy)
  - Local environmental crises (e.g., heatwaves, floods)
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** 
  - City government: Primary driver and coordinator
  - Research institutions (e.g., CARTIF): Technical expertise and innovation
  - Private sector (e.g., El Corte Inglés): Investment and implementation
  - Citizens: Beneficiaries and participants in co-creation processes
  - NGOs and community groups: Advocacy and engagement

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** The project was coordinated by the City of Valladolid, with support from the European Commission and local partners. The governance structure included a project management team, technical working groups, and citizen advisory boards.
- **Relevant Policies:** 
  - Urban Management Plan (PGOU, 2017): Aimed at freezing the expansive model of the city and promoting urban regeneration
  - Integrated Sustainable Urban Development Strategy (EDUSI): Focused on sustainable and inclusive growth
  - Action Plan for Sustainable Energy (SEAP): Targeted CO2 emissions reduction
  - Local Agenda 21: Promoted environmental sustainability and citizen participation
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** 
  - Enablers: Strong commitment from the city government, EU funding, and supportive national policies
  - Barriers: Bureaucratic delays, limited public awareness, and resistance from some stakeholders

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The project is guided by the principles of re-naturing cities, integrating nature-based solutions with smart technologies to create resilient urban environments.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Green corridors: Renaturation of existing cycle lanes and creation of new green corridors
  - Vertical gardens: Installation of green façades and living walls
  - Green roofs: Vegetative roofs on canopies and buildings
  - Sustainable drainage systems: Rain gardens, green filter pavements, detention ponds
  - Urban forests: Planting of new trees and creation of shaded green areas
- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Phase 1: Diagnosis and planning (2017)
  - Phase 2: Implementation of green infrastructure (2018–2020)
  - Phase 3: Monitoring, evaluation, and dissemination (2021–2022)

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - Remote monitoring sensors
  - IoT (Internet of Things)
  - Smart irrigation systems
  - Digital platforms for data collection and analysis
- **Innovation Type:**
