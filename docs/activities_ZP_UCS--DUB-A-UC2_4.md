# Executive summary## SmartEnCity: Integrated Smart Solutions for Urban Regeneration

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** SmartEnCity: Integrated Smart Solutions for Urban Regeneration
- **Alternative Names:** SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz; Proyecto SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz
- **Location:** Coronación neighborhood, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Basque Country, Spain
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 42.8462° N, 2.6727° W
- **Timeline:**  
  - **Start:** 2016  
  - **Key Milestones:**  
    - Project launch and planning: 2016  
    - Building retrofits and infrastructure works: 2017–2021  
    - Implementation of digital platform and monitoring: 2018–2022  
    - Project conclusion: 2022
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Coronación neighborhood (~60,000 m² retrofitted)  
  - Population affected: ~1,300+ homes, mainly multifamily residential dwellings  
  - Investment: EU Horizon 2020 funding, municipal contributions, private co-financing
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Vitoria-Gasteiz City Council  
  - European SmartEnCity Consortium (38 partners from 6 countries)  
  - Local agencies such as CEA (Centro de Estudios Ambientales)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020  
  - City of Vitoria-Gasteiz  
  - Regional government of the Basque Country  
  - Private homeowners (co-investment)  
  - International project partners

#### **Executive Summary**
SmartEnCity in Vitoria-Gasteiz is a flagship EU Horizon 2020 initiative that aimed to demonstrate scalable, digitally enabled solutions for low-carbon urban regeneration in the Coronación neighborhood—one of the city’s most vulnerable districts in both social and energy terms. The project combined large-scale building retrofits, deployment of a new biomass district heating network, and the implementation of a sophisticated digital platform that leveraged IoT sensors and building digital twins for real-time monitoring and optimization of indoor comfort and energy performance. Residents and facility managers gained access to actionable insights through user-facing apps, supporting behavior change and informed decision-making. The project also renewed public spaces and improved neighborhood accessibility, integrating participatory approaches that placed local stakeholders at the center of planning and evaluation. The initiative produced a demonstrable reduction in energy use (up to 35%), significant improvements in indoor air quality and comfort, and a replicable governance and technical model for other European cities. The project’s holistic approach addressed environmental sustainability, social inclusion, and participatory governance, positioning Vitoria-Gasteiz as a leader in the transition toward climate-neutral, smart urban neighborhoods[^1][^2][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country, with approximately 240,000 inhabitants. The city is recognized for its progressive environmental policies, winning the European Green Capital award in 2012 and the Global Green City Award in 2019. It boasts a high proportion of green public areas, ensuring nearly all residents live within 300 meters of an open green space. The city’s economy is diverse, with strong industrial, service, and public administration sectors. Vitoria-Gasteiz is a regional leader in sustainability and digital innovation[^1][^3].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical development:** Coronación is a central, densely built residential area characterized by mid-20th-century apartment blocks, many with poor thermal performance and limited accessibility.
  - **Land use:** Predominantly residential, with local retail, services, and some public spaces.
  - **Demographics:** The area is socioeconomically vulnerable, with a high proportion of low- to middle-income households, elderly residents, and immigrants[^1][^2].
  - **Infrastructure quality:** Prior to the project, buildings were poorly insulated, resulting in high energy bills and low indoor comfort. Public spaces and mobility infrastructure were outdated.
  - **Environmental conditions:** High energy consumption, significant GHG emissions, and risk of energy poverty[^5].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Widespread energy inefficiency and high heating costs in aging residential stock  
  - Poor indoor environmental quality, affecting health and comfort  
  - Social vulnerability and risk of exclusion  
  - Outdated public spaces and inadequate accessibility  
  - Lack of integration of digital technologies for energy and building management

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 call for lighthouse smart city projects  
  - City’s commitment to climate neutrality and the Urban Agenda 2030[^3]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (policy leadership)  
  - Local residents (beneficiaries, co-investors, co-designers)  
  - Private homeowners (property owners, key to retrofit participation)  
  - Regional government (policy, funding)  
  - Technology providers and research institutions (solution design and monitoring)[^2][^4]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by Vitoria-Gasteiz City Council, in partnership with EU and regional authorities  
  - Integrated in the city’s Strategic Urban Agenda and Sustainable Energy and Climate Action Plan (SECAP 2030)[^3]

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Local Agenda 21  
  - Covenant of Mayors for Climate & Energy  
  - Regional and European energy efficiency directives  
  - Specific citywide strategies for energy, mobility, and social inclusion

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive legal framework for building retrofits and renewable energy  
  - Challenges in aligning private property regulations and collective action for common infrastructure (e.g., district heating)[^3]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Achieve deep energy retrofitting of residential buildings  
  - Deploy a renewable district heating system  
  - Integrate digital technologies (IoT, digital twins) for real-time monitoring and optimization  
  - Enhance public space and mobility  
  - Foster social inclusion and participatory governance[^1][^2][^3]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  Toward a “smart, climate-neutral, inclusive city where technological innovation and citizen participation drive urban regeneration and improved quality of life.”

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Directly supports Vitoria-Gasteiz’s Urban Agenda 2030, SECAP 2030, and EU climate neutrality targets[^3]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By retrofitting buildings, deploying renewable energy, and enabling digital management, the project would reduce energy use and emissions, improve resident comfort, and create a replicable urban transformation model.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  Primarily low- and middle-income residents of the Coronación neighborhood, but also the wider city through reduced emissions and improved public spaces.

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Large-scale integration of building digital twins and IoT for residential energy management  
  - Participatory retrofitting and co-design of interventions  
  - Holistic neighborhood-scale approach (not just isolated buildings)[^1][^2][^4]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  City leadership’s commitment to sustainability and climate action; participation in EU smart city programs.

- **Economic:**  
  Need to reduce energy poverty and stimulate local green jobs.

- **Social:**  
  Community demand for improved living conditions and social cohesion.

- **Environmental:**  
  Urgent need to reduce GHG emissions and adapt to climate change.

- **Technological:**  
  Rapid advances in IoT, energy management, and digital urban platforms[^2][^4][^6].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Based on smart city and sustainable neighborhood regeneration principles: integration of energy, mobility, ICT, and social innovation.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Retrofitting of 1,313 housing units (insulation, windows, roofs)  
  - Construction of new central biomass district heating system  
  - Public space improvements (streets, plazas, benches, landscaping)[^1][^5]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial planning and co-design (2016–2017)  
  - Building retrofits and infrastructure works (2017–2021)  
  - Digital platform deployment and resident engagement (2018–2022)

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Building digital twin models  
  - IoT sensors (monitoring temperature, humidity, air quality, energy use)  
  - Integrated energy management systems (building and neighborhood scale)  
  - Biomass boilers and district heating network  
  - User app interfaces for residents and facility managers[^1][^2][^4][^6]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  Technological (digital twins, IoT), social (participatory design), governance (integrated management), financial (mixed public-private co-investment)

- **Integration Approach:**  
  Technologies were layered onto existing infrastructure, with new systems interoperable with legacy building elements and city data platforms[^6].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Competitive tendering for retrofit works, heating system construction, and ICT solution providers.

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  Extensive public workshops, resident meetings, and participatory planning sessions.

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  Residents involved in decision-making on retrofit options, energy management preferences, and public space design[^2][^4].

- **Project Management:**  
  Led by city council with a dedicated project office, coordinated with EU partners and local agencies.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  Multi-million euro investment (exact figures vary by source), covering building works, technology, and public realm.

- **Funding Mix:**  
  Majority EU Horizon 2020 grant, with city and regional co-financing, and private homeowner contributions for retrofits.

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  District heating funded through user fees; potential for energy savings to offset costs.

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  Long-term savings in energy costs for residents; district heating designed for ongoing cost recovery[^2][^3].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  Integrating new digital and energy systems with old building stock; ensuring reliable operation of digital platforms and sensors.

- **Financial:**  
  Securing co-financing from private homeowners, some of whom were reluctant to invest.

- **Social and Political:**  
  Overcoming skepticism, building trust, addressing concerns about gentrification and displacement.

- **Administrative:**  
  Coordinating among multiple agencies and across city, regional, and EU levels.

- **Environmental:**  
  Biomass supply logistics and air quality management for the district heating system[^1][^2][^3].

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  Iterative co-design with residents to build buy-in; technical pilots to resolve integration issues.

- **Design Modifications:**  
  Adjusted retrofit packages to meet resident preferences and budget constraints.

- **Lessons Learned:**  
  Need for early and ongoing engagement; importance of transparent data-sharing and flexibility in project delivery[^2][^4].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical:**  
  - 26 buildings retrofitted, covering 524–1,313 dwellings (sources vary)[^5][^3]  
  - New biomass district heating system  
  - Upgraded public spaces and mobility infrastructure

- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Digital platform for energy and indoor environment monitoring  
  - Improved heating, comfort, and accessibility

- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Up to 35% reduction in energy use  
  - 3 GWh/year energy demand reduction, 1,000 tCO2/year emissions avoided in the neighborhood[^3]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  Improved comfort, lower energy bills, better indoor air quality, health and productivity gains.

- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  Targeted support for low-income and vulnerable households.

- **Displacement/Gentrification:**  
  No major displacement reported; effort to ensure inclusivity.

- **Social Capital:**  
  Strengthened networks through participatory processes.

- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  Enhanced neighborhood identity through co-designed public spaces[^2][^7].

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  Local construction and installation jobs during retrofit phase.

- **Economic Activity:**  
  Increased demand for green technologies and services; property value stabilization.

- **Cost Savings:**  
  Significant household energy cost reductions.

- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  Focus on supporting vulnerable groups through subsidies and targeted engagement[^3].

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  Major reduction in neighborhood GHG emissions; city is on track for 61.5% direct emissions cuts and 29% energy savings by 2030[^3].

- **Resource Management:**  
  Sustainable use of biomass; improved energy and water efficiency.

- **Environmental Quality:**  
  Air quality improvements; enhanced green spaces.

- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  Integrated circular economy measures in waste collection.

- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  Alignment with SDGs and climate neutrality pathways; performance tracked via digital platform[^3].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  Strengthened city capability in project management, digital innovation, and cross-sector collaboration.

- **Policy Influence:**  
  Model for citywide roll-out and national/EU policy learning.

- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  Open data platform and reporting mechanisms[^3][^6].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  Most key objectives achieved, with measurable energy, comfort, and participation gains.

- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  Some delays and participation challenges, but mitigated through adaptive management.

- **Long-term Viability:**  
  Continued monitoring and citywide expansion planned; strong institutionalization[^2][^3].

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**

- **Contextual Dependencies:**  
  Success depends on policy support, funding, and strong local governance; technical solutions transferable with adaptation to local building types.

- **Critical Success Factors:**  
  - Strong city leadership  
  - Integrated digital and physical interventions  
  - Deep resident engagement  
  - Stable funding

- **Scalability:**  
  Approach is replicable at district or city scale, as evidenced by parallel projects in Tartu (Estonia) and Sonderborg (Denmark) under SmartEnCity[^2].

#### **Comparative Analysis**

- **Similar Initiatives:**  
  Comparable to other EU “Lighthouse” smart city projects (e.g., in Tartu, Sonderborg, and beyond).

- **Best Practices:**  
  - Holistic integration of energy, ICT, and social innovation  
  - Participatory retrofitting  
  - Data-driven management[^4][^7]

- **Cautionary Lessons:**  
  - Importance of early engagement of private owners  
  - Need for robust ICT infrastructure and flexible financial models

#### **Parallels to [Your Specific Use Case]**

- **Contextual Similarities:**  
  Both involve retrofitting aging residential stock, focus on energy poverty, and require resident engagement.

- **Methodological Parallels:**  
  Use of real-time monitoring, scenario simulation for energy and comfort, and participatory design.

- **Technological Overlaps:**  
  Digital twin platforms, IoT sensors, integrated energy management.

- **Stakeholder Dynamics:**  
  Need for cross-sector partnerships and active involvement of end-users.

  Transparent reporting, co-creation processes, layered integration of digital and physical upgrades.

- **Adaptation Considerations:**  
  Adjust for local funding, regulatory, and building typologies; engage residents early and often to ensure uptake.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Strong integration of technology, social inclusion, and environmental goals.
- Demonstrated, measurable impacts on energy, emissions, and resident well-being.
- Participatory processes fostered local ownership and trust.
- Created an actionable, scalable model for smart urban regeneration[^2][^3][^4][^7].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Technical and social complexity slowed some phases, with challenges in coordinating among stakeholders and engaging all private homeowners.
- Upfront costs and financial sustainability for ongoing digital system maintenance require continued attention.
- Replication may be harder in cities without strong policy and funding frameworks[^2][^3].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Focused on a vulnerable neighborhood, with explicit aims to reduce energy poverty and promote inclusion.
- Participatory processes aimed to ensure all voices were heard, though some groups may have been harder to engage fully.

#### **Future Outlook**

- The Coronación model is being scaled and adapted elsewhere in Vitoria-Gasteiz and other EU cities.
- Ongoing monitoring, digital platform extension, and new rounds of funding are planned.
- Further evolution will focus on even deeper digital integration (citywide digital twin), expanded community energy projects, and continued emphasis on social equity and climate resilience[^3][^4].
