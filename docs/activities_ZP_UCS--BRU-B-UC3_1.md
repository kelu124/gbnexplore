# Executive summary## Savonnerie Heymans: A Former Soap Factory Turned Into a Sustainable Housing Project

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Savonnerie Heymans
- **Alternative Names:** La Savonnerie, Savonnerie Heymans Social Housing Project
- **Location:** Rue d’Anderlecht 139–147, Anderlecht, Brussels, Belgium
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 50.8492° N, 4.3347° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - 2005: Acquisition and decontamination of the site  
  - 2010: Design competition and rehabilitation phase  
  - 2011: Completion and occupancy  
  - Ongoing: Community management and maintenance
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: 6,500 m²  
  - Units: 42 social housing units (studios, apartments, lofts, maisonettes, duplexes)  
  - Population: Approximately 100–120 residents  
  - Investment: Not publicly disclosed, but funded by public social services
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - CPAS de Bruxelles (Centre Public d’Action Sociale de Bruxelles, social services department of central Brussels)  
  - MDW Architecture (Marie Moignot, Xavier De Wil, Gilles Debrun)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Public funding (Brussels regional government, CPAS)  
  - No significant private or international aid reported

---

#### **Executive Summary**

The Savonnerie Heymans project is a landmark example of sustainable urban regeneration in Brussels, transforming a former soap factory into a vibrant, mixed-use social housing complex. Completed in 2011, the initiative created 42 diverse, energy-efficient housing units—ranging from studios to six-bedroom apartments—alongside community amenities, urban gardens, and a playground. The project is notable for its integration of passive house standards, rainwater harvesting, solar panels, and bioclimatic loggias, all within a framework that prioritizes social diversity and ecological construction. By retaining and repurposing industrial heritage elements such as the factory chimney and main 19th-century house, the project preserves local history while fostering a sense of community and belonging. The complex is structured around three large communal voids: a mini-forest, a large courtyard, and a 3D landscaped park, which serve as hubs for social interaction and environmental enrichment. The project has received numerous international awards and is widely recognized as a model for sustainable urban regeneration, setting a benchmark for future social housing developments in Europe.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - The project is guided by the principles of sustainable development, social inclusion, and the preservation of industrial heritage.  
  - The design emphasizes the creation of a mixed-use, high-density urban village that integrates residential, community, and green spaces.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - The complex is structured around three large communal voids: a mini-forest, a large courtyard, and a 3D landscaped park.  
  - The site includes four new buildings and two renovated ones, with a mix of studios, apartments, lofts, and maisonettes.  
  - The design incorporates bioclimatic loggias, which provide effective acoustic and thermal barriers, ensuring privacy and reducing energy consumption.  
  - The project features a collective heating system (cogeneration), solar panels for hot water, rainwater harvesting for toilets and gardens, and natural materials for insulation (hemp fibers, expanded cork, etc.).

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - The project was structured in phases, with the acquisition and decontamination of the site in 2005, the design competition and rehabilitation phase in 2010, and the completion and occupancy in 2011.  
  - Ongoing community management and maintenance ensure the long-term sustainability of the project.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Passive house standards and bioclimatic loggias for energy efficiency and thermal comfort.  
  - Solar panels for hot water heating.  
  - Rainwater harvesting for toilets and gardens.  
  - Natural materials for insulation (hemp fibers, expanded cork, etc.).  
  - Collective heating system (cogeneration).

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: Use of advanced energy-efficient technologies and sustainable materials.  
  - Social: Community engagement and co-creation in the design and management of the project.  
  - Governance: Integration of public and private stakeholders in the planning and implementation process.

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - The technologies are integrated with existing urban systems, such as the public transportation network and the local energy grid.  
  - The project also incorporates smart technology, such as IoT and AI systems for security and energy efficiency.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - The project was led by CPAS de Bruxelles, with MDW Architecture selected through a design competition.  
  - The implementation was managed by a team of consultants and contractors, including SETESCO, Waterman TCA, MK Engineering, ATS, FORUM, and SECO Group.

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - The project involved extensive community engagement, with residents and community groups participating in the design and management process.  
  - The project also included a concierge and a caretaker, who worked closely with residents to develop a collective vegetable garden, beehives, a pond, and a henhouse.

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Community input and co-creation were central to the project, with residents and community groups involved in the design and management of the project.  
  - The project also included a public Ludothèque (game library) and other community amenities, which are available to both residents and the wider community.
