# Executive summary

## Trinity College Dublin Living Lab – Air Quality Monitoring

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Trinity College Dublin Living Lab – Air Quality Monitoring
- **Alternative Names:** TCD Living Lab; Trinity Sustainability Air Quality Project
- **Location:** Trinity College Dublin Main Campus, Dublin, Ireland
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.3438° N, 6.2546° W (central campus)
- **Timeline:** 
  - **Installation:** July 28, 2025 – June 30, 2026 (initial deployment of sensors)[^1]
  - **Ongoing:** Community engagement, data collection, and policy integration continue post-installation
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Campus-wide; locations include Front Gate, Arts Building, Pearse Street, New Square, Creche, Lincoln Gate, Science Gallery, and Unit 18[^1]
  - Primary beneficiaries: ~18,000 students and staff, plus local schools and community
- **Leading Organization(s):** Trinity College Dublin (TCD) – Trinity Sustainability; Academic lead: Prof. John Gallagher; Operational lead: Jane Hackett[^1]
- **Funding Sources:** University internal funding, with advisory support from Safe Routes to School and Globe Ireland Citizen Science[^1]

#### **Executive Summary**
The Trinity College Dublin Living Lab – Air Quality Monitoring project is a pioneering campus initiative designed to monitor, analyze, and improve air quality using a network of solar-powered sensors and NOx test tubes strategically placed across the TCD campus. Launched in 2025, the project is led by a multidisciplinary team of faculty, staff, and students under the Trinity Sustainability banner. Its primary objectives are to generate real-time, high-resolution air quality data, empower evidence-based campus sustainability policies, and foster a living laboratory for environmental education and community engagement.

Key technological features include the deployment of PurpleAir particulate sensors, NOx diffusion tubes, and solar energy systems, all integrated into a digital platform for open data dissemination. The Living Lab model emphasizes participatory research, with students and staff involved in every stage—from data collection to interpretation and outreach. The initiative also extends its impact through collaborations with local schools, providing hands-on learning experiences and fostering broader environmental literacy.

By informing campus transport and sustainability strategies, the project aims to directly improve air quality, support targeted interventions, and raise environmental awareness among campus users and the surrounding community. It stands out as a replicable example of urban environmental monitoring, integrating citizen science, digital innovation, and educational outreach to address pressing urban air quality challenges[^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Dublin is Ireland’s capital and largest city, with a metropolitan population exceeding 1.2 million. It is a major economic hub with a strong knowledge economy, significant tourism, and a growing tech sector. The city faces typical European urban challenges: traffic congestion, rising property prices, and air quality concerns, especially related to transport emissions.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - **Historical development:** The TCD campus is located in the city centre, surrounded by dense urban fabric, historic sites, and key transport corridors.
  - **Land use:** Mix of academic, residential, commercial, and green spaces.
  - **Demographics:** The campus community is diverse (students, academics, staff), with nearby neighborhoods featuring a mix of income groups and cultural backgrounds.
  - **Infrastructure:** Well-connected by public transport (buses, LUAS light rail, DART suburban trains), though also subject to heavy road traffic.
  - **Environmental conditions:** Central Dublin records periodic exceedances of NO2 and particulate matter, largely from vehicle emissions and some solid fuel use[^2].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Gaps in real-time, localized air quality data for campus and immediate environs.
  - Limited evidence base to inform sustainability and transport policies at TCD.
  - Need for hands-on, data-driven environmental education for students and local schools.
- **Triggering Events:** 
  - Building on TCD’s prior Tobacco-Free Campus initiative, which highlighted campus wellbeing as a strategic priority[^1].
  - Citywide and national policy shifts prioritizing clean air (aligned with the EU’s and Ireland’s Clean Air Strategy)[^2].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Internal: TCD students, staff, researchers, campus operations.
  - External: Local schools, Globe Ireland Citizen Science, Safe Routes to School, broader Dublin community.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** University-led, with input from city and national environmental policy frameworks.
- **Relevant Policies:** Dublin City Council’s climate and transport strategies; Ireland’s Clean Air Strategy (targets WHO air quality guidelines by 2040)[^2].
- **Legal/Institutional Enablers and Barriers:** No significant legal barriers for campus-based sensor deployment; data privacy and public communication governed by university policy.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:** 
  - Enhance campus air quality through evidence-based monitoring and interventions.
  - Support sustainable transport and urban planning on and around campus.
  - Provide a living lab for environmental education and citizen science[^1].
- **Vision Statement:** To establish Trinity as a model for healthy, sustainable urban campuses by leveraging data-driven environmental management and broad community engagement.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Directly supports TCD’s Sustainable Transport Plan and the national strategy for clean air and public health[^1][^2].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** 
  - Problem: Inadequate localized air quality data and lack of real-time evidence for policy.
  - Solution: Deploy networked sensors and NOx tubes; engage campus and local community; analyze and share data openly; inform interventions and planning.
  - Outcomes: Cleaner air, improved health, policy integration, educational enrichment.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Campus users (students, staff), local schools, broader community, and policy-makers.
- **Innovation Elements:** 
  - Integration of solar-powered, low-cost sensors and NOx tubes.
  - Open data platforms.
  - Participatory, living lab approach including educational outreach[^1].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** Alignment with public health and sustainability agendas.
- **Economic:** Value-added to campus attractiveness and operational efficiency.
- **Social:** Student and staff wellbeing, community partnership, environmental justice.
- **Environmental:** Mitigate pollution exposure, support climate goals.
- **Technological:** Advances in low-cost, IoT-based monitoring devices.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Living Lab model—real-world experimentation, participatory research, open innovation.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Sensors sited across high-traffic and sensitive campus locations (gates, main buildings, childcare, science and arts zones)[^1].
- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Phase 1: Planning, procurement, stakeholder engagement.
  - Phase 2: Sensor installation (July 2025–June 2026).
  - Phase 3: Data collection, analysis, outreach, and iterative policy feedback.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - **PurpleAir sensors:** Real-time particulate matter monitoring.
  - **NOx test tubes:** Passive diffusion tubes for nitrogen oxides sampling.
  - **Solar power:** Off-grid, sustainable sensor operation.
  - **Open data platform:** Public access to air quality data.
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (sensors, solar); social (living lab, community engagement); process (integrated data-policy loop).
- **Integration Approach:** Sensors linked to central data platform; data feeds into campus sustainability planning and public dashboards[^1].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** University-managed procurement; advisory input from established citizen science partners.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Ongoing collaboration with students, staff, and local schools; public communication via events and open data.
- **Participatory Elements:** Students and staff involved in data collection and analysis; outreach to school groups for hands-on science learning.
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by sustainability, travel, and healthy campus officers under academic leadership[^1].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** Not publicly disclosed; likely modest (equipment, installation, operational support).
- **Funding Mix:** University internal funds; in-kind support/advice from external partners.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** None; open data model.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Low operational costs; integration with ongoing campus sustainability functions.

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:** Sensor calibration and maintenance; ensuring data reliability and accuracy in a complex urban microclimate.
- **Financial:** Limited external funding; need to optimize value-for-money.
- **Social and Political:** Sustaining engagement and participation among diverse stakeholders.
- **Administrative:** Coordination across academic, operational, and community actors.
- **Environmental:** Possible vandalism or weather-related sensor damage.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Regular sensor maintenance schedules; use of robust, field-tested technologies (PurpleAir, NOx tubes).
- **Design Modifications:** Iterative selection of sensor sites based on pilot data and stakeholder feedback.
- **Lessons Learned:** Importance of clear communication, open data for trust, and educational partnerships for lasting impact[^1].
