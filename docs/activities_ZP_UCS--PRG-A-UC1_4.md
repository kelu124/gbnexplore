# Executive summary## ASCEND Smart Energy Neighborhood Demonstration

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** ASCEND Smart Energy Neighborhood Demonstration
- **Alternative Names:** ASCEND Project, Positive Clean Energy District (PCED) Prague
- **Location:** Dolní Počernice, Prague, Czech Republic
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.093°N, 14.587°E (center of Dolní Počernice)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Launch: 2023  
  - Construction/Implementation: 2023–2028 (ongoing)  
  - Key Milestones: Energy Master Plan developed (2023–2024), district buildout (2024–2028), first occupancy and data collection (from 2025 onward)
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: 13 hectares (of which 4 hectares are green spaces)  
  - Residential units: 550  
  - Inhabitants: ~1,300  
  - Social amenities: primary school, public spaces, mobility infrastructure[^1][^2]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - City of Prague (Prague Development Company - PDS)  
  - Czech Technical University (CVUT UCEEB)  
  - Municipal agencies: Operátor ICT, Environmental Protection Department
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Horizon Europe (EU Research and Innovation Program)  
  - City of Prague municipal budget  
  - Potential co-funding from private sector and energy community investments[^3][^4]

#### **Executive Summary**
ASCEND is a flagship EU project piloting the transformation of Dolní Počernice, a district in Prague, into a **Positive Clean Energy District (PCED)**—an urban neighborhood where annual renewable energy production exceeds consumption. The initiative is a cornerstone of Prague’s climate and smart city ambitions, integrating advanced technologies (PV microgrids, heat pumps, seasonal storage) with an innovative energy master plan, participatory approaches, and a social mix strategy.

The project aims to:
- Reduce heat demand by 30% and electricity demand by 25% compared to business-as-usual scenarios.
- Meet all energy needs using renewables, with a strong focus on electrification, district-scale energy management, and data-driven optimization.
- Embed smart systems for comprehensive monitoring and control, leveraging Prague’s urban data platform.
- Foster an inclusive, affordable, and replicable model for sustainable neighborhoods—addressing environmental, economic, and social goals.

ASCEND’s significance lies in its demonstration of scalable solutions for climate-neutral urban living, its commitment to social equity (through social mix and rent regulation), and its position as a replicable model for cities across Europe. The project’s outcomes include new governance models, open data tools, and guidelines for replicating PCEDs in other Prague districts and beyond[^1][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:**  
  - Prague is the capital of the Czech Republic with a population of ~1.3 million.  
  - It is a political, economic, and cultural hub, with a strong service sector, advanced knowledge economy, and rich historical heritage.  
  - As one of Central Europe’s most prosperous cities, Prague faces rising housing demand, energy transition pressures, and the need to balance growth with sustainability[^5].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics (Dolní Počernice):**  
  - Historically a peripheral, low-density area with agricultural and residential land uses.  
  - Current land use: mixture of green space, low-rise housing, and underdeveloped plots slated for urbanization.  
  - Demographic profile: target is a diverse social mix, with new inhabitants representing a cross-section of incomes and ages.  
  - Infrastructure: limited existing utilities, necessitating new investment in energy, transport, and community services.  
  - Environmental: relatively high ecological value due to extensive green spaces, but also subject to urban development pressures[^1][^2].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Prague’s dependence on fossil energy and centralized heating systems.  
  - High energy costs and emissions from existing building stock.  
  - Lack of integrated, district-scale energy solutions.  
  - Housing shortages and rising rents, risking social exclusion.

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU climate targets and funding opportunities (Horizon Europe, Climate-Neutral Cities Mission).  
  - Prague’s strategic commitment to climate neutrality and smart city innovation[^3][^5].  
  - Local pressure for affordable housing and sustainable urbanization.

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City agencies (PDS, Operátor ICT, environmental, planning departments),  
  - Academia (Czech Technical University),  
  - Local residents and future tenants,  
  - Energy providers, mobility operators, and technology suppliers[^1][^2][^6].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by the City of Prague (municipal ownership and oversight).  
  - Coordination through the Smart Prague Office and the Prague Development Company.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Smart Prague 2030 strategy (sustainability, innovation, digitalization)[^5].  
  - Metropolitan Plan (urban growth management, mixed-use development).  
  - EU directives on energy efficiency, renewable energy, and positive energy districts.

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive regulatory framework for experimental urban districts.  
  - Complexity in integrating energy communities within Czech legal structures.  
  - Barriers: potential conflicts with heritage preservation, procurement constraints, and social housing regulations[^1][^3][^5].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Demonstrate a scalable model for climate-neutral, smart neighborhoods powered by renewables.  
  - Achieve deep reductions in energy demand and carbon emissions.  
  - Ensure affordability, inclusivity, and replicability for future districts[^1][^3][^4].

- **Vision Statement:**  
  - "To accelerate the transition to positive, clean energy districts that deliver environmental, economic, and social value for all Prague residents, serving as a lighthouse for European cities"[^3][^4].

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Central to Prague’s climate action plan and Smart Prague 2030 strategy.  
  - Aligned with EU missions for climate-neutral and smart cities.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:**  
  - If a district is designed with advanced energy planning, integrated renewables, and smart controls, then it can achieve net-positive energy status while improving affordability and quality of life.  
  - Data-driven tools and participatory governance ensure the model is robust and adaptable.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - New residents (mixed social groups), local government (reduced emissions/costs), broader city (replicable model), and the smart city community.

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Combination of passive building standards, district-scale PV microgrids, seasonal storage, and digital twin/data analytics.  
  - Integration of energy, mobility, and social systems at neighborhood scale[^1][^2].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:**  
  - City leadership’s climate-neutrality pledge, EU funding incentives.

- **Economic:**  
  - Urban growth pressures, need for cost-effective housing and energy solutions.

- **Social:**  
  - Commitment to social mix, rent regulation, and quality of life.

- **Environmental:**  
  - EU Green Deal, local air quality, and emissions reduction imperatives.

- **Technological:**  
  - Availability of mature smart grid, storage, and data management technologies[^1][^7][^5].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Positive Clean Energy District (PCED) model: district-level balance of renewable production and demand reduction.  
  - Urban design prioritizes walkability, green space, and mixed-use development.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - 550 residential units, primary school, pedestrian/public spaces, bus terminal.  
  - 15,000 m² of pedestrian roads, 4 ha green space, integrated mobility solutions[^1][^2].

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Planning, data collection, master plan (2023–2024).  
  - Phase 2: Infrastructure/building construction (2024–2028).  
  - Ongoing: Data monitoring, resident engagement, replication guidelines[^1][^2][^3].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Rooftop and district-scale PV microgrids.  
  - High-efficiency heat pumps, combined heat and power (CHP) units, and seasonal thermal storage.  
  - Passive and near-zero energy building standards.  
  - Smart meters, digital twin for real-time management, urban data platform integration (Golemio)[^1][^2][^7].

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (energy, ICT), social (energy communities, co-creation), governance (municipal ownership, participatory planning).

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - All energy, mobility, and data flows monitored and optimized through Golemio platform.  
  - Centralized energy planning, but with decentralized participation via energy community structures[^2][^7].

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - City-led, with public procurement for construction and technology providers.  
  - Academic partners (CTU UCEEB) provided planning and technical expertise[^1][^2][^6].

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Participatory design workshops for energy and mobility planning.  
  - Target group framing for social mix and tenant relations[^1][^2].

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Public engagement in planning, digital feedback channels, and data transparency.

- **Project Management:**  
  - Led by PDS with oversight by Smart Prague Office and city departments.  
  - Work packages aligned with Horizon Europe protocols[^3][^4].

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not all specific figures public; typical PCED investments run €30–50 million for this scale.  
  - Cost breakdown: construction, energy infrastructure, ICT/data, social amenities.

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Major funding from Horizon Europe and city budget.  
  - Potential for energy community or private sector co-investment in renewables[^3][^4].

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Municipal ownership allows rent regulation.  
  - Savings from energy efficiency, revenues from surplus renewable generation, possible user fees for mobility services.

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term savings from reduced energy costs and optimized operation.  
  - Replication to other districts intended to leverage economies of scale[^1][^3][^4].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:**  
  - Integrating new energy systems into a greenfield development.  
  - Modeling and forecasting district-wide energy flows with high renewables penetration.

- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront capital costs, balancing affordability with advanced technologies.

- **Social and Political:**  
  - Ensuring true social mix and avoiding gentrification.  
  - Engaging residents in energy behavior change.

- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination across municipal departments and with EU partners.  
  - Navigating procurement and legal constraints on energy communities[^1][^3][^6].

- **Environmental:**  
  - Managing construction impacts, protecting green spaces.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Parametric and scenario-based planning to optimize energy systems before construction.  
  - Ongoing stakeholder engagement and feedback loops.

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Preference for passive solutions to reduce reliance on complex systems.  
  - Adaptive phasing to allow learning and adjustments[^1][^2].

- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Early and continuous data collection is critical.  
  - Social engagement must be embedded from the outset to ensure acceptance of innovative solutions[^1][^2][^6].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- **Ambitious integration** of energy, mobility, and social planning at district scale.
- **Municipal ownership** ensures long-term affordability and replicability.
- **Advanced data and digital systems** enable optimization and transparency.
- **Clear alignment** with city, national, and EU policy frameworks[^1][^3][^4].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- **High initial investment** and technical complexity may limit replicability in less-resourced contexts.
- **Social mix** may be challenged as market pressures mount post-completion.
- **Legal and administrative hurdles** in establishing energy communities.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Strong commitment to **social inclusion** and affordability, but ongoing vigilance required to prevent gentrification.
- **Participatory process** helps address equity, but effectiveness depends on sustained engagement.

#### **Future Outlook**
- **Long-term trajectory** is positive, with planned expansion of PCED principles to other districts.
- **Ongoing challenges** include maintaining social mix, ensuring technology robustness, and scaling lessons learned.
- **Planned evolution:** Iterative improvement via data feedback, policy learning, and adaptation to new EU and city targets[^1][^2][^3].
