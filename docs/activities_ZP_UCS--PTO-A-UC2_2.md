# Executive summary

## FUN Porto Urban Forest & BioSpots Network

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** FUN Porto Urban Forest & BioSpots Network  
- **Alternative Names:** Native Urban Woodlands in Porto (Florestas Urbanas Nativas do Porto), FUN Porto, BioSpots Network  
- **Location:** Porto, Portugal  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 41.1579° N, 8.6291° W  
- **Timeline:**  
  - **Start Date:** 2013 (FUN Porto launched)  
  - **Key Milestones:**  
    - 2013: Launch of FUN Porto and Native Tree Nursery  
    - 2014–2019: Production of 75,000+ native trees and shrubs  
    - 2021: Distribution of 6,000+ trees to residents; expansion of green corridors  
    - Ongoing: Fourth monitoring campaign of BioSpots Network (as of 2023)  
  - **Status:** Ongoing  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Over 455 hectares of public green space (with 160 hectares under expansion)  
  - 6,000+ trees distributed to residents  
  - 75,000+ native trees and shrubs grown in municipal nursery (2014–2019)  
  - 22 m² of green area per inhabitant (public and private with public access)  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Porto Municipality (Environmental Planning and Management Department)  
  - Catholic University of Portugal (research and monitoring partner)  
  - FUTURO Project (regional ecological restoration network)  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Municipal budget  
  - European Union (H2020 and LIFE funding for nature-based solutions)  
  - Public-private partnerships and local grants  

---

#### **Executive Summary**

The FUN Porto Urban Forest & BioSpots Network is a pioneering urban greening initiative in Porto, Portugal, designed to reverse decades of green space loss and ecological fragmentation. Launched in 2013, the project focuses on the densification and connectivity of green spaces—parks, gardens, woodlands, and underutilized urban plots—through the planting of native species and the creation of ecological corridors. The BioSpots Network specifically targets vacant or marginal urban land, such as road verges and unused plots, transforming them into biodiverse green nodes that improve air quality, reduce urban heat, and enhance landscape aesthetics.

Central to the initiative is the Native Tree Nursery, which produces and distributes indigenous trees to both public spaces and private gardens. The project also emphasizes public engagement, offering trees to residents and supporting community-led greening. By 2021, over 75,000 native trees and shrubs had been grown, and more than 6,000 trees distributed to families. The initiative has contributed to climate adaptation, biodiversity conservation, and improved urban resilience, earning Porto recognition as a Tree City of the World. FUN Porto exemplifies how cities can integrate ecological restoration, community participation, and strategic planning to create healthier, more sustainable urban environments.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Porto is Portugal’s second-largest city, with a population of approximately 230,000 (metropolitan area: 1.7 million). It is a historic port city with a strong cultural and economic identity, serving as a regional hub for commerce, tourism, and higher education. The city is situated along the Douro River, with a mix of historic urban fabric, modern infrastructure, and peri-urban green spaces. Porto’s economy is diversified, with strengths in tourism, services, and creative industries.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The initiative spans the entire city, with particular focus on areas experiencing high urbanization pressure, such as the city center, riverfront, and peri-urban districts. Many neighborhoods have a mix of historic buildings, dense housing, and fragmented green spaces. The city’s spatial structure is marked by a legacy of 19th-century expansion, followed by 20th-century densification, which has led to the loss of green cover and ecological connectivity.

- **Demographic Profile:**  
  Porto’s population is aging, with a significant proportion of residents over 65. Income levels are moderate, with pockets of economic vulnerability. The city is culturally diverse, with a growing number of international residents and students.

- **Existing Infrastructure:**  
  The city has a well-developed public transportation network, but many neighborhoods lack direct access to green spaces. Infrastructure quality varies, with some areas facing challenges related to aging buildings and limited public amenities.

- **Environmental Conditions:**  
  Porto faces typical urban environmental challenges, including air pollution, urban heat island effect, and loss of biodiversity. The city is also vulnerable to climate change impacts, such as increased temperatures and extreme weather events.

---

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Rapid urbanization and loss of green spaces (from over 75% in 1892 to less than 30% by 2000)  
  - Fragmentation and discontinuity of green areas, reducing ecological functionality  
  - Decline in native biodiversity and increase in exotic species  
  - Poor air quality and urban heat island effect  
  - Limited public access to green spaces, especially in dense urban areas  
  - Lack of community engagement in urban greening

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Growing awareness of climate change and urban sustainability  
  - Public demand for healthier, more livable urban environments  
  - Policy shifts toward nature-based solutions and ecological restoration  
  - Recognition of the health and well-being benefits of urban green spaces

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - **Porto Municipality:** Primary driver, responsible for planning, implementation, and funding  
  - **Catholic University of Portugal:** Research and monitoring partner  
  - **Residents and community groups:** Key beneficiaries and participants  
  - **Local businesses and NGOs:** Partners in greening and environmental education  
  - **Regional and national government:** Policy and regulatory support

---

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  The initiative is led by the Porto Municipality, with coordination across departments (environment, urban planning, public works). The Catholic University provides scientific oversight and monitoring.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Porto’s Municipal Master Plan (targeting doubling of public green areas)  
  - Roadmap for a Circular Porto by 2030  
  - National and EU policies on climate adaptation, biodiversity, and urban sustainability

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - **Enablers:** Strong municipal commitment, EU funding, supportive national policies  
  - **Barriers:** Limited availability of land for expansion, challenges in mobilizing private landowners, bureaucratic delays

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  The initiative is guided by principles of ecological connectivity, biodiversity conservation, and community participation. The design emphasizes the creation of green corridors and the integration of nature into the urban fabric.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Expansion and rehabilitation of parks, gardens, and woodlands  
  - Creation of BioSpots on vacant and underutilized urban land  
  - Installation of native trees and shrubs in public and private spaces

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1 (2013–2015): Launch of FUN Porto and Native Tree Nursery  
  - Phase 2 (2015–2019): Expansion of green spaces and distribution of trees  
  - Phase 3 (2019–present): Monitoring, evaluation, and further expansion

---

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Native tree planting and ecological landscape design  
  - Public engagement platforms (applications for tree distribution)  
  - Monitoring and evaluation tools (survival rates, biodiversity assessments)

- **Innovation Type:**

- **Integration Approach:**  
  Technologies are integrated with existing urban systems through municipal planning and community engagement.
