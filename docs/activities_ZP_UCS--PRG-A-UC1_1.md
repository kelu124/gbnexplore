# Executive summary## Residential Building in Prague Monitors Energy Consumption via M-Bus

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Residential Building in Prague Monitors Energy Consumption via M-Bus  
- **Alternative Names:** Prague Condominium Energy Monitoring Project  
- **Location:** Prague, Czech Republic (specific district undisclosed)  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Not publicly specified; centrally located within Prague  
- **Timeline:** Initiated in 2024; full implementation and operation confirmed by April 2025; ongoing  
- **Project Scale:** 20 privately owned apartments plus common areas (serving ~60–80 residents); building footprint ~1,500–2,000 m²; investment value undisclosed  
- **Leading Organization(s):** Condominium Association (Homeowners’ community); HW server (technology distributor); HW group (technology supplier)  
- **Funding Sources:** Privately funded by the condominium association, possibly with partial support from residents’ fees  

#### **Executive Summary**
This case study examines a pioneering **energy consumption monitoring project** in a Prague residential building, reflecting the shift toward data-driven, sustainable urban living. The initiative, led by a proactive condominium association, deployed a **Perseus Energy 285 device** connected to 23 M-Bus electricity meters—20 for individual apartments and three for common areas. Data is continuously transmitted to a secure cloud portal (HWportal.cz, based on SensDesk Technology), enabling both **real-time and historical monitoring**. Each resident can privately access their own consumption, while administrators have a comprehensive building-level overview. Automated monthly reports facilitate trend analysis, early detection of inefficiencies, and informed decision-making.

The project’s core objective is to **reduce energy waste and greenhouse gas emissions** by equipping residents and managers with actionable insights. The approach supports both individual behavioral change and collective management, enhancing transparency and accountability. Key challenges addressed include ensuring **data privacy**, configuring access permissions, and integrating diverse hardware and software systems. Outcomes to date include measurable reductions in energy use, improved cost efficiency for residents, and greater awareness of sustainable practices. The Prague case demonstrates the **scalability and replicability** of intelligent submetering, offering a model for similar urban communities across Europe and beyond[^1].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Guided by smart building and green neighborhood principles, emphasizing transparency, efficiency, and user engagement.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Installation of the Perseus Energy 285 device within the building’s main switchboard  
  - Wiring to 23 M-Bus meters (one per apartment, three for common areas)  
  - LTE & LAN connectivity for data transmission[^1]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - *Phase 1:* Needs assessment, technology selection, resident consultation  
  - *Phase 2:* Hardware installation, software configuration  
  - *Phase 3:* Resident onboarding, training, system go-live  
  - *Phase 4:* Ongoing monitoring and reporting

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Perseus Energy 285 monitoring device  
  - 23 M-Bus electricity meters  
  - SensDesk Technology cloud platform (HWportal.cz)  
  - LTE & LAN for data transfer and redundancy

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (IoT submetering, cloud analytics)  
  - Governance (granular access rights, privacy-by-design)

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Meters connected via wired M-Bus protocol to the Perseus device, which aggregates and transmits data to the portal  
  - Portal manages user accounts, permissions, and reporting

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Technology procured through HW server (Czech distributor), leveraging established supplier relationships

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Resident information sessions to explain benefits and privacy protections  
  - Feedback mechanisms for ongoing improvement

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Residents provided input on reporting formats and data access  
  - Customizable user permissions

- **Project Management:**  
  - Led by the condominium administrator, with technical support from HW server and HW group

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Capital investment in meters, monitoring device, and installation (proprietary; likely shared among owners)  
  - Ongoing costs for cloud portal subscription and maintenance

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Fully private (condominium reserve funds, resident contributions)

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Energy cost savings accrue directly to residents  
  - No external revenue streams or subsidies

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Lower operational costs and energy bills expected to offset initial investment over several years
