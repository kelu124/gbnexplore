# Executive summary

## Social Green – Green Renovation for Social Housing (Interreg Europe)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Social Green – Regional Policies towards Greening the Social Housing Sector
- **Alternative Names:** Social Green Project, Interreg Europe Social Green
- **Location:** Multi-city initiative across six EU countries: Portugal, Spain, Croatia, Estonia, Sweden, and Romania. Kick-off meeting held in Porto, Portugal.
- **Geographic Coordinates:** N/A (multi-regional, no single geographic point)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Launch: October 2025 (kick-off in Porto, Portugal)  
  - Duration: 4 years (2025–2029)  
  - Status: Ongoing
- **Project Scale:**  
  - 8 partner organizations  
  - Covers multiple regions and cities in 6 EU countries  
  - Focuses on policy development, pilot renovations, and knowledge transfer rather than a single physical site  
  - Estimated impact: thousands of social housing units across partner regions
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Nordregio (lead partner and scientific/technical advisor)  
  - Municipalities, energy agencies, and Managing Authorities from partner countries
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Interreg Europe Programme (European Regional Development Fund)  
  - National and regional public funds  
  - In-kind contributions from partner organizations

#### **Executive Summary**
The Social Green project is a four-year Interreg Europe initiative launched in October 2025, aiming to accelerate the greening of the social housing sector across six EU countries. The project brings together eight partners from Portugal, Spain, Croatia, Estonia, Sweden, and Romania to develop and implement regional policies that promote energy efficiency, reduce greenhouse gas emissions, and combat fuel poverty in social housing. By fostering interregional cooperation, mutual learning, and the transfer of innovative methodologies, Social Green supports the renovation of existing buildings and the sustainable construction of new social housing. The initiative emphasizes stakeholder engagement, data-driven policy development, and the creation of scalable best practices. Its outcomes include improved living standards for vulnerable populations, reduced energy costs, and significant environmental benefits, positioning Social Green as a model for sustainable urban development and equitable climate action in Europe.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:**  
  The project operates across diverse urban and peri-urban contexts in six EU countries. Partner cities include Porto (Portugal), Madrid (Spain), Zagreb (Croatia), Tallinn (Estonia), Stockholm (Sweden), and Bucharest (Romania). These cities range from large metropolitan centers to mid-sized regional capitals, each with unique demographic, economic, and environmental profiles. Common characteristics include aging housing stock, rising energy costs, and increasing pressure to meet EU climate targets.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The initiative targets social housing neighborhoods, which are typically characterized by:
  - High concentration of low-income households
  - Older, less energy-efficient buildings
  - Limited access to green spaces and sustainable infrastructure
  - Diverse ethnic and cultural backgrounds
  - Varying levels of infrastructure quality, with some areas facing challenges in accessibility and maintenance
- **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:**  
  Many partner cities face climate-related challenges such as heatwaves, flooding, and air pollution. Social housing districts often have higher vulnerability to these risks due to poor building quality and limited adaptive capacity.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High levels of energy poverty among social housing residents
  - Inadequate energy performance of existing social housing stock
  - Lack of coordinated regional policies for green renovations
  - Limited stakeholder engagement in policy development
  - Insufficient funding and technical expertise for large-scale renovations
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU Green Deal and Fit for 55 targets
  - Rising energy prices and cost-of-living crises
  - Previous success of the RE-GREEN project (2012–2014), which laid the groundwork for Social Green
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Municipalities and local authorities
  - Social housing providers
  - Energy agencies
  - Resident associations
  - NGOs and community organizations
  - National and regional policymakers

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  The project operates within the Interreg Europe Programme, which supports cross-border cooperation and policy learning. Each partner region has its own governance structures for housing and energy policy.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - EU Green Deal
  - Energy Efficiency Directive
  - Renewable Energy Directive
  - Urban Agenda for the EU
  - National and regional housing and climate strategies
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Enablers: EU funding, supportive national policies, existing networks of energy agencies and municipalities
  - Barriers: Fragmented regulatory frameworks, limited coordination between sectors, bureaucratic hurdles in accessing funding

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  The project is guided by principles of sustainable urban development, social inclusion, and climate resilience. It emphasizes policy innovation, stakeholder engagement, and the transfer of best practices.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  The initiative focuses on the renovation of existing social housing and the sustainable construction of new units. Key interventions include energy efficiency retrofits, green building techniques, and the integration of renewable energy systems.
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  The project is structured in four phases:
  1. Policy analysis and benchmarking
  2. Development of regional self-assessment reports and action plans
  3. Implementation of pilot renovations and policy changes
  4. Evaluation, knowledge transfer, and scaling up

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Energy efficiency retrofits (insulation, windows, heating systems)
  - Green building techniques (sustainable materials, passive design)
  - Digital platforms for stakeholder engagement and data-driven policy development
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: Energy-efficient technologies and digital tools
  - Social: Stakeholder engagement and participatory planning
  - Governance: Interregional policy learning and transfer
- **Integration Approach:**  
  Technologies are integrated with existing urban systems through pilot projects, policy changes, and capacity-building activities.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Implementers are selected through competitive processes and in-kind contributions from partner organizations.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  The project involves residents, municipalities, energy agencies, and NGOs in policy development and implementation. Engagement platforms facilitate dialogue and co-creation.
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  Residents are involved in decision-making processes, particularly in the design and implementation of pilot renovations.
- **Project Management:**
