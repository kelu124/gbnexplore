# Executive summary## LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN: Horta das Figueiras School Green Roof

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN – Horta das Figueiras School Green Roof
- **Alternative Names:** LIFE mBiG; LIFE17 CCA/ES/000088; EB1 Horta das Figueiras Green Roof
- **Location:** Évora, Horta das Figueiras district, Alentejo, Portugal
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 38.5705° N, 7.9137° W
- **Timeline:**
  - Project selection: 2019
  - Installation: 2021 (final works completed in last project month)
  - After-LIFE monitoring: 2022–2028[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Three pilot buildings (Spain and Portugal), total 13,642 m² surface area, 3,911.61 m² new green areas; Évora’s school roof: ~600 m²[^1][^2]
  - Population: Primary school students, staff, and surrounding neighborhood
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - Royal Botanic Garden (RJB-CSIC, Spain)
  - CARTIF Foundation (Spain)
  - Câmara Municipal de Évora (Évora City Council)
  - CIMAC – Comunidade Intermunicipal do Alentejo Central[^3][^4]
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - European Union LIFE Programme (main grant)
  - Local public funds (municipal and regional government)
  - In-kind contributions from partners[^1][^5]

#### **Executive Summary**
The Horta das Figueiras School Green Roof is a flagship demonstration site under the LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN project, a European Union-funded initiative focused on advancing nature-based solutions (NBS) for climate adaptation in educational and social buildings. Located in Évora, Portugal—a city facing intensifying heat waves and water stress—the project retrofitted the mid-20th-century school with an extensive green roof and green facades, integrating environmental sensors for real-time monitoring. The primary objectives are to reduce indoor thermal stress, enhance energy efficiency, and improve urban biodiversity, transforming the school into a living laboratory for climate resilience. The initiative aligns with broader municipal and EU policies on sustainable building and climate adaptation, serving as a replicable model for Southern European cities with similar climatic and infrastructural challenges. Key outcomes include improved thermal comfort and learning environments, lower energy bills, increased flora and fauna diversity, and active engagement of students and staff in environmental education. Implementation challenges—mainly linked to retrofitting aged infrastructure and ensuring long-term maintenance—have been addressed through multi-stakeholder collaboration and capacity building. The project’s impact is amplified by its integration into local governance and its role in influencing regulations and construction standards for urban resilience.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Évora is a UNESCO World Heritage city with ~56,000 residents, recognized for its historic core, educational institutions, and service-based economy. It is located in the hot, dry Alentejo region, characterized by warm summers, water scarcity, and increasing vulnerability to climate change[^4].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - *Historical Development:* Horta das Figueiras is a peripheral, post-war expansion area with a mix of residential, educational, and recreational uses.
  - *Land Use:* Predominantly low-rise multifamily housing, schools, and modest green spaces.
  - *Demographics:* Mixed-income population, with a significant proportion of families and children.
  - *Infrastructure Quality:* Aging school buildings, generally fair accessibility; some underinvestment in modern energy and water systems.
  - *Environmental Conditions:* Exposed to urban heat island effects, low vegetation cover, and periodic drought.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - High indoor temperatures during spring and summer, leading to thermal discomfort and reduced student performance[^3].
  - High energy costs for cooling in a context of limited school budgets.
  - Lack of biodiversity and green space, exacerbating urban heat and ecological fragmentation.
- **Triggering Events:** 
  - EU policy push for climate adaptation (post-2016 Paris Agreement)
  - Notable heat wave events and increased frequency of extreme weather in Southern Portugal[^6]
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Local authority (Câmara Municipal de Évora): owner/operator, policy driver.
  - School community: users and beneficiaries, also co-designers via participatory activities.
  - Technical partners (RJB-CSIC, CARTIF): design, monitoring, and capacity-building roles.
  - Regional and national policy actors: observers, regulators, and potential replicators.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** 
  - Multi-level governance: municipal ownership, regional coordination (CIMAC), EU-level funding and oversight[^4].
- **Relevant Policies:**
  - EU LIFE Programme climate adaptation strategies.
  - National/municipal building codes (Decreto-Lei n.º 40/90, Technical Building Code).
  - Regional sustainability and urban greening plans.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
  - Supportive: EU and local climate adaptation policies, willingness to pilot innovative solutions, and public sector engagement.
  - Barriers: Outdated building regulations insufficiently covering NBS; technical challenges in retrofitting older buildings[^1][^7].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:**
  - Enhance climate resilience and energy efficiency in educational buildings via nature-based solutions[^1][^5].
  - Improve well-being and comfort for students and staff.
  - Demonstrate replicable models for integrating NBS in urban environments.
- **Vision Statement:** 
  - To transform schools into climate-resilient, healthy, and educationally enriching environments by applying innovative, nature-based adaptations.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**
  - Supports Évora’s urban sustainability and green infrastructure plans.
  - Contributes to EU climate adaptation and biodiversity strategies.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** 
  - By retrofitting school roofs and facades with living vegetation and smart monitoring, the project reduces indoor overheating, lowers energy consumption, and creates urban habitat, leading to improved learning conditions, operational savings, and stronger community engagement.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** 
  - Primary: School students, teachers, and staff.
  - Secondary: Families, local community, municipal authorities, and broader urban ecosystem.
- **Innovation Elements:**
  - Integration of extensive green roofs/facades with environmental sensors.
  - Participatory design and educational activities.
  - Use of the school as a demonstration and policy-influencing pilot[^1][^3].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:** Commitment to EU climate targets; local government leadership in adaptation.
- **Economic:** Desire to reduce operational costs and attract investment in sustainable infrastructure.
- **Social:** Improve student health and educational outcomes; foster environmental awareness.
- **Environmental:** Respond to increasing heat stress and biodiversity loss; manage stormwater.
- **Technological:** Leverage advancements in NBS, green roof systems, and digital monitoring tools.

---

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Guided by climate adaptation, ecosystem-based urbanism, and participatory design principles.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Installation of an extensive green roof system (mBiGTray prototype) on the flat roof of the primary school[^8].
  - Green facades for additional shading and biodiversity.
  - Placement of environmental sensors for real-time monitoring inside and outside classrooms[^3].
- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Phase 1: Baseline assessment (2019–2020)
  - Phase 2: Design and procurement (2020)
  - Phase 3: Installation and commissioning (2021)
  - Phase 4: After-LIFE monitoring and dissemination (2022–2028)[^1][^2]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Modular green roof trays (mBiGTray), selected for ease of installation and maintenance.
  - Green facade systems anchored to existing walls.
  - Environmental monitoring: temperature, humidity, CO₂, and rainwater infiltration sensors.
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (NBS, sensors), social (participatory education), governance (policy integration).
- **Integration Approach:** 
  - Harmonized with school operations and municipal maintenance.
  - Data-sharing platforms for real-time performance tracking.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Public tendering for green roof/facade systems; technical support from research partners.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** 
  - Extensive involvement of school community in design, planting, and monitoring.
  - Workshops and practical lessons for students and teachers[^7].
- **Participatory Elements:**
  - Co-design of outdoor spaces.
  - Student-led biodiversity monitoring and data collection.
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by RJB-CSIC and CARTIF Foundation, with regular joint meetings and reporting to LIFE Programme officials[^5].

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** LIFE grant is the principal funding source (total project ~€1.8M across all pilots); Évora pilot estimated at €200,000–€300,000[^1][^2].
- **Funding Mix:** 
  - 60–70% EU LIFE grant
  - 30–40% local/regional government and in-kind partner contributions
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** No direct revenue; cost savings accrue through reduced energy use.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Long-term monitoring and maintenance by municipal authorities; plans to leverage savings for ongoing upkeep.

---

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:** 
  - Retrofitting onto aged school infrastructure required structural assessment and reinforcement.
  - Ensuring plant survival in a hot, dry climate during establishment phase[^1][^8].
- **Financial:** 
  - Upfront investment costs higher than conventional roofing, though offset by long-term savings.
- **Social and Political:** 
  - Need for buy-in from school leadership and parents; initial skepticism regarding maintenance.
- **Administrative:** 
  - Coordination across transnational project partners.
  - Navigating procurement and compliance with EU LIFE reporting.
- **Environmental:** 
  - Drought risk during plant establishment; risk of summer heatwaves impacting green roof performance.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** 
  - Modular green roof system chosen for speed and ease of retrofitting.
  - Irrigation system and drought-tolerant native plant species selected to ensure survival[^8].
- **Design Modifications:** 
  - Iterative adjustment of plant palette based on local performance.
  - Added educational signage and interactive elements for students.
- **Lessons Learned:** 
  - Early and continuous stakeholder engagement crucial for success.
  - Ongoing monitoring and adaptation to maintenance needs essential.

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:**
  - ~600 m² extensive green roof installed
  - Green facades and environmental sensors deployed
  - Upgraded learning environments[^1][^8][^2]
- **Service Delivery:**
  - Improved thermal comfort in classrooms
  - Enhanced environmental education programming
- **Quantitative Metrics:**
  - No final post-intervention data for Évora as of 2023; results expected by 2026[^1].
  - Simulation and experience from similar pilots: up to 4°C indoor temperature reduction, 10–11% lower energy use for cooling[^1][^3].

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:** 
  - Improved comfort and focus for students and staff.
  - Increased environmental awareness and engagement through hands-on activities[^7].
- **Equity Outcomes:** 
  - Benefits targeted at a public school serving a mixed-income, diverse neighborhood.
- **Displacement/Gentrification:** No negative impacts reported.
- **Social Capital:** 
  - Strengthened ties between school, families, and local authorities.
- **Cultural Impacts:** 
  - Reinforced Évora’s reputation as a center for sustainable innovation.

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:** Temporary employment during installation; potential for new green jobs in urban greening sector.
- **Economic Activity:** Stimulated local suppliers of NBS systems.
- **Property Values:** Likely positive externalities for school and neighborhood, though not directly measured.
- **Cost Savings:** Projected reduction in energy bills; long-term O&M to be covered through savings.
- **Economic Inclusion:** Public sector leadership ensures broad access to benefits.

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate and Emissions:** 
  - Direct reduction in cooling energy demand and associated GHG emissions.
- **Resource Management:** 
  - Improved rainwater infiltration; reduced runoff.
- **Environmental Quality:** 
  - Increased local biodiversity: preliminary data indicate 16 new plant species, 77 new animal species in similar pilots[^1].
- **Waste and Pollution:** 
  - Reduced heat island effect, improved air quality.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** 
  - Model for EU-level NBS standards; potential for national green building certifications.

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Institutional Capacity:** 
  - Enhanced municipal knowledge of NBS implementation.
- **Policy Influence:** 
  - Findings inform updates to building codes and local adaptation strategies.
- **Transparency and Accountability:** 
  - LIFE Programme reporting and open data sharing.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:** 
  - Early results and simulations indicate significant improvements in comfort and energy metrics; full performance data pending ongoing monitoring[^1][^3].
- **Unintended Consequences:** 
  - No significant adverse effects reported; some maintenance learning curve.
- **Long-term Viability:** 
  - Maintenance plans in place; strong community and municipal commitment[^2].

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**
- **Contextual Dependencies:** 
  - Hot, dry climate and flat-roofed, pre-1980s buildings—common in Southern Europe—well suited for this model[^3].
- **Critical Success Factors:** 
  - Strong partnership between technical experts, local authorities, and school community.
  - Appropriate selection of drought-tolerant species and robust monitoring.
- **Scalability:** 
  - Approach can be scaled to other public buildings and districts; modular systems aid replication.

#### **Comparative Analysis**
- **Similar Initiatives:** 
  - Porto and Badajoz pilots under same LIFE project; other EU LIFE and New European Bauhaus projects[^1][^7].
- **Best Practices:** 
  - Participatory co-design, robust monitoring, integration into policy frameworks.
- **Cautionary Lessons:** 
  - Importance of maintenance planning; need for long-term engagement beyond installation phase.

- **Methodological Parallels:**

- **Technological Overlaps:** 
  - Environmental sensors, green roof/facade systems.
- **Transferable Strategies:** 
  - Participatory workshops, multi-level governance, integration into educational programming.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- Demonstrates technical and social feasibility of retrofitting green roofs in older public schools[^1][^8].
- High degree of stakeholder engagement; strong educational and awareness-raising component[^7].
- Integration with policy and potential for replication at multiple scales.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- Upfront costs and technical complexity of retrofitting.
- Maintenance burden requires ongoing institutional commitment and funding.
- Long-term performance data for Évora still pending; full impact assessment awaits multi-year monitoring[^1].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Prioritizes public education infrastructure serving diverse and vulnerable populations.
- Enhances environmental justice through improved microclimate and health outcomes.

#### **Future Outlook**
- Ongoing monitoring and adaptive management through 2028[^2].
- Potential for further expansion to other municipal buildings and city districts.
- Lessons learned to inform Portuguese and EU policy on NBS integration in building codes and urban planning.
