# Executive summary## Tivoli GreenCity – Sustainable Neighbourhood Impact Evaluation

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Tivoli GreenCity – Sustainable Neighbourhood Impact Evaluation
- **Alternative Names:** Tivoli Sustainable Neighbourhood, Tivoli Ecodistrict
- **Location:** Laeken, Brussels, Belgium (Northwest Brussels, Canal Area)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.8688° N, 4.3463° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - Conception: Early 2010s  
  - Masterplan approval: 2011  
  - Construction: 2016–2019  
  - Impact Study: 2023–2024
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: ~47,000–49,958 m²  
  - Housing Units: 406 (397 delivered, 22 additional by CLTB)  
  - Population affected: >1,000 residents  
  - Investment Value: €100–120 million[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Citydev.brussels (Regional public developer)  
  - MSA Architecture & Urbanism (Masterplan)  
  - Sciences Po Urban School (Impact study)  
  - Greenbizz (business incubator)  
  - Community Land Trust Brussels (CLTB)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Primarily public investment (Citydev.brussels)  
  - Private financing for commercial elements  
  - PPPs for Greenbizz incubator  
  - Subsidies for social and cohousing[^1][^2]

#### **Executive Summary**

Tivoli GreenCity is a pioneering sustainable neighbourhood in northwestern Brussels, developed on a former industrial brownfield in Laeken. Conceived as both a response to urban regeneration needs and a laboratory for climate-resilient urbanism, Tivoli integrates passive housing, rainwater harvesting, urban greenhouses, rooftop vegetable gardens, and a diversity of social and commercial functions. Its development from 2016–2019 marked a major urban transformation, aiming to harmonize environmental performance with social inclusion and economic innovation.

The neighbourhood consists of 406 housing units, including social, subsidized, and cohousing formats, and is supported by robust green infrastructure, public parks, and the Greenbizz incubator for circular economy businesses. An impact study, commissioned by Citydev.brussels and conducted by Sciences Po Urban School, used an open-data matrix of 71 indicators to systematically evaluate outcomes. The study found strong energy efficiency and biodiversity improvements, but also identified challenges in the adoption of shared spaces and sustaining citizen engagement post-occupancy.

Tivoli GreenCity’s open-data evaluation approach and its adaptive management strategies are now guiding future projects in Brussels and beyond. The initiative has received multiple sustainability awards, including BREEAM Communities ‘Outstanding’ certification, and is recognized for its replicable innovations in participatory planning, green building, and urban governance[^3][^4][^2][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Brussels, Belgium’s capital, is a major European metropolis with a population of ~1.2 million in the metro area. It is a multilingual, multicultural hub with significant economic activity in services, government, logistics, and manufacturing. The city faces typical Western European urban challenges: housing shortage, aging infrastructure, social inequality, and environmental pressures[^5].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  Tivoli GreenCity is located in Laeken, a historically working-class district near the Canal and the Port of Brussels. The area has a mix of industrial legacy (brownfields), dense residential blocks, and increasing redevelopment activity.  
  - *Historical patterns*: Industrial zone with fragmentation and limited green space[^2][^5].  
  - *Current land use*: Mixed residential, commercial (Greenbizz), new public parks.  
  - *Demographics*: Diverse, lower-income, multicultural, including both longstanding residents and new arrivals.  
  - *Infrastructure*: Previously degraded, now improved with new roads, slow traffic areas, public transport access.  
  - *Environmental*: Urban heat island effect, air and noise pollution, but also proximity to new green spaces and waterway[^1][^5].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Brownfield contamination and underutilization  
  - Lack of affordable, sustainable housing  
  - Fragmented urban fabric and social segregation  
  - Low biodiversity, poor public space quality  
  - Limited economic opportunities and circular economy infrastructure[^1][^5]
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Regional policy push for canal area regeneration  
  - Political priority for sustainable development (arrival of Minister Huytebroeck, 2009)[^2]  
  - Citydev.brussels strategic acquisition of land (mid-2000s)  
  - Community advocacy for affordable housing
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Citydev.brussels (public developer)  
  - Local authorities (Brussels-Capital Region)  
  - Future residents and community groups  
  - NGOs (Community Land Trust Brussels)  
  - Businesses and start-ups (Greenbizz)  
  - Urban designers and architects

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Brussels-Capital Region’s urban planning authority  
  - Citydev.brussels as project leader  
  - Compliance with BREEAM Communities and EPB standards
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Canal Area regeneration strategy  
  - Sustainable neighbourhoods policy  
  - Social housing and mixed tenure mandate
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Favorable zoning for mixed-use development  
  - Legal requirements for social housing allocation  
  - Institutional support for PPP models  
  - Complexity of brownfield redevelopment (site contamination, permits)[^2][^1]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Create an exemplary sustainable neighbourhood integrating environmental, social, and economic objectives  
  - Requalify a brownfield site, reconnecting fragmented urban areas  
  - Provide high-quality, affordable housing and community amenities  
  - Foster behavioural change towards sustainability among residents[^1]
- **Vision Statement:**  
  “Building the city of tomorrow by harmonizing innovation, environmental performance, and social cohesion.”[^4]
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Brussels Canal Area regeneration  
  - Regional climate adaptation and resilience strategies  
  - National and EU sustainability targets (energy, housing, circular economy)

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By combining passive buildings, green infrastructure, and participatory governance, Tivoli GreenCity seeks to deliver measurable improvements in energy efficiency, social inclusion, and urban biodiversity, fostering a model for sustainable urban living[^3][^4][^1].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Low and middle-income households  
  - Cohousing residents  
  - Local entrepreneurs and circular economy start-ups  
  - Wider Brussels community via open data and transferability[^1]
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Passive housing at scale  
  - Rooftop urban agriculture  
  - Open-data evaluation matrix for neighbourhoods  
  - Mixed tenure and social cohesion focus[^3][^4][^2]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - High-level commitment to climate and social policy  
  - Regional election cycles and leadership priorities[^2]
- **Economic:**  
  - Urban renewal investment opportunities  
  - Incubation of green and circular businesses[^4]
- **Social:**  
  - Advocacy for affordable housing, participatory governance  
  - Cohousing and community land trust experiments[^6]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Regulatory push for energy/global sustainability standards  
  - Urgency of climate adaptation
- **Technological:**  
  - Advances in passive building techniques  
  - Digital tools for impact assessment and open data sharing

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - BREEAM Communities sustainability principles  
  - Urban ecology and biodiversity integration[^2][^7]  
  - Social mix and participatory planning
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Mix of building types (social, subsidized, cohousing)  
  - 10,000 m² of public spaces and parks (Jardin des Justes)  
  - Greenbizz incubator for business  
  - Slow traffic and bicycle-oriented infrastructure  
  - Adaptive reuse of existing structures[^2]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Masterplan approval (2011)  
  - Construction in two major phases (2016, 2019)  
  - Ongoing development of final cohousing and CLTB units

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Passive building standards (high insulation, air tightness, heat recovery)[^2][^1]  
  - Rainwater harvesting and urban heat network[^6]  
  - Rooftop vegetable gardens and greenhouses  
  - Open-data impact evaluation matrix (71 indicators on flows, use, metabolism)[^3][^4]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: Energy, water, waste, mobility systems  
  - Social: Cohousing, participatory design  
  - Governance: Open-data tools, collaborative management
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - New infrastructure embedded in urban fabric  
  - Green spaces and biodiversity corridors linked to surrounding city  
  - Digital tools for monitoring accessible to all stakeholders

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive tender for masterplanning and design[^2]  
  - Partnerships for Greenbizz and CLTB units
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Participatory planning workshops (residents, NGOs)[^6]  
  - Cohousing developed through resident-led co-creation
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Community input in masterplan and shared spaces  
  - Social mix embedded in allocation policies
- **Project Management:**  
  - Citydev.brussels coordinated activities  
  - Scientific committee for impact study (Agnès Bastin, Sciences Po)[^4]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - €100–120 million total investment (including Greenbizz)[^1]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Majority public funds (Citydev.brussels)  
  - Private investment for commercial facilities  
  - PPP for Greenbizz incubator
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Sale and rental of housing units  
  - Commercial rents (Greenbizz, retail)
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term management plan for public spaces and infrastructure  
  - Greenbizz supports ongoing economic activity[^4][^1]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Complexity of passive housing on a large scale  
  - Integration of green infrastructure into dense urban context[^2][^1]
- **Financial:**  
  - High initial capital requirements  
  - Cost management for social housing provision
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Sustaining resident engagement post-occupancy  
  - Incomplete adoption of shared spaces (greenhouses, laundry rooms) due to access and investment misunderstandings[^3][^1]
- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination between multiple stakeholders  
  - Delays due to regulatory approvals and brownfield remediation
- **Environmental:**  
  - Remediation of contaminated soils  
  - Managing urban biodiversity in the face of intensive development

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Use of scientific committee and stakeholder workshops to troubleshoot issues  
  - Iterative adaptation of management for shared spaces
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusting access protocols to communal areas  
  - Responsive programming for public events to foster engagement
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Need for ongoing resident support and education  
  - Importance of flexible, open data tools for monitoring and adjustment[^3][^4]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - 406 housing units delivered (social, subsidized, cohousing)[^1]  
  - Greenbizz incubator with production workshops and FabLab[^4]  
  - 10,000 m² of new public parks and squares  
  - Biodiversity corridors and rooftop gardens[^2]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Eco-crèches, retail outlets, mobility infrastructure  
  - Shared laundry rooms, greenhouses
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 71 indicators tracked in open-data matrix (energy, water, social use)[^3][^4]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved housing quality and affordability  
  - Social cohesion fostered through shared spaces and events  
  - New models for cohousing and participatory governance[^6]
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - High proportion of units for lower-income groups  
  - CLTB units reserved for disadvantaged households
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No major displacement reported; social housing allocation mitigates risk
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened resident networks  
  - Increased civic engagement through participatory processes
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Reinforcement of local identity (urban agriculture, adaptive reuse of heritage structures)

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Construction, ongoing facility management  
  - Start-up incubation through Greenbizz[^4]
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Retail and business growth in district  
  - Increased property values in adjacent zones
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Reduced energy costs for residents (passive housing)[^1]
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Opportunities for local entrepreneurs in circular economy

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Significant reduction in energy use; 35% zero-energy, 70% passive units[^8]  
  - Decreased carbon footprint
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Rainwater harvesting, efficient heating network  
  - Urban farming and biodiversity labs[^2][^6]
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Increased green space and urban biodiversity  
  - Improved air and noise environment
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Construction waste minimized; recycling protocols in place
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - BREEAM Communities ‘Outstanding’ rating (95%)[^2]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened public developer role (Citydev.brussels)  
  - New collaborative mechanisms for neighbourhood management[^4]
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for future Brussels neighbourhoods  
  - Inspiration for open-data, participatory evaluation tools[^3][^4]
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Open-data matrix enables public scrutiny and learning

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Strong performance in energy, biodiversity, and social mix  
  - Some gaps in sustained resident engagement and shared space utilization[^3][^1]
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Challenges in access and management of communal facilities  
  - Need for greater post-occupancy support
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Evidence of lasting positive impacts; continuous adaptation planned[^3][^4]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Technological innovation** (passive housing, urban agriculture)
- **Integrated social and environmental objectives**
- **Robust open-data evaluation and knowledge sharing**
- **Strong public sector leadership and multi-stakeholder engagement**
- **Recognition and awards for sustainability (BREEAM, be.exemplary)**

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Incomplete adoption of some shared spaces** due to access and management issues[^3][^1]
- **Limits in sustained citizen engagement post-occupancy**
- **High initial costs and complexity of large-scale passive standards implementation**

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Strong commitment to social mix and affordability**
- **Community Land Trust model for lowest-income groups**
- **Some risk of uneven benefit distribution if access issues persist**

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Ongoing adaptation and refinement of management strategies**
- **Continuous use and evolution of open-data evaluation tools**
- **Potential expansion and replication within Brussels and other European cities**
- **Need for sustained investment in resident engagement and shared space programming**
- **Monitoring of long-term impacts (social, economic, environmental) to ensure enduring benefits**
