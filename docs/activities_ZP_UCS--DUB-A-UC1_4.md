# Executive summary## Smart Green Roof Monitoring Project

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Smart Green Roof Monitoring Project
- **Alternative Names:** Smart Green Roof Pilot, UCD Smart Green Roof Project
- **Location:** Dublin, Ireland (multiple sites including buildings in central Dublin and at UCD Rosemount)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.3498° N, 6.2603° W (for central Dublin)
- **Timeline:** Initiated 2021, with pilot installations and ongoing data collection at least through 2024
- **Project Scale:** Four green roofs monitored; pilot scale, covering multiple buildings, exact area not specified but roof areas consistent with Dublin’s policy threshold (>100 m²)[^1][^2] )
- **Leading Organization(s):** University College Dublin (UCD) School of Architecture, Planning & Environmental Policy; Hibernia Real Estate Group; supported by City of Dublin and technology partners NanoPower
- **Funding Sources:** Likely a mix of university research funding, private sector (Hibernia Real Estate Group), and possible public innovation grants (detailed funding breakdown not publicly available)

#### **Executive Summary**
The **Smart Green Roof Monitoring Project** in Dublin represents Ireland’s most advanced pilot of real-time, sensor-based green roof performance assessment. Deploying the AquaRoot IoT sensor platform (NanoPower’s EcoMet), the project tracks environmental variables—primarily water retention, soil moisture, and rainfall—across green roofs installed on four diverse buildings in Dublin[^1][^2] ). This initiative aims to empirically assess the effectiveness of green roofs for stormwater management, directly addressing urban flood risks that threaten Dublin’s aging and capacity-constrained drainage infrastructure.

Data gathered from the smart sensors informs both operational decisions (such as maintenance and irrigation needs) and strategic urban planning, providing city authorities and developers with tangible evidence to justify the integration of green infrastructure into new and existing developments. The project also considers the biodiversity and urban cooling benefits of green roofs within the dense Dublin context, where open space is scarce and resilience to climate change is a growing policy imperative[^3].

By aligning with Dublin City Council’s progressive green and blue roof policies and leveraging advanced data analytics, the project not only contributes to local flood mitigation and ecological enhancement, but also establishes a replicable model for smart, climate-adaptive urban infrastructure. The outcomes—improved flood risk management, enhanced resilience, and a strong evidence base for future investment—position this initiative at the forefront of sustainable city innovation in Ireland and Europe[^1][^2] ).

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Dublin is Ireland’s capital and largest city, with a metropolitan population of over 1.4 million. It serves as the nation’s economic hub, housing a diverse mix of service, technology, and financial sectors. Geographically, Dublin is situated on the east coast, with the River Liffey running through the city and large areas at low elevation, susceptible to flooding.

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The pilot green roofs are installed on a mix of central Dublin commercial and institutional buildings, including those owned by Hibernia Real Estate and at UCD’s Rosemount facility. Central Dublin is characterized by:
    - High urban density and limited open space
    - A mix of historic and modern development
    - Diverse population with variation in income and age across neighborhoods
    - Aging drainage infrastructure, with combined sewers prone to overflow in even moderate rainfall events[^3]
    - Limited green infrastructure, which exacerbates urban heat island effects and stormwater challenges

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Chronic urban flooding due to high impervious surface coverage and outdated drainage systems
  - Overloaded combined sewers, resulting in frequent spillages of untreated water into watercourses even after minor rainfall[^3]
  - Limited urban biodiversity and growing heat stress in central areas
  - Lack of empirical data on green roof performance in the Irish context, impeding wider adoption

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Persistent surface water and sewer flooding, particularly during summer months
  - New planning policies (Dublin City Development Plan 2022–2028) mandating green and blue roofs for large developments
  - Institutional push for climate adaptation strategies and sustainable urban drainage solutions

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Dublin City Council: regulator and policy driver
  - UCD and Hibernia Real Estate: pilot implementers
  - Building owners and managers: operational stakeholders
  - Local communities: indirect beneficiaries (reduced flood risk, improved amenity)

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Dublin City Council oversees planning and environmental policy, implementing the city’s Climate Action Plan and Biodiversity Action Plan[^3].

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - **Development Plan Policy SI23**: Requires green/blue roofs on new developments with roof areas >100 m²
  - **Sustainable Urban Drainage Systems (SuDS) Policy SI22**: Promotes decentralized stormwater management
  - **Biodiversity Policies (GI16, GI17)**: Encourage habitat creation through urban greening

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Policy mandates in the Development Plan enabled project justification and integration.
  - Exemptions are limited, reinforcing the need for innovative compliance solutions.
  - Institutional collaboration between academia, private developers, and city authorities facilitated pilot implementation[^3].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Quantitatively assess green roof effectiveness for stormwater retention and flood mitigation in Dublin
  - Provide real-time, actionable data to inform operational and planning decisions
  - Build the case for broader adoption of green infrastructure in urban development

- **Vision Statement:**  
  To create a data-driven, resilient urban environment in Dublin, leveraging smart infrastructure to mitigate flooding, enhance biodiversity, and support sustainable development for all residents.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Directly supports Dublin’s Climate Action Plan, Biodiversity Action Plan, and the Development Plan’s green infrastructure mandates[^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  Installing smart-monitored green roofs will reduce stormwater runoff, protecting drainage infrastructure and downstream ecosystems. Real-time data validates performance, informing citywide scaling and policy refinement.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - City residents (reduced flood risk)
  - Building owners (compliance, property value, reduced insurance/potential costs)
  - Urban planners and policymakers (evidence for policy development)
  - Biodiversity (new urban habitats)

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Use of IoT sensor platforms (AquaRoot – NanoPower’s EcoMet) for continuous, granular environmental monitoring[^1]
  - Data-driven approach to urban infrastructure planning
  - Integration of biodiversity and climate adaptation objectives

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Regulatory requirements, public awareness of climate risks, and municipal leadership in sustainability

- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Risk reduction for property assets, compliance with planning mandates, potential long-term savings in flood-related costs

- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Health and safety improvements, community support for green infrastructure

- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Climate adaptation, improved urban ecosystem function, water management

- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Advances in IoT sensor technology, real-time analytics, and smart city strategies[^1][^2] )

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Guided by sustainable urban drainage and climate adaptation principles, the initiative integrates green infrastructure with smart technology to optimize ecosystem services and urban resilience.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  Four pilot green roofs installed, with waterproofing, root barriers, drainage layers, engineered substrates, and pre-grown sedum vegetation blankets[^4].
  - Sensor arrays embedded within the substrate to monitor soil moisture, water retention, and outflow rates in real time[^1].
  - Roofs located on both commercial (Hibernia Real Estate) and institutional (UCD Rosemount) buildings for comparative analysis.

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial pilot installations (2021–2022)
  - Ongoing data monitoring and analysis (2022–present)
  - Adaptive management and potential scaling pending results

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - **AquaRoot/NanoPower EcoMet IoT Sensors:** Collect high-frequency hydrological and environmental data[^1]
  - **Data Analytics Platform:** Real-time analytics for building managers and city planners
  - **Green Roof Construction Technologies:** Multilayer systems with sedum blankets, engineered substrates, waterproof membranes, and drainage layers[^4][^5]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Primarily technological (IoT, sensors, data analytics), with process innovation in data-driven planning

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Sensors and data platforms fully integrated into new/existing building management systems and city drainage monitoring networks

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - University-led research partnership with private developers and technology firms

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Collaborative design workshops with contractors, architects, and supply chain partners (notably for bespoke installation details)[^4]
  - Coordination with Dublin City Council for regulatory compliance

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Limited direct community participation due to technical nature, but broader stakeholder engagement in dissemination and planning

- **Project Management:**  
  - Oversight by UCD and Hibernia Real Estate, with technical support from NanoPower and contractors (e.g., Soprema, Crannog Roofing)[^4]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Detailed project cost breakdown not published; green roofs typically represent a 10–30% premium over conventional roofs, with sensor and monitoring costs additional

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Academic research funding, private sector investment, and possible public grants

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - No direct revenue; value generated via cost avoidance (flood risk), policy compliance, and increased asset value

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Demonstrates financial case for future green roof investment, especially as policy mandates expand

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Sensor deployment complexity (placement, calibration, robustness)[^1]
  - Data reliability and integration with existing building management/drainage systems
  - Ensuring water tightness and structural stability of roof assemblies, especially in high-density urban settings[^4]

- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Higher upfront costs for smart green roof systems
  - Uncertainty regarding long-term maintenance and monitoring expenses

- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Developer wariness of untested technologies
  - Potential skepticism regarding cost-benefit balance for retrofit projects

- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Navigating new regulatory frameworks; need to demonstrate compliance with evolving policy requirements[^3]

- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Unpredictable weather patterns affecting data consistency
  - Need for robust design to withstand local climate stresses

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Collaborative design and installation process involving all supply chain partners to address bespoke technical challenges[^4]
  - Phased pilot deployment to refine system performance before scaling

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Use of modular green roof units for easier sensor integration and maintenance
  - Adjustments in substrate depth and drainage design to optimize both stormwater retention and plant viability

- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of early and ongoing technical oversight to ensure sensor accuracy and system durability
  - Need for comprehensive stakeholder coordination to align technical, regulatory, and operational objectives

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Installation of green roofs on four pilot buildings with embedded sensor arrays
  - Creation of new urban green space with sedum and other vegetation[^1][^4]

- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Real-time environmental data delivered to building managers and city planners
  - Improved stormwater management services

- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Early results indicate significant retention of rainfall (extensive systems can retain approximately 50–62% of rainfall; intensive systems up to 75–90%, though project-specific data is forthcoming)[^5]
  - Enhanced biodiversity through introduction of diverse sedum species

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Reduced flood risk and associated disruption/costs for residents and businesses
  - Improved urban amenity and aesthetics

- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Benefits distributed citywide; pilot sites in diverse urban contexts

- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No reported negative effects, as interventions are rooftop-based

- **Social Capital:**  
  - Increased awareness and acceptance of green infrastructure among stakeholders

- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Contributes to Dublin’s emerging identity as a climate-adaptive, innovative city

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Direct employment for installation, monitoring, and maintenance teams

- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Supports local green technology and construction sectors

- **Property Values:**  
  - Green roofs may increase property values by up to 6.9% for accessible roofs; evidence suggests enhanced asset appeal[^5]

- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Potential reductions in flood-related damages, insurance premiums, and municipal drainage costs

- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - No direct evidence, but citywide scaling could benefit a broad cross-section of residents

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Reduced urban heat island effect, lower building energy use, and improved climate resilience[^3][^5]

- **Resource Management:**  
  - Significant water retention, delaying and reducing runoff to overstressed drainage systems

- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality, increased urban biodiversity, and expanded green space in dense urban areas[^3][^5]

- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Lower risk of sewer overflows and waterway pollution

- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Performance supports BREEAM and similar sustainability certification criteria[^4]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthens municipal and private sector ability to manage green infrastructure

- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Provides data and evidence for future policy refinement and scaling

- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Real-time data improves accountability in infrastructure management

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Demonstrated stormwater retention, biodiversity gains, and data-driven planning[^1][^2] )
  - Validates rationale for expanding green roof requirements

- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - No significant negative outcomes reported; minor technical setbacks addressed adaptively

- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Strong prospects for scaling and institutionalization, pending continued positive results
