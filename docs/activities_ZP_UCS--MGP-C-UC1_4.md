# Executive summary## Aspern Smart City Demonstration (Smarter Together)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Aspern Smart City Demonstration (Smarter Together)
- **Alternative Names:** Aspern Smart City Research (ASCR); Smarter Together Vienna
- **Location:** Aspern Seestadt, Vienna, Austria
- **Geographic Coordinates:** ~48.241°N, 16.482°E (Aspern Seestadt)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Planning: 2005–2012 (masterplan adoption in 2008)
  - ASCR Launch: October 2013
  - Smarter Together: February 2016–2021
  - Ongoing research until at least 2023
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Area: ~240 hectares (Seestadt Aspern)
  - Population affected: Up to 21,000 residents in Vienna’s Smarter Together area
  - Investment: €7–7.5M EU funding (Vienna), €80–100M total investment[^1][^2][^3]
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - Siemens Österreich, Wien Energie, Wiener Netze, City of Vienna (Vienna Business Agency, Wien 3420)
  - Municipal Department 25 (MA 25), Urban Renewal Authority[^4][^2]
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - EU Horizon 2020 Programme
  - City of Vienna (housing and school budgets)
  - Private sector (Siemens, energy utilities)
  - Partnerships and local investments[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**
Aspern Smart City Demonstration, integrated within the Horizon 2020 Smarter Together project, represents one of Europe’s largest and most innovative urban energy and smart city research ventures. Centered in Vienna’s Aspern Seestadt, the initiative serves as a living laboratory to pioneer digital platforms and smart infrastructure for sustainable urban development. Using web-based dashboards, IoT sensors, and collaborative tools, the project empowers a diverse array of stakeholders—including residents, building managers, and city planners—with real-time data on energy, mobility, and comfort. Key ambitions include the creation of high-efficiency, renewable-powered buildings, the integration of smart mobility, and the establishment of participatory platforms for decision-making. The initiative’s impact is demonstrated in measurable energy savings, increased use of renewables, and significant progress toward Vienna’s carbon neutrality goals. Facing challenges such as data privacy and platform interoperability, the project has fostered adaptive solutions and robust governance, positioning Vienna as an international smart city leader. Smarter Together’s holistic approach—spanning social inclusion, economic renewal, and environmental stewardship—offers critical lessons for cities seeking scalable, evidence-based pathways toward urban sustainability[^4][^1][^5][^2].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Vienna, Austria, is a leading European metropolis (pop. ~1.9 million), renowned for quality of life, economic vitality, and progressive urban planning. The city is a national hub for culture, governance, and innovation, with a robust public infrastructure and emphasis on green development[^6][^7].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - **Aspern Seestadt:** A new urban quarter developed on former airfield land, planned as Vienna’s “Urban Lakeside.” Compact, mixed-use, and transit-oriented, it features high-density residential, office, and commercial spaces around an artificial lake. The Simmering district—core of Smarter Together activities—contains older housing stock, diverse demographics, and significant industrial/logistics activities[^6][^3].
  - **Demographics:** The area hosts a mix of young professionals, families, and older residents. Income levels are rising due to gentrification but remain mixed; cultural diversity is maintained through inclusive planning[^6].
  - **Infrastructure Quality:** New and refurbished buildings feature advanced energy systems, green spaces, and high public transport accessibility.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** The project area prioritizes green space, water management (rainwater harvesting), and biodiversity, with attention to climate resilience and air quality[^6].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Aging housing stock; high energy consumption; limited renewable integration
  - Social fragmentation, limited civic engagement
  - Economic stagnation in older districts, lack of innovative business opportunities
  - Inadequate mobility options, traffic congestion
  - Climate and sustainability imperatives (carbon neutrality goals)[^1][^6][^3]
- **Triggering Events:**
  - Vienna 2025 masterplan adoption (2008) emphasizing smart, sustainable growth
  - EU Horizon 2020 call for urban innovation projects
  - Population growth projections and increased demand for housing/jobs[^6]
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - City government (urban renewal, energy, and mobility departments)
  - Private sector (utilities, Siemens, property developers)
  - Residents, local businesses, NGOs
  - EU institutions (research and funding coordination)[^4][^1][^2]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Led by Vienna’s municipal departments, in partnership with private energy and technology firms. Multi-level governance integrates city, regional, and EU stakeholders.
- **Relevant Policies:** Vienna Smart City Framework Strategy; urban renewal and zoning regulations; EU climate and energy directives.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** Strong enabling environment for energy innovation and data-sharing, but subject to strict Austrian/EU data privacy laws and complex public procurement procedures[^4][^1].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:** 
  - Demonstrate integrated smart city solutions for energy, mobility, and user comfort
  - Advance the transition to renewable energy and high-efficiency buildings
  - Foster social inclusion and participatory governance
  - Achieve measurable progress toward Vienna’s carbon neutrality targets[^1][^5]
- **Vision Statement:** To create a resilient, sustainable urban district that leverages digital innovation and civic engagement for climate protection and enhanced quality of life[^4][^8].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Fully aligned with Vienna’s Smart City Framework Strategy, the UN Sustainable Development Goals, and EU green transition mandates[^1][^9].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** By integrating real-time data on energy, mobility, and user needs into decision-making platforms, the initiative enables resource optimization, behavioral change, and systemic efficiency—delivering environmental, economic, and social benefits[^4][^10].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents, city planners, building managers, and businesses—both direct users of smart systems and indirect beneficiaries of improved urban services.
- **Innovation Elements:** Pioneering use of digital twins/BIM, real-time KPI dashboards, participatory urban labs, and cross-sectoral co-creation[^4][^10][^11].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:** Vienna’s commitment to climate goals and international urban leadership
- **Economic:** Attraction of investment, job creation, and property value enhancement
- **Social:** Addressing equity, inclusion, and community empowerment
- **Environmental:** Urgency of climate adaptation, decarbonization, and sustainable resource management
- **Technological:** Rapid advances in IoT, digital simulation, and platform interoperability[^4][^1][^10][^8]

---

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Grounded in principles of compact city development, mixed-use planning, resource efficiency, and participatory urbanism[^6].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Construction of high-efficiency buildings, integration of district heating (renewable sources), creation of green spaces and artificial lake, refurbishment of existing housing and schools with zero-energy standards[^5][^6][^2].
- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Initial planning (2005–2012)
  - Launch of living laboratory (2013)
  - Smarter Together implementation (2016–2019)
  - Monitoring/evaluation (up to 2021)
  - Ongoing innovation and testing (to 2023)[^4][^2][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - Web-based dashboards for KPI visualization
  - IoT sensors (energy, comfort, mobility)
  - Automated reporting tools
  - Smart grid infrastructure (12 stations, 24 transformers, 5 grid storage units)
  - BIM and digital twins for planning and operations
  - Renewable energy systems (solar PV, district heating)
  - E-mobility platforms (charging stations, network management)
  - Home automation and data platforms[^4][^10][^5][^11]
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digital platforms, smart grids); social (participatory labs, co-creation); governance (integrated public-private management)[^4][^1][^3]
- **Integration Approach:** Digital and physical systems interlinked via urban data platform; interoperability across energy, mobility, and user engagement domains[^4][^10][^5]

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Joint venture among Siemens, utilities, and city agencies; EU tendering for research and innovation components[^4][^1][^2]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Systematic involvement of residents via participatory labs, surveys, and feedback mechanisms; local schools and businesses engaged in pilots[^1][^2][^3]
- **Participatory Elements:** SIMmobil (mobile hands-on lab), workshops, energy training, co-design of mobility stations, citizen data consent[^1][^5][^2]
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by MA 25 (Urban Renewal), with steering committees spanning public, private, and research sectors[^2]

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** >€7M EU funding for Vienna; total investment in Vienna >€80M; overall program (across cities) >€100M[^1][^2][^3]
- **Funding Mix:**  EU grants, municipal budgets, private sector contributions, PPP models
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Savings from energy efficiency; potential monetization of data platforms; increased land/property values
- **Financial Sustainability:** Leveraging long-term municipal investment, incremental returns from operational efficiencies, and value capture from urban renewal[^1][^2]

---

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:** Ensuring interoperability between diverse digital platforms and legacy infrastructure; scaling smart grid capacity; managing complex data flows[^4][^10]
- **Financial:** Budget overruns due to expanded project scope; aligning multiple funding sources[^1][^2]
- **Social and Political:** Raising citizen awareness, overcoming skepticism, ensuring equitable participation (gentrification risks)[^6]
- **Administrative:** Coordination across departments and private partners; navigating EU and local regulations
- **Environmental:** Managing site-specific constraints (water management, open space needs), unforeseen climate impacts[^6]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Iterative prototyping (17 use cases), rapid cycle testing, and incremental extension of project duration[^4][^2]
- **Design Modifications:** Expansion of participatory programs, integration of feedback into system upgrades, adaptive scheduling based on evaluation results
- **Lessons Learned:** Need for robust data privacy protocols, ongoing stakeholder education, and modular design for future scalability[^4][^2]

---

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:** Construction and refurbishment of high-efficiency buildings; installation of smart grid infrastructure; creation of zero-energy gyms, solar benches, green roofs, charging stations[^1][^5][^2]
- **Service Delivery:** Deployment of urban data platforms, e-mobility solutions, and participatory labs for civic engagement[^4][^5]
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 
  - 21,000 residents directly impacted
  - 1,300 housing units refurbished (75,000 m²)
  - 6,000 MWh annual energy savings
  - 9,000 MWh renewable energy supplied
  - 17 pilot use cases; >60 research questions answered[^4][^5][^2]

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:** Improved quality of life, comfort, and health; increased civic participation; strengthened community networks
- **Equity Outcomes:** Enhanced access to affordable housing and energy; efforts to mitigate gentrification; active inclusion of diverse groups[^1][^6][^2]
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** Affordable unit pricing and mixed-use design aim to limit displacement, though rising property values may create future pressures[^6]
- **Social Capital:** New collaborative mechanisms, increased civic trust, and participatory governance structures
- **Cultural Impacts:** Support for local identity through public spaces, creative programming, and inclusive planning[^6]

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:** Up to 20,000 jobs projected in Aspern Seestadt; direct employment in construction, energy, research, and service sectors[^7]
- **Economic Activity:** Attraction of investment, business innovation, and increased productivity
- **Property Values:** Significant appreciation due to new infrastructure and amenities
- **Cost Savings:** Demonstrated reductions in energy and heating costs for residents[^5]
- **Economic Inclusion:** Support for SMEs, training programs, and local enterprise[^1][^2]

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate and Emissions:** Reduced GHG emissions via renewable energy and efficiency; progress toward carbon neutrality
- **Resource Management:** Advanced water management, efficient energy distribution, and high-performance building materials[^6]
- **Environmental Quality:** Enhanced air quality, noise reduction, and increased green space/biodiversity
- **Waste and Pollution:** Lower diesel consumption, improved logistics for industrial sites, and innovative waste management pilots[^3]
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Recognition in international awards (World Smart City Award 2016; IDC 2020 Smart Cities and Communities Award)[^4][^10]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Institutional Capacity:** Strengthened city administration, new collaborative models, and knowledge transfer mechanisms
- **Policy Influence:** Shaped updates to Vienna’s Smart City Strategy; model for EU smart city policies[^9][^3]
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Enhanced through public dashboards, participatory labs, and open data initiatives[^4][^1][^5]

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:** Most stated objectives achieved, including energy savings, renewable integration, and participatory governance
- **Unintended Consequences:** Some risks of gentrification and challenges in maintaining long-term engagement
- **Long-term Viability:** Positive trajectory, with ongoing research and scaling potential[^4][^2]

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**
- **Contextual Dependencies:** Strong local governance, EU funding, and civic culture were critical; elements like digital platforms and smart grid can be transferred with adaptation
- **Critical Success Factors:** Robust public-private partnerships, modular technology, participatory processes, and flexible funding
- **Scalability:** Demonstrated scalability across neighborhoods and potential for expansion to other cities[^1][^12]

#### **Comparative Analysis**
- **Similar Initiatives:** Comparable to smart city pilots in Munich and Lyon (Smarter Together), and other EU Lighthouse Cities[^1][^12][^13]
- **Best Practices:** Integration of technology, governance, and social innovation; participatory labs; data-driven decision-making
- **Cautionary Lessons:** Ensure equity and affordability; anticipate data privacy and interoperability challenges

#### **Parallels to [Your Specific Use Case]**
- **Contextual Similarities:** KPI visualization and export for stakeholder groups; data-driven resource optimization
- **Methodological Parallels:** Participatory engagement, integration of digital dashboards, iterative prototyping
- **Technological Overlaps:** IoT sensors, urban data platforms, and real-time reporting
- **Stakeholder Dynamics:** Multi-sector partnerships, strong municipal leadership, active citizen role
- **Transferable Strategies:** Modular design, participatory labs, robust data governance
- **Adaptation Considerations:** Local funding sources, regulatory landscape, and stakeholder engagement models must be tailored

---

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- Holistic integration of technology, governance, and social innovation
- Robust data platforms enabling evidence-based decision-making
- Significant, measurable environmental and economic impacts
- International recognition and transferability of key innovations[^4][^10][^2]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- Complexity of interoperability across platforms
- Risks of gentrification and unequal access to benefits[^6]
- Need for sustained citizen engagement beyond initial project phases

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Proactive inclusion of low-income and diverse residents
- Efforts to maintain affordability and prevent displacement
- Ongoing need for vigilance as property values rise[^6]

#### **Future Outlook**
- Continued research and innovation (ASCR extended to 2023)
- Ongoing scaling of solutions across Vienna and potentially other cities
- Focus on maintaining participation, equity, and adaptive governance
- Next phases include further integration of e-mobility, renewable energy pools, and advanced predictive building controls[^4][^10][^2]
