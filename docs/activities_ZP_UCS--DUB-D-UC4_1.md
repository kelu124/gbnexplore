# Executive summary## SmartEnCity – Vitoria-Gasteiz Smart District

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** SmartEnCity – Vitoria-Gasteiz Smart District  
- **Alternative Names:** SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz Demonstration Site  
- **Location:** Coronación district, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Basque Country, Spain  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 42.84998° N, -2.67268° W  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project start: 2016  
  - Major retrofitting works: 2017–2021  
  - Monitoring and operation: 2021–2023 (with a 12-month extension for monitoring)[^1]  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Focus on Coronación neighborhood  
  - Dwellings affected: 655 to 1,313 homes (sources report both figures due to project phasing)[^2][^3]  
  - Population: Estimated >4,000 residents  
  - Investment value: Multi-million Euro, funded primarily by the EU Horizon 2020 program  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - City of Vitoria-Gasteiz  
  - Consortium partners: Tecnalia, Mondragon, local utilities, regional government, research institutions  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union (Horizon 2020)  
  - Municipal and regional funding  
  - Private sector contributions and in-kind support

#### **Executive Summary**

The SmartEnCity project in Vitoria-Gasteiz represents a flagship initiative for the transformation of an existing, socially vulnerable urban neighborhood into a model of energy-efficient, climate-adaptive urban living. Centered on the Coronación district—a dense, mixed-use area with aging building stock and significant energy poverty—the project focused on deep energy retrofitting of residential buildings, deployment of a renewable-fueled district heating system, and the integration of advanced digital management platforms. The initiative aimed to reduce energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions, improve indoor comfort, and create new models for resident engagement and smart neighborhood management.

Key actions included the insulation of over 60,000 m² of building envelope, introduction of building and neighborhood energy management systems using IoT sensors and digital twins, and installation of a biomass-based central heating network. Public space upgrades and mobility improvements complemented the energy actions, while participatory processes sought to empower residents as co-creators of their neighborhood’s transformation. The project’s significance lies in its systemic approach—combining technological, social, and governance innovations—while serving as a scalable model for other European cities facing similar challenges of aging infrastructure, energy transition, and social inclusion. The initiative demonstrates how targeted investments in smart, sustainable urban regeneration can deliver environmental, economic, and social benefits, positioning Vitoria-Gasteiz as a reference city in the European green building and smart district movement[^2][^4][^5].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country’s Álava province, with ~250,000 inhabitants. It is a regional administrative, service, and industrial center, known for progressive environmental policies and high quality of life. The city has won recognition as European Green Capital (2012) and is a national leader in sustainable mobility and green infrastructure[^4].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Coronación** is a historic, centrally located, high-density neighborhood, characterized by:  
    - Mid-20th-century apartment blocks, often with poor insulation and outdated heating  
    - Social vulnerability—above-average rates of elderly, low-income, and immigrant residents  
    - Mixed land use: Residential focus, with supporting commercial and social facilities  
    - Aging infrastructure: Inadequate building envelopes and inefficient energy systems  
    - Limited green space and degraded public realm  
    - Environmental concerns: High energy poverty, local air quality issues[^2][^3]

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High energy consumption and CO₂ emissions from inefficient buildings  
  - Prevalence of energy poverty, with many residents unable to afford adequate heating  
  - Aging housing stock with low comfort and health standards  
  - Social vulnerability and risk of exclusion  
  - Lack of neighborhood-scale climate adaptation and resilience measures

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Adoption of citywide climate and energy strategies (e.g., Vitoria-Gasteiz Plan to Combat Climate Change, Covenant of Mayors commitments)[^4][^6]  
  - Successful application for EU Horizon 2020 funding for SmartEnCity  
  - Community mobilization around issues of energy poverty and neighborhood decline

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (urban planning, energy, social services)  
  - Resident associations and local NGOs  
  - Private sector partners (utilities, technology providers)  
  - Research and innovation agencies  
  - EU and national funding bodies

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  Multi-level: Municipal, regional, and EU institutions. City led, with strong interdepartmental collaboration and public-private partnerships.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Urban regeneration and housing improvement programs  
  - Basque and Spanish energy transition strategies  
  - EU climate and smart city directives  
  - Local plans: Vitoria-Gasteiz Climate Action Plan, Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan[^4][^6]

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Enabling: EU and regional climate legislation, city’s tradition of participatory governance  
  - Barriers: Complexities in retrofitting private multi-owner buildings, data privacy regulations for digital systems[^2]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Demonstrate large-scale, deep energy retrofitting in an existing neighborhood  
  - Establish a renewable-fueled district heating system  
  - Integrate digital technologies for smart energy management  
  - Reduce energy poverty and improve quality of life  
  - Engage residents as co-producers of urban transformation[^2][^3][^5]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  Transform Coronación into a smart, inclusive, low-carbon neighborhood, setting a replicable precedent for sustainable, resilient cities.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Direct support for Vitoria-Gasteiz’s citywide climate neutrality and decarbonization goals  
  - Contribution to Basque and Spanish commitments under the Paris Agreement  
  - Alignment with EU Smart Cities and Communities objectives[^4][^6]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By retrofitting buildings, deploying renewable energy, and empowering residents with digital tools, the project expected to cut energy use and emissions, reduce costs, increase comfort, and foster social cohesion.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Primary: Residents of Coronación, especially vulnerable and low-income households  
  - Secondary: City as a whole, through model replication and system learning

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Use of a district-scale digital twin for real-time energy optimization  
  - Integration of energy, mobility, and public space interventions  
  - Participatory co-design and resident engagement in system operations[^2][^5]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - Leadership commitment to European Green Capital legacy  
  - Compliance with Covenant of Mayors and national climate policy

- **Economic:**  
  - EU funding opportunities  
  - Desire to reduce city and household energy costs  
  - Stimulation of local green economy and building sector

- **Social:**  
  - Addressing energy poverty and improving living standards  
  - Community empowerment and inclusion

- **Environmental:**  
  - Urgent need for GHG emission reductions  
  - Urban resilience and adaptation to climate change

- **Technological:**  
  - Maturity of digital twin, IoT, and renewable heating technologies  
  - Smart city innovation agenda[^2][^5]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Integrated urban regeneration, prioritizing energy, environment, and social inclusion, guided by principles of systemic, data-driven transformation.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Deep retrofit of apartment buildings (façade insulation, new windows, roof upgrades)  
  - Centralized biomass district heating network  
  - Upgraded public spaces (streets, squares, landscaping)  
  - Smart mobility infrastructure (e-charging, clean vehicles)[^2][^3]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial diagnostic and design (2016–2017)  
  - Building retrofits and system installation (2017–2021)  
  - Monitoring, optimization, and resident engagement (2021–2023)[^1]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Digital twin of the district  
  - IoT sensors and smart meters in buildings  
  - Centralized building and neighborhood energy management system  
  - Biomass (wood chip) boilers for district heating  
  - Clean mobility solutions (EVs, charging stations)[^2][^3][^5]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: Digital twinning, smart controls, renewable heating  
  - Social: Participatory co-design, resident feedback loops  
  - Governance: Cross-sectoral partnership, new models for multi-owner building retrofits

- **Integration Approach:**  
  Existing buildings were upgraded and integrated with new digital and energy systems; real-time data enabled predictive maintenance and optimization.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Combination of public tenders for construction and technology, direct municipal management, and collaborative R&D.

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Resident assemblies and workshops  
  - Communication campaigns and digital dashboards for feedback  
  - Involvement of local business and social organizations[^2][^6]

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  Residents involved in design choices, system operation preferences, and ongoing monitoring.

- **Project Management:**  
  Led by municipal project office, with technical committees and regular consortium meetings.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Substantial EU investment (exact figures vary, estimated several million Euros)  
  - Cost breakdown: Building retrofits, heating network, digital systems, public realm, management

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Predominantly EU (Horizon 2020)  
  - Municipal/regional co-funding  
  - In-kind private sector contributions

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Savings from reduced energy consumption  
  - Potential user fees for district heating

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term reduction in household energy bills  
  - City’s ongoing commitment to monitoring and maintenance[^2][^3][^1]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integration of new systems with old building stock  
  - Coordination of retrofits across multiple, separately owned apartments  
  - Ensuring data privacy and cybersecurity for digital systems[^2]

- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront costs and co-financing for low-income residents  
  - Budget management across multiple funding streams

- **Social and Political:**  
  - Resident skepticism and engagement fatigue  
  - Potential displacement or gentrification pressures (though limited in this case compared to other European cities)[^7]

- **Administrative:**  
  - Complex inter-organizational coordination  
  - Navigating legal frameworks for property and data management

- **Environmental:**  
  - Logistics of biomass supply and storage  
  - Ensuring real carbon savings versus baseline

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Technical assistance and financial incentives for residents  
  - Strong communication and capacity-building activities  
  - Iterative piloting and real-time adjustment using digital monitoring tools

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Phased implementation to allow learning and adjustment  
  - Added extension for post-retrofit monitoring and optimization[^1]

- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Early, sustained resident engagement is critical  
  - Technical solutions must be tailored to local building typologies  
  - Data-driven management enables rapid response to performance issues

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical:**  
  - Insulation and energy system upgrades in 655–1,313 dwellings  
  - Installation of district heating network and biomass boiler  
  - Upgraded public spaces and mobility infrastructure[^2][^3]

- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Reliable, renewable heating for hundreds of households  
  - Real-time energy management and feedback for users

- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Energy demand reduced by 3 GWh/year (in pilot phase)[^4]  
  - GHG emissions reduced by ~1,000 tons CO₂e/year (pilot phase)[^4]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Lower energy bills  
  - Improved indoor comfort and air quality  
  - Increased sense of neighborhood pride and cohesion

- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Targeted support for low-income and vulnerable residents  
  - Efforts to limit displacement or exclusion

- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No significant displacement observed; focused on existing residents  
  - Ongoing monitoring to assess longer-term impacts

- **Social Capital:**  
  - Enhanced resident participation and empowerment

- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Revitalization of neighborhood identity through design and engagement

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Construction, installation, and ongoing maintenance jobs

- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Stimulated local building sector and supply chains

- **Property Values:**  
  - Potential for moderate increase in property values, but mitigated by social housing protections

- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Reduced heating costs for residents and city services

- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Focus on affordability and inclusion of vulnerable households

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Significant reduction in building-related GHG emissions  
  - Progress toward city’s climate neutrality targets[^4]

- **Resource Management:**  
  - Increased share of renewable energy in heating  
  - Improved building energy efficiency

- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality (reduced fossil fuel use)  
  - Enhanced urban ecosystem services through public realm upgrades

- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Reduced emissions from heating; waste management practices improved during retrofits

- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Alignment with EU and national standards for energy and climate performance

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened municipal expertise in smart district management

- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for replication in other Spanish and European cities

- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Digital dashboards and participatory processes improved governance quality[^6]

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Substantial progress on energy, emissions, and social inclusion goals  
  - High levels of resident satisfaction reported (initial feedback)

- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Potential for longer-term gentrification (ongoing monitoring required)

- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Strong prospects due to institutionalization and ongoing monitoring

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Systemic integration** of energy, digital, and social innovation  
- **High resident engagement** and participatory governance  
- **Significant environmental and social impacts** in a vulnerable area  
- **Model value** for replication in other cities

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity of retrofitting** multi-owner buildings  
- **Upfront financial barriers** for some residents, despite subsidies  
- **Data privacy concerns** with digital monitoring  
- **Long-term risk** of gentrification and affordability pressures

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Project prioritized social inclusion and protection of vulnerable residents  
- Proactive monitoring and mitigation of displacement risks  
- Ongoing attention needed to ensure long-term affordability and access

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Continued monitoring** and optimization of energy and social outcomes  
- **Potential expansion** of district heating and smart management to other neighborhoods  
- **Ongoing adaptation** of engagement and technical models as citywide decarbonization accelerates  
- **Long-term challenge**: Ensuring sustained inclusion, affordability, and climate resilience as the city evolves
