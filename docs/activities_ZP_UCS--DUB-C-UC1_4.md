# Executive summary## Green Canteen Digital Twin – Helsinki, Finland

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Green Canteen Digital Twin
- **Alternative Names:** Green Canteen Pilot, Food Flow Digital Twin (within GROW Observatory)
- **Location:** University of Helsinki, Helsinki, Finland
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 60.1719° N, 24.9417° E (central Helsinki, university campus)
- **Timeline:** Initiated in 2018 as a Horizon 2020 GROW Observatory pilot; ongoing refinement through 2021
- **Project Scale:** University campus canteen serving thousands daily; affects students, staff, and food service operators
- **Leading Organization(s):** University of Helsinki, GROW Observatory (Horizon 2020); in partnership with canteen operators and IT providers
- **Funding Sources:** European Commission Horizon 2020 (GROW Observatory grant); university resources; in-kind support from technology partners

#### **Executive Summary**
The **Green Canteen Digital Twin** is a pioneering urban sustainability initiative piloted at the University of Helsinki under the GROW Observatory’s Horizon 2020 program. The project employs an advanced digital twin platform to virtually mirror the operations of a large university canteen, integrating real-time and historical data from inventory management, procurement, and food consumption. By modeling the flow of food, resource use, and waste, the digital twin enables canteen managers to simulate alternative menu strategies, procurement policies, and waste reduction tactics—quantifying the environmental impact of each scenario. The platform’s Internet of Things (IoT) integration automates the tracking of inventory and waste, providing granular data that supports both operational optimization and strategic decision-making.

The initiative’s core objectives are to reduce food waste, align daily operations with sustainability targets, and serve as a demonstration for data-driven resource efficiency in institutional catering. Challenges included achieving seamless data integration across supply, kitchen, and service points, as well as ensuring the reliability of environmental impact assessments. Outcomes include measurable reductions in over-ordering and food waste, lower operational costs, and a shift toward more sustainable dietary habits among thousands of daily users. The Green Canteen Digital Twin positions Helsinki as a leader in digital urban sustainability and provides a replicable model for campuses and institutions worldwide.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Guided by circular economy and smart campus principles; operationalized through digital twin and IoT integration.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  No major physical changes; focus on overlaying digital monitoring and management atop existing canteen infrastructure.
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Data mapping and system integration.
  - Phase 2: IoT deployment and digital twin calibration.
  - Phase 3: Scenario modeling, staff training, and continuous improvement.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - **Digital twin platform** for real-time and scenario-based modeling.
  - **IoT sensors** for inventory and waste tracking.
  - **Environmental impact modeling** tools for menu and procurement choices.
- **Innovation Type:**  
  Primarily technological (digital twin, IoT, data analytics), with process and behavioral innovation.
- **Integration Approach:**  
  Linkage of digital twin to both procurement and service point data, enabling closed-loop feedback.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Collaboration with digital solution providers and university IT teams; internal and external partnerships for sensor deployment.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  Staff training, regular feedback loops with canteen workers, and outreach to students for behavioral nudges.
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  User feedback integrated into menu offerings and waste reduction campaigns.
- **Project Management:**  
  Coordinated by the university’s sustainability office, with technical oversight from IT and GROW Observatory partners.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:**  
  Estimated pilot investment in the range of €250,000–€400,000, including technology, training, and management.
- **Funding Mix:**  
  Horizon 2020 grant as primary source; in-kind university contributions; potential for private sector sponsorship.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  Cost savings retained within canteen operations.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  Projected payback via waste reduction and procurement efficiency; potential for scaling to other university facilities.
