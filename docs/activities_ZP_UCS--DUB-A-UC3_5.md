# Executive summary## SmartEnCity – Vitoria-Gasteiz Green Neighborhood Retrofit

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** SmartEnCity – Vitoria-Gasteiz Green Neighborhood Retrofit
- **Alternative Names:** SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz Demo Site; Coronación District Retrofit
- **Location:** Coronación district, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Basque Country, Spain
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approximately 42.8467° N, 2.6716° W
- **Timeline:**
  - **Project Start:** 2016 (SmartEnCity project launch)
  - **Major Milestones:**
    - 2016–2018: Detailed planning, community engagement, tendering
    - 2018–2022: Phased construction and retrofitting, technology deployment
    - Ongoing: Monitoring, evaluation, and knowledge transfer
  - **Status:** Implementation largely completed; monitoring ongoing[^1][^2].
- **Project Scale:** 
  - **Area:** Coronación district (approx. 60,000 m²)
  - **Population Affected:** 750–1,313 dwellings; approx. 2,000–4,000 residents[^1][^3]
  - **Investment Value:** Part of €28 million SmartEnCity H2020 budget (Vitoria-Gasteiz share: approx. €10 million)[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - Vitoria-Gasteiz City Council
  - VISESA (Basque Government Housing Agency)
  - Tecnalia (Research and technical partner)
  - Local residents’ associations
- **Funding Sources:**
  - European Union Horizon 2020 (SmartEnCity grant)
  - Basque regional funding
  - Municipal budget
  - Resident co-financing for building retrofits
  - Private sector/energy service companies[^1][^2]

#### **Executive Summary**
The SmartEnCity project in Vitoria-Gasteiz represents a comprehensive neighborhood-scale retrofit initiative, aiming to transform the socially and energetically vulnerable Coronación district into a model of low-carbon, smart urban living. Backed by the EU Horizon 2020 SmartEnCity program, the project integrates deep energy retrofits—primarily building envelope insulation and smart HVAC upgrades—alongside the deployment of a new biomass district heating network, smart energy management platforms, and IoT-based air quality monitoring. Residents are empowered through real-time dashboards, enabling behavioral change and optimized comfort. Beyond technical intervention, the project emphasizes participatory planning, local economic inclusion, and public space renewal. SmartEnCity Vitoria-Gasteiz is significant not only as a technical demonstration of green building neighborhoods but also as a social experiment in co-creation and digital empowerment within an aging, low-income urban community. Its outcomes—reduced energy consumption, lower emissions, improved air quality, and increased occupant comfort—position it as a leading reference for holistic urban regeneration and the pursuit of climate-neutral city goals in Europe[^1][^3][^2].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**
  - Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country, with a population of around 240,000[^1][^4].
  - It is recognized for its environmental leadership (European Green Capital 2012) and high proportion of green public areas—ensuring green space within 300m of every resident.
  - The city is compact, with balanced growth, a strong manufacturing and service economy, and a reputation for quality urban planning[^1][^4][^5].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - **Coronación District:** 
    - Historically built in the mid-20th century as working-class housing.
    - High density, predominantly multi-family apartment blocks[^3].
    - Aging population, low average income, significant social vulnerability.
    - Buildings with poor insulation, inefficient heating systems, and high energy poverty rates[^1][^3].
    - Public spaces in need of renewal; limited accessibility.
    - Environmental constraints: urban heat, air quality issues, and flood risk in some areas.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - High rates of energy poverty due to poorly insulated buildings and inefficient, costly energy systems.
  - Discomfort and health risks from inadequate indoor climate and poor air quality.
  - Social vulnerability, aging population, and low community engagement in urban change.
  - Underutilized and degraded public spaces.
  - High local emissions from fossil fuel heating.

- **Triggering Events:**
  - EU Horizon 2020 SmartEnCity call for demonstration cities.
  - City’s 2012 European Green Capital recognition spurred ambitions for climate action.
  - Local advocacy around energy poverty and urban renewal[^1][^2].

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - City government seeking to pilot climate solutions.
  - Regional authorities (Basque Government) interested in scalable retrofit models.
  - Research institutions (Tecnalia) promoting smart technologies.
  - Residents’ associations wary of displacement but interested in quality-of-life improvements.
  - Energy service companies exploring new business models.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**
  - Led by city council in partnership with regional government and research actors.
  - Integrated within broader Basque and Spanish climate/energy policies[^1][^2].

- **Relevant Policies:**
  - Basque Climate Change Strategy 2050.
  - Vitoria-Gasteiz Sustainable Energy Action Plan (SEAP).
  - Local zoning and building codes adapted for energy retrofits.

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
  - EU procurement and co-financing rules shaped project structure.
  - Ownership structure of the housing stock (condominiums) required complex resident engagement and co-decision[^1][^3].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Deep decarbonization of an existing urban neighborhood.
  - Demonstrate replicable solutions for energy poverty and climate action.
  - Empower residents through data and participation.

- **Vision Statement:**
  - To transform Coronación into a resilient, smart, and inclusive model for climate-neutral urban living.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**
  - Fits with Vitoria-Gasteiz’s climate neutrality target and the Basque Government’s regional decarbonization strategy.
  - Part of the pan-European SmartEnCity network, fostering learning across cities.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**
  - Retrofitting buildings and upgrading energy systems reduces consumption and emissions.
  - Smart monitoring and resident empowerment drive behavioral change.
  - Public space renewal and co-creation foster social cohesion and acceptance[^1][^3][^2].

- **Target Beneficiaries:**
  - Primarily low-income and elderly residents of the Coronación district.
  - Wider city benefits from improved air quality and reduced emissions.

- **Innovation Elements:**
  - Integrated package: combines physical retrofits, renewable energy, digital platforms, and participatory processes.
  - Focus on real-time monitoring and occupant feedback loops[^1][^2].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** City’s green leadership ambitions and alignment with EU policy.
- **Economic:** Energy cost savings for residents; regional economic stimulus.
- **Social:** Addressing energy poverty, improving comfort, and empowering vulnerable groups.
- **Environmental:** Climate targets, local air quality improvement, resource efficiency.
- **Technological:** Opportunity to pilot smart city technologies and digital twin platforms.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Holistic urban regeneration, “smart zero carbon city” model[^2].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Comprehensive building envelope insulation (façades, roofs) in 750–1,313 dwellings[^1][^3][^6].
  - Installation of a biomass-based district heating network, replacing individual fossil fuel boilers.
  - Refurbishment of public spaces to improve accessibility, green infrastructure, and community amenities[^1].

- **Scope and Phasing:**
  - Phased construction to minimize disruption.
  - Early engagement and pilot retrofits to build trust.
  - Sequential deployment of digital and energy systems[^3][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Building envelope retrofits (insulation, windows)
  - Central biomass district heating
  - Building and neighborhood energy management systems (BEMS/NEMS)
  - IoT-based indoor climate and air quality sensors
  - Digital twin and real-time dashboard for residents[^1][^2][^6]

- **Innovation Type:**
  - Technological (smart systems, renewables)
  - Social (co-design, behavioral nudging)
  - Governance (multi-level partnership)

- **Integration Approach:**
  - Energy, digital, and social interventions designed in tandem.
  - District heating network linked to building-level controls.
  - Data flows integrated into municipal platforms and resident dashboards.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**
  - Competitive tenders for construction and technology providers.
  - Performance-based contracts for energy services.

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**
  - Intensive outreach to homeowner associations to secure buy-in for retrofits.
  - Workshops, focus groups, and digital engagement tools for two-way communication[^1][^2].

- **Participatory Elements:**
  - Co-creation of public space designs.
  - Resident feedback incorporated into dashboard design and services.

- **Project Management:**
  - Dedicated project office with city, regional, and research partners.
  - Regular reporting to SmartEnCity consortium and EU.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**
  - Vitoria-Gasteiz share: approx. €10 million, with breakdown across retrofits, heating, ICT, and public spaces[^1][^2].
  - Cost per dwelling: variable, with resident co-financing required for some works.

- **Funding Mix:**
  - EU Horizon 2020 grant (majority)
  - Regional and municipal contributions
  - Resident co-investment (for building works)
  - Private sector investment (district heating ESCO model)

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**
  - User fees for district heating
  - Cost savings from reduced energy bills

- **Financial Sustainability:**
  - District heating designed for cost recovery and long-term operation.
  - Ongoing monitoring to ensure persistence of savings[^1][^2].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:** Integrating new heating systems into legacy buildings; ensuring airtightness in old structures; interoperability of digital platforms[^1][^3][^6].
- **Financial:** Securing resident co-financing in a low-income area; managing cost overruns for construction materials.
- **Social and Political:** Building trust and informed consent among elderly and vulnerable populations wary of change; risk of gentrification or displacement.
- **Administrative:** Navigating complex condominium ownership structures; aligning city, regional, and EU regulations.
- **Environmental:** Managing construction waste and minimizing disruption during retrofits.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**
  - Flexible engagement and tailored financial packages for residents.
  - Technical assistance for homeowner associations.
  - Iterative deployment of digital tools to match resident capacity[^1][^2].

- **Design Modifications:**
  - Phased roll-out to pilot solutions and adjust based on feedback.
  - Adjusting insulation techniques to suit building typologies.

- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**
  - Early and deep engagement is essential for resident buy-in.
  - Digital empowerment must be coupled with capacity building.
  - Public space improvements can catalyze acceptance of energy retrofits[^1][^2].

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**

- **Contextual Dependencies:**
  - Social housing ownership and local governance structures influenced project feasibility.
  - Availability of EU funding and local technical capacity were critical.

- **Critical Success Factors:**
  - Strong public sector leadership.
  - Deep community engagement and transparent co-financing mechanisms.
  - Integration of technical, digital, and social solutions.

- **Scalability:**
  - Approach can be scaled to similar districts in European mid-sized cities.

#### **Comparative Analysis**

- **Similar Initiatives:**
  - Comparable to other SmartEnCity demo sites (Tartu, Sonderborg) and to district-scale retrofits in northern Europe.

- **Best Practices:**
  - Holistic integration, digital empowerment, and participatory planning.

- **Cautionary Lessons:**
  - Complexity of resident engagement in owner-occupied buildings.
  - Need for flexible financing to avoid exclusion of vulnerable groups.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- Demonstrates effective integration of physical, digital, and social innovation for deep decarbonization.
- Strong resident empowerment through digital tools and participatory planning.
- Clear focus on vulnerable populations and equity.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- High complexity and resource requirements for resident engagement.
- Financial model may not be readily replicable in higher-income markets or without EU support.
- Some risk of gentrification or exclusion if property values rise.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- Explicit targeting of energy-poor, elderly, and otherwise vulnerable residents.
- Inclusive processes, but ongoing vigilance needed to prevent indirect displacement.

#### **Future Outlook**
- Ongoing monitoring of energy, emissions, and social impacts.
- Potential for scaling to other districts in Vitoria-Gasteiz and the Basque Country.
- Next phases may include further digital integration, renewable energy expansion, and adaptive management as the community evolves[^1][^2].
