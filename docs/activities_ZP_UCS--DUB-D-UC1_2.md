# Executive summary## Ethos Engineering Living Lab Digital Twin

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Ethos Engineering Living Lab Digital Twin
- **Alternative Names:** Ethos Living Lab, Ethos Digital iLab, Ethos HQ WELL Performance Lab
- **Location:** Sandyford, Dublin, Ireland (Ethos Engineering Headquarters)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.2766° N, 6.2178° W (Sandyford Business District)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Concept Initiation: Early 2021  
  - Launch of Living Lab: November 2021  
  - WELL Performance Rating Achieved: 2022  
  - Ongoing Iteration: 2022–present
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: ~1,000–2,000 m² (Ethos HQ, mid-size office building)  
  - Population Affected: ~250 staff directly; scalable insights for clients and wider industry
  - Investment Value: Not publicly disclosed; includes substantial in-kind investment via in-house R&D and partnerships
- **Leading Organization(s):** Ethos Engineering (Ireland’s largest Mechanical, Electrical & Plumbing (MEP) consultancy)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Private (Ethos Engineering)  
  - Potential partial support via R&D tax credits[^1]  
  - Technology partnerships (e.g., Autodesk, Spaceti)

#### **Executive Summary**
The Ethos Engineering Living Lab Digital Twin represents a pioneering step in smart, sustainable building operation and design. Situated at Ethos Engineering’s Dublin headquarters, the Living Lab transforms the office into a real-time testbed for digital building management, integrating IoT sensors, digital twin technology, and advanced analytics on a unified platform. The initiative’s primary objectives are to optimize energy efficiency, enhance occupant well-being, and support Ethos’s net zero carbon targets, all while generating actionable insights for both internal use and external client advisory.

The Lab continuously collects data on air quality, occupancy, energy, noise, and light, enabling real-time adjustments to building systems and proactive space management. The project is distinguished by its integration of a digital twin—leveraging Autodesk Tandem and Spaceti platforms—to visualize and simulate building operations, thereby enabling predictive maintenance and design-driven decision-making. The Living Lab’s impact extends beyond technical optimization: it achieved the world’s first WELL Performance Rating for an office, strengthening its role as a benchmark for health and sustainability in the built environment.

Key implementation challenges included integrating heterogeneous data sources, ensuring data accuracy, and upskilling facility managers. The Lab’s success in overcoming these challenges has yielded measurable improvements in energy efficiency, indoor environmental quality, and operational cost savings, while also supporting ESG reporting and carbon reduction commitments. As both a demonstration and research platform, the Living Lab provides scalable, transferable lessons for green building neighborhoods and the wider smart building sector[^2][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

##### **City Profile**
- **Population:** Dublin is Ireland’s capital, with a metropolitan population exceeding 1.2 million.
- **Economic Base:** Major hub for finance, technology, professional services, and international corporate headquarters.
- **Geographic Characteristics:** Coastal city, temperate climate, characterized by a mix of historic and modern development.
- **Urban Hierarchy:** Dublin is Ireland’s primary global city and the country’s dominant knowledge economy center.

##### **Neighborhood/District Characteristics**
- **Sandyford Business District:**  
  - Historically an industrial estate, now a prominent office and tech cluster.
  - Land use: High-density commercial, mixed-use, some residential.
  - Demographics: Professional, diverse, predominantly working-age population.
  - Infrastructure: Modern offices, good public transport links (Luas light rail), strong digital infrastructure.
  - Environmental Conditions: Urban heat island effect, air quality generally good but challenged by traffic and development intensity.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Lack of real-time, actionable data on building performance.
  - Fragmented building management systems (multiple platforms, Excel sheets).
  - Difficulty achieving net zero/ESG goals due to limited operational visibility.
  - Post-COVID concerns about workplace air quality and occupant well-being.
  - Inefficiencies in energy and space usage; rising operational costs[^2][^5][^3].

- **Triggering Events:**
  - Industry shift toward sustainability and net zero (driven by investor and client demand).
  - Introduction of the WELL Performance Rating (new benchmarking opportunity).
  - COVID-19 pandemic highlighting need for healthy, adaptable workplaces[^3].

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Ethos Engineering management and staff.
  - Technology partners (Autodesk, Spaceti).
  - Facility managers and building users.
  - Clients (Ethos intended to use HQ as a demonstrator for client engagement).

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Private sector-led, but aligned with city and national sustainability agendas.
  - Facility and workplace health subject to Irish and EU occupational safety regulations.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Irish Climate Action Plan (national net zero targets).
  - Dublin City Council’s Smart City and sustainability strategies.
  - International WELL Building Standard.

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - No major legal barriers; project leveraged emerging standards (WELL, ESG reporting).
  - R&D tax credit regime in Ireland encouraged innovation[^1].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - *Data-driven design*—“You can’t manage what you don’t measure.”
  - Smart building/healthy building paradigms (WELL, ESG, digital twin).
  - “Place-activation”—buildings as dynamic, responsive environments.

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Retrofit of HQ with extensive IoT sensors: environmental (air quality, noise, light), occupancy (desk, people counters), energy meters.
  - Minimal physical intervention; focus on technology integration.

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Sensor installation, air quality and occupancy monitoring.
  - Phase 2: Energy optimization, integration with digital twin, advanced analytics.
  - Ongoing: Expansion of data types, user engagement tools, platform enhancements[^2][^3].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - IoT sensors: 13 environmental quality, 110 desk, 6 people counting, plus others.
  - Unified IoT and analytics platform (Autodesk Tandem, Spaceti).
  - Digital twin: real-time and historical data visualization, simulation, and predictive analytics.
  - Mobile tenant engagement app for occupant interface.
  - Data integration and API development for ESG reporting[^2][^3][^4].

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (IoT, digital twin, advanced analytics).
  - Process (continuous improvement, data-driven management).
  - Social (user engagement, WELL Performance Rating).

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Seamless data flow from sensors to unified platform.
  - Integration with BIM for design feedback.
  - Interactive visualization for non-technical users[^2][^5][^4].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - In-house project by Ethos Engineering, with vendor partnerships (Autodesk, Spaceti).

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Internal: staff surveys, wellness committee, facility manager upskilling.
  - External: transparency in reporting, client showcases.

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Occupant feedback mechanisms.
  - Mobile app for user control of microenvironments.

- **Project Management:**  
  - Cross-disciplinary team (engineering, digital, HR, management).
  - Iterative, agile approach[^3].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not disclosed; likely moderate for technology, substantial in staff time and R&D.

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Company investment.
  - R&D tax incentives[^1].
  - Technology partnership in-kind contributions.

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Indirect: improved operational efficiency.
  - Enhanced client service offerings (consultancy, design).

- **Financial Sustainability:**

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integrating multiple sensor types and data streams.
  - Ensuring accuracy and calibration of devices.
  - Data security and privacy (especially with occupancy monitoring).
  - Maintaining interoperability with legacy systems[^2][^3][^4].

- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront technology investment.
  - Uncertainty regarding ROI from emerging digital solutions.

- **Social/Political:**  
  - Need for staff buy-in and upskilling of facility managers.
  - Managing change in workplace practices.

- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination across engineering, digital, and HR teams.
  - Continuous data management and reporting.

- **Environmental:**  
  - Minimal direct issues (no new build, no major site impacts).

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Comparative sensor placement for calibration (breathing zone vs. ceiling).
  - Collaboration with manufacturers to improve sensor firmware.
  - Agile iteration based on user and data feedback[^3].

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusted cleaning materials to reduce VOCs after data analysis.
  - Increased air flow in response to sensor findings.
  - Added new sensor types and expanded platform modules.

- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of user training and engagement for technology adoption.
  - Real-time data can uncover unexpected issues (e.g., cleaning chemical spikes).
  - Value of a unified platform for actionable insights[^2][^3].
