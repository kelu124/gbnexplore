# Executive summary

## CIVITAS ECCENTRIC – Electric Vehicle Monitoring and Optimization

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** CIVITAS ECCENTRIC – Electric Vehicle Monitoring and Optimization
- **Alternative Names:** CIVITAS ECCENTRIC Turku EV Monitoring; Turku ECCENTRIC Living Lab
- **Location:** Turku, Kupittaa District, Southwest Finland, Finland
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 60.4500° N, 22.2667° E (Kupittaa area)
- **Timeline:**  
  - **Start:** 2016 (CIVITAS ECCENTRIC program launch)  
  - **Key Milestones:**  
    - 2017: Living lab operational in Kupittaa  
    - 2018: Full-scale real-time EV monitoring system launched  
    - 2020: Project conclusion and final evaluation  
  - **Status:** Completed, with ongoing legacy activities and integration into city mobility strategies
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Direct impact: Kupittaa and adjacent districts (several thousand residents, businesses, and municipal fleet users)  
  - Broader influence: City-wide policy and operational changes affecting over 190,000 residents
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - City of Turku  
  - Regional Council of Southwest Finland  
  - Turku University of Applied Sciences  
  - European Commission (CIVITAS ECCENTRIC consortium)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020 (CIVITAS ECCENTRIC Grant Agreement 690699-37)  
  - City of Turku municipal funds  
  - Regional Council of Southwest Finland  
  - Private and academic partners (in-kind contributions)

#### **Executive Summary**

CIVITAS ECCENTRIC – Electric Vehicle Monitoring and Optimization in Turku, Finland, stands as a flagship urban mobility initiative, integrating advanced data analytics and telematics to revolutionize municipal and shared electric vehicle (EV) management. Launched in 2016 as part of the broader European CIVITAS ECCENTRIC program, Turku’s living lab in the Kupittaa district piloted real-time energy consumption monitoring for both public fleet and shared EVs. The core objective was to maximize energy efficiency, reduce municipal emissions, and foster broad uptake of EVs by providing actionable, data-driven recommendations for route planning, charging practices, and energy management.

The program featured cutting-edge technologies, including fleet telematics, user-facing mobile applications, and robust analytics platforms. A participatory approach engaged city officials, fleet operators, residents, and business stakeholders, ensuring solutions were responsive to real-world needs and behaviors. The initiative directly supported Turku’s strategic ambitions for carbon neutrality, modal shift toward sustainable transport, and improved urban livability.

Significant outcomes included measurable reductions in fleet emissions and operational costs, increased public awareness and education around sustainable mobility, and the establishment of a replicable model for data-driven EV optimization in urban settings. The project’s collaborative structure—integrating local government, academia, and civil society—strengthened institutional capacity and paved the way for continued innovation in Turku’s mobility ecosystem. Its legacy is visible in subsequent policy integration, expanded EV infrastructure, and ongoing efforts to scale smart mobility solutions citywide[^1][^2][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Turku is Finland’s sixth-largest city, a historic and cultural hub with a population of approximately 190,000 in the urban core and over 300,000 in the metropolitan region. The city is known for its diverse economy, anchored by education, technology, maritime industries, and regional governance. Geographically, Turku is situated on the southwest coast, characterized by a temperate climate, proximity to the Baltic Sea, and a mix of urban and green landscapes. Turku is recognized as a progressive city within Finland’s national urban hierarchy, often piloting innovative climate and mobility policies[^1][^2].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The Kupittaa district, east of the city center, is a dynamic area with strong links to educational institutions, high-tech industries, and growing residential communities. The district features a mix of commercial, academic, and residential land use, and has been targeted for strategic growth in knowledge-sector jobs.  
  - *Historical Development:* Transitioned from industrial and railway uses to a vibrant innovation hub.  
  - *Current Land Use:* Mixed-use, with business parks, university campuses, and new housing developments.  
  - *Demographics:* Diverse, skewing younger due to university presence; varied income levels, multicultural composition.  
  - *Infrastructure:* High-quality public transport, bike paths, and pedestrian walkways; emerging EV charging network.  
  - *Environmental Conditions:* Air quality generally good, but traffic and emissions concerns persist in busy corridors; municipal climate targets drive sustainability efforts[^2][^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High reliance on fossil-fuel vehicles in municipal and private fleets  
  - Rising transportation emissions, conflicting with carbon neutrality goals  
  - Underutilization of data for optimizing fleet operations and energy use  
  - Limited public and stakeholder awareness of sustainable mobility options  
  - Infrastructure gaps for EV charging and fleet management[^1][^2][^3]

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Adoption of ambitious climate targets by City of Turku  
  - Inclusion in the European CIVITAS ECCENTRIC program  
  - Regional push for innovation in smart mobility and sustainable transport[^1][^4]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (mobility, environment, planning departments)  
  - Regional Council (strategic development)  
  - Universities and research institutes (technology, analytics)  
  - Fleet operators (municipal, shared services)  
  - Residents, businesses, civil society organizations  
  - European Commission (funding, oversight)[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - City-led initiative, integrated with regional and EU governance structures  
  - Multi-level coordination: municipal departments, regional council, European partners[^1][^4][^3]

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Turku’s city strategy for carbon neutrality  
  - National and EU policies on sustainable mobility, air quality, and innovation  
  - Local zoning and transport planning regulations enabling EV infrastructure[^1][^2][^3]

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - EU grant funding facilitated innovation  
  - Data privacy and security regulations required careful management  
  - Institutional capacity for cross-sectoral collaboration was critical[^1][^3]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Optimize energy consumption and route planning for municipal/shared EVs  
  - Reduce emissions and operational costs in city transport  
  - Increase public and stakeholder engagement in sustainable mobility  
  - Support city’s transition toward carbon neutrality[^1][^2][^3]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  *“To establish Turku as a leader in data-driven, sustainable urban mobility by leveraging technology, stakeholder participation, and evidence-based management of electric vehicles.”*

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Directly connected to Turku’s city strategy and Finland’s national climate action plan  
  - Contributed to EU-wide goals for sustainable, smart urban transport[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Real-time data collection enables better management and optimization of EV fleets  
  - Improved efficiency and transparency encourage wider adoption of EVs  
  - Public education and participatory processes drive behavioral change  
  - Overall, reduced emissions and costs lead to improved urban sustainability[^1][^2][^3]

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Municipal fleet operators  
  - Shared mobility service users  
  - City residents (health, environment)  
  - Local businesses (cost savings, reputation)[^1][^2][^3]

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Integrated telematics for fleet monitoring  
  - User-facing mobile apps for feedback and behavior change  
  - Living lab approach for real-world testing and rapid iteration[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** City and regional leadership prioritizing climate action and innovation[^1][^2]
- **Economic:** Cost savings for municipal operations, potential for new business models[^1][^3]
- **Social:** Desire for improved urban quality of life, equity in mobility access[^1][^4]
- **Environmental:** Urgency to reduce emissions, improve air quality, and meet climate targets[^1][^2][^3]
- **Technological:** Maturation of smart city technologies, telematics, and data analytics[^1][^3]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Living lab methodology: real-life experimentation and co-creation with users  
  - Data-driven urban management: evidence-based decision-making  
  - Integration of mobility, energy, and urban planning[^1][^2][^3]

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Deployment in Kupittaa district and adjacent areas  
  - Installation of EV charging stations, telematics devices, and sensor networks  
  - Creation of digital and physical interfaces for stakeholder engagement[^2][^3]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Pilot in Kupittaa (2017-2018)  
  - Citywide scaling and institutionalization (2018-2020)[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - **Fleet telematics:** GPS and energy sensors for real-time monitoring  
  - **Data analytics platforms:** Processing and visualization of energy and mobility data  
  - **Mobile applications:** User feedback, behavioral nudges, real-time status updates  
  - **EV charging infrastructure:** Smart chargers with data integration[^1][^2][^3]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (smart mobility, analytics)  
  - Social (participatory design, stakeholder engagement)  
  - Governance (cross-sectoral collaboration)[^1][^2][^3]

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Technologies linked to existing urban IT and transport systems  
  - Data shared across municipal departments and with project partners  
  - Feedback loops established between users, operators, and planners[^1][^2][^3]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive tendering for technology providers; use of local academic expertise  
  - Collaboration agreements among public, private, and research stakeholders[^3]

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Participatory workshops, surveys (Mobility Barometer), and digital platforms  
  - Involvement of vulnerable groups (elderly, disabled) in planning and feedback[^1][^3]

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Co-creation sessions  
  - Open data and transparent reporting  
  - Gamification and incentive structures for user involvement[^1][^3]

- **Project Management:**  
  - Steering committees with cross-sectoral representation  
  - Periodic review and adaptation based on monitoring data[^1][^3]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total measure funding: €145,450 (EV monitoring component)  
  - Overall CIVITAS ECCENTRIC Turku budget: multi-million euro across measures[^3]

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EU CIVITAS grant (majority)  
  - Municipal and regional co-financing  
  - In-kind contributions from academic and private partners[^1][^3]

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - No direct user fees for municipal EVs; potential cost recovery via efficiency savings  
  - Shared mobility services explored for fee structures[^3]

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Cost savings from reduced energy and maintenance expenditures  
  - Strategies for ongoing integration into municipal budgets and operations[^1][^3]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integrating new sensors and telematics into legacy fleet systems  
  - Ensuring data security and privacy compliance  
  - Scaling from pilot to full citywide deployment[^1][^3]

- **Financial:**  
  - Balancing upfront investment with long-term savings  
  - Navigating grant conditions and funding cycles[^3]

- **Social and Political:**  
  - Maintaining user engagement beyond initial pilot phase  
  - Addressing skepticism or inertia among fleet operators and users[^1][^3]

- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordinating across departments and partner organizations  
  - Managing data sharing and governance[^1][^3]

- **Environmental:**  
  - Ensuring reliable EV performance in Nordic climate conditions  
  - Addressing site-specific constraints for charger installation[^1][^2]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Iterative testing and rapid feedback loops (living lab model)  
  - Active communication and education campaigns  
  - Technical support for users and operators[^1][^3]

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments to telematics hardware based on user feedback  
  - Refinements to mobile app functionality to increase engagement[^3]

- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Early and ongoing stakeholder involvement is critical  
  - Data-driven approaches must be paired with user-friendly interfaces  
  - Flexibility and adaptation to unforeseen challenges enhances project resilience[^1][^3]
