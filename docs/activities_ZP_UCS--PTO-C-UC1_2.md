# Executive summary

## Falcão Primary School Green Roofs – LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Falcão Primary School Green Roofs (Escola EB-JI Falcão)
- **Alternative Names:** LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN Porto Pilot; Escola do Falcão NBS Demonstrator
- **Location:** Campanhã District, Porto, Portugal
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 41.1557° N, 8.5871° W
- **Timeline:**  
  - Start: 2019 (project conceptualization)  
  - Implementation: 2021–2023  
  - Ongoing: Monitoring and educational programs continue through 2024–2025[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: 700 m² green roofs and facades[^3][^4]  
  - Population affected: School community (~200 students, staff, visiting public)  
  - Investment value: Funded under LIFE17 CCA/ES/000088 (exact site budget not publicly specified)
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Royal Botanic Garden (RJB-CSIC, Spain – Project Coordinator)[^5][^6][^7]  
  - Porto City Council  
  - Porto Energy Agency  
  - CARTIF Technology Centre  
  - CIMAC (Intermunicipal Community of Central Alentejo)
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union’s LIFE Programme (Climate Change Adaptation)[^5][^8][^6]  
  - Municipal budget (Porto City Council)[^9][^1]  
  - In-kind support from partners

#### **Executive Summary**

The Falcão Primary School Green Roofs initiative, under the LIFE-myBUILDINGisGREEN project, is a pioneering demonstration of **nature-based solutions (NBS)** for climate adaptation in educational buildings. Implemented in Porto, Portugal, the project transformed the school with **700 m² of green roofs, green facades, and solar photovoltaic panels**, aiming to improve **energy efficiency, bioclimatic comfort, and climate resilience**[^9][^3][^4]. These interventions address multiple urban challenges: reducing the urban heat island effect, improving stormwater management, fostering biodiversity, and lowering energy demands for heating and cooling.

The project stands out for its **multi-functional approach**, integrating vegetation-based systems with renewable energy, monitored through advanced energy and biodiversity metrics. The initiative also serves as a **living laboratory** for students, staff, and the community, embedding sustainability education into the curriculum and school culture[^1][^10]. The results are tangible: **surface temperature reductions of over 5°C** compared to conventional roofs, **rainwater loss reduced by 31%**, and the emergence of over 25 new species in school grounds[^5].

Stakeholder engagement, maintenance planning, and education were central, addressing challenges in retrofitting older infrastructure and ensuring long-term viability. The project’s visibility has catalyzed broader NBS adoption in Porto and informed EU-level policy discussions. As a scalable and transferable model, the Falcão School demonstrates the potential of **nature-based and technical solutions** to meet urban climate, social, and educational objectives simultaneously[^9][^5][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Porto, Portugal’s second-largest city, has a population of ~230,000 (municipal), situated along the Douro River. It is a regional hub for commerce, education, and culture, with a temperate Atlantic climate—mild, humid winters and warm summers[^9][^11]. The city is actively positioning itself as a leader in urban sustainability and climate adaptation[^9].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  Campanhã is a historically industrial area east of the city center, now transitioning with new urban regeneration efforts.  
  - *Historical Development:* Mixed-use, traditionally working class, with aging building stock.
  - *Land Use:* Residential, educational, and emerging green spaces.
  - *Demographics:* Diverse, with significant youth population and lower average incomes than central districts.
  - *Infrastructure:* School built pre-1990 thermal regulation; prior to intervention, limited green space and poor energy performance[^11].
  - *Environmental Constraints:* Susceptible to urban heat, localized flooding, and air pollution.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High indoor temperatures during heatwaves, poor thermal comfort, high energy costs[^11][^5].
  - Inadequate stormwater management, leading to runoff and localized flooding[^9][^5].
  - Limited biodiversity and green space in school grounds.
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU calls for climate adaptation in public buildings.  
  - Local policy initiatives for sustainable urban regeneration.  
  - Community and expert advocacy for healthy learning environments.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - School community (students, staff, parents) seeking improved comfort and learning conditions.
  - City officials focused on climate resilience.
  - National Association of Green Roofs promoting NBS uptake[^9].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Porto City Council provided leadership, supported by national and EU climate adaptation frameworks.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Municipal Forestation Plan[^9].
  - PDM (Porto Master Plan) with Environmental Index for NBS promotion[^9].
  - EU LIFE Programme guidelines for adaptation and resilience[^5].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive regulatory environment for NBS in public buildings.
  - Challenges in retrofitting older public infrastructure within safety and accessibility codes.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Increase climate resilience and comfort in educational buildings through NBS.
  - Lower energy consumption and operational costs.
  - Enhance urban biodiversity and ecosystem services.
- **Vision Statement:**  
  Creating healthy, resilient, and educational urban spaces where nature and technology work together for community well-being.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Porto’s municipal climate adaptation strategy.
  - EU and national goals for sustainable infrastructure and energy transition[^5][^9].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By installing green roofs, facades, and PV panels, the project reduces heat stress, improves rainwater retention, and lowers energy demand, resulting in better comfort, lower costs, and richer biodiversity[^5][^11].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  Primary: School students and staff.  
  Secondary: Wider community, urban planners, policymakers, and NBS practitioners.
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - “Bio-solar roof” combining vegetation and solar power[^9].
  - Real-time energy and biodiversity monitoring.
  - Use of multiple green roof types (extensive, GUL/cork, bio-solar)[^9][^3].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Municipal leadership for climate action.
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Prospects for energy savings and operational cost reduction.
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Advocacy for healthier learning environments and community engagement.
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Response to climate change, flood risks, and biodiversity loss.
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Advances in green roof systems, PV integration, and energy monitoring.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Nature-based solutions for climate adaptation (NBS), emphasizing bioclimatic comfort, ecosystem enhancement, and energy efficiency[^5][^11].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Installation of 700 m² green roofs and facades across school buildings[^3][^4].
  - Integration of solar PV panels (“bio-solar” roofs)[^9].
  - Rainwater management systems and educational signage[^12].
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Planning and design (2019–2020)
  - Construction and installation (2021–2023)
  - Monitoring and education (2023–2025 ongoing)[^1][^2]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Extensive green roofs (vegetated, soil-based, low-maintenance)[^13]
  - Green facades for shading and temperature regulation
  - Photovoltaic panels for renewable energy[^9][^12]
  - Energy and environmental monitoring sensors
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (roof/solar integration)
  - Social (education, engagement)
  - Process-oriented (multi-actor collaboration)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Combined green roof and PV installation (“bio-solar”)
  - Retrofitting on existing structures with attention to permeability, insulation, and safety[^9][^5]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Selection of specialized NBS contractors and solar installers via EU public procurement.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Workshops, open days, and participatory design activities with students, teachers, and community[^10][^12].
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Educational programs, interactive research projects, student-led biodiversity monitoring[^1][^14][^10].
- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by RJB-CSIC, with local execution by Porto City Council and technical support from the Porto Energy Agency.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - LIFE Programme grant (EU funding covers implementation, research, monitoring, and education)[^5][^8].
  - Municipal co-financing for maintenance and educational activities.
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Primarily public/EU, with in-kind partner contributions.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Energy savings from PV panels reduce operational costs[^9].
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Ongoing monitoring to optimize maintenance and maximize energy savings for reinvestment.

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Retrofitting green roofs on pre-1990s building structure[^11].
  - Ensuring waterproofing, load-bearing, and compatibility with PV systems[^9][^12].
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Upfront investment; maintenance cost planning for long-term sustainability.
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Stakeholder education and overcoming skepticism about NBS effectiveness.
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Coordinating among multiple EU partners and local agencies.
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Initial uncertainty about biodiversity response; need for adaptive management.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Use of modular green roof systems and cork insulation (GUL technology) for easier retrofitting[^9].
  - Pilot monitoring to adapt maintenance and optimize NBS performance.
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusted plant species mix to local microclimate and biodiversity goals.
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of robust stakeholder engagement and ongoing education.
  - Value of flexible design to accommodate building constraints and user needs[^5][^1][^15].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Comprehensive integration of nature-based and technical solutions[^9][^5]
- Strong educational and community engagement[^1][^14][^10]
- Measurable, positive impacts on comfort, energy, and biodiversity[^5]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Maintenance of green roofs and facades requires long-term commitment and expertise[^9][^5]
- Retrofitting constraints for older buildings; may not be universally applicable[^11]
- Upfront costs higher than conventional upgrades

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Delivered benefits to a diverse, relatively underserved school community[^9][^11]
- Designed for accessibility and inclusion; educational programs reached all students[^1][^14]

#### **Future Outlook**

- Long-term monitoring and educational programming continue through 2025 and beyond[^1][^2]
- Potential for expansion to other public buildings and broader urban regeneration
- Ongoing challenges: ensuring maintenance, scaling up, and adapting to evolving climate risks
