# Executive summary## Sponge Parks and Social Housing Energy Communities (EXHAUSTION Horizon Project)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Sponge Parks and Social Housing Energy Communities
- **Alternative Names:** EXHAUSTION Porto Demonstrator; Porto Climate Adaptation and Energy Communities
- **Location:** Porto, Portugal (several social housing districts, city-wide interventions)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 41.1579° N, 8.6291° W (center of Porto)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Initial planning: 2013–2018  
  - EXHAUSTION Horizon Project: June 2019 – May 2023[^1]  
  - Porto Climate Pact launch: 2022[^2]  
  - Ongoing implementation and monitoring as of 2025
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Multiple neighborhoods, including social housing districts  
  - Population affected: ~12% of Porto’s population lives in social housing (~30,000+ residents)[^2]  
  - Investment value: €150 million for social housing retrofit[^2]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - City of Porto (Vice Mayor’s office, Environment and Innovation)  
  - University of Porto  
  - EXHAUSTION Horizon2020 Consortium (CICERO, European research partners)[^3][^1]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 (grant agreement No 820655)[^4]  
  - Municipal budget  
  - Additional public/private sector involvement via Porto Climate Pact[^2]

#### **Executive Summary**

The Sponge Parks and Social Housing Energy Communities initiative in Porto is a flagship demonstration of nature-based solutions and community-driven energy transition, developed under the umbrella of the EXHAUSTION EU Horizon 2020 project. Facing acute climate risks—including urban flooding, heatwaves, and energy poverty—Porto mapped local vulnerabilities and deployed integrated strategies combining sponge parks (for stormwater management), green roofs, urban forests, and community-run solar energy systems. The project emphasizes operational optimization through real-time data monitoring, automated reporting, and the sharing of best practices across social housing networks.

Porto’s approach is distinctive for its holistic, ecosystem-based urban planning, which places ecological restoration at the core of city development. By retrofitting social housing with insulation and green roofs, and by forming energy communities that co-own and operate solar installations, the city improved energy efficiency by 45%, reduced energy bills, and increased resilience for vulnerable populations[^2]. The initiative’s collaborative governance, rooted in the Porto Climate Pact, includes residents, academia, and local organizations, fostering inclusive decision-making and long-term engagement.

The EXHAUSTION project’s interdisciplinary research and monitoring enable continuous adaptation and knowledge transfer, making Porto a model for European cities seeking scalable, data-driven solutions to climate adaptation and urban sustainability. The local experience directly informs EU-level policy debates on energy decentralization, water management, and equitable climate action[^2][^3][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**  
Porto, Portugal’s second-largest city, has a population of over 230,000 within city limits and over 1.7 million in the metropolitan area. Economically, Porto is a historic port and commercial center, with a diverse base in services, tourism, manufacturing, and education. Geographically, Porto is characterized by dense urbanization, hilly terrain, and proximity to the Atlantic coast, making it vulnerable to flooding, sea level rise, and heat island effects[^2].

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
The initiative targets several social housing districts, which:
- Developed largely in the postwar period, often with low-quality building stock
- Feature mixed-use land, but predominantly residential
- Exhibit high concentrations of elderly residents, low-income families, and ethnically diverse groups
- Suffer from aging infrastructure, limited public green space, and poor insulation
- Face environmental challenges: urban heat islands, flood risk, and air pollution[^2]

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

**Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
- Urban flooding due to impermeable surfaces and obsolete drainage  
- Heatwaves exacerbated by dense built form and lack of vegetation  
- High energy poverty and inefficient housing, especially in social housing  
- Limited resident engagement in climate adaptation  
- Fragmented data and reporting on neighborhood vulnerabilities[^2]

**Triggering Events:**  
- Successive heatwaves and droughts (esp. post-2017)[^2]  
- European policy mandates (Covenant of Mayors since 2008)  
- Local political mobilization following the election of Vice Mayor Filipe Araújo in 2013[^2]

**Stakeholder Landscape:**  
- City government (policy and planning lead)
- University of Porto (research, monitoring, technical support)
- Residents of social housing districts (beneficiaries and co-designers)
- NGOs, local businesses, and service providers (implementation partners)
- EU research partners (EXHAUSTION Consortium)[^3][^1]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

**Governance Framework:**  
- Municipal authority via Porto’s Master Plan and Climate Pact[^2]
- EU Horizon2020 research and demonstration governance[^4]

**Relevant Policies:**  
- Porto Master Plan (focus on ecological structure and land use)
- Covenant of Mayors Pledge to Climate and Energy (EU-wide targets)
- Portuguese national energy and housing standards

**Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
- Supportive regulatory environment for renewables and water reuse, but limited incentives for grid feed-in[^2]
- Complex zoning and heritage conservation constraints in historic neighborhoods

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

**Primary Goals:**  
- Reduce urban flooding and heat risk using nature-based solutions  
- Retrofit social housing to improve energy efficiency and reduce energy poverty  
- Establish community energy systems for local renewable generation  
- Foster data-driven, participatory climate adaptation

**Vision Statement:**  
To build a healthy, resilient, and inclusive city where ecological restoration and community empowerment transform vulnerable neighborhoods into climate-adaptive green building neighborhoods[^2].

**Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
- Integrated into Porto’s Master Plan and the Porto Climate Pact (2022)[^2]
- Direct contribution to EU Green Deal and Covenant of Mayors targets

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

**Theory of Change:**  
Mapping local risks enables targeted interventions—sponge parks absorb water, green roofs cool buildings, and energy communities reduce bills and emissions. Real-time data and collaborative governance ensure the lasting impact and scalability of solutions[^2].

**Target Beneficiaries:**  
- Social housing residents (especially elderly, low-income, and marginalized groups)
- Wider urban population (flood, heat, and air quality improvements)

**Innovation Elements:**  
- Large-scale use of “sponge” parks in dense city settings  
- Energy communities as co-owned, locally managed solar systems  
- Integration of landscape architecture, bioclimatic expertise, and digital monitoring platforms[^2]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** Strong mayoral leadership, EU climate commitments, local demand for action
- **Economic Drivers:** Energy savings, reduced public health costs, job creation via retrofits
- **Social Drivers:** Resident empowerment, equity in climate adaptation, collaborative governance
- **Environmental Drivers:** Urgent need for flood and heat resilience, biodiversity restoration
- **Technological Drivers:** Advancements in sensors, solar PV, and digital platforms for energy management[^2][^3]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

**Conceptual Framework:**  
Ecological urbanism, climate resilience, and participatory community development[^2]

**Physical/Spatial Design:**  
- Sponge parks (landscape engineered for water absorption in parks and open spaces)
- Green roofs on schools, transport hubs, and retrofitted older buildings
- Urban forests and tree nurseries with resident involvement
- Distributed solar PV systems on social housing rooftops and community buildings

**Scope and Phasing:**  
- Initial mapping and pilot in selected districts
- Gradual rollout of green roofs, sponge parks, and energy communities
- Ongoing monitoring and iterative design

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

**Technologies Deployed:**  
- Sponge parks (bioengineered soils, permeable surfaces)
- Green roofs (insulation, water reuse systems)
- Solar PV and battery storage for energy communities
- Digital energy management platforms (real-time monitoring, automated reporting)
- Data-driven mapping of neighborhood risks and assets[^2]

**Innovation Type:**  
- Technological (nature-based infrastructure, renewables, digital platforms)
- Social (community co-design, participatory decision-making)
- Governance (cross-sector partnerships, decentralized energy ownership)

**Integration Approach:**  
- Embedded in existing building stock and urban fabric
- Linked to master plan regulations and citywide climate strategies
- Interoperable digital platforms for monitoring and reporting

#### **Implementation Methodology**

**Procurement and Contracting:**  
- Competitive tendering for retrofit and infrastructure works
- Partnership agreements with University of Porto and EXHAUSTION Consortium[^3][^1]

**Stakeholder Engagement:**  
- Participatory mapping and co-design sessions with residents
- Stakeholder forums as part of Porto Climate Pact

**Participatory Elements:**  
- Residents select tree species for gardens
- Energy communities operate and manage solar assets collaboratively

**Project Management:**  
- City-led coordination with dedicated project office
- Scientific advisory from University of Porto and international partners[^3]

#### **Financing Model**

**Budget and Costs:**  
- Social housing retrofit: €150 million[^2]
- Sponge parks, green roofs, and solar installations: additional municipal and EU funds

**Funding Mix:**  
- EU Horizon2020 funding (research and demonstration)[^4]
- Municipal budget allocations
- Stakeholder contributions via Porto Climate Pact

**Revenue Mechanisms:**  
- Energy cost savings for residents
- Potential future incentives for grid feed-in (pending regulatory changes)

**Financial Sustainability:**  
- Emphasis on long-term operational savings
- Advocacy for supportive EU-level regulation to ensure viability of local energy systems[^2]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

**Technical Challenges:**  
- Retrofitting green roofs on old buildings
- Integrating sponge parks in dense, historic urban areas
- Ensuring interoperability of digital monitoring platforms[^2]

**Financial Challenges:**  
- Upfront costs for retrofits and infrastructure
- Limited incentives for energy grid feed-in[^2]

**Social and Political Challenges:**  
- Coordinating diverse stakeholders
- Sustaining long-term resident engagement
- Navigating conflicting interests in tree planting and land use[^2]

**Administrative Challenges:**  
- Bureaucratic delays in permitting and contracting
- Capacity constraints in working across multiple districts

**Environmental Challenges:**  
- Unforeseen impacts of droughts on green infrastructure
- Site contamination in some neighborhoods

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

**Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
- Use of landscape architects and bioclimatic experts for tailored design[^2]
- Iterative mapping and data collection to adjust interventions

**Design Modifications:**  
- Experimentation with green roofs on historic and older buildings
- Adjustment of sponge park layouts based on local feedback

**Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
- Early and continuous stakeholder involvement is key
- Real-time data allows rapid response to emerging challenges
- Long-term engagement requires visible benefits for residents[^2]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

**Physical Outputs:**  
- Multiple sponge parks constructed
- Green roofs installed on schools, transport hubs, and social housing
- Social housing blocks retrofitted for energy efficiency
- Solar PV systems operational in energy communities[^2]

**Service Delivery:**  
- Improved insulation and energy management in social housing
- Community-run renewable energy generation

**Quantitative Metrics:**  
- 45% improvement in energy efficiency in retrofitted housing[^2]
- Aim to produce 6MW solar energy in social housing buildings[^2]
- By 2020, Porto reduced emissions by 52.2% compared to 2004[^2]

- Strong integration of ecological, technological, and social innovation
- Demonstrated improvements in energy efficiency, climate resilience, and equity
- High level of institutional and scientific collaboration

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Upfront costs and regulatory barriers for local energy systems
- Ongoing challenge of sustaining community engagement

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Initiative directly targets and benefits vulnerable populations
- Emphasis on participatory decision-making and empowerment
- No major reported negative equity impacts

- Commitment to climate neutrality by 2030; further expansion of nature-based solutions and energy communities
- Ongoing adaptation to emerging climate risks and regulatory changes
- Potential for Porto to influence EU policy on decentralized climate adaptation and energy transition[^2][^3][^1]
