# Executive summary## GreenCharge – Incentivising Sustainable Urban Mobility

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** GreenCharge – Incentivising Sustainable Urban Mobility  
- **Alternative Names:** GreenCharge Project, H2020 GreenCharge  
- **Location:** Vienna, Austria (as part of broader European pilot network)  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 48.2082° N, 16.3738° E (Vienna city center)  
- **Timeline:** September 2018 – February 2022 (36-month project duration)  
- **Project Scale:** Multi-city pilot network (Vienna, Barcelona, Bremen, Oslo); focused on urban neighborhoods with EV charging infrastructure, targeting thousands of residents and stakeholders  
- **Leading Organization(s):** Consortium led by SINTEF (Norway), with participation from city authorities, technology SMEs, and research institutions  
- **Funding Sources:** European Union Horizon 2020 programme (€5 million total grant)

#### **Executive Summary**
GreenCharge is a Horizon 2020-funded initiative designed to accelerate the transition to zero-emission urban mobility by piloting smart, sustainable charging solutions for electric vehicles (EVs) in European cities. The project’s core innovation lies in integrating advanced energy management technologies, dynamic pricing models, and sharing economy-inspired business models to optimize the use of renewable energy and minimize grid strain. In Vienna, the pilot focused on deploying smart charging stations, user-friendly mobile applications, and real-time feedback systems to encourage off-peak charging and maximize the use of locally produced renewable energy. The initiative sought to empower citizens, reduce energy costs, and support the development of energy communities. By combining technical innovation with participatory stakeholder engagement, GreenCharge delivered actionable recommendations for urban planners, facility managers, and policymakers, contributing to broader efforts to achieve climate-neutral cities by 2030. The project’s outcomes have informed the design of future sustainable urban mobility systems and provided a replicable model for other cities seeking to integrate EVs into their energy and transport networks.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Vienna is Austria’s capital and largest city, with a population of approximately 1.9 million. It is a major economic, cultural, and political hub in Central Europe, known for its high quality of life, extensive public transport network, and commitment to sustainability. The city has set ambitious climate targets, including carbon neutrality by 2040.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The GreenCharge pilot in Vienna targeted urban neighborhoods with mixed residential and commercial land use, characterized by medium to high population density. These areas have a diverse demographic profile, with a mix of income levels, age groups, and cultural backgrounds. Existing infrastructure includes a well-developed public transport system, but there is ongoing pressure to reduce car dependency and improve air quality.
- **Environmental Conditions:** Vienna faces challenges related to urban heat islands, air pollution, and the need to increase renewable energy adoption. The city has significant potential for solar energy generation, particularly on rooftops and public buildings.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** The primary challenges addressed by GreenCharge included high peak electricity demand from EV charging, limited grid capacity, and the need to increase the share of renewable energy in urban mobility. There was also a lack of user-friendly charging solutions and incentives for off-peak charging.
- **Triggering Events:** The project was catalyzed by the European Union’s push for climate-neutral cities and the rapid growth of EV adoption in urban areas. Local policy changes, such as incentives for EV ownership and renewable energy installations, also played a role.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Key actors included city authorities, energy providers, private EV owners, fleet operators, technology companies, and residents. Each group had different interests, ranging from grid stability and cost savings to user convenience and environmental benefits.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** The initiative was coordinated by a consortium of public and private partners, with oversight from the European Commission. Local implementation involved collaboration between city departments, utility companies, and community organizations.
- **Relevant Policies:** GreenCharge aligned with Vienna’s Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan (SUMP), climate action strategy, and energy efficiency regulations. The project also supported EU-wide policies on renewable energy and smart cities.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** The main enablers were supportive local policies and access to EU funding. Barriers included regulatory complexity, data privacy concerns, and the need for standardization across different energy and transport systems.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:** To demonstrate and validate viable business models and technologies for sustainable EV charging in urban neighborhoods, with a focus on maximizing renewable energy use and minimizing grid impact.
- **Vision Statement:** A future where urban mobility is fully electrified, powered by locally produced renewable energy, and managed through smart, user-centric systems that benefit all stakeholders.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** GreenCharge directly supports Vienna’s climate neutrality goals, the EU’s Green Deal, and the CIVITAS initiative for sustainable urban mobility.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:** By deploying smart charging infrastructure and dynamic pricing models, GreenCharge enables residents to charge their EVs using renewable energy during off-peak hours, reducing grid congestion and lowering energy costs. This, in turn, increases EV adoption and supports the transition to a zero-emission transport system.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents, EV owners, fleet operators, energy providers, and city authorities.
- **Innovation Elements:** The project’s distinctive features include the integration of smart charging stations, user apps, and energy management software, as well as the development of sharing economy-inspired business models.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political Drivers:** Commitment to climate action and sustainable urban development.
- **Economic Drivers:** Investment in green technologies and the potential for cost savings.
- **Social Drivers:** Community advocacy for cleaner air and better quality of life.
- **Environmental Drivers:** Climate adaptation, sustainability goals, and resource management.
- **Technological Drivers:** Availability of smart city technologies and digital platforms.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The initiative was guided by principles of smart city development, sustainable urban mobility, and energy efficiency.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** The project involved the installation of smart charging stations in residential and commercial areas, as well as the development of a user-friendly mobile app for charge planning, booking, and billing.
- **Scope and Phasing:** The project was structured in phases, including pilot component preparation, full-scale pilot implementation, and evaluation. Each phase involved iterative testing and refinement of technologies and business models.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** Smart charging stations, user apps, dynamic pricing models, renewable energy integration, and energy management software.
- **Innovation Type:** Technological, social, and financial innovations.
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies were integrated with existing urban systems, including the electricity grid, public transport network, and building energy management systems.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Implementers were selected through a competitive process, with a focus on expertise in smart city technologies and sustainable urban mobility.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** The project involved extensive engagement with residents, businesses, and city authorities through workshops, surveys, and pilot testing.
- **Participatory Elements:** Community input was sought at every stage, from design to evaluation, to ensure that the solutions met local needs and preferences.
- **Project Management:** The project was managed by a consortium of partners, with regular coordination meetings and progress reviews.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Total investment of €5 million, with costs covering technology development, infrastructure installation, stakeholder engagement, and evaluation.
- **Funding Mix:** Primarily EU funding, with additional contributions from local authorities and private partners.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** User fees, dynamic pricing, and potential revenue from energy trading.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Plans for long-term financial viability included commercial exploitation of the developed technologies and business models.
