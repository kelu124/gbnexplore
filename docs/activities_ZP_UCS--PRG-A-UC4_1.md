# Executive summary

## Modřanský cukrovar Blue-Green District

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Modřanský cukrovar Blue-Green District
- **Alternative Names:** Modřanský cukrovar, Prague’s First Blue-Green District
- **Location:** Modřany, Prague 12, Czech Republic; Southern Prague, near the Vltava River
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.0010° N, 14.4040° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - **Start Date:** Planning initiated in late 2010s; construction began circa 2020  
  - **Milestones:** Phase 1 completed (2025), Phase 2 under construction (completion planned for 2026), Phase 3 in sales  
  - **Status:** Multi-phase development ongoing
- **Project Scale:**  
  - **Area:** ~58,000 m² total site  
  - **Housing Units:** Over 800 flats planned across six phases  
  - **Investment Value:** Not publicly specified, but major multi-stage urban redevelopment
- **Leading Organization(s):** Skanska Reality a.s. (developer), Chybik + Kristof (architects), supported by municipal authorities
- **Funding Sources:** Primarily private (Skanska), with potential public infrastructure support; PPP elements in land and community facilities

#### **Executive Summary**

Modřanský cukrovar represents a flagship sustainable urban regeneration project in Prague, transforming a historic brownfield sugar refinery site into the city’s first blue-green district. Spearheaded by Skanska, the initiative aims to set new benchmarks for residential sustainability in Central Europe, targeting top-tier BREEAM certification for its holistic approach to environmental, social, and economic performance[^1][^2][^3]. The district integrates cutting-edge blue-green infrastructure, including rainwater harvesting, greywater recycling, green roofs, solar thermal and photovoltaic panels, biodiversity-supporting landscapes, and recycled construction materials (Rebetong)[^1][^4][^5].  

Community engagement and human-centered design are core, with new public spaces, a central square, community gardens, rooftop amenities, and participatory planning processes fostering social cohesion and active neighborhood life[^1][^6]. Real-time monitoring and smart metering underpin resource efficiency, and the district’s phased implementation allows scaling and adaptation of innovative technologies[^4][^5].  

Outcomes include a documented 47–59% reduction in drinking water use, notable energy savings, robust ecosystem services, improved air quality, and enhanced biodiversity[^1][^5]. Modřanský cukrovar stands as a replicable model for sustainable district development, emphasizing long-term viability, resident well-being, and urban resilience in the face of climate change[^1][^2][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - **Prague:** Capital of the Czech Republic; population ~1.3 million. Major economic, cultural, and political hub in Central Europe. Situated on the Vltava River, with a diverse urban fabric and robust infrastructure. Rapidly modernizing, with a history of industrial development and recent emphasis on sustainability.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Modřany:** Southern district, long associated with industry (notably the sugar factory).  
  - **Historical development:** Site formerly occupied by the Modřanský sugar refinery—a key industrial site since the 19th century.  
  - **Land use:** Transition from industrial brownfield to mixed-use residential, commercial, and community functions.  
  - **Demographics:** Traditionally working-class, now attracting young families, professionals, and retirees.  
  - **Infrastructure:** Existing transport links (tram, bus, proximity to cycling routes), but previously underutilized and environmentally degraded.  
  - **Environmental conditions:** Riverfront location, but with legacy contamination (brownfield), poor biodiversity, and limited recreational space pre-development[^4][^7].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Brownfield contamination and underutilized industrial land  
  - Deficient housing quality, energy inefficiency  
  - Poor water management and lack of green space  
  - Social fragmentation, limited community amenities
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Closure of the sugar refinery opened opportunity for large-scale redevelopment  
  - Municipal and developer interest in piloting sustainable urban models  
  - Shifts in Czech policy favoring brownfield regeneration and climate adaptation
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Skanska (developer), city authorities, local residents, environmental NGOs, architects, utility providers

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - City of Prague and Prague 12 municipal district as planning authorities; national sustainability standards
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Czech urban regeneration and brownfield policies  
  - BREEAM, WELL, and SBToolCZ sustainable building certifications[^2][^8]
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive regulatory regime for brownfield redevelopment  
  - Need for environmental remediation and compliance with water/energy standards  
  - Zoning modifications to permit mixed-use, higher densities

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Deliver Prague’s first blue-green residential district  
  - Achieve highest BREEAM rating for residential development in Czechia[^9][^10][^3]  
  - Demonstrate scalable, replicable sustainable urbanism
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - “Building communities, neighborhoods, and cohesion of people with nature”[^1][^11]
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Supports Prague’s climate adaptation, housing quality, and brownfield regeneration strategies  
  - Aligns with EU urban sustainability objectives

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - By integrating blue-green infrastructure, smart technologies, participatory design, and resource-efficient construction, the district will transform environmental and social outcomes while reducing costs and enhancing resilience.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents (current and future), city government, environment, broader Prague community
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Large-scale deployment of greywater recycling, rainwater harvesting, green roofs, recycled concrete (Rebetong), smart metering, and real-time monitoring[^1][^4][^5]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - Municipal push for brownfield regeneration, urban sustainability, and EU alignment
- **Economic:**  
  - New housing market demand, cost savings via resource efficiency, investment attraction
- **Social:**  
  - Desire for improved community spaces, public health, and inclusion  
  - Resident-driven participatory planning
- **Environmental:**  
  - Climate change adaptation, biodiversity restoration, water/energy conservation
- **Technological:**  
  - Availability of advanced water, energy, and construction technologies; smart city trends

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Blue-green urbanism; regeneration of brownfields; participatory, community-focused district planning
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Mixed-use neighborhood with residential blocks, central square, multifunctional public space, linear park, revitalized Vltava embankment[^1]
  - Car-light design: underground parking, pedestrian-focused streets, safe access[^12]
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Six phases; first phase completed, second under construction, third in sales; adaptive scaling of innovations[^2][^5]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Greywater recycling for toilet flushing and laundry[^1][^5]  
  - Rainwater harvesting with 19 storage tanks (190,000 liters capacity) for irrigation[^1][^4]  
  - Green roofs (14,000 m²), combined with solar thermal and photovoltaic panels[^1][^4][^5]  
  - Smart metering and real-time performance monitoring (energy, water use)[^4]  
  - Biodiversity features: meadows, orchards, native species landscaping[^1][^4]  
  - Rebetong recycled concrete (~20% of structures)[^1][^5]  
  - Energy-saving elevators with regeneration, heat recovery from greywater[^4]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (water, energy, materials), social (community design), financial (resource efficiency), governance (participatory planning)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Systems integrated with district-wide resource management; building-level and area-level optimization; digital displays for resident feedback[^4]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Skanska as lead developer, competitive selection of architects (Chybik + Kristof), use of specialist contractors for innovative materials
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Community workshops, participatory design, feedback loops during planning and construction[^6]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Public consultation on amenities, green spaces, social facilities
- **Project Management:**  
  - Phased delivery, with lessons from pilot buildings informing district-wide rollout; continuous monitoring and adjustment

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not fully disclosed; major private investment by Skanska; cost breakdown likely includes land acquisition, remediation, infrastructure, building, technology
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Predominantly private, with public facilitation (infrastructure, planning)
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Apartment sales (limit one per buyer to prevent speculation)[^11], commercial leases
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term savings in water/energy costs; enhanced asset value via sustainability credentials

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Scaling water/energy technologies from pilot to district level; integrating new systems with legacy infrastructure
- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront investment in advanced technologies; ensuring cost-effectiveness at scale
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Sustaining community engagement; managing expectations and potential disruptions during construction
- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination across multi-phase, multi-stakeholder project; regulatory compliance
- **Environmental:**  
  - Brownfield contamination requiring remediation; managing impacts on river ecosystem[^7]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Piloting technologies in small-scale projects before district-wide adoption[^1][^4]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments to rainwater and greywater systems for local regulations; phased roll-out of solar and green roofs
- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of participatory planning, transparent communication, and monitoring to ensure maintenance and resident buy-in[^6]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - >800 new flats, central square, multifunctional hall, linear park, revitalized embankment[^1][^4][^13]
  - 14,000 m² green roofs  
  - 19 rainwater tanks; solar collectors and PV arrays; Rebetong in up to 20% of concrete structures[^1][^5]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Reduced water/energy bills; new community spaces; improved air quality and green amenities
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Drinking water use reduced by 47–59%[^1][^5]  
  - Up to 15 tons CO₂ absorbed per year by vegetation[^1]  
  - PENB energy rating: B in phase 1, A in phase 2 (44% more efficient than legislative standard)[^5]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Enhanced public spaces, rooftop gardens, community cohesion, participatory culture[^1][^6]
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Efforts to prevent speculation (one flat per buyer)[^11]; some risk of gentrification as area becomes more desirable
- **Displacement/Gentrification:**  
  - No major displacement reported; brownfield nature minimized direct impact on existing residents
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened networks via community amenities, events, public spaces
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Preservation of local identity via references to sugar refinery history in architecture[^1]

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Construction, community facility management, local retail
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Attraction of investment; boost to neighborhood property values
- **Property Values:**  
  - Likely significant appreciation due to sustainability and amenities
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Reduced utility bills via water/energy efficiency[^1][^5]
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Mixed outcomes; improved access to quality housing, but long-term affordability concerns

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate/Emissions:**  
  - Major reductions in greenhouse gas emissions via energy efficiency and renewable energy[^5]
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Water use reductions, stormwater management, energy conservation
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality, biodiversity, noise reduction, green space provision[^1][^4]
- **Waste/Pollution:**  
  - Use of recycled materials (Rebetong), composting, biowaste management[^5]
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - BREEAM certification “Excellent” (score: 75%)[^9][^10][^14][^15]; highest ever for Czech residential development

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened expertise in sustainable urban development; new collaborative models between developer, city, and community[^6]
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for future brownfield regeneration and sustainable neighborhood design in Prague and beyond
- **Transparency/Accountability:**  
  - Real-time monitoring, public displays, participatory process[^4]

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - High BREEAM score, documented resource savings, vibrant community life, strong market response
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Potential for rising property values and affordability pressures; need for ongoing maintenance and community engagement
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Early phases indicate strong sustainability and replicability; future depends on continued innovation and management

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Comprehensive sustainability:** Highest BREEAM rating, cutting-edge blue-green infrastructure
- **Community-centric design:** Strong participatory planning, vibrant public spaces
- **Water and energy efficiency:** Documented reductions in resource consumption, innovative technologies
- **Brownfield regeneration:** Transformative impact on previously degraded land
- **Replicability:** Model for future urban districts in Prague and beyond

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Affordability risks:** Rising property values may limit access for lower-income residents
- **Maintenance challenge:** Long-term performance depends on ongoing management and resident engagement
- **Technology scaling:** Initial technical challenges in rolling out district-wide systems
- **Limited data:** Not all economic and social impacts yet fully documented (project ongoing)

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Positive:** Efforts to prevent speculation, participatory design, improved access to green amenities
- **Negative:** Potential gentrification; need for more explicit inclusion strategies for vulnerable groups

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory:** Continued development through six phases, ongoing innovation in sustainability and community engagement
- **Ongoing challenges:** Ensuring affordability, maintaining high environmental performance, adapting to evolving technologies
- **Next phases:** Expansion of community facilities, further optimization of resource management, monitoring of long-term impacts
