# Executive summary## Cleanwatts Living Lab: AI-powered Renewable Energy Communities

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Cleanwatts Living Lab: AI-powered Renewable Energy Communities
- **Alternative Names:** CER Cleanwatts Living Lab, Cleanwatts Renewable Energy Communities Pilot
- **Location:** Coimbra, Portugal (including five areas in Coimbra, Condeixa-a-Nova, and Anadia)[^1][^2]
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 40.2110° N, 8.4292° W (Coimbra central)
- **Timeline:** Initiated in 2022; recognized as an official regulatory pilot in July 2023; ongoing[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:** 40 households in the pilot phase, with scalable ambitions; broader Cleanwatts projects cover hundreds of community members[^2][^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Cleanwatts SA (energy technology company); collaboration with local authorities and ERSE (Portugal’s Energy Services Regulatory Authority)
- **Funding Sources:** Public and private mix; EU Horizon 2020 R&D funding, Cleanwatts private investment, local partnerships[^2]

#### **Executive Summary**
The Cleanwatts Living Lab is a pioneering initiative in Coimbra, Portugal, designed to demonstrate and advance the integration of renewable energy, digital technologies, and community-driven energy management. The project operates as a real-world testbed where 40 homes are equipped with solar photovoltaic (PV) panels, batteries, electric vehicles (EVs), and smart appliances, all orchestrated by a digital twin and AI-based control system. 

This Living Lab facilitates dynamic energy sharing and real-time optimization, allowing residents to maximize renewable self-consumption (+30%), reduce reliance on the grid, and decrease their carbon footprint. Cleanwatts’ interoperable platform (Cleanwatts™ OS) enables seamless integration of diverse devices and energy flows, supporting local energy markets and flexibility services for the grid. Recognized as a regulatory pilot, the initiative not only validates new business models and technical solutions but also empowers residents as active participants in the energy transition. 

By blending advanced technology, regulatory sandboxes, and strong stakeholder engagement, the Cleanwatts Living Lab seeks to provide a blueprint for scalable, community-led decarbonization and increased energy resilience—demonstrating substantial social, economic, and environmental benefits for both individuals and the broader urban ecosystem[^1][^2].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**
- **Coimbra** is a mid-sized city in central Portugal (~140,000 population, 2021), historically known for its university and as a regional services hub. The economy blends education, health, technology, and small industry.
- The city lies in a temperate, sunny region, making it well-suited for solar energy investments[^2].

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
- The Living Lab is distributed over five areas, including urban and peri-urban neighborhoods in Coimbra, Condeixa-a-Nova, and Anadia.
- These districts feature mixed land use (residential, small commerce, some institutional buildings).
- Demographically, the pilot targets a range of middle- and lower-income households, with some areas affected by energy poverty[^2].

**Existing Infrastructure:**
- Good access to electricity and digital infrastructure.
- Varying building ages; some retrofitting required for energy assets.
- EV charging infrastructure previously limited; enhanced by the project.

**Environmental Conditions:**
- High solar exposure; moderate air quality challenges; some vulnerability to energy price volatility and fuel poverty.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Challenges Identified:**
  - High energy costs and vulnerability to price spikes for residents.
  - Underutilization of local renewable resources.
  - Limited local energy autonomy and flexibility.
  - Social risks of energy poverty and lack of community engagement in energy systems.
  - Fragmented digitalization and lack of integrated energy management[^2][^1].

- **Triggering Events:**
  - National policies promoting collective self-consumption and renewable energy communities.
  - EU funding opportunities for digital and green transitions.
  - Local advocacy for sustainable urban development.

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Cleanwatts SA (project leader and technology provider).
  - Local governments and residents (beneficiaries).
  - ERSE (regulator and policy enabler).
  - Energy market actors, technology partners.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**
  - National and EU energy directives supporting renewable energy communities.
  - Piloted under ERSE’s regulatory sandbox and self-consumption regulation[^1][^2].

- **Relevant Policies:**
  - Portuguese legislation enabling collective self-consumption (Decree-Law 162/2019).
  - Recent amendments supporting dynamic energy sharing and digital market models[^1].

- **Legal/Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
  - Regulatory sandboxes facilitate experimentation.
  - Still-evolving national rules for dynamic/hierarchical energy sharing.
  - Need for local permitting and grid operator cooperation[^1].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Demonstrate and scale collective self-consumption using distributed renewables.
  - Optimize local energy flows via AI and digital twins.
  - Empower residents as energy market participants.

- **Vision Statement:**
  - “Connecting people and technology to build a cleaner, fairer and more sustainable future”[^2].

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**
  - Supports Portugal’s national energy and climate plans.
  - Contributes to EU Green Deal targets for decarbonization and social inclusion.
  - Synergistic with local urban sustainability strategies.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**
  - By integrating advanced digital management with local renewables and storage, communities can maximize self-consumption, reduce costs, and become active energy market participants, creating economic, social, and environmental value.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**
  - Local residents (direct energy savings and empowerment).
  - Broader community (reduced emissions, increased resilience).
  - Vulnerable households (reduced energy poverty).

- **Innovation Elements:**
  - AI-driven digital twin for real-time energy optimization.
  - Interoperable, open innovation approach.
  - Real-world testing of business models and regulatory frameworks[^2].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** Support for national/EU decarbonization and digitalization.
- **Economic:** Reduce household energy costs, stimulate investment, foster local green jobs.
- **Social:** Address energy poverty, engage citizens in transition.
- **Environmental:** Maximize renewables, cut CO2, support grid stability.
- **Technological:** Leverage new digital platforms, smart devices, and AI[^1][^2].

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**
  - Open innovation ecosystem; regulatory sandbox; distributed, user-centric energy systems.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**
  - Distributed deployment of rooftop PV, batteries, EV charging, and smart appliances across 40 homes.
  - Community-scale energy sharing and storage.
- **Scope and Phasing:**
  - Initial pilot in five areas, with phased scaling and integration into broader city/region[^1][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Digital twin and AI-powered energy management (Cleanwatts™ OS).
  - Rooftop solar PV, battery storage, smart meters/appliances.
  - EV charging stations.
  - Local energy markets and flexibility service provision[^2].

- **Innovation Type:**
  - Technological (AI, IoT, digital twins).
  - Social (community models, participatory engagement).
  - Regulatory/process (sandboxing, dynamic sharing)[^1][^2].

- **Integration Approach:**
  - Interoperable platform integrates legacy and new devices, interacts with grid, and supports flexibility services[^2].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**
  - Cleanwatts as technology provider and system integrator.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**
  - Resident recruitment, collaboration with local governments, education campaigns.
- **Participatory Elements:**
  - Community co-design and feedback on system operation.
- **Project Management:**
  - Centralized coordination by Cleanwatts, with local support and regulatory oversight[^2].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**
  - Not publicly disclosed for this specific pilot; Cleanwatts projects typically leverage private and EU funds.
- **Funding Mix:**
  - EU Horizon 2020 (for R&D), private equity, local partnerships, possibly some municipal support[^2].
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**
  - User subscriptions, local energy sales, grid services.
- **Financial Sustainability:**
  - Designed for scalability and replication, with cost savings for users as a core value proposition[^2].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** 40 homes equipped with PV, storage, smart devices, and EV charging.
- **Service Delivery:** Reliable, lower-cost, cleaner energy; local energy sharing.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 30% increase in renewable self-consumption; 44% carbon neutrality in similar projects; up to 60% energy cost reductions in parallel Cleanwatts initiatives[^2][^3][^4].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Improved energy access, resilience, and affordability.
- **Equity Outcomes:** Special focus on vulnerable households; reduced energy poverty.
- **Displacement/Gentrification:** No evidence of negative impacts.
- **Social Capital:** Increased community engagement and energy literacy.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Fosters sustainability as part of local identity[^2][^3].

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Green jobs in installation, management, and digital services.
- **Economic Activity:** Stimulates local investment, supports local businesses.
- **Property Values:** Likely positive, though not quantified.
- **Cost Savings:** Residents save up to 30–60% on energy costs in similar Cleanwatts projects[^4][^3].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Prioritizes accessibility for lower-income households.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate/Emissions:** Reduced CO2 emissions (e.g., 19 tons/year in comparable REC); increased renewable penetration.
- **Resource Management:** Improved energy efficiency and storage use.
- **Environmental Quality:** Local air quality benefits; reduced fossil fuel use.
- **Waste/Pollution:** End-of-life management for PV and batteries required.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Targets and tracks self-consumption, carbon neutrality, and grid flexibility[^2][^3][^4].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** Strengthens local ability to manage and innovate in energy.
- **Policy Influence:** Informs national and local policy; supports regulatory evolution.
- **Transparency/Accountability:** Digital platforms provide granular data and foster trust[^1][^2].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Achieved substantial increases in self-consumption, cost savings, and participation.
- **Unintended Consequences:** No major negative effects reported; some learning curve for residents.
- **Long-term Viability:** Strong potential for scaling and replication; ongoing monitoring[^2][^3].

#### **Future Outlook**

- Potential for integration with broader city/district energy planning.
- Next phases may expand participation, integrate additional technologies (e.g., vehicle-to-grid, demand response), and inform national/EU policy[^2].
- Continued evaluation will be needed to ensure long-term impact, financial sustainability, and adaptability to evolving regulations and market conditions.
