# Executive summary## SSE Airtricity Living Lab (EU DREAM Project)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** SSE Airtricity Living Lab (EU DREAM Project)
- **Alternative Names:** EU-DREAM Living Lab Ireland, LL4: Empowering consumers for energy management
- **Location:** Dublin, Ireland (specific neighborhood not named; urban residential context)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.3498° N, 6.2603° W (Dublin city)
- **Timeline:** 
  - **Start Date:** July 2024 (EU-DREAM project launch)
  - **Key Milestones:** Living lab setup (2024), technology deployment (2024–2025), ongoing data collection and optimization (2025–2027)
  - **Completion/Ongoing Status:** Scheduled for completion end of 2027; currently ongoing[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - 5 smart family homes
  - Total area: 450 m²
  - Population affected: 5 households (pilot phase), with designed scalability[^3]
- **Leading Organization(s):** SSE Airtricity (lead), with partners EPRI Europe and DCSix Technologies[^1][^2]
- **Funding Sources:** European Union Horizon Europe programme (Grant Agreement No. 101160614)[^1][^2][^4]

#### **Executive Summary**

The SSE Airtricity Living Lab is a pioneering Irish demonstration site within the EU-DREAM (Effective Uptake of Digital Services to Repower European Consumers and Communities as Active Participants in Energy Transition and Markets) project, funded by Horizon Europe. Located in Dublin, this Living Lab consists of five smart family homes equipped with solar photovoltaic (PV) panels, electric vehicle (EV) charging, heat pumps, home energy storage, and advanced real-time energy monitoring systems. At its core, the initiative aims to empower households to play an active role in the energy transition by leveraging digital tools, AI-driven optimization, and digital twin simulations. 

Through real-time data analysis and AI algorithms, the project enables residents to optimize energy consumption, increase self-consumption of onsite renewable energy, and provide flexibility services to the grid. Notably, the Living Lab also experiments with user engagement strategies and co-creation approaches to ensure solutions are accessible, user-friendly, and trusted by consumers. The project directly aligns with the EU Action Plan on Digitalising the Energy System and Ireland’s net-zero ambitions, offering a replicable model for advanced green building neighborhoods (GBN) and smart energy communities across Europe. Its anticipated impacts include reduced energy bills, increased renewable energy use, improved grid stability, and heightened consumer awareness and engagement, thus addressing both climate and social dimensions of the energy transition[^3][^1][^2][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Dublin is Ireland’s capital and largest city, with a population of ~1.4 million in the metropolitan area. As a major economic and innovation center, it hosts a robust technology sector, significant public transport infrastructure, and is a policy leader in sustainability[^5]. Dublin’s maritime temperate climate (Oceanic/Marine) supports year-round renewable energy opportunities[^3].
  
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical development:** Dublin neighborhoods blend historic housing stock with modern developments.  
  - **Land use:** Primarily residential, with proximity to services, public transport, and urban amenities.  
  - **Demographics:** Pilot participants are middle-high socioeconomic households, diverse in gender representation[^3].  
  - **Infrastructure:** Good access to electricity, broadband, and mobility infrastructure (including EV charging).  
  - **Environmental conditions:** Urban air quality issues, but solid potential for rooftop solar and electrification; city policy is climate-forward[^5].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Low consumer participation in energy transition (nearly 80% of Europeans not actively involved)[^6][^2]
  - Lack of user-friendly digital tools for energy management
  - Underutilization of home renewable generation and flexibility assets
  - Growing electricity demand from electrification (heating, EVs) and grid stability concerns

- **Triggering Events:**
  - EU policy push for digitalization and decarbonization
  - Horizon Europe calls for citizen-centric energy innovation
  - Ireland’s net-zero 2050 and climate action plans[^1][^2]

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Residents interested in sustainability and savings
  - SSE Airtricity as a utility focused on clean energy services
  - Innovation partners (EPRI, DCSix) with expertise in AI and digital platforms
  - Policy actors supporting climate and digital agendas

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** National and city authorities support innovation districts and smart city pilots; regulatory oversight for energy data privacy, grid operations, and renewables[^5].
  
- **Relevant Policies:**
  - EU Clean Energy Package
  - EU Action Plan on Digitalising the Energy System
  - Ireland’s Climate Action Plan and Smart Dublin strategy[^1][^4][^5]

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
  - GDPR compliance for energy and user data
  - Supportive policy for prosumer participation
  - Grid codes and market rules for demand response and flexibility

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Empower households to actively manage and optimize energy consumption
  - Increase renewable self-consumption and enable grid support
  - Enhance user engagement, awareness, and trust in digital energy tools[^3][^1][^2]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  Enable ordinary citizens to become active, informed, and empowered participants in a digitally enabled, sustainable energy system.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  The Living Lab supports both the EU digitalization strategy for the energy sector and Ireland’s national sustainability and innovation targets[^1][^2].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  Digital, AI-powered tools and real-time data empower users to shift energy use, maximize renewables, and participate in demand response, leading to lower bills, emissions, and greater grid flexibility.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  Pilot: 5 Dublin households (middle-high socioeconomic status); scalable to broader urban populations.

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - AI-powered digital twins for scenario planning
  - Real-time optimization of EV charging and home energy storage
  - User-centric design with proactive system alerts and recommendations[^3][^1][^2]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** EU and Irish climate and digitalization policy priorities
- **Economic:** Rising energy costs, market for smart home/energy services
- **Social:** Demand for sustainable, cost-saving solutions; community engagement
- **Environmental:** Decarbonization, air quality, and grid resilience goals
- **Technological:** Advances in AI, IoT, digital twins, and smart home systems

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Follows principles of *living lab* innovation (real-world, user-centered, iterative), digitalization, and integrated energy management[^3][^2].

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  Retrofit of five existing single-family homes with PV, heat pumps, home batteries, EV chargers, and digital monitoring[^3].

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Homes selection and baseline data collection (2024)
  - Phase 2: Technology deployment and integration (2024–2025)
  - Phase 3: Operation, optimization, and user engagement (2025–2027)
  - Phase 4: Evaluation and knowledge transfer (2027)[^1][^2]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Solar PV panels (onsite generation)
  - Electric vehicle charging infrastructure
  - Heat pumps (low-carbon heating/cooling)
  - Home energy storage (batteries)
  - Advanced energy monitoring (IoT sensors)
  - AI-powered digital twins for data-driven optimization
  - Proactive maintenance and system alerts[^3][^1][^2]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  Primarily technological/digital, with social and institutional innovations in user engagement and participatory design.

- **Integration Approach:**  
  All assets are digitally linked, with real-time data flowing to a central AI platform for optimization; digital twin technology simulates scenarios and informs both user and system-level actions.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Implemented by SSE Airtricity in partnership with EPRI Europe and DCSix Technologies, leveraging both internal R&D and external expertise[^1][^2].

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  Residents are involved in co-creation workshops, feedback cycles, and scenario testing; expert partners provide technical and analytical support[^3][^2].

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  User input shapes interface design, service features, and optimization priorities; digital literacy and trust-building are key.

- **Project Management:**  
  Coordinated by SSE Airtricity under the EU-DREAM consortium governance, reporting to Horizon Europe funders.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  Not specified for this lab alone; part of a multi-million euro EU-DREAM project budget covering six labs.

- **Funding Mix:**  
  Predominantly EU Horizon Europe grant funding, with potential in-kind contributions from partners[^1][^2][^4].

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  None during pilot; future business models may include subscription services, grid flexibility payments, or data-driven value streams.

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  Pilot phase relies on grant; scalability depends on demonstrating cost savings and value for consumers and system operators.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Holistic, user-centered design bridging technology and citizen engagement
- Demonstrated gains in energy efficiency, grid flexibility, and awareness
- Alignment with broader policy and market trends for decarbonization and digitalization[^3][^1][^2]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Small pilot scale limits immediate impact and generalizability
- Focus on middle-high income households may miss equity opportunities
- Financial sustainability post-grant phase remains unproven

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Direct benefits currently limited to participating households
- Opportunity to expand to lower-income or vulnerable groups in future phases
- Good practices in privacy and user empowerment, but digital divide is a risk

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory:** Potential for scale-up across Dublin and replication in other EU cities, subject to proof of concept and business model development
- **Ongoing challenges:** Ensuring broader accessibility, maintaining user engagement, integrating with evolving energy markets
- **Next phases:** Evaluation, policy engagement, and potential commercial rollout of digital energy services
