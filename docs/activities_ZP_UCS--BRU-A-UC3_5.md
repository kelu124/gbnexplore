# Executive summary## Lyon Confluence Smart Neighborhood (Smarter Together)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Lyon Confluence Smart Neighborhood (Smarter Together)
- **Alternative Names:** Lyon-Confluence Smart Community; Lyon-Confluence (Horizon 2020 – Smarter Together Project)
- **Location:** Lyon, Confluence District, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.7411° N, 4.8198° E
- **Timeline:** 
  - Project start: 2016 (Smarter Together program), with earlier smart city pilots from 2011
  - Major implementation: 2016–2021
  - Ongoing: Further development as part of "Lyon 2030" and PCED (Positive & Clean Energy District) plans[^1][^2]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - District area: 150 hectares
  - Floor area: 600,000 m² existing, 1,000,000 m² new buildings
  - Population: Targeting thousands of residents and users
  - Investment: €24.7 million EU funding (Smarter Together consortium), €50 million (Lyon Smart Community, prior phase), €379 million for current PCED construction phase[^3][^2][^4]
- **Leading Organization(s):** SPL Lyon Confluence (public development agency); Métropole de Lyon; multiple public-private partners including Toshiba, Transdev, NEDO, and others[^3][^2]
- **Funding Sources:** European Commission (Horizon 2020), Japanese NEDO, local government, private sector, PPPs[^3][^5][^2]

#### **Executive Summary**

Lyon Confluence Smart Neighborhood, centered in the southern peninsula of Lyon, is a flagship urban regeneration initiative and a "lighthouse" site for the EU’s Horizon 2020 Smarter Together program. The project’s vision is to demonstrate how a dense urban district can dramatically reduce carbon emissions, promote energy efficiency, and foster innovative, inclusive urban living through integrated smart technologies and participatory governance.

Key interventions include large-scale building retrofits, construction of positive-energy buildings, deployment of digital twins and IoT for real-time monitoring and control, renewable energy integration (solar PV and wood-fired cogeneration), and the creation of an urban data platform. Residents are directly engaged through participatory digital platforms and living labs, empowering them to understand and influence energy and climate performance. Sustainable mobility is advanced through electric vehicle sharing, autonomous shuttles, and smart charging infrastructure.

The initiative is both a testbed for advanced urban solutions and a replicable model for other European cities. Its significance lies in balancing technological innovation with deep stakeholder engagement, producing measurable reductions in energy use and greenhouse gas emissions while enhancing social, economic, and environmental quality of life[^1][^3][^5][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Lyon is France’s third-largest city, with around 515,000 residents (2.3 million in the metro area). It is an economic, cultural, and transport hub, traditionally strong in manufacturing, healthcare, and services, and situated at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers[^3][^2].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - The Confluence district is a former industrial and logistics area at the southern tip of Lyon’s Presqu’île.
  - Since the late 1990s, it has been the focus of one of Europe’s largest urban regeneration projects, transforming brownfields into mixed-use neighborhoods.
  - Land use is now a mix of new and retrofitted residential, office, retail, cultural, and civic spaces.
  - Demographics: Mixed-income, with a significant proportion of social/affordable housing (55% of new housing in latest phases), growing diversity, and a blend of young professionals, families, and older residents[^2].
  - Infrastructure: Modernized utilities, public transit links, walkable public realms, but challenges remain in integrating old and new urban fabrics.
  - Environmental conditions: The area was formerly polluted, flood-prone, and underutilized; significant remediation and green infrastructure have been implemented[^1][^2].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - High energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions, especially from older building stock.
  - Need for brownfield remediation and environmental quality improvement.
  - Social integration and equitable access amidst large-scale redevelopment.
  - Mobility and congestion concerns, with a desire to reduce car dependence.
  - Limited resident engagement in sustainability decision-making[^1][^2].
- **Triggering Events:** 
  - National and EU climate targets, local policy ambitions (e.g., Lyon 2030, SECAP), and opportunity provided by the EU’s Smart Cities initiative and the Franco-Japanese partnership (NEDO)[^3][^2].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Public: City of Lyon, Métropole de Lyon, SPL Lyon Confluence.
  - Private: Developers, energy companies, technology firms (Toshiba, Transdev).
  - Civic: Residents, neighborhood associations.
  - International: EU, NEDO (Japan), research institutes[^3][^5][^2].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** Local government-led, with strong national and EU policy alignment; SPL Lyon Confluence acts as the principal development agency[^3][^2].
- **Relevant Policies:** 
  - EU 2020 and 2030 climate/energy targets.
  - France’s Energy Transition Act (2015), Energy Policy Framework Law (2005), National Energy and Climate Plan.
  - Lyon’s Territorial Climate and Energy Plan, SECAP[^3][^2].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** 
  - Supportive regulatory frameworks for energy efficiency, renewable energy, and building retrofits.
  - Challenges in integrating new technology within legacy buildings, data privacy regulations, and procurement rules for public-private partnerships[^3][^2].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Achieve ambitious reductions in energy use and carbon emissions while increasing built area and population.
  - Demonstrate large-scale integration of renewables and smart technologies in an urban district.
  - Enhance quality of life and social inclusion for all residents[^1][^3][^4].
- **Vision Statement:** To create a zero-carbon, smart, and inclusive urban district that serves as a replicable model for sustainable city living.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Directly supports city, metropolitan, regional, and national sustainability strategies, including Lyon 2030 (climate neutrality), SECAP, and EU Smart Cities objectives[^3][^2].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** By retrofitting buildings, deploying digital monitoring/control, integrating renewables, and engaging residents, the district can decouple growth from increased emissions, foster new social/economic opportunities, and serve as a living laboratory for urban innovation[^1][^3].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Local residents, especially social/affordable housing occupants; businesses; the wider city via knowledge transfer; future urban developments.
- **Innovation Elements:** Digital twin and IoT integration at the neighborhood scale; participatory urban data platforms; positive-energy building demonstration; community energy management system; multi-modal e-mobility integration[^1][^3][^5].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** Local and EU leadership on climate and smart urbanism; demonstration value for policy innovation[^3][^4].
- **Economic:** Attract investment, support local businesses and green jobs, enhance property values, reduce operational costs[^3][^5].
- **Social:** Improve equity, social cohesion, and quality of life via engagement and affordable, efficient homes[^3][^2].
- **Environmental:** Climate mitigation/adaptation, GHG reduction, improved air and urban ecology[^1][^3][^2].
- **Technological:** Leverage emerging digital, energy, and mobility tech for urban transformation[^1][^3].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Integrated smart city, sustainability, and social inclusion principles; participatory and user-centric design; living lab/co-creation model[^1][^5][^4].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** 
  - Retrofitting 35,000 m² of existing buildings for energy efficiency (private, social, office, public).
  - Construction of new high-performance, positive-energy buildings.
  - Green public spaces, improved walkability, and sustainable mobility nodes[^1][^3][^2].
- **Scope and Phasing:** Phased implementation over several years, with pilot demonstration projects (e.g., Hikari, Sunmoov) leading into broader Smarter Together deployment and ongoing PCED expansion[^3][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - IoT sensors and digital twin for real-time building and district monitoring/control.
  - Smart grid, district heating/cooling, wood-fired cogeneration (2 MWe/4 MWth).
  - Photovoltaic systems (1 MWp new, 1 MWp existing).
  - Urban data platform for energy, mobility, and environment.
  - Electric vehicle sharing, autonomous shuttles, smart charging[^1][^3][^4][^6].
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digital twin, IoT, renewables); social (co-creation, living lab); governance (multi-actor partnerships); process (integrated retrofits, cross-sector data management)[^1][^3][^5].
- **Integration Approach:** Interoperable systems linked via urban data platform; integration with existing buildings and utilities; collaborative governance structures[^1][^3][^4].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** Competitive tendering for technology providers; strategic partnerships (e.g., with Toshiba, Transdev, local SMEs); EU and Japanese funding mechanisms[^3][^5].
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Urban Living Lab for co-design; participatory digital platforms; regular community consultations; collaborative governance bodies[^1][^5][^2].
- **Participatory Elements:** Residents co-design services and provide feedback via digital and in-person channels; demonstration projects serve as open learning environments[^1][^5].
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by SPL Lyon Confluence; cross-sector working groups; robust monitoring and reporting systems[^3][^2].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** 
  - Smarter Together: €24.7 million (EU), with additional city, regional, and private contributions.
  - Prior phase (Lyon Smart Community): €50 million (NEDO, public/private partners).
  - Current PCED: €379 million for buildings, €6 million for renewables[^3][^5][^2].
- **Funding Mix:** Public (EU, local), international (NEDO), private sector, PPPs[^3][^2].
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Not detailed, but likely include energy cost savings, value uplift, and possible service fees.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Emphasis on demonstrated operational savings, increased property values, and business creation to ensure ongoing viability[^3][^5].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:** Integrating advanced tech in old structures; ensuring interoperability; maintaining data security; limitations of existing utility grids[^1][^3].
- **Financial:** High upfront investment; balancing public and private returns; funding for ongoing innovation[^3][^2].
- **Social and Political:** Ensuring equitable access; managing displacement/gentrification risks; aligning diverse stakeholder interests[^1][^3][^2].
- **Administrative:** Coordination among multiple organizations; regulatory compliance; capacity for long-term management[^3][^2].
- **Environmental:** Remediating industrial contamination; adapting to climate risks (flooding, heatwaves)[^1][^2].

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Living lab for iterative testing and feedback; technical support for retrofits; transparent data sharing to build trust[^1][^3].
- **Design Modifications:** Adjustments to retrofit standards; phased/incremental integration of new tech; ongoing stakeholder consultations[^1][^2].
- **Lessons Learned:** Importance of resident engagement; need for robust data governance; value of cross-sectoral partnerships[^1][^3][^5].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** 35,000 m² retrofitted; 1,000,000 m² new buildings; renewable energy plants; smart mobility infrastructure[^1][^3][^2].
- **Service Delivery:** Smart energy, mobility, and data services; participatory platforms for residents; improved public spaces[^1][^3][^4].
- **Quantitative Metrics:**
  - Hikari buildings: 50–60% less energy use than standard (RT2012).
  - 67% electricity and 87% heating from renewables (Hikari).
  - 82% solar electricity in Sunmoov e-mobility.
  - CMS reduced energy use by 8–12% in monitored homes[^3].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Improved air quality; enhanced comfort and affordability; increased awareness and engagement in sustainability[^1][^3][^4].
- **Equity Outcomes:** High share of social/affordable housing; targeted support for vulnerable households[^2].
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** Not explicitly detailed; ongoing monitoring and affordable housing provisions aim to mitigate risk[^2].
- **Social Capital:** Strengthened networks through co-design processes; increased civic participation[^1][^5].
- **Cultural Impacts:** Revitalization of an industrial area, blending heritage with innovation[^2].

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Over 1,500 jobs across the three lighthouse cities (project-wide)[^4].
- **Economic Activity:** Attraction of new businesses; increased investment; new green markets[^3][^4].
- **Property Values:** Likely increases due to regeneration and improved amenities (not quantified).
- **Cost Savings:** Lower energy bills for residents; operational efficiencies for city[^3].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Emphasis on access to affordable housing and mobility[^2].

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** Marked decrease in GHG emissions; pathways to climate neutrality by 2030[^1][^3][^2].
- **Resource Management:** Significant gains in energy efficiency and renewable integration; district-scale resource optimization[^1][^3].
- **Environmental Quality:** Improved air quality; expanded green space; biodiversity enhancements in public realm[^1][^3][^2].
- **Waste and Pollution:** Brownfield remediation; improved waste management (details not specified).
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Meeting or exceeding EU/France targets for energy/carbon reduction[^3][^2][^4].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** Enhanced through cross-sector collaboration and living lab model[^5][^2].
- **Policy Influence:** Model for other districts in Lyon and peer cities in Europe; inform new policies and standards[^2][^4].
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Open data platforms and participatory processes improve governance quality[^1][^3].

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Achieved energy and climate targets; delivered new services and infrastructure; fostered sustainable urban culture[^1][^3].
- **Unintended Consequences:** Potential gentrification; need for continuous community engagement[^2].
- **Long-term Viability:** Embedded in ongoing citywide climate neutrality plans; structures in place for sustained operation and learning[^2].

### **VII. TRANSFERABILITY AND BROADER RELEVANCE**

#### **Replicability Assessment**

- **Contextual Dependencies:** Strong local leadership, robust policy frameworks, and access to EU/international funding were critical; some elements (e.g., scale, tech integration) may be less feasible in smaller or less-resourced cities[^1][^3][^2].
- **Critical Success Factors:** Stakeholder engagement, integrated planning, adaptable technology, cross-sector partnerships[^1][^3][^5][^2].
- **Scalability:** Demonstrated at district scale; lessons applied to broader Lyon metro and other EU cities[^2][^4].

#### **Comparative Analysis**

- **Similar Initiatives:** Related to Smart City projects in Vienna, Munich (Smarter Together partners); shares DNA with other urban regeneration and positive-energy district projects in Europe[^5][^4].
- **Best Practices:** Living lab/co-creation, digital twin/IoT integration, holistic retrofit, and renewable energy scaling[^1][^3][^5].
- **Cautionary Lessons:** Need for long-term affordability, careful management of displacement, ongoing technical support[^2].

- **Contextual Similarities:** Urban brownfield regeneration; challenges of integrating new tech with legacy infrastructure.
- **Methodological Parallels:** Participatory planning, scenario analysis, real-time KPI visualization.

- **Stakeholder Dynamics:** Multi-level governance, public-private collaboration, resident engagement.

- **Adaptation Considerations:** Funding mechanisms, data privacy, and building typology may require adaptation.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Pioneering integration of digital twin/IoT at district scale.
- Strong, sustained resident engagement and co-creation.
- Measurable reductions in energy use and GHG emissions.
- Deep alignment with policy, economic, and social objectives.
- Transferable model for other cities/regions[^1][^3][^5][^2].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Upfront costs and funding complexity.
- Interoperability challenges between legacy and new systems.
- Risks of gentrification and affordability pressures over time.
- Data privacy and digital literacy gaps among some residents[^3][^2].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Proactive affordable housing provision and participation.
- Ongoing need to monitor and mitigate displacement or exclusion.
- Efforts to ensure benefits are widely shared, but vigilance required[^2].

#### **Future Outlook**

- Continued expansion under Lyon 2030 and PCED programs.
- Ongoing replication and scaling across Lyon and beyond.
- Opportunities: further decarbonization, integration of new tech (AI, blockchain), deeper social inclusion.
- Challenges: Maintaining affordability, evolving governance, and adapting to new climate and economic pressures[^2].
