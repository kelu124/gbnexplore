## Madrid Connected Green Neighbourhood Demonstrator (PROBONO Horizon Project)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Madrid Connected Green Neighbourhood Demonstrator (PROBONO Horizon Project)
- **Alternative Names:** PROBONO Madrid Living Lab, Madrid Nuevo Norte Green Neighbourhood, PROBONO Madrid
- **Location:** Madrid, Spain (specifically the Madrid Nuevo Norte district, Las Tablas Oeste area)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 40.4820° N, 3.6880° W (approximate center of Madrid Nuevo Norte)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Project Start: October 2021
  - Key Milestones: 
    - First General Assembly: October 2022 (Crete, Greece)
    - Pilot Section Construction: Ongoing (2023–2025)
    - Full Demonstration Phase: 2025–2027
  - Completion/Status: Ongoing (expected completion 2027)
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Area: 3.5 million m² (Madrid Nuevo Norte)
  - Population: Estimated 100,000–120,000 residents and workers
  - Investment: Part of a larger €6 billion urban regeneration plan; PROBONO-specific funding under Horizon Europe (exact breakdown not public)
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - ACCIONA Engineering (Lead Coordinator)
  - IDOM (Design and Engineering)
  - Crea Madrid Nuevo Norte (formerly Distrito Castellana Norte)
  - Madrid City Council
  - Consortium of 47 European partners (construction, consulting, technology, research)
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - European Commission (Horizon Europe, H2020-LC-GD-2020 call)
  - Public-private partnership (PPP) with local and national government, private developers, and EU grants

#### **Executive Summary**
The Madrid Connected Green Neighbourhood Demonstrator is a flagship initiative under the EU-funded PROBONO Horizon Project, aiming to transform the Madrid Nuevo Norte district into a net-zero, energy-positive green neighbourhood. The project integrates smart building technologies, renewable energy systems, and participatory urban planning to optimize resource use, reduce carbon emissions, and enhance community engagement. Key features include a low-temperature geothermal district network, real-time monitoring of building systems (elevators, HVAC), digital dashboards, and citizen participation platforms. The initiative is designed to serve as a scalable model for urban regeneration, demonstrating how large-scale districts can achieve positive energy balance, lower operational costs, and improved occupant well-being. The project aligns with the European Green Deal and Madrid’s climate action plans, positioning Madrid as a leader in sustainable urban development.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Madrid is Spain’s capital and largest city, with a population of over 3.3 million. It is a major economic, cultural, and political hub in Southern Europe. The city faces challenges related to urban sprawl, air pollution, and energy consumption, but has committed to ambitious climate targets, including carbon neutrality by 2050.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Madrid Nuevo Norte:** A large-scale urban regeneration project in the northern part of Madrid, transforming former industrial and underutilized land into a mixed-use, sustainable district. The area is planned for residential, commercial, and public spaces, with a focus on connectivity, green infrastructure, and smart technologies.
  - **Historical Development:** The district was historically dominated by industrial and logistics activities, with limited residential or green spaces. The regeneration plan aims to create a balanced, inclusive, and resilient urban environment.
  - **Land Use:** Mixed-use (residential, commercial, public, green spaces), with a focus on high-density, energy-efficient buildings.
  - **Demographics:** Diverse population, including young professionals, families, and students. The area is expected to attract a mix of income levels, with a focus on social inclusion.
  - **Infrastructure:** Well-connected by public transport (metro, bus, rail), with ongoing investments in cycling and pedestrian infrastructure.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban heat island effect, moderate air quality challenges, and a Mediterranean climate with hot summers and mild winters.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** 
  - High energy consumption and carbon emissions from buildings and transportation.
  - Inefficient building systems (HVAC, elevators) leading to high operational costs.
  - Limited community engagement in sustainability initiatives.

  - Regulatory and technical barriers to integrating renewable energy and smart technologies.
- **Triggering Events:** 
  - EU Green Deal and national climate policies.
  - Madrid’s commitment to carbon neutrality and sustainable urban development.
  - Community demand for healthier, more resilient neighborhoods.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** 
  - **Public Sector:** Madrid City Council, regional government.
  - **Private Sector:** Developers (Crea Madrid Nuevo Norte), construction companies (ACCIONA, IDOM).
  - **Civil Society:** Local residents, community organizations, environmental NGOs.
  - **Academia and Research:** Universities, research institutes.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Multi-level governance involving local, regional, and EU authorities. The project is coordinated by a consortium of public and private partners.
- **Relevant Policies:** 
  - European Green Deal.
  - Madrid Climate Action Plan.
  - Urban Regeneration and Sustainable Development Strategies.
  - Building codes and energy efficiency regulations.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** 
  - **Enablers:** EU funding, supportive local policies, strong public-private partnerships.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The project is guided by principles of sustainable urban development, smart city technologies, and participatory planning. The design emphasizes energy efficiency, resource optimization, and community engagement.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** The district features mixed-use buildings, green spaces, and integrated infrastructure for renewable energy and smart technologies. The geothermal district network is a key element, providing heating and cooling for the entire area.
- **Scope and Phasing:** The project is implemented in phases, starting with the design and construction of the geothermal network and smart building systems, followed by the rollout of digital platforms and community engagement initiatives.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - Geothermal district network (low-temperature, underground).
  - Smart meters and IoT-enabled building systems (elevators, HVAC).
  - Digital dashboards and participatory apps.
  - Digital twins for urban planning and management.
  - Renewable energy systems (solar PV, micro-turbines).
  - Efficient HVAC technologies, green roofs, custom insulation.
  - Energy storage and EV charging infrastructure.
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (smart systems, renewable energy), social (community engagement), and governance (multi-stakeholder collaboration).
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies are integrated through a centralized energy management system, with real-time monitoring and analytics. Community platforms facilitate participatory planning and feedback.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Public-private partnerships, with contracts awarded to leading construction and technology firms.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Regular consultations with residents, businesses, and community organizations. Participatory workshops and digital platforms for feedback and co-creation.
- **Participatory Elements:** Community input in design and planning, citizen science initiatives, and awareness campaigns.
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by a consortium of partners, with regular progress reviews and adaptive management.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Part of a larger €6 billion urban regeneration plan; PROBONO-specific funding under Horizon Europe (exact breakdown not public).
- **Funding Mix:** EU grants, public investment, private sector contributions.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** User fees, energy savings, and potential land value capture.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Long-term financial viability is ensured through energy savings, reduced operational costs, and ongoing public and private investment.
