# Executive summary

## mySMARTLife – Interconnected Smart Districts (Horizon 2020 Project)

### I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION

#### Basic Information

- **Initiative Title:** mySMARTLife: Interconnected Smart Districts
- **Alternative Names:** mySMARTLife H2020; mySMARTLife Nantes Pilot
- **Location:** Nantes, France (Île de Nantes principal demo district; with pilots also in Hamburg, Germany and Helsinki, Finland)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Île de Nantes: approx. 47.2080° N, 1.5417° W
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Start: December 2016  
  - Major Implementation: 2017–2020  
  - Project Completion: 2021 (monitoring and evaluation continued post-2021)
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Nantes Metropole: 600,000 residents (metro area)  
  - Île de Nantes: 4.9 km long, 1 km wide  
  - 40+ actions in Nantes; total project budget €21.5M (Nantes: €4M+)
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Nantes Métropole (city government)  
  - European consortium: Nantes Métropole Habitat, Semitan (public transport), Armines, Atlanpole, Télécom Bretagne, Cerema, Ecole des Mines de Nantes, Engie, Enedis, Nobatek
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020: €18.6M  
  - Local public funding (Nantes: €4M+ for pilot)  
  - Partner and private sector contributions

#### Executive Summary

mySMARTLife, funded by the EU Horizon 2020 program, is a large-scale demonstration project designed to accelerate the urban energy transition in three major European cities: Nantes, Hamburg, and Helsinki. In Nantes, the project targeted the Île de Nantes district as a living laboratory for integrating digital urban platforms, renewable energy systems, and smart mobility solutions to create a truly interconnected, sustainable neighborhood. The initiative's core objectives included improving energy efficiency, advancing large-scale renewable integration, and fostering collaborative urban planning by leveraging digital twins and real-time data exchange standards. 

Key action areas ranged from deep retrofits of existing buildings and the deployment of digital boilers, to the installation of smart grids, large photovoltaic plants, and the transformation of public transport with electric buses and smart charging infrastructure. The project also prioritized social innovation, supporting new models for energy communities, and empowering citizens through open-data platforms (notably the “monprojetrenov” retrofit desk). 

Implementation in Nantes was characterized by cross-sectoral collaboration, experimentation with new governance and financing models, and a strong focus on monitoring, evaluation, and replicability. The outcomes include demonstrable reductions in energy demand and emissions, new economic opportunities, and the creation of a scalable blueprint for digital urban sustainability—one that is already influencing policy and practice across Europe[^1][^2][^3][^4].

### II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS

#### Urban Context

- **City Profile:** Nantes, France’s sixth largest city, is a regional economic and cultural hub with a population of 600,000 in its metropolitan area. It is recognized for its high quality of life, robust public transport, and proactive approach to sustainable urban development. The city has experienced 22% population growth over 25 years and anticipates 100,000 new residents by 2030, creating demand for innovative urban solutions[^1][^3].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Île de Nantes:** Central island district, 4.9 km long, 1 km wide, bounded by branches of the Loire River.  
  - **Historical Development:** Former industrial/port area, now a major site for urban regeneration and innovation.  
  - **Land Use:** Mix of residential (including social housing), commercial, institutional, and public spaces, with ongoing development of new buildings and retrofitting of existing stock[^1][^3].
  - **Demographics:** Diverse, including students, families, low-income residents, and creative professionals.  
  - **Infrastructure:** Modern tramways, e-bus rapid transit, extensive district heating, and emerging digital infrastructure.  
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban heat island risk, air quality issues, but also strong potential for renewable energy thanks to riverine and solar resources.

#### Pre-Initiative Conditions

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Rising energy demand and emissions due to population growth  
  - Need for large-scale building retrofits (energy efficiency and comfort)  
  - Social inequality and fuel poverty  
  - Legacy of fragmented data and siloed urban systems  
  - Pressure on mobility infrastructure and need for modal shift
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU policy drivers (Climate & Energy Package, Horizon 2020 calls)  
  - Local commitment to climate action and sustainable urban development
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (Nantes Métropole) seeking innovation  
  - Public transport operator (Semitan)  
  - Social housing providers (Nantes Métropole Habitat)  
  - Utility companies (Enedis, Engie)  
  - Local research institutions, SMEs, citizens, and NGO partners[^2].

#### Policy and Regulatory Environment

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Local authority (Nantes Métropole) with delegated powers for urban planning, transport, housing, and energy policy[^2].
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - SCoT (Schéma de Cohérence Territoriale – regional master plan)  
  - PLUm (urban master plan), PLH (local housing plan)  
  - Nantes Climate Plan, Sustainable Mobility Plan (PDU)
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Strong support for innovation in public procurement (environmental clauses)  
  - National and EU frameworks enabling energy communities, but some uncertainty around legal status for collective self-consumption and data sharing[^2].

### III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES

#### Strategic Vision

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Accelerate the transition to low-carbon, resilient urban neighborhoods  
  - Demonstrate the integration of digital, energy, and mobility solutions at district scale  
  - Foster citizen engagement and new economic models for energy communities
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - “Smart urban transformation for citizens”—a city where data-driven innovation creates more liveable, sustainable, and inclusive neighborhoods[^3].
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Directly supports Nantes’ climate, energy, and urban resilience targets  
  - Fits within EU Smart Cities and Communities agenda and Horizon 2020 priorities

#### Problem-Solution Logic

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - By integrating digital platforms, renewable energy, and citizen engagement at district scale, the project creates a replicable model for sustainable urban transformation. The use of digital twins and open data empowers both city managers and communities, while new business and governance models make change durable.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Residents (especially in social and multi-owner housing), local businesses, municipal authorities, and other European cities seeking to replicate the model
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Urban digital twins for real-time management  
  - Open, interoperable urban data platforms  
  - Smart grids, digital boilers, large PV plants, and soft mobility  
  - Integrated, cross-sectoral governance and new financing mechanisms[^1][^3][^4][^5]

#### Drivers and Motivations

- **Political:** Strong mayoral and council commitment to climate leadership; EU smart city policy targets
- **Economic:** Need for green jobs and business models; managing growth without increasing inequality or energy poverty
- **Social:** Reducing fuel poverty; co-creating solutions with citizens
- **Environmental:** Climate adaptation, reduced GHG emissions, improved air quality
- **Technological:** Maturation of digital twins, IoT, and smart grid platforms; opportunity for leadership in digital urban innovation

### IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH

#### Project Design

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Smart district urbanism: integration of energy, mobility, data, and citizen services  
  - Emphasis on both hardware (infrastructure, buildings, renewables) and software (data, platforms, governance)
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Three zones: new construction (smart buildings, digital boilers, renewable integration), retrofitting (multi-owner buildings, houses), and city-wide (mobility, digital platforms)
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial focus on Île de Nantes (2017–2019), expansion to broader city in later phases; staged deployment of technologies and platforms[^1][^3].

#### Technologies and Innovations

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Urban digital twins and city data platforms  
  - Digital boilers (waste heat from data centers)  
  - Smart thermostats, hybrid solar PV+thermal systems  
  - Battery storage, smart metering, e-buses (TOSA system), e-bike and e-car charging  
  - Web-based retrofit platform (“monprojetrenov”), solar cadaster, citizen engagement portals[^1][^3][^4]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digital twins, smart energy)  
  - Social (energy communities, citizen platforms)  
  - Financial (new retrofit financing schemes)  
  - Governance (cross-sectoral partnerships)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Data interoperability via open APIs and standards  
  - Vertical integration (building-district-city scale)

#### Implementation Methodology

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Public tenders with environmental clauses; selection of technology and delivery partners via competitive EU processes
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Multi-stakeholder governance model; citizen dialogue prioritized in project design and rollout  
  - Participatory workshops, digital feedback, and co-design of services (e.g., retrofit desk, solar cadaster)[^2][^3]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by Nantes Métropole and European consortium; external monitoring and evaluation by Cerema

#### Financing Model

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total project: €21.5M (Nantes: €4M+ for pilot), €56M in local/retrofit incentives over 2018–2025[^2][^4]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - 86% EU H2020 grants, local/regional government funds, and in-kind partner contributions
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - User fees for mobility services, energy savings, and local economic returns from retrofit and solar projects
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Emphasis on business models for energy communities and ongoing city budgetary support for retrofit/renewable incentives

### V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES

#### Implementation Challenges

- **Technical:**  
  - Data interoperability across legacy and new systems  
  - Integration of digital twins and real-time data from diverse sources  
  - Smart grid and storage integration complexities
- **Financial:**  
  - Balancing up-front investment and long-term savings  
  - Securing co-financing for large retrofits and renewable installations
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Sustaining community engagement and participation  
  - Addressing equity in access to new solutions (avoiding “digital divide”)
- **Administrative:**  
  - Cross-departmental coordination within city government and between partners  
  - Managing a large, multinational consortium[^2][^4]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Ensuring new infrastructure did not create unintended environmental impacts  
  - Managing site-specific constraints (e.g., riverine flooding risk on Île de Nantes)

#### Adaptive Strategies

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Iterative development and piloting of platforms and services  
  - Use of real-time monitoring to adjust implementation (e.g., energy load balancing, mobility services rollout)
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Phased deployment to allow learning and course correction  
  - Modifications to digital platform architecture to address latency and data quality issues[^4][^6]
- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of robust stakeholder engagement  
  - Need for flexible, open data standards  
  - Value of local leadership and cross-sectoral trust

### VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS

#### Direct Outputs

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Deep retrofit of multi-owner residential buildings (over 1,300 apartments)  
  - New digital boilers in social housing (approx. 20 MWh/year each)  
  - Large PV installations (5 MWp plant at M.I.N. industrial estate)  
  - Battery storage and solar canopies at public facilities  
  - Deployment of 22 e-buses (Busway line), 90 e-car charging stations, 1,500 e-bikes, and 200 electric plugs for bikes
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Web-based retrofit desk (over 500 subsidized housing units/year targeted)  
  - Urban Data Platform integrating 74,000 sensors and 3,000+ datasets, real-time data for traffic, charging, etc.[^4]
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - Over 40 distinct actions in Nantes, 150+ across the three cities  
  - €56M allocated to retrofit grants (2018–2025)[^4]

#### Social Impacts

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Lower household energy bills; improved comfort and health  
  - Enhanced mobility and access to green transport options
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Targeted subsidies for low-income households  
  - Focus on social housing and inclusive engagement
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No major displacement reported; ongoing monitoring for gentrification risks
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Strengthened networks via energy communities and participatory platforms
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Reinforced civic identity as a “city of innovation”

#### Economic Impacts

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Green jobs in retrofit, renewable energy, mobility, and ICT sectors
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - New business opportunities in construction, energy, and digital services
- **Property Values:**  
  - Improved infrastructure likely enhanced values, especially in retrofitted areas
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Significant energy savings for households and city government
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Focus on reducing fuel poverty and supporting economic access

#### Environmental Impacts

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Reduced greenhouse gas emissions through energy efficiency, renewables, and modal shift
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Higher efficiency in water, energy, and materials use; improved grid balancing
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality, more green space, noise reduction via e-mobility
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Enhanced waste management via smart infrastructure
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Performance tracked against EU and local climate/energy standards; real-time data used for continuous improvement[^4][^6]

#### Governance and Institutional Impacts

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened city capability for digital innovation and cross-sectoral governance
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model adopted by other European cities; influenced EU smart city policy
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Open data and participatory processes improved governance quality

#### Performance Against Objectives

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Most key objectives met or exceeded: energy savings, emissions reduction, citizen engagement, replicable models
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Some technical delays and data integration challenges; risk of digital exclusion for vulnerable groups
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Strong prospects for lasting impact, supported by ongoing city investment and EU interest

### VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION

#### Strengths of the Initiative

- **Comprehensive integration** of digital, energy, and mobility solutions at district scale
- **Strong participatory approach** engaging residents, businesses, and institutions
- **Open data standards and platforms** enabling transparency, replication, and innovation
- **Robust monitoring, evaluation, and learning** processes built in

#### Limitations and Weaknesses

- **Technical complexity and latency issues** in data platform integration
- **Potential for digital exclusion** if vulnerable populations are not proactively included
- **Dependent on substantial public funding**—challenges for replication in less-resourced contexts

#### Equity and Justice Considerations

- **Advance:** Targeted subsidies and retrofits for low-income/social housing, participatory design
- **Hinder:** Risk that benefits accrue disproportionately to more digitally literate or connected groups; need for ongoing vigilance against gentrification

#### Future Outlook

- **Long-term trajectory:** Expansion of data platform, further mainstreaming of retrofit incentives, ongoing digital twin development
- **Ongoing challenges:** Ensuring financial sustainability, bridging digital divides, adapting governance to evolving technologies
- **Next phases:** Scaling up to additional districts, refining citizen platforms, deepening integration with regional/National climate strategies
