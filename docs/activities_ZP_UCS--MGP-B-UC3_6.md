# Executive summary## 3G to 5G District Heating and Cooling Networks (Horizon Europe)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** 3G to 5G District Heating and Cooling Networks  
- **Alternative Names:** Next-Generation District Heating and Cooling (DHC) Networks, Horizon Europe DHC Demonstration Project  
- **Location:** Multiple European cities (networked demonstration sites across the EU, including cities in Germany, Spain, Italy, and Eastern Europe)  
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Varies by demonstration site; typically urban centers with existing or planned district heating infrastructure  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project Start: 2022 (aligned with Horizon Europe launch)  
  - Key Milestones:  
    - 2023: Deployment of pilot networks in selected cities  
    - 2024: Integration of smart management platforms and real-time monitoring  
    - 2025: Evaluation and policy recommendations  
  - Completion/Ongoing Status: Ongoing, with final results expected by 2026  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Urban districts in multiple cities (total area varies by site, typically 10–50 km² per demonstration)  
  - Population Affected: Estimated 50,000–100,000 residents across all demonstration sites  
  - Investment Value: €20–30 million (Horizon Europe funding, with additional local and national co-funding)  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Horizon Europe (EU Framework Programme for Research and Innovation)  
  - NetZeroCities (implementation and coordination)  
  - Local city authorities and energy utilities (e.g., municipal energy companies, district heating operators)  
  - Research institutions (e.g., universities, technical institutes)  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Public: Horizon Europe, EU Structural Funds, national and local government grants  
  - Private: Energy utilities, technology providers, private investors  
  - PPP: Public-private partnerships for technology deployment and network operation  

#### **Executive Summary**

The 3G to 5G District Heating and Cooling Networks project is a flagship Horizon Europe initiative that pilots the transition from traditional third-generation (3G) to advanced fifth-generation (5G) district heating and cooling (DHC) systems in multiple European cities. The project focuses on ultra-low temperature networks, renewable and waste heat integration, and smart digital management platforms to maximize energy efficiency, decarbonization, and user comfort. By demonstrating the technical and economic viability of next-generation DHC systems, the initiative aims to provide scalable models for climate-neutral urban districts, support the EU’s climate neutrality goals, and inform policy and regulatory frameworks. The project’s outcomes include significant energy savings, major reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, and the creation of new green jobs, while also addressing challenges such as legacy system integration, interoperability, and large-scale data management. The initiative is a critical step toward the widespread adoption of sustainable, resilient, and equitable urban energy systems in Europe.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - The demonstration sites are located in mid-sized to large European cities with diverse economic bases, ranging from industrial and service-oriented economies to knowledge and innovation hubs.  
  - Geographic characteristics include temperate climates with distinct heating and cooling seasons, making DHC systems particularly relevant.  
  - These cities are positioned as leaders in urban sustainability and innovation within their respective regions and countries.  

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical Development Patterns:** Many demonstration sites are in urban districts with a mix of older, post-war housing and newer, energy-efficient buildings.  
  - **Current Land Use Composition:** Residential, commercial, and public buildings, with a focus on high-density urban areas.  
  - **Demographic Profile:**  
    - Income levels: Mixed, with a significant proportion of middle- and lower-income households.  
    - Age distribution: Diverse, with a growing elderly population in some areas.  
    - Ethnic/cultural composition: Increasingly multicultural, reflecting broader urban trends.  
  - **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:**  
    - Many districts have aging district heating networks, with varying levels of efficiency and reliability.  
    - Accessibility to public services and amenities is generally good, but energy poverty remains a concern in some neighborhoods.  
  - **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:**  
    - Urban heat island effects, air quality issues, and climate change impacts (e.g., extreme weather events) are significant challenges.  
    - Limited space for new infrastructure and the need to minimize disruption during upgrades.  

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High carbon emissions from heating and cooling in urban areas.  
  - Energy insecurity and vulnerability to fossil fuel price fluctuations.  
  - Aging and inefficient district heating infrastructure with high heat losses.  
  - Energy poverty and social inequality in access to affordable, clean energy.  
  - Limited integration of renewable and waste heat sources in existing systems.  

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU climate neutrality targets (2050) and the Mission for 100 Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities (2030).  
  - Rising energy prices and concerns over energy security following the 2022 energy crisis.  
  - Advances in smart grid and digital technologies, enabling new approaches to energy management.  

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - **Key Actors:**

    - Energy utilities and technology providers  
    - Research institutions and universities  
    - Residents and community organizations  
    - National and EU policymakers  
  - **Interests:**  
    - City authorities: Decarbonization, energy security, and improved quality of life.  
    - Energy utilities: Operational efficiency, regulatory compliance, and new business opportunities.  
    - Residents: Affordable, reliable, and sustainable energy services.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

  - Multi-level governance involving local, national, and EU authorities.

- **Relevant Policies:**

  - RePowerEU Plan: Accelerates the transition to renewable energy and energy efficiency.
