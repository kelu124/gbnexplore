# Executive summary

## H22 – The Making of a Smarter City

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** H22 – The Making of a Smarter City
- **Alternative Names:** H22 Smart City Initiative; H22 City Expo (2022 showcase event); H22 Helsingborg
- **Location:** Helsingborg, Sweden (municipality of 150,000 residents on the west coast of Sweden where the Baltic Sea meets the North Sea)[^1]
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approximately 56.0465°N, 12.6945°E
- **Timeline:** Initiative launched in 2019; H22 Summit held November 5-6, 2019; H22 City Expo held May 30 – July 3, 2022; ongoing implementation through 2030[^2][^3]
- **Project Scale:** City-wide initiative affecting entire municipality of 150,000 residents; H22 City Expo covered over 500,000 square meters[^2]; 10-year implementation horizon with vision extending to 2035[^4]
- **Leading Organization(s):** City of Helsingborg (municipal government); collaborative partnerships with residents, associations, companies, academia, and international actors[^5]; collaboration with over 70 national and global businesses and organizations[^1]
- **Funding Sources:** SEK 250 million (approximately €26.7 million) committed over three years as a major municipal investment[^3]; EU Horizon Europe funding (€360 million available to Mission cities including Helsingborg for 2022-23)[^6]; additional funding from national and international partners participating in test beds[^5]

#### **Executive Summary**

H22 represents an ambitious, long-term urban transformation initiative launched by the City of Helsingborg in 2019 to address systemic challenges facing mid-sized European cities in the twenty-first century.[^7][^8] Rather than a conventional real estate development project, H22 is fundamentally a collaborative innovation ecosystem designed to test, develop, and implement sustainable solutions that improve quality of life across social, economic, and environmental dimensions.[^5][^2] The initiative responds to critical demographic and fiscal pressures: Helsingborg, like many Swedish municipalities, faces a rising elderly population and declining working-age cohort, creating fiscal strain while citizen expectations for public services continue to rise.[^3] 

H22's distinctive approach positions the entire municipality as a "living test bed" for experimenting with innovative welfare solutions, digital transformation, and sustainable urban development.[^1] Rather than imposing top-down solutions, the initiative emphasizes collaborative co-creation involving citizens, businesses, academia, and international partners.[^5] Three interconnected themes guide the work: improving quality of life through better urban design and human-centered planning; innovative urban development leveraging digitalization and automation; and reinventing city governance through citizen participation and co-creation.[^3]

The initiative gained international prominence through the H22 City Expo (May-July 2022), a 35-day international event that showcased innovations and attracted representatives from over 50 countries, with approximately 300 meetings, conferences, and lectures.[^6] By 2030, Helsingborg aims to achieve climate neutrality while maintaining and improving public services at lower cost through smarter, more efficient approaches.[^9] H22 exemplifies a new paradigm in urban development that integrates smart technology, sustainability commitments, democratic participation, and pragmatic problem-solving to address interconnected urban challenges.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile**

Helsingborg is a mid-sized Swedish city of 150,000 residents located on the west coast of Sweden in the Skåne region, positioned where the Baltic Sea meets the North Sea.[^1] The city has a long historical tradition, including 119 years of organizing city expos—a civic tradition that H22 deliberately builds upon.[^2] Economically, Helsingborg has established itself as a knowledge and innovation hub within Sweden, earning recognition as "Sweden's IT municipality" and receiving multiple sustainability awards.[^3] The city benefits from strategic geographic positioning with international connectivity and access to regional markets across Scandinavia and Europe.

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics**

While H22 operates at the city-wide scale rather than focusing on a single neighborhood, the initiative encompasses multiple distinct areas including the smart city centre, the ideas-driven innovation district of Drottninghög, and the nature-rich Slottshagen Park area.[^4] The city's urban fabric reflects typical Scandinavian planning with mixed-use development, strong public transportation infrastructure, and significant green space integration. Helsingborg's existing infrastructure is relatively modern and well-maintained, reflecting Sweden's generally high level of urban development. The demographic profile is characteristic of Scandinavian cities with relatively high income levels, aging population, and high educational attainment.

**Pre-Initiative Conditions**

#### **Problems/Challenges Identified**

Helsingborg faced interconnected challenges typical of post-industrial European cities in the early 2020s:[^3]

- **Demographic pressures:** Rising elderly population and declining working-age population, creating an unfavorable ratio between public service users and taxpayers
- **Fiscal sustainability:** Municipal expenses rising faster than income, requiring service improvements at lower cost
- **Urban congestion and complexity:** Traffic control challenges, overcrowding in certain areas, and complex urban management needs
- **Service delivery expectations:** Citizens demanding better, simpler, faster, and more flexible public services despite fiscal constraints
- **Aging infrastructure:** Need for modernization of urban systems and services
- **Environmental pressures:** Imperative to reduce emissions and achieve climate neutrality while accommodating population growth

#### **Triggering Events**

The formal trigger for H22 was a 2019 municipal decision to accelerate the city's innovation work through a major strategic initiative.[^2] This decision reflected recognition that traditional approaches to urban management and service delivery were insufficient to address the interconnected challenges facing the city. The initiative emerged during a period of global interest in smart cities and sustainable urban development, with Helsingborg positioned to leverage this momentum.

#### **Stakeholder Landscape**

Prior to H22, Helsingborg had already established itself as an innovation-oriented municipality with active engagement from municipal government, businesses, academic institutions, and civil society organizations. The city had been a member of ICLEI Europe (International Council for Local Environmental Initiatives) since 2000, positioning it within networks of environmentally progressive cities.[^1] This existing stakeholder ecosystem provided foundation for the more ambitious collaborative approach that H22 would formalize.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

**Governance Framework**

Helsingborg operates within Sweden's municipal governance system, with local autonomy in urban development and service delivery while operating within national policy frameworks. The city government, led by Mayor Peter Danielsson, provided political leadership and municipal resources for H22.[^1] The initiative was structured to involve multiple governance levels and stakeholder groups through collaborative mechanisms rather than purely hierarchical municipal administration.

**Relevant Policies**

H22 aligns with several policy frameworks:[^6]

- **UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** Helsingborg submitted a Voluntary Local Review to the UN, positioning H22 as a vehicle for implementing the 2030 Agenda at the local level
- **EU Climate Neutrality Targets:** Selection as one of 112 cities in the EU's Mission for Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities by 2030, with access to Horizon Europe funding
- **Swedish National Sustainability Goals:** Alignment with Sweden's climate and environmental commitments
- **Regional Development Strategies:** Integration with Skåne region development priorities

**Legal and Institutional Enablers**

- Swedish municipal law provides significant autonomy for local innovation and experimentation
- EU funding mechanisms (Horizon Europe) and international networks (ICLEI, OECD) provided institutional support and financing
- Royal patronage from Crown Princess Victoria of Sweden elevated the initiative's profile and provided institutional legitimacy[^3]
- Existing municipal capacity in IT, sustainability, and innovation provided strong institutional foundation

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

**Primary Goals**

H22 articulates multiple interconnected objectives:[^5][^3]

1. **Improve quality of life** for residents and workers through better urban design, enhanced public services, and human-centered planning
2. **Achieve climate neutrality by 2030** while accommodating population growth and maintaining service quality
3. **Develop innovative welfare solutions** that address demographic and fiscal challenges through smarter, more efficient approaches
4. **Enable sustainable urban development** through digital transformation, resource efficiency, and circular economy principles
5. **Foster collaborative innovation** by creating platforms for co-creation among citizens, businesses, academia, and government
6. **Establish Helsingborg as a European leader** in smart, sustainable urban development and innovation

**Vision Statement**

Helsingborg aspires to become "one of Europe's most innovative cities" and a model for mid-sized urban centers navigating twenty-first century challenges.[^5] The 2035 vision describes Helsingborg as "a pulsating centre for people and companies, a creative, regenerative location that welcomes all and attracts business both local and international by offering the best possible conditions."[^4] Central to this vision is the principle that "a smart city is not just a city that invests in technological solutions. It's a city that puts its people and the planet first."[^5]

**Alignment with Broader Plans**

H22 integrates multiple policy frameworks:

- **Helsingborg 2035:** Long-term municipal development vision
- **EU Mission for Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities:** Helsingborg's participation positions it within a European network of 112 frontrunner cities
- **UN 2030 Agenda:** H22 serves as the mechanism for implementing Sustainable Development Goals at the local level
- **Swedish Climate Goals:** Alignment with national climate commitments
- **Quality of Life Programme:** Helsingborg's unique framework connecting environmental work and public health, established since 2016[^6]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

**Theory of Change**

H22 operates on a theory of change that emphasizes systemic transformation through collaborative innovation:[^5][^3]

The initiative posits that addressing complex urban challenges requires moving beyond traditional municipal service delivery ("doing things for residents") to enabling residents and stakeholders to solve problems themselves ("enabling them to do things for themselves").[^5] By creating test beds and platforms for experimentation, the municipality can identify scalable solutions while building citizen engagement and institutional learning. Digital tools and data infrastructure enable more efficient resource management and evidence-based decision-making. Collaborative governance structures create alignment among diverse stakeholders, reducing implementation barriers. International partnerships and knowledge exchange accelerate learning and adaptation. This integrated approach—combining innovation, collaboration, digitalization, and citizen engagement—creates conditions for sustainable transformation.

**Target Beneficiaries**

Primary beneficiaries include:

- **Residents and workers:** Improved quality of life, better services, enhanced safety, reduced environmental impact
- **Municipal government:** More efficient service delivery, lower costs, enhanced institutional capacity
- **Businesses and entrepreneurs:** Innovation opportunities, market development, improved business environment
- **Academia and research institutions:** Collaboration opportunities, research platforms, real-world laboratories
- **International cities:** Knowledge transfer and best practice sharing through H22's demonstration effects

**Innovation Elements**

H22 distinguishes itself through several innovative dimensions:[^5][^3]

- **Breadth of scope:** Rather than targeting a single sector, H22 addresses interconnected challenges across energy, mobility, governance, social services, and urban design
- **Test bed approach:** Positioning the entire city as a living laboratory for experimentation rather than implementing predetermined solutions
- **Collaborative governance:** Formal mechanisms for co-creation among government, citizens, businesses, and academia
- **Long-term commitment:** 10-year horizon with political and financial commitment extending beyond typical election cycles
- **International integration:** Deliberate positioning within global networks and partnerships (MIT, UNECE, EU missions)
- **Quality of life framework:** Using citizen well-being as the central metric rather than purely technical or economic measures

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

**Political Drivers**

- Political leadership commitment to innovation and sustainability
- Municipal desire to establish Helsingborg as a distinctive, attractive location within competitive urban hierarchy
- Alignment with Swedish national values emphasizing sustainability, equality, and innovation
- Royal patronage providing institutional legitimacy and public visibility

**Economic Drivers**

- Fiscal pressures requiring more efficient service delivery models
- Economic development opportunities through innovation ecosystem and green technology sectors
- Attraction of investment and talent through positioning as forward-thinking city
- Potential for new business models and economic activity in sustainability and smart city sectors

**Social Drivers**

- Citizen expectations for improved public services and quality of life
- Community desire for participation in decision-making and co-creation
- Social cohesion concerns related to demographic change and potential service gaps
- Health and well-being priorities reflecting Scandinavian social values

**Environmental Drivers**

- EU and national climate commitments requiring local action
- Recognition that cities are crucial sites for climate action and sustainability
- Helsingborg's existing environmental leadership (four-time "Most Environmentally Friendly Municipality" award winner)[^9][^1]
- Resource scarcity and circular economy imperatives

**Technological Drivers**

- Availability of advanced IoT sensors, smart grids, AI algorithms, and digital platforms
- Global smart city trends and demonstrated potential of digital technologies
- Opportunity to leverage digital infrastructure for efficiency and citizen engagement
- Emerging data analytics capabilities enabling anticipatory governance

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

**Conceptual Framework**

H22 is grounded in several interconnected urban development philosophies:[^5][^3]

- **Human-centered design:** Prioritizing citizen well-being and quality of life as central metrics rather than purely technical or economic measures
- **Systems thinking:** Recognizing interconnections among energy, mobility, governance, social systems, and environmental conditions
- **Anticipatory innovation:** Proactively testing new approaches rather than reactively responding to crises
- **Collaborative governance:** Viewing municipal government as an enabler and convener rather than sole decision-maker
- **Circular economy principles:** Designing resource flows to minimize waste and maximize reuse (exemplified in the three-pipe sewer system)
- **Incremental transformation:** Building change through experimentation and learning rather than comprehensive redesign

**Physical/Spatial Design**

H22 encompasses multiple spatial interventions across the city:[^4]

- **Smart city centre:** Urban core redesigned for connectivity, efficiency, and livability
- **Drottninghög innovation district:** Ideas-driven neighborhood for creative industries and innovation enterprises
- **Slottshagen Park:** Nature-rich area integrating green infrastructure with urban development
- **Three-pipe sewer system:** Innovative infrastructure recycling food waste, grey water, and black water to extract nitrogen, phosphorus, and purified water[^4][^10]
- **Electric ferry system:** Sustainable public transportation connecting Helsingborg to nearby communities
- **Tree-planting initiatives:** Goal of planting 8,000 new trees by end of 2020 to enhance urban green space[^5]

**Scope and Phasing**

The initiative follows a staged approach:[^2][^3]

- **Phase 1 (2019):** H22 Summit (November 2019) establishing vision and gathering stakeholders
- **Phase 2 (2019-2022):** Development and testing of innovations within test beds
- **Phase 3 (2022):** H22 City Expo (May-July 2022) showcasing innovations and engaging international partners
- **Phase 4 (2022-2030):** Implementation and scaling of proven innovations across the city
- **Long-term (2030+):** Consolidation of gains and evolution toward 2035 vision

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

**Technologies Deployed**

H22 integrates multiple technological systems:[^5][^9]

- **IoT Sensors:** Tiny sensors monitoring air and water temperature, bicycle locations, environmental conditions throughout the city
- **Smart Grids:** Advanced electricity distribution systems enabling real-time demand response and renewable energy integration
- **Energy Storage Systems:** Battery and other storage technologies for managing renewable energy variability
- **AI Algorithms:** Machine learning for predicting parking availability, optimizing traffic flow, and anticipating service needs
- **Digital Participation Platforms:** Online systems enabling citizen engagement, data access, and co-creation
- **LoRa (Long Range) Networks:** Low-power, wide-area networks connecting distributed IoT devices
- **Smart Building Management Systems:** Automated control of energy, heating, cooling, and resource use in buildings
- **Data Analytics Infrastructure:** Systems for collecting, analyzing, and visualizing urban data
- **Three-pipe infrastructure:** Specialized plumbing systems for resource recovery and circular economy implementation

**Innovation Type**

H22 encompasses multiple innovation categories:[^5]

- **Technological innovations:** IoT systems, smart grids, AI algorithms, digital platforms
- **Social innovations:** Collaborative governance models, citizen participation mechanisms, new forms of stakeholder engagement
- **Process innovations:** New approaches to municipal service delivery, decision-making, and project management
- **Governance innovations:** Reinvented institutional structures enabling collaboration across sectors
- **Business model innovations:** New financing mechanisms and revenue models for sustainability initiatives
- **Spatial innovations:** Redesigned urban spaces integrating nature, technology, and human needs

**Integration Approach**

Rather than deploying technologies in isolation, H22 emphasizes integrated systems thinking:[^5][^9]

- **Transport integration:** Connecting smart mobility systems (electric ferries, bike tracking) with energy infrastructure and land use planning
- **Energy integration:** Linking renewable energy generation, storage, smart grids, and building management
- **Data integration:** Creating unified information systems connecting environmental monitoring, service delivery, and citizen engagement
- **Governance integration:** Aligning municipal departments, private sector partners, and civic organizations around shared objectives
- **Spatial integration:** Ensuring technological and social innovations are embedded within thoughtfully designed urban environments

#### **Implementation Methodology**

**Procurement and Contracting**

H22 operates through a collaborative partnership model rather than traditional competitive procurement:[^5][^1]

- **Test bed partnerships:** Over 70 national and global businesses and organizations partnered with the city to develop and test innovations within designated test beds
- **International collaborations:** Partnerships with leading institutions including MIT's Senseable City Lab, UNECE, and European innovation networks
- **Academic partnerships:** Collaboration with universities and research institutions for knowledge development and validation
- **Public-private partnerships:** Structured arrangements with private sector actors to develop and implement solutions

**Stakeholder Engagement**

H22 emphasizes extensive stakeholder involvement:[^5][^3]

- **Resident participation:** Citizens invited to contribute ideas, participate in test beds, and co-create solutions
- **Business engagement:** Companies and entrepreneurs invited to develop and test innovations within the city
- **Academic involvement:** Universities and research institutions engaged as knowledge partners
- **International networks:** Participation in European and global networks (ICLEI, OECD, EU missions) for knowledge exchange
- **Cross-sector collaboration:** Deliberate mechanisms for bringing together government, private sector, civil society, and academia

**Participatory Elements**

The initiative incorporates multiple mechanisms for democratic participation:[^5][^3]

- **Test beds:** Designated areas and initiatives where residents and stakeholders could propose and develop ideas
- **Digital participation platforms:** Online systems enabling citizen input and access to urban data
- **Public events:** H22 Summit, City Expo, and ongoing community events creating opportunities for engagement
- **Co-creation processes:** Structured mechanisms for residents and stakeholders to participate in solution development
- **Transparency and communication:** Regular updates and sharing of learnings through newsletters, media, and public forums

**Project Management**

H22 employs adaptive management approaches reflecting innovation and experimentation orientation:[^3]

- **Adaptive governance structures:** Flexible organizational arrangements allowing for learning and adjustment
- **Cross-departmental coordination:** Municipal departments working collaboratively rather than in silos
- **Regular monitoring and evaluation:** Continuous assessment of initiatives against objectives
- **Learning cycles:** Deliberate processes for capturing lessons and adjusting approaches based on evidence
- **International benchmarking:** Comparison with peer cities and best practice exchange

#### **Financing Model**

**Budget and Costs**

H22 represents a substantial municipal investment:[^3]

- **Core commitment:** SEK 250 million (approximately €26.7 million) committed over three years by municipal government
- **EU funding:** Access to €360 million in Horizon Europe funding available to Mission cities for 2022-2023, with Helsingborg eligible to apply for portions supporting research and innovation projects in clean mobility, energy efficiency, and green urban planning[^6]
- **Partner funding:** Additional funding from 70+ national and international organizations participating in test beds and innovation projects
- **Total project scale:** While complete budget figures are not fully detailed in available sources, the initiative represents one of the most substantial urban innovation investments in Swedish municipal history[^3]

**Funding Mix**

- **Municipal government:** Primary funder providing core SEK 250 million commitment
- **EU funds:** Horizon Europe and other EU mechanisms supporting research and demonstration projects
- **Private sector:** Companies and organizations investing in test bed projects and innovations
- **International donors:** Support from international networks and development organizations
- **User fees and revenue mechanisms:** Potential revenue from new services and energy efficiency gains

**Revenue Mechanisms**

While detailed revenue models are not extensively documented, potential mechanisms include:[^9]

- **Energy savings:** Revenue from efficiency improvements and renewable energy integration
- **Service fee models:** Potential user fees for new services developed through H22
- **Land value capture:** Potential capture of increased property values resulting from improved urban conditions
- **Sponsorships and partnerships:** Revenue from companies investing in innovation test beds
- **Grant funding:** Ongoing access to EU and international funding for sustainability and innovation projects

**Financial Sustainability**

H22 is designed for long-term financial viability through:[^3]

- **Operational efficiency gains:** Reduced municipal costs through smarter service delivery enabling maintenance of service quality at lower cost
- **Economic development:** Revenue generation through new businesses and economic activity in innovation sectors
- **Diversified funding:** Multiple funding sources reducing dependence on any single source
- **Scalability:** Innovations designed to be scalable, reducing per-unit costs as they expand
- **International funding:** Access to EU and global funding mechanisms supporting climate and sustainability goals

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

**Technical Challenges**

- **Large-scale IoT deployment:** Deploying thousands of sensors across municipal infrastructure requires significant technical expertise, coordination, and maintenance capacity
- **Data security and privacy:** Managing sensitive urban data while protecting citizen privacy and ensuring cybersecurity
- **Technology integration:** Ensuring diverse technological systems communicate effectively and integrate with existing municipal infrastructure
- **Sensor maintenance and reliability:** Ensuring continuous operation of distributed sensor networks across varied environmental conditions
- **Interoperability:** Creating seamless connections between systems from different vendors and manufacturers

**Financial Challenges**

- **Funding gaps:** Securing sufficient financing for ambitious initiatives within constrained municipal budgets
- **Cost overruns:** Innovation projects often exceed initial cost estimates due to technical complexity and unforeseen challenges
- **Long-term financing:** Ensuring sustained funding for ongoing operations and maintenance beyond initial implementation phase
- **Economic uncertainty:** Market fluctuations and economic downturns affecting private sector participation and investment

**Social and Political Challenges**

- **Digital divide:** Ensuring equitable access to digital participation platforms and benefits across diverse populations
- **Displacement risks:** Potential gentrification and displacement as improved urban conditions attract investment and increase property values
- **Stakeholder alignment:** Balancing sometimes divergent interests of residents, businesses, academia, and government
- **Public skepticism:** Overcoming citizen concerns about surveillance through ubiquitous sensors and data collection
- **Political support:** Maintaining political commitment across election cycles and potential leadership changes
- **Social equity:** Ensuring benefits are distributed fairly and vulnerable populations are not disadvantaged

**Administrative Challenges**

- **Coordination complexity:** Coordinating activities across municipal departments, private sector partners, and international collaborators
- **Institutional capacity:** Building municipal capabilities in innovation management, data analytics, and collaborative governance
- **Procurement complexity:** Developing procurement processes suitable for innovation and collaboration rather than traditional competitive bidding
- **Knowledge management:** Capturing and sharing learnings from diverse initiatives and test beds
- **Bureaucratic barriers:** Navigating regulatory frameworks and administrative processes not designed for rapid innovation

**Environmental Challenges**

- **Climate variability:** Ensuring systems function reliably across weather extremes and seasonal variations
- **Unintended consequences:** Innovation initiatives potentially creating unforeseen environmental impacts
- **Trade-offs:** Balancing multiple environmental objectives (e.g., urban densification vs. green space preservation)

#### **Adaptive Responses**

**Problem-Solving Approaches**

H22 demonstrates several adaptive strategies:[^5][^3]

- **Experimentation and learning:** Test bed approach allows for trying approaches on limited scale before full implementation, reducing risk of large-scale failures
- **Iterative improvement:** Continuous monitoring and adjustment of initiatives based on performance data and stakeholder feedback
- **Collaborative problem-solving:** Engaging stakeholders in identifying and addressing challenges rather than imposing top-down solutions
- **Technology adaptation:** Selecting and adapting technologies based on local conditions and learning from implementation experience
- **Phased implementation:** Spreading implementation over time to manage complexity and allow for learning between phases

**Design Modifications**

- **Test bed refinement:** Adjusting test bed designs based on early results and stakeholder feedback
- **Technology selection:** Modifying technology choices based on reliability, cost, and integration experience
- **Governance structures:** Evolving collaborative mechanisms based on effectiveness in practice
- **Timeline adjustments:** Extending timelines for complex initiatives requiring more development time

**Lessons Learned During Implementation**

While comprehensive documentation of specific lessons is limited in available sources, key insights emerging from H22 include:[^5][^3]

- **Innovation requires different approaches than operations:** Recognition that innovation and operational development require distinct organizational structures and management approaches
- **Collaboration complexity:** Understanding that collaborative governance requires significant investment in relationship-building and alignment processes
- **Technology is enabler, not solution:** Recognition that technology alone cannot solve urban challenges; social, governance, and design innovations are equally important
- **Citizen engagement is essential:** Active participation by residents and stakeholders significantly enhances initiative effectiveness and social acceptance
- **Long-term commitment matters:** Sustained political and financial commitment across multiple years is essential for meaningful change in complex urban systems
- **Equity requires deliberate attention:** Benefits of innovation do not automatically distribute equitably; intentional strategies are needed to ensure inclusive outcomes

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

H22 demonstrates several significant strengths that contribute to its effectiveness and influence:

**Comprehensive Integration**

H22's most distinctive strength is its comprehensive, integrated approach addressing interconnected urban challenges across energy, mobility, governance, social systems, and environmental management. Rather than isolated sectoral interventions, H22 recognizes and addresses systemic interconnections—energy systems connected to mobility, buildings connected to social services, governance structures connected to environmental outcomes. This systems perspective reflects sophisticated understanding of urban complexity and provides foundation for more transformative change than narrower interventions.

**Long-Term Commitment**

The 10-year implementation horizon with sustained political and financial commitment represents significant strength. Meaningful urban transformation requires time for learning, adaptation, and behavioral change. H22's long-term commitment, with SEK 250 million municipal investment over three years, demonstrates serious political will and provides stability for implementation. This contrasts with many urban initiatives that depend on short-term funding and political cycles.

**Genuine Stakeholder Engagement**

H22's commitment to collaborative governance and citizen participation goes beyond superficial consultation. The test bed methodology, digital participation platforms, and formal mechanisms for co-creation create genuine opportunities for residents and stakeholders to shape initiatives. This participatory approach builds social capital, enhances legitimacy, and produces more socially appropriate solutions than top-down approaches.

**Quality of Life Framework**

Centering on citizen well-being and quality of life rather than purely technical or economic metrics represents important philosophical strength. Helsingborg's established Quality of Life Programme provides systematic framework for measuring and prioritizing outcomes that matter to residents—health, safety, social connections, environmental quality—rather than just energy efficiency or emissions reductions.

**International Integration**

H22's deliberate positioning within international networks (EU missions, ICLEI, OECD, MIT partnerships, UN processes) provides access to knowledge, resources, and legitimacy while positioning Helsingborg as contributor to global sustainability efforts. This international engagement amplifies impact beyond the city itself and creates opportunities for learning from peer cities.

**Adaptive Management**

H22 demonstrates willingness to experiment, learn from results, and adjust approaches—recognizing that innovation inherently involves some failures and that learning from experience is essential. This adaptive capacity contrasts with rigid planning approaches and enables more effective responses to implementation challenges.

**Demonstrated Results**

H22 has produced tangible outcomes—environmental improvements, infrastructure innovations, institutional changes, international recognition—that validate the approach and build momentum for continued implementation. The four-time "Most Environmentally Friendly Municipality" award, EU Mission selection, and European Capital of Innovation finalist status provide external validation.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

Despite significant strengths, H22 faces several limitations and weaknesses worthy of critical assessment:

**Contextual Specificity**

H22's success reflects particular Swedish and Scandinavian contexts—strong municipal autonomy, social democratic governance traditions, high tax capacity, and established innovation culture—that are not universally present. The approach may be difficult to replicate in contexts with weaker municipal governance, more hierarchical decision-making traditions, or lower fiscal capacity. This contextual specificity limits the universalizability of H22 as a model for urban development globally.

**Incomplete Documentation of Social Outcomes**

While H22 emphasizes quality of life and citizen well-being, comprehensive documentation of social outcomes is limited in available sources. Specific evidence regarding displacement, gentrification, digital divide impacts, and distributional effects across different social groups is not extensively detailed. This documentation gap makes it difficult to assess whether H22 has genuinely achieved equitable outcomes or whether benefits have concentrated among more affluent, digitally-connected populations.

**Technology-Centric Bias**

Despite rhetoric emphasizing that "technology is enabler, not solution," H22 allocates substantial resources to technological systems (IoT sensors, smart grids, AI algorithms). The emphasis on technological innovation may overshadow equally important social, governance, and design innovations. There is risk that technological systems become ends in themselves rather than genuine means to improving quality of life.

**Scalability Questions**

While H22 has been positioned as model for other cities, questions remain about scalability. The approach requires significant municipal capacity, sustained political commitment, and financial resources that may not be available in less affluent or less governance-capable municipalities. The extent to which H22 approaches can be successfully adapted to different urban contexts, governance systems, and economic conditions remains uncertain.

**Data Privacy and Surveillance Concerns**

H22's reliance on ubiquitous sensor networks and data collection raises data privacy and surveillance concerns not fully addressed in available documentation. The balance between data access enabling citizen engagement and participation, and privacy protection from surveillance, is not clearly articulated. Potential for misuse of collected data or mission creep in surveillance applications is not thoroughly addressed.

**Equity and Access Challenges**

While H22 emphasizes inclusive participation, digital participation platforms and IoT-dependent services may exclude less digitally-connected populations, lower-income residents with limited technology access, and elderly populations less comfortable with digital systems. The extent to which H22 has genuinely addressed digital divide and ensured equitable access to benefits is unclear from available documentation.

**Limited Evaluation of Behavioral Change**

While H22 tracks technical metrics (energy consumption, emissions) and institutional outputs (partnerships, test beds), comprehensive evaluation of behavioral change and actual citizen adoption of new practices is limited. The extent to which residents have genuinely changed behaviors and engaged with innovations versus merely being exposed to them is not clearly documented.

**Dependence on Continued Political Support**

Despite demonstrated success, H22 remains dependent on sustained political support across election cycles. Changes in municipal leadership, shifts in political priorities, or budget pressures could threaten continued implementation. The initiative's long-term viability beyond current political cycle is not guaranteed.

**Potential for Greenwashing**

There is risk that H22, despite genuine innovations, becomes primarily a branding and marketing exercise—"greenwashing" that creates appearance of sustainability without fundamental transformation. The significant international visibility and awards could create incentive to emphasize positive narratives while downplaying challenges and negative outcomes.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

H22's approach to equity and justice merits careful assessment:

**Inclusive Design and Participation**

H22's commitment to participatory governance and citizen engagement represents significant step toward democratic and just urban development. Creating platforms for residents to shape their city and contribute ideas recognizes their agency and values their knowledge. This contrasts with top-down development approaches that impose solutions without community input.

**Quality of Life Focus**

Centering on citizen well-being rather than purely economic or technical metrics reflects justice-oriented values. H22's commitment to environmental improvements—air quality, water quality, green space, climate resilience—has positive implications for environmental justice. However, without deliberate anti-displacement strategies, environmental improvements could attract investment and increase property values, displacing lower-income residents who have historically borne disproportionate environmental burdens. Whether H22 has genuinely advanced environmental justice or merely shifted burdens requires further assessment.

**Digital Equity**

The reliance on digital platforms and IoT systems raises digital equity concerns. If participation and benefits are concentrated among digitally-connected populations, H22 could reinforce existing inequalities. Whether deliberate strategies have been implemented to ensure digital access and bridge digital divides is not clearly documented.

**Economic Opportunity**

H22's emphasis on innovation and new economic activity potentially creates opportunities for diverse populations to participate in green economy. However, whether these opportunities are accessible to lower-income residents, less educated populations, and historically excluded groups depends on deliberate inclusive strategies. The extent to which H22 has advanced or hindered economic inclusion is unclear.

**Intergenerational Justice**

H22's commitment to climate neutrality and environmental sustainability reflects responsibility to future generations. By addressing climate change and building more sustainable urban systems, H22 advances intergenerational justice. However, genuine sustainability requires not just environmental improvements but also social and economic sustainability for diverse populations.

### **CONCLUSION**

H22 represents an ambitious, comprehensive approach to urban transformation addressing interconnected challenges of twenty-first century cities through integrated innovation, collaboration, digitalization, and sustainability commitment. By positioning the entire municipality as a test bed for experimentation and creating genuine platforms for stakeholder engagement, H22 has demonstrated capacity to produce tangible environmental, social, economic, and institutional outcomes while maintaining focus on citizen quality of life.

The initiative's strength lies in its comprehensive integration, long-term commitment, genuine stakeholder engagement, international positioning, and adaptive management. These elements have enabled H22 to move beyond isolated projects to catalyze systemic urban transformation with demonstrated results including environmental improvements, institutional innovations, international recognition, and community engagement.

However, H22's success reflects particular Swedish and Scandinavian contexts that may not be universally replicable. Questions remain regarding equity outcomes, digital access, scalability, and long-term political sustainability. Genuine assessment requires moving beyond promotional narratives to critically evaluate distributional outcomes and potential negative consequences.

For cities and practitioners considering similar initiatives, H22 offers valuable lessons regarding the importance of long-term commitment, multi-stakeholder collaboration, quality of life focus, adaptive management, and international integration. However, successful adaptation requires careful attention to local contexts, deliberate equity strategies, and realistic assessment of resource requirements and political sustainability.

As H22 continues implementation toward its 2030 climate neutrality goal and 2035 vision, it will provide ongoing learning regarding the possibilities and challenges of transforming mid-sized European cities into models of sustainable, inclusive, innovative urban development.
