# Executive summary## 10-Minute City Urban Planning Tool

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** 10-Minute City Urban Planning Tool
- **Alternative Names:** 10-Minute City, BXLGIS-10-Minute City, City of Proximity
- **Location:** Brussels, Belgium (City of Brussels, encompassing all 19 districts)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 50.8503° N, 4.3517° E
- **Timeline:** 
  - 2018: Conceptualization and planning phase
  - 2022: Roadmap and strategic planning published
  - 2023: Beta version of the interactive mapping tool launched
  - Ongoing: Continuous refinement, expansion, and integration with broader urban planning and digital twin initiatives
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Area: Entire City of Brussels (32.61 km²)
  - Population Affected: ~190,000 residents
  - Investment Value: Funded through municipal budget, EU projects (Forthcoming, T4R), and regional research grants (Innoviris Brussels)
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - City of Brussels (Urban Department, Smart City Unit)
  - Multidisciplinary academic consortium (Belgian universities)
  - External consultants (for Local Digital Twin and platform development)
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - Municipal budget
  - EU DUT Partnership and Innoviris Brussels (research and innovation grants)
  - T4R (Transnational 4R) project (EU-funded)

#### **Executive Summary**
The 10-Minute City Urban Planning Tool is a pioneering digital initiative by the City of Brussels aimed at transforming the urban landscape into a more accessible, equitable, and sustainable environment. The project leverages GIS-based interactive mapping and real-time data visualization to ensure that all residents can access essential services, green spaces, and mobility options within a 10-minute walk or bike ride. By integrating over 70 local facilities and services, the tool provides actionable insights for urban planners, policymakers, and citizens, guiding strategic interventions and monitoring progress towards the city’s sustainable development goals. The initiative is a core element of the Communal Sustainable Development Plan Bxl2050, promoting polycentric development, reducing car dependency, and enhancing quality of life. The tool’s development is supported by a multidisciplinary academic consortium and external consultants, ensuring a comprehensive and evidence-based approach. The project’s success is measured by improved service and mobility access, greater equity in urban planning, and actionable insights for decision-makers, making it a model for other cities seeking to implement similar green building neighborhood (GBN) strategies.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Brussels is the capital of Belgium and a major European city, with a population of nearly 190,000. It is a hub for international institutions, including the European Union and NATO. The city has a diverse economic base, with significant sectors in services, finance, and government. Brussels is characterized by a mix of historic and modern architecture, high-rise buildings, and a dense urban fabric.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** 
  - **Historical Development Patterns:** Brussels has a long history of urban development, with a mix of medieval, 19th-century, and 20th-century architecture. The city has experienced significant urban sprawl and densification, particularly in the post-war period.
  - **Current Land Use Composition:** The city is highly urbanized, with a mix of residential, commercial, and institutional land uses. There are also significant green spaces, including parks and gardens.
  - **Demographic Profile:** Brussels is a multicultural city with a diverse population in terms of income levels, age distribution, and ethnic/cultural composition. The city has a relatively high proportion of young professionals and families.
  - **Existing Infrastructure Quality and Accessibility:** The city has a well-developed public transportation network, including metro, tram, and bus services. However, there are ongoing challenges with traffic congestion and air quality.
  - **Environmental Conditions and Constraints:** Brussels faces environmental challenges such as the urban heat island effect, air pollution, and the need for more green spaces. The city is also vulnerable to the impacts of climate change, including increased temperatures and extreme weather events.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** The initiative is guided by the principles of the 10-Minute City and the 15-Minute City, emphasizing proximity, accessibility, and impact assessment. The project is designed to be flexible and adaptable to the unique characteristics of each district.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** The project involves the reorganization of urban space to bring essential services and facilities closer to residents. This includes the creation of new green spaces, the repurposing of public spaces for pedestrians, and the improvement of mobility options.
- **Scope and Phasing:** The project is structured in phases, with the initial focus on developing the interactive mapping tool and integrating it with existing urban planning processes. Future phases will expand the tool’s capabilities and integrate it with the Local Digital Twin.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:** 
  - GIS-based interactive mapping
  - Real-time accessibility heatmaps
  - Data analytics and visualization
  - Digital platforms for citizen engagement
- **Innovation Type:** 
  - Technological: Use of advanced GIS and data analytics
  - Social: Focus on community engagement and participatory planning
  - Governance: Integration of data-driven decision-making into urban planning
- **Integration Approach:** The technologies are integrated with existing urban systems, including public transportation, urban planning, and citizen engagement platforms.

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** The project is led by the City of Brussels, with support from external consultants and a multidisciplinary academic consortium. Implementers are selected through a competitive process, ensuring a high level of expertise and innovation.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** The project involves extensive stakeholder engagement, including residents, businesses, and academic institutions. The city uses a variety of channels and methods to consult with stakeholders and gather input.
- **Participatory Elements:** The project includes opportunities for community input and co-creation, such as public consultations, workshops, and online platforms for feedback.
- **Project Management:** The project is managed through a dedicated team within the City of Brussels, with clear roles and responsibilities. The team uses agile project management methodologies to ensure flexibility and responsiveness.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** The total investment is funded through the municipal budget, EU projects, and regional research grants. The exact budget breakdown is not publicly available, but the project is supported by a combination of public and private funding.
- **Funding Mix:** 
  - Municipal budget
  - EU DUT Partnership and Innoviris Brussels
  - T4R project
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** The project does not generate direct revenue but is designed to create long-term cost savings and economic benefits for the city.
- **Financial Sustainability:** The project is designed to be financially sustainable through ongoing public funding and the potential for future revenue generation from improved service delivery and economic activity.
