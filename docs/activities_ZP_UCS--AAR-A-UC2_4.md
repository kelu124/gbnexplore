# Executive summary## The Dome – Green Community House (Domen)

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** The Dome – Green Community House (Domen)
- **Alternative Names:** Dome of Visions Aarhus; Domen
- **Location:** Aarhus Harbor, Aarhus, Central Denmark Region, Denmark
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 56.1629° N, 10.2039° E (Aarhus Harbor)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Conceptualization: 2012  
  - Initial Construction: 2013 (Copenhagen)  
  - Relocation to Aarhus: 2017  
  - Operational: Ongoing  
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Structure: 450 m² dome; 10.5 meters tall  
  - Users: Hundreds per event; open to all city residents and visitors  
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Architect: Kristoffer Tejlgaard  
  - Project Partners: NCC (construction), Metsä Wood, City of Aarhus  
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Public (City of Aarhus)  
  - Corporate (NCC, Metsä Wood)  
  - Event and space rental revenues

#### **Executive Summary**

The Dome – Green Community House (Domen) in Aarhus is a pioneering urban project that blends experimental sustainable architecture with vibrant community engagement. Conceived as a living laboratory, the Dome serves as both a striking demonstration of low-impact construction and a dynamic local hub for cultural, educational, and social activities. The Dome’s design leverages modular construction and recycled materials—its iconic structure is built from laminated veneer lumber (Kerto® LVL) and polycarbonate, chosen for their sustainability and low environmental footprint[^1][^2]. With a flexible, greenhouse-like interior, the Dome accommodates diverse uses: public workshops, performances, urban gardening, and professional forums on green building. As a united city life house, it emphasizes co-creation, inviting citizens, professionals, and policymakers to experiment with and learn about sustainable urban living[^3][^4]. The project’s broader objective is to inspire the next generation of sustainable neighborhoods by making sustainable design visible, tangible, and participatory. The Dome’s impact extends beyond environmental gains, fostering social cohesion, local skill development, and awareness of green practices—positioning Aarhus as a model for urban sustainability and community-led innovation in Europe[^4][^5].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Aarhus, Denmark’s second-largest city (population ~340,000), is a regional cultural and economic center with a strong university presence and a dynamic knowledge economy. The city is known for progressive urban planning, environmental leadership, and a vibrant arts scene. Geographically, Aarhus sits on the east coast of Jutland, with a prominent harbor district undergoing regeneration[^5].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical Patterns:** Aarhus Harbor was historically industrial but is now transitioning to mixed-use developments.
  - **Land Use:** The area features a blend of cultural, residential, and public spaces, part of a wider urban renewal strategy.
  - **Demographics:** Diverse, including students, young professionals, families, and immigrants.
  - **Infrastructure:** Well-developed transit, cycling infrastructure, and public amenities.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Coastal, with a focus on climate adaptation and green space integration.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Urban disengagement in post-industrial harbor areas.
  - Limited public awareness of sustainable building.
  - Lack of accessible, hands-on learning spaces for green technologies.
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Aarhus’s focus on sustainability as European Capital of Culture 2017.
  - Ongoing citywide push for innovative, participatory urban projects.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government, local residents, architects, sustainability advocates, cultural organizations, construction sector partners[^3][^4].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Managed as a municipal initiative with private-sector partnership.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Aarhus’s sustainability action plans; national Danish targets for resource efficiency and green building.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive local zoning and city life house frameworks.
  - Some challenges in sourcing approved recycled materials within existing building codes[^4].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Rooted in sustainability, co-creation, and the circular economy. The design is modular, mobile, and visually transparent—making sustainable techniques visible and understandable to lay users[^3][^4].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - 450 m² dome structure, 10.5 m high.
  - Constructed from curved Kerto® LVL wood beams and polycarbonate panels with minimal steel.
  - Two-story internal wooden structure with conference, studio, terrace, and garden spaces[^2][^6][^1][^7].
  - Lush interior garden with olive and eucalyptus trees, grapevines, herbs, and flowers[^6][^7].
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initially constructed in Copenhagen (2013), relocated to Aarhus (2017).
  - Ongoing adaptation and programming, including events and urban gardening.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Modular timber grid (Kerto® LVL) for structure.
  - Polycarbonate “fish scale” facade for insulation and recyclability.
  - Natural and passive climate control strategies.
  - Mobile brick/clay/linseed oil stove and liquid-gas based heat pump.
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (materials, energy use).
  - Social (open, participatory programming).
  - Process (modular, mobile structure).
- **Integration Approach:**  
  Designed to plug into existing city infrastructure, with flexible use for events, learning, and community gatherings[^3][^6].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Led by architects and construction partners (NCC, Metsä Wood).
  - Partnership with City of Aarhus for site and operational support.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Extensive community involvement in programming and design adaptation.
  - Partnerships with cultural and educational organizations.
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Open events, workshops, urban gardening, and co-creation activities[^3][^4][^5].
- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by city and project partners with ongoing feedback loops for programming and maintenance.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not publicly disclosed in detail.
  - Major costs: structure fabrication, transport, site set-up, programming.
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - City investment, private sector material sponsorship, event revenues.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Space and event rentals, partnerships.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Low operating costs due to passive design; ongoing city and partner support.
