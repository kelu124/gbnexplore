# Executive summary## The Fifth Façade Project – Praça de Lisboa Olive Grove Green Roof

### I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION

#### Basic Information

- **Initiative Title:** The Fifth Façade Project – Praça de Lisboa Olive Grove Green Roof
- **Alternative Names:** Jardim das Oliveiras; Praça de Lisboa Green Roof; PQAP (Porto Quinta Fachada Project)
- **Location:** Praça de Lisboa, Porto, Portugal (Downtown, adjacent to Torre dos Clérigos)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 41.1469° N, 8.6146° W
- **Timeline:**
  - Pre-2007: Site was Mercado do Anjo, a derelict, unhygienic market space
  - 2007: Exclusive competition for urban revitalization launched
  - 2012–2013: Construction and public opening
  - November 2013: Jardim das Oliveiras completed and opened
  - 2016: Fifth Façade Project formally initiated
- **Project Scale:**
  - Area: 4,500 m² green roof (approx. 48,000 ft²)
  - Population affected: Hundreds daily; major tourist and local node
  - Investment value: Not publicly disclosed
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - Architectural Concept: Balonas & Menano
  - Architect: Pedro Balonas
  - Green Roof Consulting: NEOTURF (Paulo Palha)
  - Landscape Project: Pedro Balonas
  - Developer: Francisco Rocha Antunes – Capital Urbano
  - General Contractor and Construction Management: BragaParques
  - Public Partner: Porto City Council; ANCV (Portuguese National Association for Green Roofs)
- **Funding Sources:** Public (Porto municipality), private development (Capital Urbano), PPP elements (UrbaClérigos lease), potential national incentives for green roofs[^1][^2]

#### Executive Summary

The Fifth Façade Project at Praça de Lisboa is a pioneering urban renewal initiative in Porto, Portugal, that transformed a neglected city square into an ecological and social landmark through the creation of a vast green roof and an olive grove. Conceived as both an architectural and environmental intervention, the project sought to revitalize a historic urban space—formerly the crowded and unhygienic Mercado do Anjo—by integrating landscape architecture, commercial activity, and public amenities within a multilayered structure.

The design features 50 mature olive trees and extensive grass atop a contemporary building housing shops, cafés, and a semi-covered pedestrian passage, all constructed over an underground car park. The green roof was not an initial requirement, but emerged as a response to heritage context, aiming to restore the city’s arboreal legacy and invoke the ancient Porta do Olival gateway.

A multidisciplinary collaboration involving architects, landscape specialists, municipal departments, and green infrastructure advocates ensured technical integration with Porto’s historic center. The project faced challenges in marrying modern design with sensitive heritage and in demonstrating the value of green roofs to a skeptical public and regulatory bodies. Today, Praça de Lisboa stands as a celebrated case of sustainable urban retrofitting, offering measurable benefits in urban cooling, biodiversity, stormwater management, and enhanced public usability. Its success catalyzed policy momentum for green roofs in Portugal and won international recognition for its innovative methodology and impact[^1][^3].

### II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS

#### Urban Context

**City Profile:**  
Porto is Portugal’s second-largest city, known for its rich architectural heritage, dense urban fabric, and vibrant economic base centered around commerce, tourism, and education. With a population exceeding 230,000 (metro area ~1.7 million), Porto is a cultural and historic hub in the north of Portugal, characterized by hilly terrain and proximity to the Atlantic coast.

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
Praça de Lisboa is located in the heart of Porto’s downtown, adjacent to landmarks such as the Clérigos Tower and Livraria Lello. The area is defined by historic masonry buildings, narrow streets, and high pedestrian activity.  
- **Historical development:** Once home to the medieval Porta do Olival city gate, later the Mercado do Anjo (market), the square was a transitional but underutilized space[^4].  
- **Current land use:** Mixed-use (retail, leisure, public space, underground parking).  
- **Demographics:** Diverse users—city workers, students, tourists; moderate-to-high income levels in surrounding commercial zones[^5].  
- **Infrastructure quality:** High accessibility on foot, integrated with transit and major tourist routes.  
- **Environmental constraints:** Dense built environment, urban heat island effects, limited green space[^1].

#### Pre-Initiative Conditions

- **Problems/Challenges:**  
  - Derelict, crowded, and unhygienic market space
  - Underutilized and unsafe public area
  - Lack of green recreational space in city center
  - Negative perception and declining social use

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Municipal decision in 2007 to revitalize the square via design competition[^1]
  - Growing recognition of sustainability and heritage integration needs

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Municipal government (landlord)
  - Private developer (UrbaClérigos, Capital Urbano)
  - Local businesses and residents
  - Heritage preservation organizations
  - Green roof advocates (ANCV, NEOTURF)[^1][^4]

#### Policy and Regulatory Environment

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Porto City Council as principal authority, with delegated management to UrbaClérigos under a 50-year lease for commercial space[^4]

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Emerging national and municipal incentives for green roofs and sustainable buildings[^2]
  - Integration with the 2019 Portuguese government’s energy transition and smart city agenda (promotion of green roofs/facades)[^1]

- **Legal/Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Need for alignment with heritage protection regulations
  - Technical guidelines from ANCV for green roof design and maintenance
  - Initial resistance from heritage and public bodies, overcome through demonstration and dialogue[^1]

### III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES

#### Strategic Vision

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Revitalize Praça de Lisboa as a safe, attractive, and multifunctional public space
  - Restore ecological function through green roofing and olive grove
  - Integrate commercial, leisure, and environmental amenities

- **Vision Statement:**  
  Aspire to create a landmark urban oasis that harmonizes contemporary design with historic context, catalyzes sustainable urban tourism, and fosters community engagement.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Supports Porto’s urban sustainability and ecological corridor strategies
  - Responds to national energy efficiency and green infrastructure policies[^1]

#### Problem-Solution Logic

- **Theory of Change:**  
  Transforming a neglected, hardscape urban square into a green roof with diverse uses would:
  - Enhance microclimate and biodiversity
  - Retain stormwater
  - Increase public comfort and social use
  - Demonstrate sustainable retrofitting for historic areas

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - City residents, workers, students, and tourists
  - Small businesses and local entrepreneurs
  - Municipal government (improved asset value and reputation)

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Extensive green roof atop a multi-level commercial/public structure
  - Restoration of historic arboreal identity with olive trees
  - Integration of ecological design with commercial and social functions[^3]

#### Drivers and Motivations

- **Political:**  
  - Municipal drive for visible urban quality improvements
  - Policy momentum for green infrastructure

- **Economic:**  
  - Enhance tourism and retail attractiveness
  - Unlock value in underutilized urban land

- **Social:**  
  - Respond to community demand for safe, high-quality public space
  - Promote health and recreation

- **Environmental:**  
  - Mitigate urban heat, air pollution, and stormwater issues
  - Advance biodiversity and climate adaptation

- **Technological:**  
  - Availability of advanced green roof systems and landscape design expertise (NEOTURF, ANCV)[^1]

### IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH

#### Project Design

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Urban landscape architecture, pedestrian-oriented design, and heritage-sensitive contemporary architecture
  - "Fifth façade" concept: roof as an active, public-facing landscape

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Construction of a multi-level building with shops, cafés, and pedestrian passage (Passeio dos Clérigos)
  - 4,500 m² green roof planted with 50 olive trees and grass
  - Situated above an underground parking garage
  - Open pedestrian pathway linking major tourist sites[^1][^4][^3]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial demolition and site preparation
  - Phased construction of underground parking, commercial space, and green roof
  - Landscape installation and public opening in 2013

#### Technologies and Innovations

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Extensive green roof system (soil substrate, irrigation, drainage)
  - Olive tree planting as biocultural reference
  - Rainwater retention and passive cooling design
  - Integrated pedestrian passage with natural lighting[^1][^3]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: green roofing, stormwater systems
  - Social: participatory urban space, multifunctionality
  - Governance: PPP model, interdisciplinary cooperation

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Coordination with historic infrastructure and regulatory bodies
  - Seamless layering of commercial, public, and ecological functions

#### Implementation Methodology

- **Procurement/Contracting:**  
  - Public competition, selection of design team (Balonas & Menano)
  - Developer (Capital Urbano) and operator (UrbaClérigos) engaged under municipal lease[^4]

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Multidisciplinary technical teams
  - Dialogue with heritage organizations and public
  - Demonstrative approach to overcome initial resistance

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Public consultations and adaptive design revisions (e.g., open passage modification)

- **Project Management:**  
  - Construction management by BragaParques
  - Ongoing landscape and commercial space maintenance by UrbaClérigos

#### Financing Model

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Not publicly disclosed; cost estimates for comparable green roof projects suggest significant investment

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Blend of public, private, and PPP funding
  - Lease revenues from commercial tenants

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Retail and hospitality rents
  - Parking fees

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term lease and shared maintenance responsibilities
  - Increased commercial and tourism revenues as indirect returns

### V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES

#### Implementation Challenges

- **Technical:**  
  - Integration of green roof with historic urban fabric and existing underground infrastructure
  - Ensuring structural stability and effective drainage/irrigation

- **Financial:**  
  - Project delays linked to anchor tenant changes
  - Unknown construction cost overruns

- **Social/Political:**  
  - Initial skepticism from heritage bodies and public about green roof concept
  - Need to demonstrate public and cultural value

- **Administrative:**  
  - Coordination among city departments, private actors, and external consultants

- **Environmental:**  
  - Selection of climate-resilient plant species
  - Managing post-installation maintenance

#### Adaptive Strategies

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Use of demonstration and dialogue to build consensus
  - Revision of design to accommodate open pedestrian passage and optimize usability

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments to commercial layout, roof accessibility, and landscape design

- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of early and sustained stakeholder engagement
  - Value of adaptive management and willingness to iterate design[^1][^4]
