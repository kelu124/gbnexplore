# Executive summary## Oxygen Office Building – BAMB Pilot (Horizon 2020 Project)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Oxygen Office Building – BAMB Pilot
- **Alternative Names:** OXY Brussels; Oxygen Office Building; BAMB Pilot – Rue de Spa Office Building
- **Location:** European Quarter, Brussels, Belgium
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.8447° N, 4.3662° E (Rue de Spa, Brussels)
- **Timeline:**
  - Design phase: 2015–2016
  - Construction: 2016–2018
  - BAMB Pilot and EU demonstration: 2017–2019
  - Ongoing monitoring and reporting: 2019–present
- **Project Scale:**
  - 43,665 m² office space
  - 6,000 m² green gardens/terraces
  - Mixed-use functions (hotel, residential, retail, leisure)
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - Brussels Environment (Project Coordinator)
  - Conix RDBM Architects (Design/Build)
  - BAMB Consortium (16 partners from 8 EU countries)
  - Supported by EU Horizon 2020
- **Funding Sources:**
  - EU Horizon 2020 (Innovation Action Grant)
  - Brussels Region Government
  - Private investment from developers and partners

#### **Executive Summary**

The Oxygen Office Building, implemented as a pilot under the EU’s Horizon 2020 BAMB (Buildings As Material Banks) initiative, represents a pioneering approach to circular economy principles in the built environment. Designed and constructed to passive building standards, the project integrates advanced digital material passports and real-time environmental monitoring to track, manage, and optimize energy and material flows throughout the building’s lifecycle[^1][^2][^3]. Achieving BREEAM ‘Excellent’ certification, the Oxygen Office Building sets a benchmark in resource efficiency, waste minimization, and readiness for future circular models, aligning with both local and European sustainability objectives[^4][^5][^6]. 

The building’s design incorporates flexible, easily reconfigurable office floors, extensive green terraces, and integrated systems for daylight, ventilation, and climate control, all supported by digital platforms for material and energy management[^1][^5]. By leveraging real-time data and transparent reporting, the project not only reduces operational costs and environmental impacts but also enhances occupant well-being and positions itself as a scalable blueprint for next-generation green neighborhoods. The pilot faced and overcame significant challenges in integrating digital circularity tools into conventional building operations, offering valuable lessons in project management, stakeholder engagement, and adaptive design. Its success has influenced policy, practice, and market adoption of circular economy concepts across Brussels and the EU[^1][^3][^7].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**
  - Brussels, the capital of Belgium and de facto capital of the European Union, has a population of approximately 1.2 million. Its economy is dominated by government, EU institutions, services, and international business. The city is a leader in sustainability and circular economy initiatives, driven by progressive regional policies[^1].
  - Brussels is situated on the Senne River and features a mix of historic and modern urban districts.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - The Oxygen Office Building is located in the European Quarter, a dense, well-connected district hosting EU institutions, offices, retail, and residential spaces.
  - The area has undergone significant regeneration and now blends commercial, governmental, and leisure functions.
  - Demographics skew towards international professionals, with high income levels and cultural diversity.
  - Infrastructure is robust, with extensive public transport (metro, bus, tram), high-quality roads, and proximity to major cultural venues.
  - Environmental quality is a concern, with urban heat, traffic, and resource consumption needing mitigation[^1][^5].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - High energy consumption and resource waste in existing office buildings.
  - Lack of transparency in building material flows, hindering recycling and reuse.
  - Need for improved occupant well-being and flexibility in building use.
  - Regulatory push for climate adaptation and circular economy adoption[^1][^7].
- **Triggering Events:**
  - Adoption of EU Horizon 2020 sustainability targets.
  - Brussels Region’s policy shift towards exemplary buildings and circular economy pilots[^1].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Government (Brussels Environment, EU agencies)
  - Developers and architects (Conix RDBM, Binst Architects)
  - Consortium partners (universities, research institutes, private firms)
  - Building occupants and local communities

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**
  - Brussels Region environmental and building regulations.
  - EU Horizon 2020 framework for innovation and demonstration projects.
- **Relevant Policies:**
  - Brussels’ Exemplary Buildings program.
  - EU Circular Economy Action Plan.
  - BREEAM certification standards[^4][^8].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**
  - Supportive regulatory context for sustainability pilots.
  - Need for harmonization of digital passport standards and circularity frameworks[^1][^3][^7].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Demonstrate feasible circular building solutions via digital tracking and reversible design.
  - Achieve high energy efficiency and minimize waste.
  - Set new standards in transparent, data-driven building management[^1][^3].
- **Vision Statement:**
  - “To create buildings that function as banks of valuable materials, enabling circular flows and maximizing resource efficiency without compromising occupant well-being.”
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**
  - Directly supports Brussels’ urban sustainability and circular economy strategy.
  - Aligned with EU-wide goals for resource efficiency, emissions reduction, and urban resilience.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**
  - By implementing digital material passports and real-time monitoring, the project enables proactive management and reuse of resources, reducing waste and environmental impact.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**
  - Building occupants (improved indoor environment, lower costs)
  - Developers and investors (asset value, regulatory compliance)
  - Local community and environment (reduced emissions, improved urban quality)
- **Innovation Elements:**
  - Integration of digital material passports.
  - Passive design and flexible spatial configuration.
  - Real-time monitoring for energy and environmental performance[^1][^5].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** EU and regional policy mandates for circular economy and climate action.
- **Economic Drivers:** Demand for future-proof, high-performance office space; cost savings in operations.
- **Social Drivers:** Enhancing workplace health, flexibility, and community integration.
- **Environmental Drivers:** Reducing greenhouse gas emissions, improving resource management[^1][^5].
- **Technological Drivers:** Adoption of digital platforms, sensors, and data analytics.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**
  - Circular economy principles: design for disassembly, material reuse, and life-cycle analysis.
  - Passive building standards: maximize energy efficiency and occupant comfort.
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**
  - Flexible floor plates without central structural elements.
  - Extensive use of natural daylight, green terraces, and adaptable spaces[^1][^5].
  - Rainwater recuperation, solar panels, and advanced climate control[^1][^5].
- **Scope and Phasing:**
  - Initial feasibility study.
  - Design, construction, and commissioning.
  - Ongoing monitoring, reporting, and stakeholder engagement[^1][^2].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - **Digital material passports:** Track material flows and enable future reuse[^1][^3].
  - **Environmental sensors:** Real-time monitoring of energy use, air quality, and occupancy[^1][^5].
  - **Passive design elements:** High insulation, triple glazing, daylight, and climate control[^9].
  - **BREEAM and WELL certification systems:** Independent validation of sustainability performance[^4][^8].
  - **Rainwater harvesting, solar PV, green roofs:** Reduce resource consumption and boost resilience[^1][^5].
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digital passports, sensors), process-oriented (circular design, flexible use), governance (integrated consortium model).
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies were embedded via digital platforms, compatible with Building Information Modeling (BIM), and linked to operational management systems[^1][^3].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** Competitive selection of architects and contractors; consortium-based governance.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Workshops, field visits, and feedback loops with consortium, local government, and user groups[^1][^2].
- **Participatory Elements:** Stakeholder network and special interest groups; open reporting of pilot outcomes.
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by Brussels Environment with EU oversight; regular progress reviews and adaptive management[^1][^3].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** Not publicly disclosed in detail; significant EU funding plus regional and private co-finance.
- **Funding Mix:** Majority from EU (Horizon 2020), with regional government and private sector contributions.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Standard commercial office leasing, mixed-use income streams (hotel, retail).
- **Financial Sustainability:** Low operational costs due to energy efficiency; high asset value from certification and performance[^1][^9].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**
  - Integration of digital passports with legacy building management systems.
  - Ensuring data accuracy and interoperability across platforms[^1][^3][^7].
- **Financial Challenges:**
  - Upfront costs of technology and certification.
  - Uncertainty in market valuation for circular, digitally tracked assets.
- **Social and Political Challenges:**
  - Need for broad stakeholder buy-in; managing expectations across diverse user groups.
- **Administrative Challenges:**
  - Coordination among multiple partners; compliance with EU and local regulatory frameworks.
- **Environmental Challenges:**
  - Unexpected site constraints; balancing new construction with circular reuse targets.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**
  - Iterative design and prototyping; feedback loops with stakeholders.
  - Use of consortium expertise to resolve technical integration issues.
- **Design Modifications:**
  - Adjustments to digital passport protocols and sensor networks based on feasibility study outcomes.
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**
  - Importance of early stakeholder engagement.
  - Need for robust data management and transparent reporting[^1][^3][^7].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** Construction of a new passive-standard office building with flexible, daylight-optimized floors; green terraces and rainwater systems; digital infrastructure for material tracking[^1][^5][^9].
- **Service Delivery:** High-performance, healthy workspaces; real-time building management services.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** 28% reduction in energy consumption; BREEAM ‘Excellent’ certification; WELL Platinum certification targeted; 43,665 m² office space[^1][^4][^5].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Improved workplace health and productivity; accessible green spaces; enhanced local urban quality.
- **Equity Outcomes:** Benefits distributed primarily to office workers and tenants; potential for broader community engagement through public amenities.
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No significant displacement reported; project built on existing office site.
- **Social Capital:** Strengthened collaboration among EU, regional government, and private partners.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Symbolic leadership in sustainable design for Brussels and Europe[^1].

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Construction and management jobs; indirect employment through enhanced business activity.
- **Economic Activity:** Attraction of high-value tenants; increased investment in circular real estate.
- **Property Values:** Upgraded asset value due to certification and performance.
- **Cost Savings:** Reduced operational costs from energy and resource efficiency.
- **Economic Inclusion:** Limited direct impacts; model offers potential for broader adoption.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** Significant reduction in operational emissions; supports Brussels’ climate targets[^1].
- **Resource Management:** Real-time tracking and optimization of material and energy flows; rainwater harvesting and reuse.
- **Environmental Quality:** Improved indoor air quality, daylight, and green space provision.
- **Waste and Pollution:** Minimized construction and operational waste through circular design[^1][^3][^7].
- **Sustainability Metrics:** BREEAM ‘Excellent’ and WELL Platinum; CO2 Neutral Label for daily use[^4][^5].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** Enhanced government and sector expertise in circular building management.
- **Policy Influence:** Model for future policies and regulations in Brussels and EU-wide.
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Digital passports and real-time data support improved governance.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Achieved energy and emissions reduction targets; successful demonstration of circular economy concepts.
- **Unintended Consequences:** Raised expectations for future projects; highlighted challenges in digital integration.
- **Long-term Viability:** High; ongoing monitoring and clear policy alignment ensure sustainability[^1][^3].

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Pioneering integration of circular economy and digital material management.
- High performance in energy and sustainability metrics.
- Robust stakeholder engagement and knowledge sharing.

- Improved workplace health and accessibility, but benefits primarily accrue to office users and tenants.
- No significant displacement or negative community impact reported.

#### **Future Outlook**

- Potential for policy scaling and adoption across Brussels and EU.
- Next phases may include expanded participatory design and community engagement, as well as integration with citywide smart systems[^1][^3][^7].
