# Executive summary

## Dublin Green Building Neighbourhood (PROBONO Project)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Dublin Green Building Neighbourhood (Dublin Living Lab, PROBONO Project)
- **Alternative Names:** Dublin Living Lab, PROBONO Dublin Demonstrator, PROBONO GBN Dublin
- **Location:** Dún Laoghaire, Dublin, Ireland
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.293°N, 6.137°W (Dún Laoghaire town center)
- **Timeline:** Project launched 2022, major demonstration phase 2023–2026; ongoing as of late 2025
- **Project Scale:** District-level (key municipal buildings and surrounding town centre; population impact: several thousand; project part of 6-city EU demonstration covering millions overall)
- **Leading Organization(s):**
  - Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown County Council (dlr)
  - University College Dublin (UCD)
  - European PROBONO Consortium (47 partners EU-wide)
- **Funding Sources:**
  - European Union Horizon 2020 (H2020) Programme
  - Local authority co-funding
  - Private sector and research partners

#### **Executive Summary**

The Dublin Green Building Neighbourhood, as one of the flagship Living Labs of the EU-funded PROBONO Project, is transforming a core district of Dún Laoghaire into a model for sustainable, zero-carbon urban regeneration. By networking key municipal buildings—such as County Hall, the ferry terminal, social housing, and the Lexicon library—the initiative pioneers the integration of smart energy management, digital twins, and green mobility at the neighbourhood scale. The project’s central objective is to demonstrate practical pathways for achieving net-zero and ultimately positive-energy districts, focusing on deep retrofits of existing, energy-inefficient public infrastructure and the creation of a replicable framework for other European cities[^1][^2][^3].

Through a participatory approach, the Living Lab engages citizens, public officials, researchers, and businesses in the co-design and deployment of innovative solutions. These include real-time energy monitoring, advanced insulation, district heating/cooling, renewable generation (such as photovoltaics), and integration with electric mobility. The Dublin Living Lab also serves as a testbed for digital models that enable immediate feedback on design and operational changes, supporting evidence-based decision-making for urban sustainability.

The project’s anticipated impacts extend beyond technical performance: it is designed to improve residents’ well-being, reduce energy costs, enhance air quality, and foster social and economic resilience. By addressing challenges of legacy infrastructure and inter-agency collaboration, and by generating policy recommendations, the Dublin GBN seeks not only to decarbonize a district but to catalyze a people-centred, climate-positive urban future for Ireland and beyond[^1][^2][^3][^4].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Dublin, the capital and largest city of Ireland, is a major European urban center with a population exceeding 1.2 million in the metropolitan area. The city is an economic hub for technology, services, tourism, and innovation, and faces challenges including housing shortages, aging infrastructure, and rising sustainability demands.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**
  - *Historical Development:* Dún Laoghaire is a historic port town, now a vibrant urban district within Greater Dublin, with a mix of Victorian and modern architecture, commercial activity, and public amenities.
  - *Land Use Composition:* The Living Lab area includes municipal offices, cultural venues, residential blocks, retail, and transport infrastructure clustered around the town center and harbour.
  - *Demographics:* Mixed-income community, diverse age groups, significant commuter and tourist flows.
  - *Infrastructure Quality:* Many city-owned buildings are over 40 years old, with legacy energy systems and limited digitalization. Public transport and cycling infrastructure present but underutilized.
  - *Environmental Conditions:* Coastal climate, moderate air pollution from urban traffic, some vulnerability to climate impacts (sea level rise, storms).

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - High energy consumption and inefficiency in legacy municipal buildings[^2][^3].
  - Fragmented management of energy and mobility systems.
  - Limited integration of renewable energy and digital monitoring.
  - Need for scalable models to meet Ireland’s 2030 climate and energy targets.
- **Triggering Events:**
  - EU climate policy acceleration post-2020.
  - Local authority commitments to sustainability and innovation.
  - Availability of Horizon 2020 funding for demonstrator projects.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - Local government (dlr, city planners)
  - Academia (UCD)
  - Residents and business owners
  - Technology providers
  - EU project partners and policymakers

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown County Council leads, with oversight from national agencies; project aligned with EU and national climate/energy frameworks.
- **Relevant Policies:** EU Green Deal, Ireland’s Climate Action Plan, local development and zoning plans.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** EU procurement rules, building codes, data protection regulations (GDPR), institutional inertia in retrofitting public assets.

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Achieve net-zero and positive-energy building performance at district level.
  - Demonstrate scalable, replicable models for other European cities.
  - Enhance well-being, economic vitality, and environmental quality[^1][^2][^3].
- **Vision Statement:** To create a people-focused, digitally-enabled, climate-positive urban neighbourhood that models the future of sustainable city living.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Integral to Ireland’s and the EU’s climate adaptation, energy transition, and urban innovation strategies.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** By integrating advanced retrofitting, smart energy and mobility systems, and participatory governance, the project will transform an inefficient municipal district into a showcase for zero-carbon urban regeneration, catalyzing broader adoption[^1][^2][^4].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Residents, public sector employees, local businesses, and wider city population.
- **Innovation Elements:** Digital twins, real-time energy analytics, collaborative planning platforms, neighbourhood-scale microgrids, and participatory co-design.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** EU and national climate commitments; local government innovation agenda.
- **Economic Drivers:** Cost savings from energy efficiency, job creation, investment attraction.
- **Social Drivers:** Health and quality of life improvements, community empowerment.
- **Environmental Drivers:** Carbon reduction targets, air quality improvement, climate resilience.
- **Technological Drivers:** Advances in IoT, digital modelling, renewable energy, and smart mobility.

---

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Based on Green Building Neighbourhood (GBN) principles—district-scale integration of energy, mobility, and digital systems, with a strong emphasis on stakeholder engagement and co-creation[^1][^2][^3][^5].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Retrofit and network of municipal and public buildings; integration with sustainable transport nodes; creation of energy-sharing microgrids.
- **Scope and Phasing:**
  - Phase 1: Audit, digital twin development, initial retrofits.
  - Phase 2: Full deployment of smart energy and mobility systems, resident engagement.
  - Phase 3: Monitoring, policy feedback, dissemination.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Smart energy management (IoT sensors, real-time data platforms)
  - Building digital twins for simulation and feedback[^6][^5]
  - District heating/cooling, advanced insulation, green/cool roofs
  - Renewable energy (PV, geothermal, micro-turbines)
  - Electric vehicle (EV) charging, green mobility integration
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digital twins, smart grids), social (participatory planning), governance (multi-stakeholder models), process (accelerated retrofitting).
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies layered onto existing infrastructure, with digital platforms enabling coordination and feedback across systems and stakeholders[^6][^5].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** EU open tendering; selection of tech providers and construction partners.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Workshops, public consultations, participatory design sessions, digital platforms for feedback[^1][^2].
- **Participatory Elements:** Citizens involved in co-design; regular reporting and knowledge exchange (e.g., General Assembly events)[^3].
- **Project Management:** Consortium model with local lead (dlr), academic partner (UCD), and EU coordination.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** Project part of €20M+ multi-city H2020 PROBONO budget; Dublin component estimated €3–5M.
- **Funding Mix:** Majority EU funding, with co-funding from local government and in-kind contributions from partners.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Potential for future savings re-investment, value capture from increased efficiency.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Focus on cost-effective retrofits and operational savings to ensure long-term viability.

---

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:** Integrating digital systems with aging infrastructure, ensuring interoperability among diverse technologies, data privacy/security[^3][^5].
- **Financial Challenges:** Upfront capital costs, balancing innovation with fiscal discipline.
- **Social and Political Challenges:** Ensuring meaningful public engagement, managing expectations, avoiding displacement.
- **Administrative Challenges:** Coordination across agencies, procurement complexity.
- **Environmental Challenges:** Retrofitting in a coastal, climate-vulnerable area.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Phased pilots, rigorous technology testing, iterative feedback loops.
- **Design Modifications:** Adjusting digital twin models to reflect real-world constraints; prioritizing buildings with highest retrofit ROI[^6][^3].
- **Lessons Learned:** Importance of early and sustained stakeholder engagement, value of open data for transparency.

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:** Retrofitted municipal buildings, district-level energy management infrastructure, new or upgraded EV charging stations.
- **Service Delivery:** Enhanced energy services, improved building comfort, access to digital tools for residents and managers.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** Targeted reductions in energy use and emissions (proportional to building stock retrofitted), area served: several hectares; population directly impacted: thousands[^1][^2].

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:** Improved indoor air quality, thermal comfort, and well-being[^1][^3].
- **Equity Outcomes:** Focus on public buildings and social housing, aiming for broad and inclusive benefit.
- **Displacement and Gentrification:** No reported negative impacts; public ownership limits displacement risk.
- **Social Capital:** Strengthened local networks via participatory planning.
- **Cultural Impacts:** Preservation and enhancement of historic buildings, integration of sustainability with heritage.

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:** Temporary construction/retrofit jobs; potential for permanent positions in building management and technology support.
- **Economic Activity:** Increased activity in local green retrofit and technology sectors.
- **Property Values:** Anticipated uplift in area attractiveness, though data still emerging.
- **Cost Savings:** Reduced energy and operational costs for the municipality and residents[^1][^3].
- **Economic Inclusion:** Focused on public assets, aiming to avoid exclusionary effects.

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:** Significant reductions in district carbon emissions; progress toward zero emissions for participating buildings[^1][^2][^3].
- **Resource Management:** Efficient use of energy and water; integration of renewables and storage.
- **Environmental Quality:** Improved air quality through reduced fossil fuel use and green mobility.
- **Waste and Pollution:** Emphasis on circularity and reduced construction/operational waste.
- **Sustainability Metrics:** Alignment with EU and Ireland’s net-zero targets; on track to exceed nearly-zero energy building (nZEB) standards[^1][^2][^4].

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:** Enhanced skills in digital planning and management; formation of new collaborative models.
- **Policy Influence:** Project serves as a testbed for national and EU policy recommendations[^6][^5].
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Use of digital twins and real-time data enhances governance quality and public trust.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:** Early results indicate strong progress on energy, emissions, and engagement targets; full results pending completion of monitoring phase.
- **Unintended Consequences:** None reported to date; close monitoring for social impacts ongoing.
- **Long-term Viability:** Designed for replication and operational self-sufficiency post-H2020 funding.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Integrates technical, social, and governance innovation at meaningful urban scale.
- Strong focus on people-centred design and participatory processes[^1][^3].
- Demonstrates practical pathways to net-zero/positive-energy neighbourhoods, not just individual buildings.
- Builds institutional capacity and policy learning for broader impact.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Complexity of integrating digital systems with legacy infrastructure can delay implementation[^3].
- High upfront costs and need for sustained political and financial support.
- Early phase: full social and economic impacts still being measured.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Designed to be broadly inclusive, with focus on public and social assets.
- Participatory methods aim to avoid exclusion or gentrification.
- Ongoing attention needed to ensure benefits reach vulnerable groups and avoid unintended displacement.

#### **Future Outlook**

- Next phases include scaling up retrofits, expanding to residential and commercial sectors, and informing citywide and national policy.
- Ongoing challenges: ensuring long-term funding, mainstreaming digital tools, and maintaining inclusive engagement.
- The Dublin Living Lab is positioned to become a template for climate-positive neighbourhoods across Ireland and the EU, contingent on rigorous monitoring and adaptive management as conditions evolve[^1][^6][^3][^4].
