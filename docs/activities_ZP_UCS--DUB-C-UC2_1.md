# Executive summary## People-Counting Sensors: Enhancing Building Efficiency with GDPR Compliance at TU Dublin

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** People-Counting Sensors: Enhancing Building Efficiency with GDPR Compliance at TU Dublin
- **Alternative Names:** TSUC People-Counting Sensors Pilot; Greenway Hub Occupancy Monitoring; TSUC Smart Sensors Initiative
- **Location:** Grangegorman, Dublin, Ireland; TU Dublin Greenway Hub
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 53.3564° N, 6.2786° W (Greenway Hub, Grangegorman Campus)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Project Launch: January 2024 (TSUC initiative)[^1]
  - Pilot Deployment: 2024 (ongoing)[^2]
  - Key Milestones: Sensor installation (Q2 2024), Data protection impact assessment (Q2 2024), User consultation sessions (mid-2024)[^3]
  - Status: Ongoing, with intention to scale and inform future campus-wide rollouts[^4][^1]
- **Project Scale:** 
  - Initial pilot in the Greenway Hub (mid-sized academic/research building, several hundred regular occupants)
  - Potential scaling to three buildings and ultimately campus-wide[^2][^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Technological University Dublin (TU Dublin Sustainability Intelligence Team, Campus Estates, Data Protection Office)
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - Higher Education Authority (HEA) funding under the Towards a Sustainable University Campus (TSUC) program[^2][^1]
  - Internal TU Dublin resources

#### **Executive Summary**
The People-Counting Sensors project at TU Dublin’s Greenway Hub is a flagship pilot within the university’s broader *Towards a Sustainable University Campus (TSUC)* initiative. Aimed at optimizing space usage, reducing energy consumption, and enhancing occupant well-being, the project leverages advanced people-counting sensors (FootfallCam™) and security camera data stream analysis to collect real-time, anonymized data on building occupancy. This data is integrated with the building management system, room booking platform, and academic scheduling tools to enable dynamic adjustments in energy use, space allocation, and comfort settings.

A defining feature of the initiative is its rigorous adherence to GDPR and data privacy best practices: only anonymized, aggregate data is processed, with no personal identifiers captured or stored. A comprehensive Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) was voluntarily conducted, and data protection policies reviewed in detail. Engagement with TU Dublin’s Data Protection Officer and clear communication with building users ensure transparency and trust.

The project’s outcomes include measurable reductions in energy consumption, increased space utilization efficiency, improved comfort for building users, and a replicable, scalable model for digital, data-driven sustainability interventions in higher education. Beyond technical achievements, the initiative transforms the Greenway Hub into a “living lab,” empowering students, staff, and visitors to engage with real-time sustainability data and encouraging behavioral change. The pilot’s lessons are positioned to inform similar efforts across TU Dublin and potentially other European institutions pursuing decarbonization and smart campus goals[^4][^2][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Dublin is Ireland’s capital and largest city, with a population of over 1.2 million in the metropolitan area. As a major economic, research, and education hub, Dublin balances a historic urban core with rapidly growing technology, health, and creative sectors. The city faces challenges such as housing shortages, traffic congestion, and ambitious climate action targets.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** Grangegorman, located north of Dublin’s historic center, has undergone significant regeneration in recent years, emerging as a campus and innovation district centered on TU Dublin. The area blends heritage buildings with new academic, research, and residential facilities.
  - **Historical Development:** Former site of a psychiatric hospital; major redevelopment began in the 2010s.
  - **Land Use Composition:** Mixed-use—university, research, residential, and community facilities.
  - **Demographic Profile:** Diverse, with a substantial student and academic presence, alongside local residents.
  - **Infrastructure Quality:** Newly built and renovated buildings, modern utilities, high accessibility via light rail and bus.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban site with limited green space; strong emphasis on sustainable redevelopment.

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Inefficient energy and space use due to lack of real-time occupancy data.
  - Manual, error-prone monitoring of building usage and energy consumption.
  - Fragmented data systems (BMS, meters, booking platforms not integrated)[^1].
  - Limited capacity for dynamic adjustment to actual usage patterns.
- **Triggering Events:**
  - National and sectoral decarbonization commitments (Public Sector Climate Action Mandate, TU Dublin Climate Action Roadmap).
  - Availability of digital and IoT solutions for smart campus management.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**
  - TU Dublin’s Sustainability Intelligence Team (project lead)
  - Estates/facilities management
  - Data Protection Office
  - Academic staff, students, and researchers (building occupants)
  - Technology suppliers (FootfallCam™)
  - National funders (HEA)

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** Led by TU Dublin, with oversight from internal sustainability, IT, and estates teams. Data protection governed by Irish and EU law (GDPR).
- **Relevant Policies:** 
  - TU Dublin Climate Action Roadmap
  - Public Sector Climate Action Mandate
  - University policies for sustainability, privacy, and IT management

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:** Smart campus/living lab paradigm; data-driven and user-centric building management; ethical technology adoption
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Sensors installed at key entry/exit points of the Greenway Hub; digital infrastructure for data aggregation and visualization
- **Scope and Phasing:** 
  - Phase 1: Greenway Hub pilot
  - Phase 2: Planned expansion to additional campus buildings
  - Phase 3: Potential campus-wide rollout and external dissemination[^2][^1]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - People-counting sensors (FootfallCam™): IR and vision-based, but with all non-essential features (e.g., MAC tracking, demographics, video recording) disabled for privacy[^2].
  - Security camera data stream analysis (only for aggregate counts, no imagery stored)
  - Integration with Building Management System (BMS), Facility Management, Room Booking, Academic Scheduling
  - Encrypted data transfer and secure storage (on-site/cloud within UK/EU)
  - User-facing dashboards for real-time feedback[^4]
- **Innovation Type:** Primarily technological (smart sensors, IoT, analytics), with social (living lab, user engagement) and governance (GDPR compliance, ethical data management) dimensions
- **Integration Approach:** Sensors feed anonymized data into existing and new digital platforms for energy, space, and facility management

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:** Technology suppliers selected for GDPR alignment; internal teams lead integration and oversight
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** 
  - Consultation sessions and Q&A with building users[^3]
  - User notifications via signage and digital communications
  - Engagement with Data Protection Officer throughout
- **Participatory Elements:** Living lab model encourages feedback, dashboard access, and behavioral change incentives[^2]
- **Project Management:** Led by the Sustainability Intelligence Team, coordinated with Estates, IT, and legal/data protection offices

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:** Not publicly disclosed, but supported by HEA funding and internal TU Dublin resources[^1]
- **Funding Mix:** Primarily public (HEA), with in-kind contributions from TU Dublin
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** Not applicable (pilot phase); cost savings expected through efficiency
- **Financial Sustainability:** Long-term value through reduced energy spend and scalable digital infrastructure[^1]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:** Ensuring robust, accurate occupancy data; integrating new sensors with legacy BMS and IT systems
- **Financial:** Managing costs within public sector budgets; justifying investment through savings
- **Social and Political:** Addressing privacy concerns; building trust among users wary of surveillance
- **Administrative:** Navigating GDPR requirements and institutional data policies; coordinating across multiple departments
- **Environmental:** No major negative impacts reported given non-intrusive installation

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**
  - Voluntary and thorough Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)[^2]
  - Disabling all non-essential sensor functions (e.g., demographic analysis, video storage)
  - Ongoing consultation and transparent communication with users
- **Design Modifications:** Initial scope limited to basic people counting; additional features only considered if privacy can be assured
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:** Early and proactive engagement with data protection authorities and users is critical; technical integration with legacy systems requires careful planning
