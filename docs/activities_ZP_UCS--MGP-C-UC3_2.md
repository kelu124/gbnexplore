# Executive summary## Smarter Together – Smart Mobility Solutions

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Smarter Together – Smart Mobility Solutions
- **Alternative Names:** Smarter Together Munich, Munich Smart Mobility Hubs
- **Location:** Munich, Neuaubing-Westkreuz and Freiham districts, Bavaria, Germany
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 48.1432° N, 11.4322° E (Neuaubing-Westkreuz)
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project start: 2015  
  - Mobility hub implementation: July 2018 (4 hubs), Dec 2018/Jan 2019 (4 additional hubs)  
  - EU funding period: 2015–2021  
  - Ongoing expansion: 2024–2027 (charging infrastructure tender)[^1][^2][^3]
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Area: Western edge of Munich, including urban redevelopment (Neuaubing-Westkreuz) and new development (Freiham)  
  - Population affected: ~30,000 residents, up to 20,000 new residents and 7,500 new jobs projected in Freiham[^2][^3]
  - Investment value: ~€20 million (total for district development, €6.85 million EU funding)[^2][^3]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - City of Munich (mobility department, planning office)  
  - Partners: MVG (public transport), SWM (utilities), STATTAUTO (car sharing), academic institutions, private operators[^4][^2][^3]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union (Horizon 2020, Smart Cities and Communities program)  
  - City of Munich  
  - Private sector partners  
  - Research institutions[^4][^2][^3]

#### **Executive Summary**

Smarter Together – Smart Mobility Solutions in Munich is a flagship initiative under the EU's Smart Cities and Communities program, aiming to pioneer **integrated smart mobility and energy solutions** within the Neuaubing-Westkreuz and Freiham districts. The project’s core ambition is to **optimize urban energy and mobility systems** by deploying **IoT-enabled mobility hubs**, EV fleet energy monitoring, charging infrastructure management, and citizen engagement tools. Eight public mobility hubs were established, each offering a mix of e-car sharing, bike sharing, cargo e-trikes, charging stations, and digital information services, with a focus on interoperability and renewable energy integration[^4][^2][^3].

Smarter Together leverages a collaborative urban living lab, where **residents, experts, and businesses co-design solutions** for energy efficiency, low-carbon mobility, and community empowerment. Innovations include real-time energy feedback, optimized charging schedules to reduce grid stress, and digital platforms for smart planning and user engagement. The initiative targets substantial reductions in CO₂ emissions, energy consumption, and air pollution, aiming for **carbon neutrality in the district by 2050**. Key challenges addressed include data privacy, device interoperability, and stakeholder engagement. Backed by approximately €20 million in funding and EU support, Smarter Together Munich is regarded as a blueprint for scalable, transferable smart city solutions, with its impacts now being replicated in other European cities[^4][^2][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Munich is Germany’s third-largest city, a major economic hub known for high living standards, strong innovation sectors, and significant population growth. The city’s western districts, including Neuaubing-Westkreuz and Freiham, are characterized by mixed residential and industrial land uses, rapid urban development, and a diverse demographic profile. Freiham is a flagship area for low-carbon urban expansion, set to accommodate 20,000 new residents and 7,500 jobs[^3].

- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical development:** Neuaubing-Westkreuz is a mature urban area with post-war housing and aging infrastructure, while Freiham represents a new, sustainable urban extension.  
  - **Land use:** Predominantly residential with pockets of commercial, light industrial, and green space.  
  - **Demographics:** Mixed income levels, multi-generational households, some migrant communities[^2][^3].  
  - **Infrastructure:** Prior to the project, mobility infrastructure was fragmented, with limited e-mobility options and low renewable energy penetration.  
  - **Environment:** Air quality challenges, limited green space, energy inefficiencies in older buildings.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges:**
  - High carbon emissions from transport and energy use
  - Limited e-mobility and renewable energy infrastructure
  - Traffic congestion and air pollution
  - Social exclusion and limited community engagement in planning[^2][^3]

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU Smart Cities funding opportunity (H2020 Lighthouse program)
  - Local government commitment to carbon neutrality and innovation
  - Growing citizen demand for sustainable mobility and energy solutions

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (policy, planning, mobility)  
  - Public utilities (energy, transport)  
  - Private mobility operators and tech firms  
  - Academic/research partners  
  - Local residents and community organizations[^2][^3]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by the City of Munich, in partnership with EU and local agencies[^4][^2][^3]

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 Smart Cities program
  - Munich’s sustainability and climate action plans
  - Bavarian urban planning and building codes  
  - Recent amendments to special use guidelines for public charging infrastructure[^1][^3]

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive EU and local frameworks for smart city innovation
  - Regulatory challenges around procurement, data privacy, and interoperability[^1]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Reduce energy consumption and CO₂ emissions by 50% in target districts  
  - Integrate renewable energy sources into urban systems  
  - Expand e-mobility options and optimize energy use  
  - Empower communities through real-time data and participatory design[^2][^3]

- **Vision Statement:**  
  A climate-neutral, inclusive, and digitally connected urban district, setting a model for sustainable urban living in Munich and beyond[^2][^3].

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Munich’s carbon neutrality goal for Neuaubing-Westkreuz/Freiham by 2050  
  - EU Smart Cities and Communities strategic objectives  
  - National Charging Infrastructure Master Plan 2030 (Germany)[^5][^6]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  Deploying smart mobility hubs, integrating renewables, and digital engagement will reduce emissions, improve energy efficiency, and foster social inclusion.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents (improved mobility, lower energy costs)  
  - Municipal and private fleet operators  
  - Vulnerable groups (better access, cleaner environment)[^2][^3]

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - IoT-enabled real-time energy feedback  
  - Integrated EV and bike sharing  
  - Multimodal mobility stations  
  - Digital platforms for planning and user engagement  
  - Smart street lighting and infrastructure[^4][^2][^3]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  - Strong city leadership; EU funding as catalyst  
  - Public demand for climate action[^2][^3]

- **Economic:**  
  - Investment opportunity; creating new jobs  
  - Lower energy and transport costs[^2]

- **Social:**  
  - Community co-design/living lab approach  
  - Equity and empowerment[^2]

- **Environmental:**  
  - CO₂ reduction, air quality improvement  
  - Urban resilience[^3]

- **Technological:**  
  - Advances in IoT, EV charging, and smart grid integration[^4][^2][^3]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Smart city principles: integration, co-design, digital innovation, sustainability  
  - Urban living lab model for participatory planning[^2][^3]

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Eight multimodal mobility hubs in Neuaubing-Westkreuz/Freiham  
  - E-mobility stations, parcel lockers, smart street infrastructure  
  - Refurbishment of 43,000 m² of housing for energy efficiency[^4][^2][^3]

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Pilot phase (2015–2021): mobility hubs, energy retrofits, digital platforms  
  - Expansion (2024–2027): new charging infrastructure, scaling up across Munich[^1][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - IoT sensors and digital information boards  
  - EV telematics, charging stations, renewable energy integration  
  - Smart grid interfaces  
  - E-bike, e-trike, and car sharing systems  
  - Parcel lockers (Quartierbox)[^4][^2][^3]

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (smart grid, IoT, EV integration)  
  - Social (co-design, participatory living labs)  
  - Process (interoperable data platforms, user engagement tools)[^4][^2][^3]

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Interoperable systems connecting multiple mobility operators  
  - Integration with citywide energy and transport infrastructure  
  - Central data platform for urban planning and management[^4][^2][^3]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive tenders for infrastructure (charging stations, hubs)[^1]  
  - Partnership agreements with public transport and sharing operators[^4]

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Urban living lab with resident participation  
  - Engagement of local businesses, research partners, and municipal agencies[^2]

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Co-design workshops  
  - Citizen proposals for infrastructure locations  
  - User feedback mechanisms via digital platforms[^2][^1]

- **Project Management:**  
  - Coordinated by City of Munich’s mobility and planning departments  
  - Quarterly reviews and adaptive planning processes[^2][^3]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - ~€20 million total investment for district, including €6.85 million EU funding[^2][^3]

- **Funding Mix:**  
  - EU funds (~34%)  
  - Local government (~33%)  
  - Private and research sector contributions (~33%)[^2][^3]

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - User fees for mobility services (car/bike sharing)  
  - Potential land value capture (Freiham development)[^2]

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term operational costs supported by city, operators, user fees  
  - Ongoing EU and national funding for scaling up[^4][^2]

  - Eight public mobility hubs  
  - Refurbished housing (43,000 m² for energy efficiency)

  - Integrated EV, bike, cargo trike sharing  
  - Real-time energy feedback for users  
  - Parcel lockers and digital information boards[^4][^2]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Integrated approach**: Mobility, energy, technology, and community engagement combined for maximum impact[^4][^2][^3]
- **Participatory design**: Living lab model fostered strong local buy-in and innovation
- **Interoperability**: Seamless integration across operators and systems
- **Scalability**: Designed for replication beyond Munich

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Technical complexity**: Integration across multiple platforms and operators required significant effort[^4][^1]
- **Regulatory challenges**: Procurement and legal hurdles delayed some components[^1]
- **Risk of exclusion**: Ongoing vigilance needed to ensure equitable access

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Advancement of equity**: Focus on accessibility, community empowerment, and inclusion[^2][^3]
- **Environmental justice**: Targeted reduction of air pollution, benefits for vulnerable populations

#### **Future Outlook**

- Ongoing expansion of charging infrastructure (2024–2027)[^1]
- Continued community engagement and policy innovation
- Monitoring and adaptation to ensure long-term sustainability, equity, and technological progress
- Potential evolution toward more advanced energy integration (V2G, bidirectional charging)[^5]
