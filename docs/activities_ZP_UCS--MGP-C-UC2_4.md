# Executive summary## SRI4Cities – Smart Readiness Indicator Demonstration in Vienna

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** SRI4Cities – Smart Readiness Indicator Demonstration in Vienna
- **Alternative Names:** SRI Pilot Vienna, SRI Austria
- **Location:** Vienna, Austria (various urban districts)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Centered around Vienna (48.2082° N, 16.3738° E)
- **Timeline:** Initiated in 2021 as part of the Horizon 2020 program; key milestones include pilot building selection (2021), technology deployment (2022), SRI assessment and reporting (2023–2025), ongoing integration into Vienna’s building certification processes.
- **Project Scale:** Pilot phase covered 20 representative buildings, scaling to 100 buildings for methodology verification; mix of residential, commercial, and public buildings[^1]. Population affected includes facility managers, residents, and city officials.
- **Leading Organization(s):** Austrian Institute of Construction Engineering (OIB), Austrian Climate Ministry (BMK), University of Natural Resources and Life Sciences Vienna (BOKU), AEE Intec; supported by EU SRI Support Team and VITO consortium[^1][^2].
- **Funding Sources:** European Union (Horizon 2020), Austrian government (BMK), technical partners; mix of public and research funding[^1][^3].

#### **Executive Summary**
The SRI4Cities initiative in Vienna represents Austria's pioneering effort to operationalize the EU’s Smart Readiness Indicator (SRI) framework in both new and existing buildings. Driven by the Horizon 2020 program, this pilot aimed to assess and improve buildings’ smart energy management capacities—especially their ability to integrate renewables, employ building automation, and support electric vehicle (EV) charging. The project deployed IoT sensors, smart meters, and digital dashboards to collect real-time and historical data on energy flows, enabling facility managers to make informed operational decisions.

Vienna’s pilot focused on benchmarking the SRI methodology against Austria’s existing tools and regulations, ensuring GDPR-compliant data handling and robust stakeholder engagement. By integrating the SRI into building certification, the initiative sought to foster awareness of smart building technologies, support decarbonization, and enhance urban resilience. The project faced challenges in scaling pilot learnings, addressing data privacy, and harmonizing with national energy flexibility strategies. Outcomes included improved decision support for facility managers, higher building valuations, and demonstrable reductions in energy waste and emissions. Vienna’s experience is now shaping SRI implementation across Austria, providing a template for smart neighborhood development in line with broader European sustainability goals[^2][^1][^4][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:** Vienna, capital of Austria, population ~1.9 million. Leading European hub for culture, research, and innovation. Strong economic base in services, technology, and public administration. Recognized for high quality of life and advanced infrastructure.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The pilot targeted diverse districts, including older urban centers and newly developed areas. Varied building stock—historic, post-war, and contemporary. Mixed-use neighborhoods with residential, commercial, and public facilities.
  - *Historical development patterns:* Gradual densification, robust public transport, emphasis on energy-efficient retrofits.
  - *Current land use composition:* High proportion of mixed-use buildings; significant public and private facilities.
  - *Demographic profile:* Broad income spectrum, aging population in some districts, multicultural communities.
  - *Infrastructure quality and accessibility:* Advanced transit, widespread broadband, established district heating/cooling networks.
  - *Environmental conditions and constraints:* Urban heat island effects, air quality concerns, need for renewable integration.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Fragmented building automation landscape.
  - Limited integration of renewables and EV infrastructure.
  - Lack of standardized smartness assessment tools.
  - Data privacy and interoperability concerns.
- **Triggering Events:** EU “Clean Energy for All Europeans” package (2016), EPBD amendment (2018) introducing SRI[^2][^4]. National push for energy flexibility and digital building certification.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Government ministries (BMK), research institutions (BOKU, OIB), energy service companies, facility managers, residents, technology vendors.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:** Led by Austrian Climate Ministry in coordination with technical bodies (OIB, BOKU), EU oversight.
- **Relevant Policies:** Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD 2010, amended 2018), Austrian building codes, energy certification processes.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** SRI introduced as voluntary scheme; national adaptation required. GDPR compliance essential for data handling. Need to harmonize with existing energy certification and smart grid regulations[^2][^1].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:** 
  - Assess and benchmark smart readiness of Vienna’s buildings.
  - Foster adoption of smart energy management and automation technologies.
  - Support integration of renewables and EV charging.
  - Provide actionable decision support for facility managers.
- **Vision Statement:** To enable Vienna’s buildings and neighborhoods to become future-proof, user-centric, and climate-resilient through digitalization and smart energy practices.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:** Fits Vienna’s climate action strategy, EU decarbonization roadmap, and national energy transition initiatives.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:** By deploying IoT and smart technologies, collecting operational data, and providing SRI-based assessments, buildings become more flexible, energy-efficient, and responsive to occupant and grid needs—leading to lower emissions and higher asset value[^4][^1].
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Facility managers (decision support), building owners (property value), residents (comfort, lower costs), city officials (data for urban planning).
- **Innovation Elements:** First systematic implementation of SRI in Austria; integration with energy certification; GDPR-compliant dashboards; participatory stakeholder engagement.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:** EU directives, national climate commitments, city leadership on sustainability.
- **Economic Drivers:** Energy cost reduction, building market competitiveness, stimulus for technology sector.
- **Social Drivers:** Community engagement in energy transition, improved living standards.
- **Environmental Drivers:** Climate adaptation, renewable integration, emissions reduction.
- **Technological Drivers:** Advances in IoT, building automation, EV infrastructure.

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:** Anchored in the EPBD’s SRI methodology; guided by principles of energy flexibility, user-centricity, and interoperability[^2][^1][^4].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:** Retrofitting existing buildings and designing new ones with IoT sensors, smart meters, and automation systems; integration of EV charging stations.
- **Scope and Phasing:** Initial pilot (20 buildings); expanded sample (100 buildings across typologies); ongoing citywide rollout.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - IoT sensors for real-time monitoring.
  - Smart meters for granular energy usage.
  - Building automation platforms (HVAC, lighting, security).
  - SRI assessment platform and dashboards.
  - EV charging infrastructure.
- **Innovation Type:** Technological (digitalization), process (SRI rating integration), social (stakeholder engagement).
- **Integration Approach:** Technologies layered onto existing building systems; interoperability ensured via open standards and EU guidelines.

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:** Competitive selection of technology providers; engagement with research partners.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Workshops with facility managers, residents, technology firms; feedback loops embedded in dashboard design.
- **Participatory Elements:** Data-driven engagement; users access dashboards and reports; input solicited on priorities and concerns.
- **Project Management:** Coordinated by BMK and OIB; technical oversight by BOKU and AEE Intec; supported by SRI Support Team.

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:** Supported by EU Horizon 2020 grant; national co-financing; technology and research costs shared among partners.
- **Funding Mix:** Predominantly public (EU, Austrian government), supplemented by research institution resources.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:** No direct user fees in pilot; potential future revenue from property value uplift, energy savings.
- **Financial Sustainability:** Integration with building certification aims for long-term viability; potential for expanded public-private partnerships.

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:** Integrating legacy systems; ensuring interoperability; developing user-friendly dashboards.
- **Financial Challenges:** Scaling beyond pilot required additional investment; aligning funding cycles.
- **Social and Political Challenges:** Addressing privacy concerns; varying stakeholder readiness.
- **Administrative Challenges:** Coordinating multi-level governance; accreditation and training of SRI assessors.
- **Environmental Challenges:** Adapting systems to aging infrastructure; ensuring renewable integration in dense urban context.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:** Iterative testing of SRI methodology; stakeholder feedback integrated into dashboard design; GDPR compliance prioritized.
- **Design Modifications:** Tailored SRI assessment tools for Austrian context; expanded sample size for verification; modular technology deployment.
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:** Need for clear communication on benefits; importance of robust data protection; value of participatory engagement.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- Comprehensive integration of SRI into building certification.
- Robust stakeholder engagement and participatory approach.
- Demonstrated energy savings and emissions reductions.
- Alignment with EU and national sustainability goals.
- Scalable methodology for broader rollout.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- Complexity of scaling to citywide/regional level.
- Need for ongoing investment and capacity building.
- Data privacy and interoperability challenges persist.
- Limited direct evidence on social equity beyond access.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Public funding ensured broad access in pilot phase.
- Attention to GDPR and inclusivity in dashboard design.
- Further work needed to address barriers for vulnerable populations and legacy buildings.

#### **Future Outlook**

- Continued expansion to more buildings and neighborhoods.
- Integration with national building renovation plans due by 2026[^3].
- Ongoing development of SRI assessment tools, training, and certification.
- Opportunities for linking SRI with broader smart city and climate resilience strategies.
- Need for adaptive management to address evolving technology, regulatory, and social challenges.
