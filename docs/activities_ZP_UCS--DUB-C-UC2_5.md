## Syn.ikia: Plus-Energy Neighbourhood Demonstrator

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Syn.ikia: Plus-Energy Neighbourhood Demonstrator
- **Alternative Names:** syn.ikia, Syn.ikia Project, Syn.ikia Horizon 2020 Project
- **Location:** Trondheim, Norway (with additional demo cases in Spain, Austria, and the Netherlands)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** 63.4305° N, 10.3951° E (Trondheim, Norway)
- **Timeline:** 2019–2023 (EU Horizon 2020 project duration)
- **Project Scale:** Multiple demonstration sites across four European countries; Trondheim site covers a mixed-use urban district with several hundred residents and commercial users
- **Leading Organization(s):** SINTEF (Norway), in partnership with universities, municipalities, and private sector actors across Europe
- **Funding Sources:** European Union’s Horizon 2020 Research and Innovation Programme, national and local government grants, private sector contributions

#### **Executive Summary**
The Syn.ikia project is a flagship European initiative under the Horizon 2020 programme, piloting the development of plus-energy neighbourhoods—urban districts that produce more energy than they consume. The project’s core focus is on integrating advanced digital technologies, including IoT sensors, digital twin platforms, and smart energy management systems, to optimize energy use, space allocation, and resident comfort. The Trondheim demonstrator serves as a living lab, testing real-time occupancy and environmental monitoring, adaptive HVAC control, and resident engagement apps. Syn.ikia’s approach emphasizes not only technological innovation but also social participation, aiming to foster sustainable urban living models that can be scaled across Europe. The initiative has demonstrated energy-positive operation, improved space utilization, and active occupant involvement, positioning itself as a model for future sustainable urban development.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Trondheim is Norway’s third-largest city, with a population of approximately 210,000. It is a regional hub for education, research, and technology, with a strong focus on sustainability and innovation. The city is situated in central Norway, with a temperate climate and a mix of urban and natural landscapes.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:** The Syn.ikia demonstrator is located in a mixed-use district undergoing urban renewal. The area features a blend of residential, commercial, and public spaces, with a diverse demographic profile. The district has a history of industrial use, but recent redevelopment has prioritized sustainability and smart city principles.
- **Demographic Profile:** The neighborhood includes a mix of age groups, income levels, and cultural backgrounds, reflecting Trondheim’s broader diversity. There is a growing emphasis on attracting young professionals and families.
- **Existing Infrastructure:** The district benefits from modern infrastructure, including public transportation, cycling paths, and digital connectivity. However, some legacy buildings and systems present integration challenges.
- **Environmental Conditions:** The area faces typical northern European climate challenges, including cold winters and variable weather, which influence energy demand and building performance.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:** The district faced issues related to energy inefficiency, underutilized spaces, and limited resident engagement in sustainability initiatives. There was also a need to address climate change mitigation and adaptation in urban planning.
- **Triggering Events:** The EU’s push for climate-neutral cities and the availability of Horizon 2020 funding catalyzed the project. Local government priorities for sustainable urban development and innovation also played a key role.
- **Stakeholder Landscape:** Key actors included SINTEF, Trondheim Municipality, local residents, businesses, and academic institutions. Each group had distinct interests, ranging from technological innovation to community well-being and economic development.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:** The project operates within a multi-level governance structure, involving EU, national, and local authorities. Collaboration between public and private sectors is facilitated through formal partnerships and agreements.
- **Relevant Policies:** The initiative aligns with EU climate and energy policies, Norwegian national sustainability strategies, and Trondheim’s local urban development plans.
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:** EU funding and regulatory support enabled the project, while data privacy laws and integration of legacy systems posed challenges.
