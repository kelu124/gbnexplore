# Executive summary## oPEN Lab – Positive Energy Neighbourhoods in Tartu, Pamplona, and Genk

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** oPEN Lab – Transition to Positive Energy Neighbourhoods Project
- **Alternative Names:** oPEN Lab Project; Open Innovation Living Labs for Positive Energy Neighbourhoods
- **Location:**  
  - Tartu, Annelinn neighborhood, Estonia  
  - Pamplona, Rochapea/San Pedro, Spain  
  - Genk, Waterschei/Nieuw Texas, Belgium
- **Geographic Coordinates:**  
  - Tartu: approx. 58.3776° N, 26.7290° E  
  - Pamplona: approx. 42.8125° N, 1.6458° W  
  - Genk: approx. 50.9667° N, 5.5000° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - Start: Autumn 2021  
  - Duration: 4.5 years (completion targeted in 2026)  
  - Key milestones: Launch of Living Labs, mid-term reviews, demonstration of positive energy operation, replication strategy development[^1][^2].
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Tartu Annelinn: >30,000 residents[^3]  
  - Pamplona: Multiple apartment blocks and former industrial area (Rochapea, San Pedro)[^3][^4]  
  - Genk: Former mining district (Waterschei/Nieuw Texas)[^3]  
  - Total investment: ~€22 million[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - Coordinator: VITO (Belgium)  
  - Partners: 32–33 partners from 7 countries, including city governments, research institutes, regional energy agencies, and private firms[^5][^1][^6].
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - European Union Horizon 2020 Research and Innovation Programme (main)  
  - Local municipality contributions  
  - Additional project partners and private investment[^1][^5].

#### **Executive Summary**

oPEN Lab is an ambitious EU-funded demonstration program designed to pioneer the transformation of conventional urban districts into **Positive Energy Neighbourhoods (PENs)**. Operating in three European cities—Tartu, Pamplona, and Genk—the initiative aims to deeply retrofit existing buildings, integrate renewable energy, and promote digital, participatory solutions at the district level. The project leverages “Living Labs,” which serve as real-world testbeds for new technologies, business models, and community engagement practices. By focusing on commercially viable, replicable solutions, oPEN Lab addresses the urgent need for scalable decarbonization of Europe’s building stock, aligning with the EU’s climate neutrality goals by 2050.

Central to oPEN Lab’s philosophy is a holistic approach that combines **deep energy renovation**, local renewable generation (such as solar PV), energy sharing, and digitalization (including digital twins and smart platforms). The project also prioritizes **co-creation with residents and stakeholders**, ensuring solutions are socially accepted and tailored to local needs. Each city’s Living Lab responds to distinct urban contexts: Tartu’s Soviet-era apartment blocks, Pamplona’s former industrial sites, and Genk’s post-coal communities. The outcomes sought include surplus renewable energy at the neighbourhood scale, improved energy affordability, enhanced urban quality of life, and robust frameworks for continuous improvement. oPEN Lab’s ultimate ambition is to establish a blueprint for positive energy transformation that can be widely adopted across European cities[^3][^7][^6].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**
- **Tartu:** Second largest city in Estonia (~95,000 residents); university and tech hub; cold continental climate; historically shaped by Soviet-era development; faces aging building stock and energy inefficiency.
- **Pamplona:** Regional capital in northern Spain (~200,000 residents); strong service sector; historical city with modern urban extensions; Mediterranean climate with seasonal extremes.
- **Genk:** Medium-sized city in Belgian Limburg (~66,000 residents); formerly a major coal mining center, now in economic transition; diverse population due to migrant labor history; significant post-industrial challenges[^3][^7].

**Neighbourhood/District Characteristics:**
- **Tartu (Annelinn):**  
  - Large 1980s-90s Soviet-era apartment district; poor energy standards; low to moderate income; diverse age and ethnicity; infrastructure in need of upgrade[^3].
- **Pamplona (Rochapea/San Pedro):**  
  - Former industrial area and adjacent residential blocks; mix of social and private housing; working-class demographic; aging infrastructure[^4].
- **Genk (Waterschei/Nieuw Texas):**  
  - Post-mining neighborhood; legacy industrial sites; multicultural, lower-income residents; significant vacancy and underuse[^3].

**Environmental Conditions:**
- Cold winters (Tartu), heating demand;  
- Urban heat and air quality concerns (Pamplona, Genk);  
- Sites often lack green space and have limited biodiversity[^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

**Problems/Challenges Identified:**
- High energy use in inefficient buildings
- Social vulnerability and lower quality of life
- Underutilized public space and limited community amenities
- Need for climate action and local economic revitalization[^7][^3][^1]

**Triggering Events:**
- EU climate targets and Renovation Wave policy
- Local sustainability and energy action plans
- Economic stagnation and urban decline in post-industrial areas

**Stakeholder Landscape:**
- City governments (driving urban regeneration and climate targets)
- Residents (concerned by affordability/comfort)
- Local businesses, energy companies, research institutions
- NGOs and social housing operators[^3][^8]

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

**Governance Framework:**
- Municipal governments lead local implementation
- National and EU policy frameworks (Renovation Wave, Green Deal)
- Project coordination by VITO and large transnational consortium[^5][^7]

**Relevant Policies:**
- EU Renovation Wave
- Local climate/energy action plans (e.g., Tartu Energy 2030)[^3]
- Urban regeneration and social inclusion policies

**Legal/Institutional Enablers and Barriers:**
- EU funding and procurement rules (enabler)
- Complex ownership and regulatory restrictions in housing (barrier)
- Need for alignment with local planning and building codes[^7][^1]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

**Primary Goals:**
- Deliver **net-positive energy balance** at district level
- Demonstrate **scalable, commercially viable solutions** for PENs
- Advance **social innovation** and citizen participation in energy transitions[^3][^7]

**Vision Statement:**
- Create inclusive, sustainable, and energy-positive neighbourhoods that enhance quality of life and set a precedent for urban transformation across Europe.

**Alignment with Broader Plans:**
- Direct contribution to EU Green Deal and Renovation Wave
- Supports local and national climate action strategies
- Model for replication in similar urban contexts[^3][^2]

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

**Theory of Change:**
- Deep retrofit + local renewables + digital tools + citizen co-creation →  
  Energy-positive, resilient, and inclusive neighbourhoods →  
  Climate, economic, and social benefits

**Target Beneficiaries:**
- Local residents (improved comfort, lower bills)
- Broader city (reduced emissions, social cohesion)
- Policy community (scalable models and lessons)[^3][^1]

**Innovation Elements:**
- Integration of smart energy systems at neighbourhood scale
- Digital twins for design and monitoring
- User-driven, participatory design and decision-making
- Novel business and financing models[^3][^7][^8]

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:** EU and local climate targets; urban regeneration mandates
- **Economic:** Stimulate investment, job creation, local value chains
- **Social:** Address vulnerability, promote inclusion, improve urban life
- **Environmental:** Mitigate emissions, improve resilience, biodiversity
- **Technological:** Leverage digitalization, smart grids, and prefabrication[^7][^3][^1]

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

**Conceptual Framework:**
- *Living Lab* model (real-world testbeds for innovation)
- Integrated design (energy, mobility, public space)
- Life-cycle and circularity principles[^3][^7]

**Physical/Spatial Design:**
- Deep renovation of residential blocks (insulation, windows, HVAC)
- Renewable energy deployment (solar PV, heat pumps)
- EV charging, energy storage, smart controls
- Public space improvements, co-created art and amenities[^3][^4]

**Scope and Phasing:**
- Pilot/demonstration in three cities, staged retrofits
- Ongoing monitoring and refinement
- Preparation for replication at regional/EU scale[^1][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Prefabricated renovation elements  
  - Local solar PV and storage  
  - Energy flexibility services, smart meters  
  - Digital twins for simulation and monitoring  
  - Citizen engagement platforms[^3][^7][^4]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (energy, digitalization)  
  - Social (co-creation, empowerment)  
  - Governance (multi-stakeholder collaboration)  
  - Financial (innovative funding, business models)[^3][^1]
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Seamless links between renovated buildings, district energy, and grid  
  - Synchronization with mobility and public services[^7][^3]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive selection of technical providers, local contractors
  - Engagement of research and innovation partners[^3][^1]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Workshops, surveys, community events
  - Creation of local working groups, participatory artwork[^8][^3]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Residents involved in design and monitoring
  - Feedback platforms and ongoing co-creation[^3][^8]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Consortium led by VITO, city-level project teams
  - Regular coordination meetings, reporting, and external monitoring[^5][^3]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Total budget: ~€22 million[^1]
  - Breakdown: Building retrofits, technology deployment, digital platforms, community engagement
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - ~90% EU Horizon 2020 grant
  - Local/partner co-funding[^1]
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Potential future revenue from energy services, local energy trading
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Focus on cost-effective, scalable solutions
  - Business models to attract private investment for replication[^1]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Interoperability of new and legacy energy systems  
  - Complex retrofits in diverse building stock  
  - Ensuring reliable data from digital platforms[^3][^1]
- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront costs, limited local funding capacity  
  - Aligning business models for long-term viability[^1]
- **Social and Political:**  
  - Sustaining resident engagement over multi-year projects  
  - Managing disruption during construction  
  - Navigating multi-owner housing structures[^3][^8]
- **Administrative:**  
  - Complex coordination among 30+ partners  
  - Navigating local/national/EU regulations[^1]
- **Environmental:**  
  - Managing unforeseen site conditions  
  - Ensuring upgrades do not cause ecological harm

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Iterative design using digital twins and scenario analysis  
  - Dedicated engagement teams, feedback loops[^3][^8]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Flexible, modular retrofit solutions  
  - Adjusted timelines and phasing based on feedback
- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of early and continuous resident involvement  
  - Need for robust technical integration testing  
  - Value of transparent communication and expectation management[^3][^1]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Holistic Integration:** Combines deep retrofit, renewables, digitalization, and social innovation.
- **Living Lab Model:** Real-world demonstration with iterative learning.
- **Scalability:** Designed for replication in diverse urban contexts; strong knowledge-sharing[^1][^3][^7].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity:** Multi-partner, multi-city coordination is resource-intensive and can slow progress.
- **Financial Barriers:** Upfront costs remain high; reliant on public/EU funding for demonstration phase.
- **Resident Fatigue:** Sustaining engagement over long project timelines is challenging[^1].

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- *Proactive focus on inclusion* through participatory design and targeting lower-income districts.
- Risks remain regarding gentrification and benefit distribution; ongoing monitoring and adaptive management are needed[^1][^3][^8].

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-Term Trajectory:**  
  - Aims to serve as a catalyst for widespread PEN adoption across Europe.
  - Ongoing monitoring to ensure positive energy balance and resident satisfaction.
- **Ongoing Challenges:**  
  - Mainstreaming business models, aligning with evolving policy, upscaling beyond pilots.
- **Next Phases:**  
  - Replication in similar cities, refinement of digital and social innovation tools, policy advocacy for supportive regulatory frameworks[^1][^3].
