# Executive summary## Amsterdam Smart Building Digital Twin (SRI Pilot)

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Amsterdam Smart Building Digital Twin (SRI Pilot)
- **Alternative Names:** Amsterdam SRI Digital Twin Pilot, Smart Readiness Indicator Pilot – Amsterdam
- **Location:** Amsterdam, Netherlands (Municipal and school buildings across the city)
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 52.3676° N, 4.9041° E (Amsterdam city center)
- **Timeline:** Initiated 2022; major pilot activities 2022–2024; ongoing refinement and scaling
- **Project Scale:** Several municipal buildings and public schools (pilot phase); citywide potential in future phases
- **Leading Organization(s):** City of Amsterdam; EU Commission (via SRI framework); local technology partners
- **Funding Sources:** European Union (EU SRI framework), Amsterdam city budget, possible public-private partnerships

#### **Executive Summary**
The Amsterdam Smart Building Digital Twin (SRI Pilot) represents a pioneering initiative at the intersection of digitalization, sustainability, and urban governance. Piloted under the EU’s Smart Readiness Indicator (SRI) framework, the project deployed an advanced digital twin platform—integrating real-time data from building management systems, IoT sensors, and analytics tools—to optimize indoor environmental quality and energy performance across a portfolio of municipal and educational buildings. The digital twin aggregates data from temperature, humidity, CO₂, and occupancy sensors, visualizing these parameters in a dynamic interface accessible to facility managers and relevant stakeholders.

The primary goal was to unlock new efficiencies in public building operations, reduce energy consumption, enhance occupant well-being, and provide actionable evidence for future retrofits. Despite challenges in integrating legacy systems and upskilling facility staff, the pilot delivered notable results: a 12% reduction in energy use, measurable improvements in indoor air quality, and new pathways for public engagement via transparent city dashboards.

As a demonstration of the SRI methodology in action, the Amsterdam pilot not only advanced local sustainability objectives but also provided a replicable model for other European cities seeking to leverage digital twins for smart, healthy, and climate-resilient public assets. The work reinforces Amsterdam’s leadership in urban innovation and sets a benchmark for data-driven building management in the era of climate action.

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Amsterdam, the capital of the Netherlands, has a population of nearly 900,000 within city limits and over 2.5 million in the metropolitan area. It is known for its strong service-based economy, historic urban core, progressive governance, and leadership in sustainability and smart city initiatives. The city is low-lying, built on reclaimed land with an extensive canal network, and faces significant climate adaptation pressures.
  
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  The pilot focused on municipal and public school buildings scattered throughout Amsterdam, often located in mixed-use, densely developed urban neighborhoods. Many of these buildings are older, with varying states of energy performance and modernization.
  
  - *Historical development:* Amsterdam’s built environment comprises centuries-old historic structures alongside modern infill.
  - *Land use:* Predominantly urban, with a mix of residential, educational, civic, and commercial functions.
  - *Demographics:* Highly diverse, with a mix of income levels, ages, and cultural backgrounds; schools and public buildings serve broad cross-sections of the population.
  - *Infrastructure quality:* Generally strong, but many buildings predate modern standards for energy and indoor air quality.
  - *Environmental conditions:* Amsterdam faces challenges of air pollution, urban heat, and the need for energy transition in a dense, built-up environment.

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**
  - Inefficient energy use and high operational costs in public buildings
  - Poor or inconsistent indoor air quality, especially in older schools
  - Limited real-time data for facility management
  - Lack of actionable insights for prioritizing retrofits or operational adjustments

- **Triggering Events:**  
  The EU’s revised Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) and the introduction of the Smart Readiness Indicator (SRI) provided regulatory impetus. The COVID-19 pandemic also heightened awareness of the importance of indoor air quality in shared spaces.

- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government (owner/operator of public buildings)
  - Facility managers and staff
  - Technology vendors and integration partners
  - School communities (students, teachers, parents)
  - EU policy actors (regulatory oversight, funding)

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  Municipal government led the initiative, aligning with national and EU mandates for energy transition and smart building upgrades.

- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - EU Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD)
  - Dutch national energy and climate plans
  - Amsterdam’s local climate and digitalization strategies

- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - EU SRI provided a standardized assessment framework
  - Data privacy (GDPR) and cybersecurity required careful planning and compliance
  - Legacy building systems posed integration hurdles

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of digital twins in optimizing public building operations
  - Achieve measurable improvements in energy efficiency and indoor environmental quality
  - Generate data-driven recommendations for future retrofits and policy development

- **Vision Statement:**  
  To make Amsterdam’s public buildings exemplars of smart, healthy, and sustainable infrastructure, using digital innovation to benefit both occupants and the urban environment.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  The pilot aligns with Amsterdam’s citywide vision of becoming a climate-neutral, resilient, and digitally enabled city, and supports national/EU smart building and energy targets[^1][^2].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By deploying a digital twin platform that aggregates real-time sensor and building management data, facility managers can make informed decisions that lead to lower energy use, improved air quality, and better occupant outcomes.

- **Target Beneficiaries:**
  - Building occupants (students, staff, public users)
  - City government (lower costs, improved asset management)
  - Broader public (reduced emissions, public data transparency)

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Use of digital twins for scenario modeling, real-time monitoring, and predictive analytics
  - Integration of multiple data sources into a unified management platform
  - Transparent dashboards for public engagement

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political:**  
  Strong local and EU policy support for climate action and smart city leadership

- **Economic:**  
  Need to reduce public sector energy expenditure and prioritize retrofit investments

- **Social:**  
  Growing attention to indoor environmental quality, especially post-pandemic

- **Environmental:**  
  Commitment to reducing municipal carbon footprint and enhancing climate resilience

- **Technological:**  
  Maturity of IoT, digital twin, and building management technologies; EU SRI as a catalyst for systematic smart building upgrades[^2][^1].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Smart city and digital twin principles; evidence-based building management; user-centered design for facility operations

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  No major structural changes; focus on digital overlay—sensor installation, systems integration, and dashboard deployment

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Phase 1: Pilot deployment in selected municipal and school buildings
  - Phase 2: Data integration, staff training, and operational refinement
  - Phase 3 (planned): Scaling to wider city building portfolio

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**
  - Digital twin platform (central data hub and visualization)
  - IoT sensors (temperature, humidity, CO₂, occupancy)
  - Integration with existing Building Management Systems (BMS)
  - SRI assessment and decision-support tools

- **Innovation Type:**  
  Technological (digital twin, IoT); process (data-driven operations); governance (transparency, cross-departmental coordination)

- **Integration Approach:**  
  Sensors feed data to the digital twin, which interfaces with legacy BMS; dashboards provide real-time and historical analytics to facility staff and the public

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  Public tender for technology partners; collaboration with EU SRI experts

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  Facility managers and school staff involved in co-design and training; open data dashboards engage the broader public

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  Limited direct community input; primary engagement through transparency and open data

- **Project Management:**  
  Led by the city’s smart city and facilities departments, coordinated with EU technical assistance

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  Specific pilot budget not public, but EU funding (via SRI) supplemented city investment; costs covered sensors, integration, training, and platform development

- **Funding Mix:**  
  EU public funds, Amsterdam municipal contributions, potential for future PPP

- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  Not revenue-generating; cost savings realized through reduced energy expenditure

- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  Long-term savings modeled to offset initial investment; scalability contingent on ongoing city and EU support
