# Executive summary## Sharing Cities: Smart City Digital Twin for Neighborhood Sustainability

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Sharing Cities – Smart City Digital Twin for Neighborhood Sustainability
- **Alternative Names:** Sharing Cities Milan Digital Twin; Sharing Cities H2020 Milan
- **Location:** Milan, Porta Romana/Vettabbia district, Italy
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.4489° N, 9.2056° E (Porta Romana/Vettabbia)
- **Timeline:** 2016 (project inception) – December 2021 (official completion of H2020 phase); ongoing digital twin development and integration
- **Project Scale:** 1,575 km² metropolitan area, 133 municipalities, with focus on demonstration district
- **Leading Organization(s):** Municipality of Milan, in partnership with CGR SpA, CycloMedia, Esri Italia, SIT Srl, Laboratory of Geomatics (University of Pavia), H2020 Sharing Cities Consortium
- **Funding Sources:** European Commission Horizon 2020 (€24 million seed), €265 million additional investment, local government, private sector[^1]

#### **Executive Summary**
The Sharing Cities initiative in Milan, under the European Union's Horizon 2020 program, deployed a pioneering **digital twin** at the neighborhood scale—primarily in the Porta Romana/Vettabbia district—to drive sustainability, decarbonization, and citizen-centered urban management[^1][^2]. The Milan digital twin integrates **real-time data streams** from IoT environmental sensors, energy management systems, and occupancy trackers across public and residential buildings, enabling scenario-based optimization and public dashboards for transparency.

The project’s goals included improving energy efficiency, reducing carbon emissions, and enhancing urban resilience while actively engaging citizens in co-creation and behavioral change. Milan’s digital twin leverages a comprehensive geospatial model—built with high-resolution aerial imagery, LiDAR point clouds, and terrestrial mapping—to support citywide planning, resource allocation, and targeted social services[^2][^3]. The initiative was one of three “lighthouse” city demonstrations (alongside London and Lisbon) within Sharing Cities, chosen for their replicability and innovation potential.

Key outcomes included measurable reductions in neighborhood energy demand, improved indoor air quality, high rates of citizen participation, and strengthened public trust in city sustainability efforts. Sharing Cities Milan stands as a benchmark in Europe for the fusion of digital transformation, sustainability, and community-centric urban governance[^1][^4][^3].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**
- **City Profile:** Milan is Italy’s second-largest city (over 1.4 million residents) and a global hub for commerce, fashion, finance, research, and tourism[^2][^3]. The metropolitan area is densely built, with a mix of historic urban fabric and modern infrastructure.
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - **Historical Patterns:** Porta Romana/Vettabbia is a mixed-use district with post-industrial redevelopment, older housing stock, and urban renewal projects.
  - **Land Use:** Residential, commercial, and public services.
  - **Demographics:** Diverse population, including elderly residents, families, and immigrants; mixed income levels.
  - **Infrastructure:** Mostly well-developed but with significant variation in building ages and energy performance.
  - **Environmental Conditions:** Urban heat island effects, air quality concerns, and limited green space in some areas[^5].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - High energy consumption and GHG emissions in buildings.
  - Poor indoor environmental quality (air, comfort).
  - Fragmented urban data and siloed municipal services[^3].
  - Public distrust and low engagement in sustainability initiatives.
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU climate and energy targets (Covenant of Mayors).
  - Horizon 2020 funding call for “lighthouse” smart city projects.
  - Local policy drive for decarbonization and digital transformation[^1].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - Municipality (lead), local utilities, ICT firms, academia, NGOs, resident associations.

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Municipality of Milan as lead authority; collaboration with EU, private sector, and civil society.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Milan’s Smart City Plan, Sustainable Energy and Climate Action Plan, and the EU Urban Agenda.
- **Legal/Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Strong EU data protection (GDPR), public procurement rules, need for interdepartmental data sharing agreements, evolving standards for digital twins[^5].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Achieve neighborhood-scale carbon reduction and energy efficiency.
  - Enhance urban resilience and liveability through data-driven management.
  - Foster civic participation and trust via open data and co-creation tools.
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - To create a dynamic, inclusive, and sustainable urban environment where data and technology empower better decision-making and collective action.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Directly supports Milan’s commitments under the EU Covenant of Mayors, aligns with citywide digital transformation and climate adaptation strategies[^1][^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Integrate real-time IoT and geospatial data into a digital twin → enable scenario modeling and resource optimization → improve environmental quality and efficiency → actively engage citizens → achieve measurable sustainability and social outcomes.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents (especially in demonstration district), vulnerable populations, city service managers, building owners, and the broader metropolitan community[^3][^1].
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Use of digital twins at the neighborhood level; integration of diverse urban data streams; participatory digital platforms for citizen input and feedback.

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:**  
  - Mayoral leadership on climate, EU and national policy alignment.
- **Economic:**  
  - Leverage EU funding, stimulate green jobs, and create investment in smart infrastructure.
- **Social:**  
  - Respond to community demand for cleaner air, safer neighborhoods, and more transparent governance.
- **Environmental:**  
  - Meet climate targets, address air pollution and urban heat, improve energy performance.
- **Technological:**  
  - Harness advances in IoT, cloud analytics, and 3D modeling; respond to global smart city trends[^2][^4][^3].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**
- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Based on “smart city” and “sharing city” models emphasizing open innovation, replicability, and citizen engagement[^1].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Retrofit of residential and public buildings, deployment of sensors, creation of open public dashboards, and urban data platform[^1].
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Initial pilot in Porta Romana/Vettabbia; incremental rollout of technologies; scaling up to metropolitan area.

#### **Technologies and Innovations**
- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - IoT environmental sensors (for air quality, temperature, occupancy).
  - Digital twin platform integrating aerial imagery, LiDAR, and mobile mapping data[^2][^4][^6].
  - Open data platform and public dashboards.
  - Citizen co-creation and engagement tools (e.g., Digital Social Market)[^1].
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (digital twin, IoT), social (co-creation), governance (data sharing, transparency).
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Interfacing with existing municipal IT systems and city operations; ensuring interoperability and scalability[^3][^5].

#### **Implementation Methodology**
- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Public tenders for technology and service providers; EU-compliant procurement.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Co-design workshops, participatory budgeting, digital platforms for feedback.
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Citizen engagement in design and monitoring; behavior-change incentives[^1].
- **Project Management:**  
  - Cross-departmental project office, EU reporting requirements, iterative evaluation cycles.

#### **Financing Model**
- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - €24 million seed (H2020); €265 million leveraged investment[^1].
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Primarily public (EU, municipal), with private sector co-investment.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Energy savings, potential service fees, indirect economic development.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Emphasis on long-term cost savings from energy efficiency, ongoing digital infrastructure support[^1].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**
- **Technical:**  
  - Data integration across diverse building types and legacy systems.
  - Ensuring cybersecurity and data privacy (GDPR compliance)[^5].
  - Maintaining high data quality and system interoperability[^2][^3].
- **Financial:**  
  - Upfront investment needs, aligning funding timelines with implementation.
- **Social/Political:**  
  - Achieving broad citizen engagement; overcoming skepticism about data use.
- **Administrative:**  
  - Navigating municipal silos, coordinating across departments and partners.
- **Environmental:**  
  - Managing unforeseen impacts of retrofitting and construction.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**
- **Problem-Solving:**  
  - Establishment of robust data governance frameworks[^3].
  - Phased approach to technology deployment, starting with demonstration area.
  - Use of open-source and interoperable solutions to mitigate lock-in[^5].
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments in sensor types/locations; iterative software platform improvements.
- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Early and continuous stakeholder engagement is key.
  - Transparency about data use builds public trust.
  - Interdepartmental coordination accelerates implementation[^1][^3][^5].

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**
- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Digital twin model for 1,575 km² area; sensor deployments in public and residential buildings; open dashboards.
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Real-time monitoring of environmental quality, energy use, and occupancy; targeted social services[^3].
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 6.1 GWh/year reduction in building energy needs; 50% energy savings in retrofitted buildings; engagement of 900 dwellings in deep energy retrofits[^1].

#### **Social Impacts**
- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Improved indoor air quality and comfort.
  - Lowered neighborhood energy bills.
  - Enhanced public engagement and trust in city government[^1].
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Targeted interventions for vulnerable populations (e.g., isolated elderly residents)[^3].
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - No major displacement reported; focus on retrofitting existing homes.
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Increased civic participation via co-creation tools and public dashboards.
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Reinforcement of Milan’s identity as an innovative, sustainable city.

#### **Economic Impacts**
- **Job Creation:**  
  - At least 100 new jobs in demonstration districts.
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Attraction of €265 million in further investment; stimulus for local green businesses[^1].
- **Property Values:**  
  - Upgraded buildings may see increased value.
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Utility bill reductions for residents and city.
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Improved access to energy efficiency for lower-income households.

#### **Environmental Impacts**
- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Over 4,500 tons of CO₂ emissions saved, significant energy demand reduction[^1].
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Improved energy and resource efficiency; smarter water and waste management.
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality monitored and addressed using digital twin data[^5].
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - More efficient city services reduce waste and pollution.
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Project performance aligns with EU and city sustainability targets.

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**
- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened city data governance and digital skills[^3].
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Playbooks and models disseminated for replication in other cities; input to EU smart city policy[^1].
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Public dashboards and reporting enhance government accountability and trust.

#### **Performance Against Objectives**
- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Most objectives met or exceeded: energy savings, emissions reductions, citizen engagement, and replicability.
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - No significant negative effects reported; some challenges with digital literacy.
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Strong foundation for continued digital transformation and sustainability efforts.

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**
- **Pioneering use of digital twins** for real-time, neighborhood-scale sustainability management.
- **Robust citizen engagement** through co-creation platforms and transparency tools.
- **Demonstrated replicability**, attracting substantial follow-on investment and policy influence[^1][^5].

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**
- **Complexity of data integration** and ensuring long-term interoperability.
- **Potential digital divide** for low-literacy or tech-averse populations.
- **Dependence on external (EU) funding** for initial implementation.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**
- **Strong focus on inclusion**, targeting interventions for vulnerable groups.
- **Commitment to data privacy** (GDPR compliance), but ongoing vigilance required.
- **No significant displacement**, but need to monitor for gentrification as property values rise.

#### **Future Outlook**
- **Ongoing expansion** of the digital twin to more districts and city services.
- **Continued investment** in digital infrastructure and skills.
- **Potential for integration** with AI, predictive analytics, and regional sustainability initiatives.
- **New challenges** include maintaining data quality, cybersecurity, and scaling citizen engagement as systems grow[^5][^1][^3].
