# Executive summary## eParkomat EV and Smart Parking System

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** eParkomat EV and Smart Parking System
- **Alternative Names:** eParkomat, City Smart Parking
- **Location:** Prague, Czech Republic
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 50.0755° N, 14.4378° E (city-wide system)
- **Timeline:** 
  - Concept initiated: 2016–2017 (startup and pilot awards)[^1][^2]
  - Major R&D project: August 2020 – July 2022[^1]
  - Ongoing operation and expansion as of 2025
- **Project Scale:** 
  - City-wide coverage; Prague population ~1.3 million[^3]
  - 100,000+ regulated parking spaces[^3]
  - Investment includes grants from EU Structural and Investment Funds[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):** 
  - City Smart Parking s.r.o. (startup, main developer)[^1]
  - Strategic partner: T-Mobile/Deutsche Telekom[^1][^2]
  - Project partners: SVI AJAK services s.r.o.[^1]
- **Funding Sources:** 
  - European Structural and Investment Funds[^1]
  - Czech Ministry of Industry and Trade[^1]
  - City of Prague[^3]
  - Private investment (including expansion/incubation support)[^1]

#### **Executive Summary**
eParkomat is a pioneering city-scale smart parking system implemented in Prague, Czech Republic, that harnesses artificial intelligence and big data analytics to predict real-time parking availability and optimize electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure. Unlike traditional sensor-based systems, eParkomat uses anonymized mobile device signaling data, in partnership with major telecom operators, to forecast on-street parking occupancy without additional hardware installation. The system provides a digital platform for drivers to locate available parking and EV charging stations, reducing the average time spent searching for parking and supporting the city’s transition toward low-emission mobility.

The initiative is part of Prague’s broader smart city and climate resilience strategies, aiming to reduce urban congestion, lower greenhouse gas emissions, and enhance user convenience. Key features include predictive analytics, mobile integration with various navigation tools, interoperability with city card systems, and open data APIs to support further innovation. The project has demonstrated quantifiable benefits, such as up to 25% reduction in parking-related traffic and improved utilization of charging infrastructure, while providing valuable data for urban planning. eParkomat stands out for its innovative use of telecom data, rapid scalability, and integration with city governance, serving as a model for data-driven urban mobility management across Europe and beyond[^1][^3][^2].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  Prague is the capital and largest city of the Czech Republic, with a population of approximately 1.3 million. It is the 17th largest city in the EU by area (49,613 hectares). Its economy is driven by services, tourism, technology, and finance. Prague is a regional transport hub facing significant traffic congestion, with 200,000 daily commuters and high car ownership (652 vehicles per 1,000 inhabitants)[^3].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  eParkomat operates city-wide, but initial rollouts targeted central and high-traffic districts (e.g., Prague 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8). These areas feature dense mixed-use land use, a diverse demographic (from affluent historical centers to working-class and multicultural peripheries), and varied infrastructure quality. Accessibility and environmental constraints include narrow historic streets, limited parking, and periodic air quality issues[^3].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**
- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Chronic parking shortages and congestion, especially in city center  
  - Inefficient use and uneven distribution of parking and EV charging infrastructure  
  - High levels of traffic caused by drivers searching for parking (“cruising” traffic)  
  - Lack of real-time information for drivers  
  - Fragmentation between public and private parking and charging networks  
  - Urban environmental concerns, including air pollution and GHG emissions[^3]
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Rapid increase in vehicle ownership post-1990s  
  - Policy push for low-emission mobility and digitalization after 2014  
  - EU funding and smart city innovation opportunities[^1][^3]
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City of Prague (urban mobility, environment, digitalization departments)  
  - Private sector (City Smart Parking s.r.o., telecoms, parking operators)  
  - Citizens and commuters (drivers, EV users)  
  - NGOs and advocacy groups (mobility, environment, accessibility)

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Centralized municipal oversight; single city company manages parking regulation since 2018[^3]
  - Collaboration with national ministries (Industry and Trade)
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Prague’s “Smart Prague” plan and digitalization strategy  
  - Urban mobility and climate action plans  
  - National law revisions enabling digital permit management and data sharing[^3]
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - GDPR-compliant data handling (anonymized mobile signaling data)[^1]
  - National and EU support for smart urban mobility projects  
  - Early fragmentation of parking management (now centralized)[^3]

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Reduce traffic congestion and emissions by optimizing parking search  
  - Support EV adoption and efficient use of charging infrastructure  
  - Provide real-time, accurate parking information to drivers  
  - Enable data-driven urban mobility planning
- **Vision Statement:**  
  Enable a seamless, sustainable urban mobility experience in Prague by leveraging AI and big data to make parking and EV charging efficient, equitable, and environmentally responsible.
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  Directly supports city and national goals for digitalization, smart mobility, air quality improvement, and climate action.

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  By providing real-time availability predictions and digital tools for parking and EV charging, eParkomat reduces time spent searching for spaces, thus decreasing traffic and emissions, while enhancing resource allocation for both users and city planners.
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Drivers and EV users (reduced stress and search time)  
  - City authorities (improved data for planning)  
  - General public (less congestion, better air quality)
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - AI/ML algorithms using telecom data (no physical sensors needed)[^1][^2]
  - Mobile-native, interoperable platform
  - Open API for third-party integration

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Municipal election pressure to address congestion and pollution  
  - National and EU support for smart city pilots
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Cost savings versus hardware-intensive solutions  
  - Potential for export/expansion of technology
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Public demand for easier parking and greener mobility  
  - Growing EV user base
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - GHG reduction targets  
  - Urban air quality mandates
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Advances in AI, big data, and mobile integration  
  - Ubiquity of smartphones and mobile networks

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  Data-driven, user-centric urban mobility management; “invisible infrastructure” through digital solutions
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  No new physical infrastructure required for parking detection; digital layers over city streets
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Pilot in selected districts (2016–2018)  
  - City-wide scaling post-2018, with ongoing upgrades and EV integration[^1][^3]

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - AI-based predictive analytics (neural networks, ML algorithms)[^1]
  - Anonymized mobile device signaling data (from telecom partners)[^1][^2]
  - Mobile app with map-based interface, routing, and parking recommendations[^1]
  - Open API and data-sharing platform[^3]
  - Integration with existing navigation apps and city card (Lítačka) system[^4]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  Primarily technological, with process and governance innovation (open data, integration with city services)
- **Integration Approach:**  
  Seamless overlay with existing urban systems; interoperable with navigation and payment platforms

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Public-private partnership with City Smart Parking s.r.o. as lead[^1]
  - Strategic telecom partnership (T-Mobile/Deutsche Telekom)[^1][^2]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - User feedback incorporated via app UX updates  
  - Coordination with city departments for regulatory compliance
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Open data enables third-party app development and community use[^3]
- **Project Management:**  
  - Agile development cycles for software  
  - City oversight for regulatory alignment

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - EU Structural Funds support for R&D and scaling[^1]
  - City funding for integration and regulatory upgrades[^3]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Public grants, city budget, private investment, PPP
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - Potential user fees for premium app features (not core service)
  - Data services for city planning
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Minimal hardware costs make scaling cost-effective[^1]
  - Ongoing revenue via partnerships and potential B2B services

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Integrating diverse data sources (telecom, city cards, EV networks)  
  - Ensuring prediction accuracy without physical sensors[^1]
  - Maintaining GDPR compliance with device data[^1]
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - Reliance on competitive grant funding for early-stage scaling[^1]
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Initial skepticism from users and traditional parking operators  
  - Concerns over digital exclusion for non-smartphone users
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Aligning system with evolving municipal and national regulations[^3]
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - No major negative impacts reported; positive environmental intent

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Continuous algorithm improvement with machine learning and calibration projects[^1]
  - Strategic partnerships for rapid scaling and integration
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Expansion to include EV charging prediction and integration with new mobility apps
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of open APIs and interoperability  
  - Need for user-friendly UI and integration with familiar navigation tools[^1]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **High-impact, low-infrastructure approach** using telecom data, minimizing costs and disruption[^1][^2]
- **Demonstrated effectiveness** in reducing congestion and supporting EV adoption
- **Integration with city governance** and open data platforms
- **Scalable and replicable** model for other cities

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Dependence on telecom partnerships**—may limit replicability where such collaboration is not feasible
- **Potential digital divide** for populations without smartphones or digital literacy
- **Accuracy depends on density of mobile users**—less effective in low-density or low-tech adoption areas

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Generally positive**: Improved urban air quality, reduced congestion, and open data support inclusivity
- **Mitigation needed** for digital exclusion (e.g., alternative access for non-smartphone users)

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term trajectory:** Continued expansion to other Czech and European cities; integration of additional mobility services (e.g., carsharing, bike-sharing)[^1][^3]
- **Ongoing challenges:** Maintaining data privacy, keeping pace with evolving mobility needs, ensuring universal access
- **Next phases:** Further EV integration, enhanced predictive analytics, expansion to multimodal urban mobility platforms
