# Executive summary

## Sharing Cities – Integrated Smart Energy Solutions in Porta Romana-Vettabbia, Milan

---

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**
- **Initiative Title:** Sharing Cities – Integrated Smart Energy Solutions in Porta Romana-Vettabbia
- **Alternative Names:** Sharing Cities Milan Demonstrator; "SharingMi" (for digital engagement platform)
- **Location:** Porta Romana–Vettabbia district, Milan, Lombardy, Italy
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.445 N, 9.210 E (Porta Romana–Vettabbia district)
- **Timeline:** Project launched January 2016 (Horizon 2020 start), major implementations 2017–2021, ongoing monitoring and scale-up
- **Project Scale:** 216,614 m² redevelopment zone; population affected includes local residents (thousands), social housing tenants, and mobility users; €25 million project budget (entire Sharing Cities, 3 lighthouse cities)[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):** Comune di Milano (City of Milan); in partnership with Greater London Authority, Camara Municipal de Lisboa, Connected Places Catapult, Politecnico di Milano, Legambiente, Siemens, A2A, RSE, and others[^1]
- **Funding Sources:** European Union Horizon 2020; public sector (municipal funds); private sector partners; research grants[^1]

#### **Executive Summary**
Sharing Cities, a €25 million EU Horizon 2020 initiative, aims to accelerate the adoption of smart, sustainable urban solutions in selected European demonstration areas. Milan’s local project focuses on the comprehensive redevelopment of the Porta Romana–Vettabbia district, a historically underutilized and socially fragmented brownfield area. Integrating citizen-centered design, advanced energy management, e-mobility, and data platforms, the project seeks to deliver replicable models for energy-efficient urban transformation. Key interventions include deep retrofitting of social and private housing, deployment of smart energy and mobility infrastructure (such as e-vehicle charging and digital dashboards), and the creation of a digital social market platform ("SharingMi") to foster active citizen engagement and behavioral change. The initiative aligns with Milan’s broader vision for a smart, inclusive city, emphasizing co-design, open data, and transparency. Early results demonstrate significant energy savings (up to 15% for building energy use), measurable reductions in air pollution, increased digital participation, and improved stakeholder coordination. The project’s approach and technologies are designed for scalability, with a focus on governance innovation and financial sustainability to enable replication in other European cities[^2][^3][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

**City Profile:**  
Milan, Italy’s second-largest city, has a population of approximately 1.4 million (city proper) and is a major economic, financial, and cultural hub in northern Italy and Europe. The city is characterized by a strong service economy, a vibrant design and fashion sector, and significant academic and research activity. Milan has faced challenges related to air quality, traffic congestion, social inequality, and the need for urban regeneration, particularly in aging or underutilized neighborhoods. The city is a leader in national urban innovation, regularly piloting new policies in mobility, energy, and governance[^2][^3].

**Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
Porta Romana–Vettabbia is a largely brownfield district, historically divided by former railway infrastructure and marked by poor energy efficiency in existing buildings. The area suffered from fragmented land use, limited connectivity, and socio-economic disparities, with a mix of aging social housing, industrial relics, and low-income populations. The redevelopment plan targets a 216,614 m² site (former railway yard), aiming to reconnect the historic city center with Milan’s agricultural periphery. Plans include new mixed housing (private and social), multimodal transport nodes, and a large public park (187,226 m²), with a focus on sustainability and inclusion[^2][^3][^4].

**Pre-Initiative Conditions:**  
- **Problems/Challenges:**  
  - Energy inefficiency and high emissions in aging social housing.
  - Limited mobility choices; car dependency; fragmented transport.
  - Social isolation and lack of digital engagement.
  - Poor air quality and environmental degradation.
  - Disconnected urban fabric, hindering economic and social integration[^2][^4].

- **Triggering Events:**  
  - EU Horizon 2020 call for lighthouse smart city projects.
  - Milan’s adoption of ambitious smart city and sustainability plans post-2012.
  - Community demand for regeneration and improved living conditions[^2][^3].

- **Stakeholder Landscape (Pre-Initiative):**  
  - City government (urban planning, sustainability, social policy).
  - Social housing tenants and local residents.
  - Utility operators and transport agencies.
  - Civil society and neighborhood associations[^2][^3].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**
- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by Comune di Milano, in coordination with national agencies, EU Horizon 2020, and private sector/NGO partners.
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Milan Smart City Strategic Plan.
  - Urban development, energy efficiency, and mobility plans (citywide)[^2].
  - National and EU legislation on energy efficiency and data protection (notably, GDPR compliance).
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Horizon 2020 funding and reporting requirements enabled innovation.
  - Legacy systems and fragmented data governance posed barriers.
  - National regulations on energy retrofitting and digital infrastructure influenced project design[^2][^3].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**
- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Achieve deep energy savings in buildings via retrofitting.
  - Integrate smart, clean mobility solutions.
  - Foster citizen engagement and behavioral change via digital platforms.
  - Create scalable, replicable models for sustainable urban transformation[^2][^3][^1].

- **Vision Statement:**  
  - A connected, energy-efficient, citizen-centered urban neighborhood that serves as a model for European smart cities.

- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Fully integrated with Milan’s citywide smart city and climate adaptation strategies.
  - Contributes to EU goals for decarbonization, digitalization, and social inclusion[^2][^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**
- **Theory of Change:**  
  - If the district’s housing stock is retrofitted and equipped with smart monitoring, and citizens are engaged through digital platforms, energy consumption will decrease, emissions will fall, and social trust in digital infrastructure will rise, leading to sustainable, inclusive urban regeneration[^2][^1].

- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Social housing tenants, local residents, commuters, small businesses, and the broader Milanese community[^2][^3].

- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Urban data platforms for integrated monitoring and management.
  - Digital social markets ("SharingMi") to incentivize sustainable behaviors.
  - Open, GDPR-compliant data sharing and user-customized dashboards[^2][^1].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**
- **Political:**  
  - Local leadership’s commitment to smart, sustainable city agenda; EU policy incentives.
- **Economic:**  
  - Need for urban regeneration, economic revitalization, and attracting investment.
- **Social:**  
  - Demand for improved quality of life, connectivity, and inclusive neighborhoods.
- **Environmental:**  
  - Pressure to reduce emissions, improve air quality, and increase climate resilience.
- **Technological:**  
  - Availability of IoT, smart energy, and e-mobility solutions; EU push for digital innovation[^2][^3][^1].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Citizen-centered smart city development, prioritizing co-design, open data, and sustainability.
  - Emphasis on replicability, interoperability, and ecosystem-based innovation[^2][^3].

- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Deep retrofitting of 21,000 m² private and 4,000 m² public buildings.
  - Installation of 60 EV charge points, 125 parking bays, 62 e-cars, 150 e-bikes, 14 e-mobility stations[^3].
  - Creation of a large public park and new mobility infrastructure (multimodal station)[^2].

- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Sequenced from building retrofits and infrastructure installation to full deployment of digital/social platforms and ongoing monitoring.
  - Pilot phase in Porta Romana–Vettabbia, with intent to scale citywide[^2][^3].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Urban Data Platform (USP): integrates data on energy, mobility, environment.
  - Smart building systems: sensors for real-time monitoring, smart meters, digital controls[^2].
  - Smart lamp posts: LoraWAN connectivity, air/noise/traffic sensors.
  - E-mobility: EV charging, e-car/e-bike sharing, e-logistics vehicles.
  - Digital Social Market: "SharingMi" app/website for citizen engagement[^2][^3][^1].

- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological: IoT, data integration, digital dashboards.
  - Social: co-design, behavioral incentives, user-centered reporting.
  - Governance: new models for data management, multi-stakeholder collaboration[^2][^1].

- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Interoperability via standard APIs.
  - Data sharing between building, transport, and environmental systems.
  - Citizen-facing dashboards for transparency and engagement[^2][^3].

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive tenders for technology providers and retrofit contractors.
  - Consortium model for EU Horizon 2020 partners[^1].

- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Co-design workshops with residents and local associations.
  - Regular public events, digital outreach, and platform onboarding for citizen users[^1].

- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Citizens invited to co-create digital services and provide feedback through “SharingMi.”
  - Incentive schemes to reward sustainable behaviors[^2][^1].

- **Project Management:**  
  - Led by Comune di Milano, supported by a cross-sector consortium.
  - Structured project milestones, regular reporting to EU funders, and adaptive governance[^1].

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - €25 million for the broader Sharing Cities program (three lighthouse cities); Milan’s share includes building retrofits, digital platforms, and mobility infrastructure[^1].
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Majority EU Horizon 2020 funds; municipal contributions; leveraged private investment.
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - User fees for e-mobility; potential monetization of digital services in the future.
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Business model reviews and replication plans built into project lifecycle; intent to scale via public-private partnerships[^1].

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical:**  
  - Integration of legacy systems with new digital platforms.
  - Ensuring interoperability across diverse data sources and devices[^2].

- **Financial:**  
  - Complexity of aligning EU, municipal, and private funding cycles.
  - Long-term operating costs for digital infrastructure.

- **Social and Political:**  
  - Achieving sustained user engagement, especially among digitally excluded groups.
  - Building trust around data privacy and digital participation[^2][^1].

- **Administrative:**  
  - Complex partnership coordination across sectors and countries.
  - Bureaucratic delays in procurement and approvals.

- **Environmental:**  
  - Potential disruption during construction/retrofit phases.
  - Need to ensure green space and biodiversity amid urban densification.

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Incremental integration of new and existing systems.
  - Targeted outreach and onboarding for residents.
  - Iterative design of digital services to match user needs[^2][^1].

- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjustments to retrofit packages based on occupant feedback.
  - Expansion of e-mobility options (e.g., adding e-bikes with child seats)[^2].

- **Lessons Learned:**  
  - Importance of continuous engagement and support for digital platforms.
  - Flexibility in governance and partnership models to adapt to local contexts[^1].

  - New e-mobility services and incentives for sustainable behavior[^2][^1].

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Comprehensive integration** of retrofitting, mobility, and digital participation.
- **Replicable, open model** with documented lessons and toolkits for other cities[^1].
- **Inclusive design** that prioritizes resident engagement and social equity.
- **Robust monitoring** and transparent data governance.

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Challenges in legacy system integration** and data interoperability.
- **Sustaining digital engagement** beyond pilot phases requires continuous investment and adaptation.
- **Potential for gentrification** if not balanced with social housing and affordability safeguards.

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- Proactive inclusion of social housing tenants and lower-income groups.
- Attention to digital divide, with tailored onboarding and support.
- Co-design and participatory governance fostered procedural equity.

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Planned scale-up** within Milan and transfer to other European cities.
- **Continuous evolution** of digital platforms and incentive schemes.
- Ongoing challenge: maintaining stakeholder engagement and adapting to new technologies and user needs.
- **Potential for influence** on EU smart city policy and further urban regeneration initiatives.
