# Executive summary## Lyon Confluence Smart Community

### **I. INITIATIVE OVERVIEW AND IDENTIFICATION**

#### **Basic Information**

- **Initiative Title:** Lyon Confluence Smart Community
- **Alternative Names:** Lyon Smart Community, Smarter Together Lyon, ASCEND Demo Site Lyon Confluence
- **Location:** Lyon, Confluence District, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France
- **Geographic Coordinates:** Approx. 45.743° N, 4.822° E
- **Timeline:**  
  - Project conceptualization: Early 2000s  
  - Main redevelopment phases: 2003–present  
  - Lyon Smart Community demonstration: 2011–2017  
  - Smarter Together: 2016–2021  
  - ASCEND (Positive Clean Energy District): 2022–2028 (ongoing)
- **Project Scale:**  
  - Redevelopment area: 150 hectares  
  - Buildings: Over 1,000,000 m² new construction (planned), 35,000–70,000 m² refurbished buildings  
  - Population affected: Several thousand residents, with 1,347 dwellings in the ASCEND phase (55% social housing)[^1]
- **Leading Organization(s):**  
  - SPL Lyon Confluence (Local Public Redevelopment Company)  
  - Métropole de Lyon  
  - NEDO (New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization, Japan)  
  - Toshiba (technology partner)  
  - European Commission (Horizon 2020/Smarter Together, ASCEND)[^2][^3]
- **Funding Sources:**  
  - Public investment (city, regional, EU)  
  - Private sector (developers, technology companies)  
  - International aid (notably €50M from NEDO for demonstration projects)[^2][^3]  
  - PPP models

#### **Executive Summary**

Lyon Confluence Smart Community is a multi-phase, multi-faceted urban regeneration initiative in Lyon’s Confluence district, designed to be a model for sustainable, resilient, and smart urban neighborhoods. The project integrates advanced energy management systems, building digital twins, renewable energy generation, and innovative mobility solutions within a redeveloped urban district previously characterized by industrial decline. Its ambition is to demonstrate that large-scale urban transformation can achieve net-zero carbon emissions even as density and activity increase.

Key technical innovations include a Community Energy Management System (CEMS), district-wide digital twins, energy-efficient building retrofitting, district heating from biomass cogeneration, and massive deployment of rooftop photovoltaics. The initiative also pioneers demand-side management, real-time analytics, and community engagement through digital tools and participatory platforms. Social objectives are central: substantial shares of social housing, co-designed public spaces, and citizen involvement in energy decision-making. Environmental goals include reducing energy consumption by 25%, boosting local renewable energy shares, and improving air quality.

Lyon Confluence Smart Community stands out in the European urban landscape for its scale, technological integration, and governance: a public-private partnership with participatory elements, aligned with Lyon’s climate neutrality targets for 2030 and the EU’s smart city vision. The project’s lessons on stakeholder coordination, adaptive design, and digital innovation are widely regarded as best practice, informing future replications across Europe[^3][^2][^1].

### **II. CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

#### **Urban Context**

- **City Profile:**  
  - Lyon: France’s third-largest city, population ~1.5 million (metro area).  
  - Economic base: Industry, finance, research, and culture.  
  - Geography: Located at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers, historically an industrial hub, now a leader in innovation and sustainability[^3][^1].
- **Neighborhood/District Characteristics:**  
  - Confluence: Southern tip of Lyon’s central peninsula, formerly industrial/logistics zone.  
  - Historical pattern: Brownfield redevelopment since early 2000s, focused on mixed-use, sustainability, and heritage retrofitting[^4].  
  - Land use: Residential (including social housing), offices, retail, schools, cultural venues, green/public spaces[^1].  
  - Demographics: Diverse, with strong emphasis on social inclusion (e.g., 55% of new dwellings as social housing)[^1].  
  - Infrastructure: Modernized transport, district heating, smart energy networks, high-quality public spaces[^5].  
  - Environmental: Remediation of contaminated sites, creation of green corridors, focus on flood resilience and urban cooling[^5][^1].

#### **Pre-Initiative Conditions**

- **Problems/Challenges Identified:**  
  - Industrial decline, underused land, environmental contamination.  
  - High energy consumption, poor air quality, lack of modern infrastructure.  
  - Social fragmentation and limited affordable housing[^4][^6].
- **Triggering Events:**  
  - Strategic city decision to redevelop Confluence as a showcase for sustainable urbanism.  
  - Policy alignment with EU and national climate targets[^2][^6].
- **Stakeholder Landscape:**  
  - City government, SPL Lyon Confluence, residents (including social housing tenants), private developers, technology partners (Toshiba, NEDO), NGOs, and the European Commission[^2][^3].

#### **Policy and Regulatory Environment**

- **Governance Framework:**  
  - Led by SPL Lyon Confluence (semi-public redevelopment authority), Métropole de Lyon, with strong public-private and international partnerships[^6].
- **Relevant Policies:**  
  - Lyon’s Territorial Climate and Energy Plan, Grand Lyon Energy Master Plan, national energy efficiency action plans, EU Horizon 2020 smart city programs[^2][^3].
- **Legal and Institutional Enablers/Barriers:**  
  - Supportive legal structure for urban regeneration, but strict heritage preservation regulations (for retrofitting historic buildings)[^4].  
  - Contracts with compulsory milestones for energy performance[^4].

### **III. INITIATIVE RATIONALE AND OBJECTIVES**

#### **Strategic Vision**

- **Primary Goals:**  
  - Achieve zero-carbon growth in the district despite an increase in built area and population[^3][^7].  
  - Demonstrate integration of smart energy management, renewable energy, and sustainable mobility.
- **Vision Statement:**  
  - “Build a future-oriented city where advanced energy technologies and participatory governance achieve climate neutrality and improve quality of life.”[^2][^1]
- **Alignment with Broader Plans:**  
  - Lyon 2030: Climate-neutral city program[^8].  
  - EU Climate and Energy targets (Europe 2020, Horizon 2020)[^2][^3].

#### **Problem-Solution Logic**

- **Theory of Change:**  
  - Digitally enabled energy management and community involvement will reduce energy consumption, increase renewable use, and foster social cohesion, leading to improved sustainability and resilience[^2][^3][^1].
- **Target Beneficiaries:**  
  - Local residents (including vulnerable groups), city government, businesses, broader urban region[^1][^4].
- **Innovation Elements:**  
  - Digital twins for buildings and neighborhoods[^1].  
  - Centralized energy management and predictive analytics[^3][^2].  
  - Community Energy Management System (CEMS)[^9][^2].  
  - Large-scale renewable energy integration and smart mobility solutions[^5][^1].

#### **Drivers and Motivations**

- **Political Drivers:**  
  - Municipal leadership prioritizing climate action; pressure to meet EU/national targets[^2][^3].
- **Economic Drivers:**  
  - Attract investment, foster innovation, create jobs, raise property values[^2][^1].
- **Social Drivers:**  
  - Improve quality of life, offer affordable housing, engage citizens[^5][^4].
- **Environmental Drivers:**  
  - Reduce greenhouse gases, improve air quality, enhance biodiversity[^3][^1].
- **Technological Drivers:**  
  - Leverage smart city trends, digital platforms, and advanced control systems[^2][^1].

### **IV. DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH**

#### **Project Design**

- **Conceptual Framework:**  
  - Sustainable urban regeneration; “smart district” integrating environmental, social, and technological innovation[^6][^3].
- **Physical/Spatial Design:**  
  - Mixed-use buildings, green public spaces, modern infrastructure (district heating, EV charging, logistics hub)[^1].
  - Heritage retrofitting and new construction to high energy performance standards[^4].
- **Scope and Phasing:**  
  - Multi-phase: brownfield remediation, infrastructure upgrades, building retrofits, deployment of smart technologies, ongoing citizen engagement[^1][^4].

#### **Technologies and Innovations**

- **Technologies Deployed:**  
  - Community Energy Management System (CEMS)[^9][^10]
  - Building and neighborhood digital twins[^1]
  - District heating (biomass cogeneration)[^3][^1]
  - Rooftop photovoltaics and on-site solar generation (1–2 MWp total)[^3][^1][^2]
  - Thermal storage (phase change materials, smart batteries)[^10]
  - Smart building controls: sensors, energy monitoring tablets, predictive analytics[^5][^10]
  - EV car-sharing and smart mobility hubs[^3][^1][^2]
- **Innovation Type:**  
  - Technological (energy, ICT, mobility), social (community engagement), financial (PPP, new revenue models), governance (participatory planning)[^2][^6][^1]
- **Integration Approach:**  
  - Digital platforms connect multiple buildings, enabling real-time and predictive energy management, demand-side response, and collective renewable self-consumption[^3][^1][^10]

#### **Implementation Methodology**

- **Procurement and Contracting:**  
  - Competitive selection for construction and technology partners (Toshiba, NEDO, local firms)[^10][^2]
  - Contracts with performance milestones (energy, environment)[^4]
- **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
  - Urban Living Lab: citizen co-design, regular consultations[^5][^1][^3]
- **Participatory Elements:**  
  - Community energy management; use of tablets for residents to monitor/control energy use[^5][^9]
- **Project Management:**  
  - SPL Lyon Confluence as coordinating entity; cross-sectoral project teams[^6][^3]

#### **Financing Model**

- **Budget and Costs:**  
  - Demonstration phase: €50M from NEDO; additional public/private investments for infrastructure, buildings, and technologies[^2]
- **Funding Mix:**  
  - Public (city, EU, region), private (developers, tech companies), international (NEDO, Horizon 2020)[^2][^3]
- **Revenue Mechanisms:**  
  - District heating fees, EV car-sharing service, energy savings, land value capture[^3][^2]
- **Financial Sustainability:**  
  - Long-term revenue from energy services and district operations; innovative business models for energy communities[^4][^1]

### **V. CHALLENGES AND ADAPTIVE RESPONSES**

#### **Implementation Challenges**

- **Technical Challenges:**  
  - Complex integration of building digital twins, CEMS, and real-time control across diverse building types[^1][^10]
  - Retrofitting heritage buildings while meeting energy standards[^4]
  - Data management, interoperability, and cybersecurity[^3][^10]
- **Financial Challenges:**  
  - High upfront costs for deep retrofitting and smart infrastructure[^4]
  - Ensuring long-term financial viability for energy services[^2]
- **Social and Political Challenges:**  
  - Coordinating multiple stakeholders, balancing interests of public, private, and residents[^4][^5][^6]
  - Ensuring equitable access to benefits (housing, energy cost savings)[^1][^5]
- **Administrative Challenges:**  
  - Bureaucratic delays, regulatory constraints (especially for heritage buildings)[^4]
- **Environmental Challenges:**  
  - Remediation of contaminated land, managing urban heat island effect, air quality improvements[^5][^1]

#### **Adaptive Strategies**

- **Problem-Solving Approaches:**  
  - Combined retrofitting with renovations to reduce costs, staged implementation of technologies[^4]
  - Strong contracts with enforceable energy performance milestones, and participatory governance to manage stakeholder coordination[^4][^6]
- **Design Modifications:**  
  - Adjusted building designs to incorporate new energy standards and renewable integration[^4][^1]
- **Lessons Learned During Implementation:**  
  - Importance of citizen engagement and co-design  
  - Need for interoperability and scalability of digital platforms  
  - Effective governance structures are key to successful coordination[^4][^6][^1]

### **VI. OUTCOMES AND IMPACTS**

#### **Direct Outputs**

- **Physical Outputs:**  
  - Over 600,000 m² of high environmental performance buildings constructed (as of 2022)[^4]  
  - 70,000 m² of historic buildings retrofitted[^4]  
  - 1–2 MWp of rooftop solar PV, biomass cogeneration plant, district heating upgrades[^3][^1][^2]
- **Service Delivery:**  
  - Community energy management, smart mobility services, digital energy monitoring for residents[^5][^2][^10]
- **Quantitative Metrics:**  
  - 25% reduction in neighborhood energy consumption[^3]  
  - Up to 60% less energy use in positive-energy buildings (Hikari project)[^2]  
  - 67% of electricity and 87% of heat provided by renewables in pilot buildings[^2]

#### **Social Impacts**

- **Community Benefits:**  
  - Reduced energy bills, improved air quality, better public spaces[^3][^5][^1]
- **Equity Outcomes:**  
  - Large share of social housing; special energy pricing for vulnerable groups[^1]
- **Displacement and Gentrification:**  
  - Managed through inclusive planning and affordable housing mandates[^1][^4]
- **Social Capital:**  
  - Stronger sense of community ownership over energy resources; increased civic engagement[^5][^1]
- **Cultural Impacts:**  
  - Heritage preservation through respectful retrofitting[^4]

#### **Economic Impacts**

- **Job Creation:**  
  - Construction, retrofitting, technology deployment, ongoing energy services[^2]
- **Economic Activity:**  
  - Attraction of investment, increased property values, business growth in the district[^2][^1]
- **Cost Savings:**  
  - Significant reductions in energy bills for residents and operating costs for public buildings[^3][^2]
- **Economic Inclusion:**  
  - Social housing provision, local employment opportunities[^1][^4]

#### **Environmental Impacts**

- **Climate and Emissions:**  
  - Significant reduction in greenhouse gases, contributing to Lyon’s climate neutrality goals[^3][^8]
- **Resource Management:**  
  - Efficient water, energy, and material use; innovative systems for storage and reuse[^5][^10][^1]
- **Environmental Quality:**  
  - Improved air quality, expanded green spaces, reduced urban heat island effect[^1][^5]
- **Waste and Pollution:**  
  - Enhanced waste management, reduced pollution through clean mobility and energy[^1][^5]
- **Sustainability Metrics:**  
  - Positive Energy Buildings, district-wide energy and carbon monitoring against EU standards[^2][^3]

#### **Governance and Institutional Impacts**

- **Institutional Capacity:**  
  - Strengthened local government and SPL Lyon Confluence through innovative project management[^6]
- **Policy Influence:**  
  - Model for new policies in urban energy management and sustainable development, informing local and European best practice[^3][^2][^8]
- **Transparency and Accountability:**  
  - Enhanced through digital data platforms and participatory governance[^3][^5]

#### **Performance Against Objectives**

- **Success Metrics:**  
  - Achieved substantial energy and carbon savings, improved quality of life, advanced digital integration[^3][^2][^1]
- **Unintended Consequences:**  
  - Slow project delivery due to technical and stakeholder complexity[^4]
- **Long-term Viability:**  
  - Ongoing adaptation, scaling, and replication in other Lyon districts and European cities[^8][^1]

### **VIII. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT AND REFLECTION**

#### **Strengths of the Initiative**

- **Technological Integration:**  
  - Advanced digital infrastructure, real-time energy management, renewable energy deployment[^1][^3][^2]
- **Social Inclusion:**  
  - Large share of social housing, participatory planning, citizen empowerment[^1][^5][^4]
- **Environmental Leadership:**  
  - Significant reductions in energy use and emissions, improved urban environment[^3][^1][^2]
- **Governance Innovation:**  
  - Effective public-private partnership, strong project management, enforceable contracts[^6][^4]

#### **Limitations and Weaknesses**

- **Complexity and Cost:**  
  - High upfront investment, slow rollout due to technical and stakeholder complexity[^4]
- **Heritage Constraints:**  
  - Retrofitting historic buildings presents regulatory and technical challenges[^4]
- **Data Integration:**  
  - Need for continued improvement in interoperability and cybersecurity[^10][^3]

#### **Equity and Justice Considerations**

- **Advancement:**  
  - Prioritization of social housing, special energy pricing, strong community engagement[^1][^5]
- **Risks:**  
  - Potential for gentrification if affordability measures weaken over time[^4]

#### **Future Outlook**

- **Long-term Trajectory:**  
  - Ongoing expansion, scaling to other Lyon districts, integration into Lyon’s climate-neutral strategy for 2030[^8][^1]
- **Ongoing Challenges:**  
  - Ensuring financial sustainability, maintaining inclusivity, adapting to new technological standards[^2][^4]
- **Next Phases:**  
  - Further digitalization, deeper citizen participation, enhanced environmental monitoring, replication in other European cities[^8][^1]
